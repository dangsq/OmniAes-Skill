# Simpsonwave

Simpsonwave is an audiovisual microgenre and internet meme that coalesced on Vine and YouTube in the mid-to-late 2010s as a subgenre of Vaporwave. Its formula is specific: clips from the "classic era" of *The Simpsons* (roughly the first eleven seasons) are slowed, looped and re-edited to Vaporwave or lo-fi electronic music, then degraded to mimic a worn VHS tape and bathed in a surreal pastel grade — often called "codeine purple." The result recontextualizes comedic broadcast moments into something melancholic, nostalgic and poignant: a fading memory of a childhood show that never existed quite this way. The style lives in still images as much as in video, because a single graded, static-speckled frame of a lonely cartoon character carries the whole mood.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The defining rule is a single veil of purple-pink pulled over everything; no local color survives untouched. The grade sits on an axis from pale dusty rose-lavender (around `#D8A8C8`, `#C9A0DC`) through mid orchid (`#9A6BB0`, `#8E5AA8`) down to deep codeine violet (`#4A2E6E`, `#3B2A5F`); night interiors and bedrooms drop into indigo-blue (`#2B2A55`, `#3A3A6E`); psychedelic variants push skies and clouds to neon magenta and electric purple (`#D857E0`, `#7A3FE0`) while keeping shadows violet. Washed-out pinks and periwinkle blues (`#E8B8D8`, `#A8A0D0`) fill highlights and haze. The characters' signature yellow survives only in muted form — amber-gold (`#E0B860`) or flushed pink-tan (`#D89898`) — never the saturated broadcast yellow. Blacks are lifted and milky; whites bloom toward pink. (The source text names only "washed-out purples, pinks, and blues" and "codeine purple"; the hex ranges above are a distillation of that grading rule, not a fixed swatch list.)

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the text documents them

1. **Classic-era Simpsons source material.** Cel-animated characters and Springfield sets: flat matte color fields, uniform black outlines, minimal shading, spiky hair silhouettes, overbites, pearl necklaces, suburbia of fences, sidewalks, bungalows, classrooms and car interiors. This is the non-negotiable core.
2. **VHS distortion.** Static speckle, scan lines, tracking errors and head-switching noise, soft blur, low resolution, color bleed, chromatic aberration fringing (red/cyan edges on outlines), sometimes on-screen display artifacts: "SP," playback timestamps, PLAY triangles, torn or distressed white tape-border frames, letterbox bars.
3. **Pastel purple/pink color grade** as described above — the "dreamlike and unnatural feel."
4. **Glitches and psychedelic surrealism.** Mirroring, cascading or repeated images, floating vaporwave geometry (wireframe triangles, grids, starfields), sparkle and star particles, drifting heart or emoji graphics; surreal juxtapositions that break the sitcom's logic.
5. **Melancholic text.** Fullwidth / wide-spaced vaporwave typography (the ＳＵＮＤＡＹ ＳＣＨＯＯＬ manner), sad captions, timestamps and tribute-style badges; text is sparse, soft and mournful when present.
6. **Sad Bart iconography** (the style's later emo-rap-influenced offshoot): Bart or another character alone, smoking, crying, staring out a window, slumped on a bed — comedy figures turned into symbols of modern melancholy.
7. **Vaporwave props and poster collages.** Cassette tapes, checkerboard planes, logo-lockup poster layouts with characters framed in triangles — secondary, borrowed from the parent genre.

### Materials and textures

Two layers always coexist: underneath, the flat printed-cel texture of 90s broadcast animation (even color, hard outlines, no gradient shading); on top, the physical wear of magnetic tape — grain, noise flecks, smudged chroma, gentle horizontal tear lines, compression mush. Nothing looks crisp, glossy, photographic or rendered in 3D. Surfaces read as *recorded*, not *drawn*: the image behaves like a paused tape.

### Recurring subjects

Solitary characters held in stillness: a child sitting alone among empty classroom desks; a figure slumped or lying on a bed at night; a character smoking or crying against a dark ground; someone gazing out of a car window or house window while rain or haze softens the background; a parent driving with a pink sky in the glass; a lone figure walking or standing on a suburban sidewalk under an impossible violet sky. Empty or near-empty Springfield spaces carry as much weight as the characters: bedrooms, kitchens, car interiors, school rooms, streets, yards, fences.

### Composition and lighting

Compositions favor isolation: one character centered or offset in medium close-up, surrounded by soft blurred environment; or wider shots where a small figure sits inside a large hazy suburban frame. Letterbox bars and 4:3 broadcast framing are common. Poster/collage variants exist (geometry frames, logo type, cassette props) but are the minority register. Lighting is the flat, sourceless light of cel animation re-graded into mood: bloomed highlights, milky shadows, night scenes lit by indigo ambience or a single lamp glow, car windows glowing pink, psychedelic skies carrying their own neon luminance and sparkle. Contrast stays low; the whole frame feels exhaled.

## Mood & Values

Nostalgia, melancholy, surrealism, dreamy ennui, deconstruction. Simpsonwave treats a universally familiar cartoon as a millennial / Gen Z memory object and deliberately degrades it so it looks like recollection rather than footage — distant, softened, half-lost. By stripping the comedic context, the edits expose the sadness, anxiety and existential dread latent in the characters: Homer's failure, Lisa's depression, Bart's loneliness. The valued feeling is poignant stillness, not joke and not terror: funny scenes made sad, sad scenes made tender.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s, online: a 2006 YouTube nostalgia edit prefigures it, but the phenomenon coalesced in 2015–2016 on Vine and YouTube — an early looping clip set to HOME's "Resonance" established the formula; the term "Simpsonwave" was coined by UK student Lucien Hughes, whose "ＳＵＮＤＡＹ ＳＣＨＯＯＬ" (Season 1 footage over Blank Banshee) became the genre's landmark, spread further by Vice, Pitchfork and FrankJavCee's satirical "How To" video. The style later fed the "Sad Bart" meme aesthetic of the mid-to-late 2010s SoundCloud and emo rap scene. It is a reaction format: born of tape-nostalgia and Vaporwave's irony-tinged longing, applied to the most familiar cartoon of a generation's childhood.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Start from classic-era cel-animation logic: flat colors, hard black outlines, Simpsons-style character silhouettes and Springfield suburbia. No digital-era sharpness.
- Grade first, degrade second: pull the entire frame toward purple-pink, then lay tape wear (grain, static, tracking lines, chromatic fringing, soft blur) over it. The scene must stay fully legible beneath the damage.
- Isolate one character in a still, melancholic pose; let empty space and haze do the emotional work.
- Imply slowed, looped time: paused gestures, drifting smoke, held gazes, repeated or mirrored figures.
- Add surrealism sparingly and dreamlike: neon clouds, sparkles, floating hearts, wireframe triangles, starfields — never aggressive corruption.
- Text, if any, is sparse, melancholic, vaporwave-typed (fullwidth letters, wide tracking) or tape-OSD (timestamps, SP, PLAY).

**Variation axes.** Subject: any classic-era-style character or Springfield interior/exterior — classroom, bedroom, car, sidewalk, yard, kitchen — including original characters drawn in that cel idiom. Composition: tight face close-up through wide empty-street shot, letterboxed or 4:3, plus the minority poster/collage register. Lighting: hazy violet daylight, indigo night, pink-window car interiors, lamp-lit rooms, neon psychedelic skies. Color handling: anywhere on the pale-rose to deep-codeine-violet axis, with blue-night and neon-magenta extremes allowed. Mood: quiet melancholy through surreal euphoria to grief. Degradation: subtle grain and fringing up to heavy static and tracking tear. Overlays: none, OSD-only, geometry/sparkle-only, or full poster lockup. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Crisp HD digital-era cartoon look: sharp lines, clean gradient shading, bright untouched primaries, sky-blue and grass-green without the purple veil.
- Photorealism, live action, 3D rendering, or clean modern vector flatness with no tape wear.
- Corruption as subject: datamoshing, pixel sorting or shred-the-figure abstraction (that is Glitch Art).
- Deep-fried saturation, cluttered ironic collage, meme-stamp chaos (Trxsh).
- Cozy warm lo-fi palettes (creams, browns, soft greens) with clean line art and study-beats comfort.
- Emo-rap grunge: photographic portraits, tattoos, skulls, black-red contrast, trap typography dominating the frame.
- Cheerful comedic staging, action poses, laugh-track energy; high-key studio lighting; modern UI elements rendered crisply.
- Pure Vaporwave iconography as the subject (classical statues, retro OS windows, palm grids) with no cartoon world present.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Overlap | Simpsonwave tell |
|---|---|---|
| Sadboi | Melancholy, purple haze, crying figures | Sadboi leans on anime or photographic subjects, tear motifs and confessional text; Simpsonwave always keeps broadcast-cel flatness plus tape wear and the Springfield world |
| Emo Rap | Sad Bart offshoot: smoking, loneliness, trap pairing | Emo Rap covers are photographic, grungy, high-contrast black/red with tattoo and skull iconography; Simpsonwave stays pastel-purple, soft, cel-animated |
| Glitch Art | Glitches, distortion | Glitch Art makes corruption the subject and abstracts the image; Simpsonwave's damage is tape *wear* over a fully legible, emotionally staged scene |
| Lo-fi Art | Chill nostalgia, muted color | Lo-fi is cozy, warm, clean-lined, detailed interiors; Simpsonwave is degraded, uncanny, violet, sourced from 90s broadcast cartoon |
| Trxsh | Ironic meme lineage | Trxsh is deep-fried, cluttered, compression-as-joke; Simpsonwave's compression is mournful, slow, and unified under one gentle grade |
| Vaporwave (parent) | Purple, geometry, fullwidth type | Vaporwave's icons are statues, old OS UI, palms and grids; Simpsonwave substitutes Simpsons clips and Springfield as the sole source iconography |

Where the record is thin, say so: the wiki documents the audio pairing (slowed, reverb-drenched Vaporwave and lo-fi tracks) as half of the genre, but a still image can only imply it through paused, loop-like stillness and tape artifacts; likewise, exact hex values are a distillation of the documented "washed-out purples, pinks, and blues," not a canonized swatch.
