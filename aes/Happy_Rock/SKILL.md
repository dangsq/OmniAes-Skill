# Happy Rock (Colorido)

Colorido ("colorful" in Portuguese), also called Happy Rock, Banda Colorida or Happy Punk, was a Brazilian youth subculture that flourished around 2010 around the pop-rock genre of the same name. Pioneered by the band Restart and carried by Cine, Hori, Replace and Lipstick, it was amplified by MTV Brasil and Capricho magazine into a massive, short-lived teen phenomenon. It arose as a deliberate, optimistic counterpoint to the melancholic emo wave that had dominated Brazil between 2005 and 2008, and it was dismissed by rock purists as "pop boy bands with guitars" — a critique that actually describes its visual DNA well: boy-band coordination, guitar-band staging, and a wardrobe of pure candy saturation. By 2011–2012 media oversaturation and commercialization ended the movement, which freezes the style firmly in the late-2000s/early-2010s Fotolog–Orkut–MySpace teen web era.

## Visual Identity

### Color language
The rule is an "explosion of colors": solid, saturated, often neon primaries and secondaries worn as blocks, with each member of a group claiming a different signature hue — most canonically in the skinny jeans. Working palette range: cherry red (#E60023–#FF2D2D), tangerine (#FF7A00), lemon yellow (#FFD400–#FFE81A), kelly and lime green (#00A651–#7ED321), turquoise and cyan (#00C2C7–#14B8C4), electric blue (#0085FF), hot pink/magenta (#FF2E93–#FF4FA3), violet (#7B2FBE–#8A2BE2). Backgrounds run to high-key seamless white, saturated daylight blue sky, or — in the graphic layer — pastel rainbow striping (#FFB3D9, #FFF3A0, #B8F2B0, #A0D8FF). Black and white appear only as grounding neutrals: a patent vest, a tee print, eyeliner, a press-wall backdrop. The style's earliest neutral black-and-white phase is historical footnote, not the canon; mud, earth tones, desaturation and monochrome are foreign to it.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the source documents them)
1. **Colorful skinny jeans** — the iconic garment; solid bright denim or coated/metallic sheen; one color per person within a group.
2. **Customized graphic T-shirts** — short-sleeved or V-neck, cartoon characters, prints, checks, peace signs; layered under vests or open shirts.
3. **Straight hair with long bangs/fringe**, often with colored streaks or spiked at the back; androgynous grooming; eyeliner; colored nails and makeup regardless of gender.
4. **Statement sunglasses and thick-rim glasses**, plus bangles, scarves, headbands, caps and fingerless gloves as occasional accents.
5. **Lace-up high-top sneakers** — Nike and Converse All Star above all — frequently in contrasting candy colorways.
6. **Leather jackets, zippered coats, glossy patent or quilted puffer vests** as the outerwear layer.
7. **The graphic-design layer**: 2000s Brazilian school-notebook looks (Tilibra-style), notebook-page textures and pencil sketches as seen in Capricho layouts and MTV Brasil vignettes, with Vectordelia and Hipness Purgatory vector gloss — torn-paper sticker banners, starbursts and sparkles, glossy badges, rainbow stripe fields, chunky playful logotypes. This layer is well documented in writing and shows up in wallpapers and promo overlays rather than in every photograph.

### Materials and textures
Patent leather and vinyl gloss; quilted nylon puff; thin cotton jersey; skinny denim, sometimes coated to a slight sheen; canvas sneaker uppers; plastic bangles; matte paper stickers set against glossy vector shapes; vinyl step-and-repeat press backdrops. Surfaces read clean, new and commercial — never worn, distressed or vintage.

### Recurring subjects
Teen pop-rock bands of four to six members; fan groups and clustered crowd snapshots; press-wall event photos; live stage moments; promo cutouts arranged in a row; wallpaper and collage graphics featuring the band. Friendship pile-ups, hugs, leaning bodies and playful hand gestures are the characteristic action.

### Composition and lighting
Group-forward and body-clustered: figures overlap, lean, crouch and stack into a single energetic mass, or line up as a color-blocked roster where each person reads as one hue. Cutout-on-white catalog rows and low-angle wide-angle outdoor shots under a saturated cloud-flecked sky are both typical. Lighting defaults to high-key even studio light on seamless white or flat bright daylight; a concert variant uses blue/magenta stage wash while the clothes keep the candy palette. Graphic overlays sit on top like stickers — angled banners, bursts, logotype slugs. Polished promo and casual fan snapshot are both valid registers.

## Mood & Values
Optimism, fun, youth, friendship, positivity, pop-rock music. The style performs joy as defiance: where emo cultivated solitude and gloom, Colorido cultivates the pack, the grin, the hug. Androgyny is embraced without angst — eyeliner and colored nails on boys are celebratory, not transgressive-in-tone. There is a commercial teen-media sheen to everything: this is a aesthetic made for magazine spreads, TV vignettes and profile-page wallpapers.

## Era & Origins
Brazil, emerging 2009, peaking 2010, declining 2011–2012. It reacts against the Brazilian emo wave (2005–2008) while borrowing energy from US scene bands such as All Time Low, BrokeNCYDE, Boys Like Girls, Forever the Sickest Kids and Danger Radio. Its distribution channels — MTV Brasil, Capricho, Fotolog, Orkut, MySpace, Blogger — and its visual ancestry — 2000s school-supply notebook design, Vectordelia vector gloss, Hipness Purgatory irony-free brightness — date it precisely.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Color-block the people, not just the frame: give each figure one dominant saturated hue, anchored in the skinny jeans.
2. Keep the silhouette skinny and layered: tight jeans, fitted tee, vest or glossy jacket, high-top sneakers. No baggy volume anywhere.
3. Fringe first: straight, flat, side-swept or full bangs, optionally dyed streaks; spiked backs acceptable.
4. Make joy compositional: contact, leaning, hugging, gesturing, grinning; the group is the hero.
5. Light it bright: high-key white seamless, flat noon daylight, saturated sky, or a colored stage wash that never dulls the clothing palette.
6. Add the sticker layer as overlay or background: torn-paper banners, starbursts, notebook ruling, sketch doodles, chunky logotypes — vector gloss or paper matte.
7. Apply androgynous grooming freely: eyeliner, colored nails, bangles on any gender.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a band promo, a fan snapshot, a concert moment, a press-wall event photo, a wallpaper or collage graphic, a notebook-page layout, a product-style cutout row, or (less commonly) a single performer portrait; groups may be boys, girls or mixed. Composition may range from clustered candid chaos to symmetric lineup to isolated cutouts on white. Lighting may range from studio high-key to outdoor sun to stage wash to flat scan-like light for graphic pieces. Color handling defaults to full neon saturation, with pastel rainbow striping permitted in graphic backgrounds and black/white restricted to accent neutrals. Mood may span exuberant playfulness to cool pop confidence. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Dominant black/goth palettes, gloom, melancholic solitary posing (emo territory).
- Baggy jeans, oversized tees, snapback-heavy streetwear silhouettes (jejemon territory).
- Scene's chaotic individualism as the main language: raccoon-tail multi-dye, tutus over leggings, kandi clutter.
- Desaturated film grain, grunge texture, earth tones, boho or minimalist Scandinavian design.
- Monochrome or duotone treatment; pastel-only wardrobes (pastels belong to background graphics, not the jeans).
- Luxury-fashion cues, couture logos, mature adult styling.
- Night-street noir, documentary gloom, distressed or vintage surfaces.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared surface | What separates Happy Rock |
|---|---|---|
| Emo | Skinny jeans, long fringe, eyeliner | Emo runs black/deep red/purple with solitary melancholy and studded hardware; Happy Rock runs neon candy color-blocking, group joy, bright light and sticker graphics |
| Scene | Bright color, skinny cuts, online teen culture | Scene is individualistic and chaotic — multi-color raccoon tails, band-tee clutter, tutus, kandi, glitter-glitch graphics, US/UK roots; Happy Rock is Brazilian, band-coordinated one-hue-per-person, cleaner promo lighting, notebook/vector sticker layer |
| Jejemon | Same late-2000s teen-web moment, bold color | Jejemon favors baggy silhouettes, clashing oversized patterns, snapbacks and a slouched posture; Happy Rock keeps skinny tailored silhouettes, solid candy hues, fringe hair and pop-promo polish |

Quick recognition check: solid neon skinny jeans in a different hue per person + straight dyed fringe + high-key brightness + group grins + sticker/notebook graphic layer = Happy Rock. Remove the joy or the color-blocking and you have drifted into a neighboring subculture.
