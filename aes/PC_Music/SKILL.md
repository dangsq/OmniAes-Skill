# PC Music

## Overview

PC Music is an aesthetic, art collective, and record label founded by producer A. G. Cook in London in 2013, launched on SoundCloud. Its visual language is inseparable from its music (Bubblegum Bass, later mainstreamed as Hyperpop): a deliberate blend of high-production gloss and DIY, internet-native sensibility, built from a nostalgic vision of the late-1990s and early-2000s digital age — corporate branding, stock photography, product advertising, GeoCities pages and 2000s minisites. The imagery simultaneously critiques and celebrates the artificiality of pop music, consumer culture, and the internet itself: a drink can for a drink that does not exist, a pop star who is openly a constructed avatar, a logo colored with the default hyperlink blue (#0000ff) to root the brand in the fabric of the web.

The result reads as advertising from a parallel, shinier universe: sterile, hyper-real, euphoric, and faintly uncanny. Both minimalism and maximalism belong to it — a clean vector emblem on white and a chaotic collage poster crammed with type are equally valid outputs of the same rules.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

Saturation is pushed toward clipping; mid-tones, earth tones and desaturated pastels are structurally absent. The palette behaves as **flat monochrome fields plus metallic/iridescent accents**:

- **Anchor blue:** pure hyperlink blue `#0000FF`, used as full-bleed background or dominant field — the label's signature color, chosen by A. G. Cook.
- **Hyper pinks/magentas:** `#FF00C9`, `#FF5FA2`, hot coral-pink; used as backgrounds, duotone washes on portraits, and cloud-sunset gradients.
- **Neon greens:** acid lime and chartreuse `#9BFF00`, `#CCFF00`, plus kelly green halftone-dot fields.
- **Supporting neons:** pure yellow `#FFE600`, cyan `#00E5FF`, violet-blue `#3300FF`.
- **Metallics:** chrome silver `#C0C0C0`–`#F5F5F5`, gold chrome, and holographic/iridescent rainbow sheen on discs, foil and liquid.
- **Sterile neutrals:** clinical white voids and pure black voids for product staging.

Any single image may lean on one field color (blue-dominant, pink-dominant, lime-dominant, yellow-dominant) or on chrome-on-void; what must hold is full-throttle saturation and the presence of at least one synthetic-gloss element.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how strongly the source documents them)

1. **Glossy, hyper-real CGI** — clean, polished 3D renders: abstract ribbon/scribble strokes, floating objects, surreal chrome architecture adrift in stylized clouds. Sterile and corporate in feel.
2. **Corporate branding and advertisement parody** — beverage cans with splash effects, product hero shots, stock-photo poses (graduation gown, presenter smile), fictional logos and brand names; imagery that satirizes branding while perfectly imitating it.
3. **Early-2000s web design** — hyperlink blue, minisite and GeoCities clutter, warped/curved Flash-era typography, vertical CJK text columns, halftone dot grids, tiny sans-serif labels ("VOLUME"), script fonts layered over photos.
4. **Hyper-real material textures: water, metal, plastic** — liquid frozen mid-splash around a can, polished chrome and gold hardware, coils and springs, glossy plastic, iridescent film.
5. **Devised pop-star personas** — heavily retouched, doll-like portraits: airbrushed skin, glassy eyes, direct centered gaze, stylized styling (bob cuts, pink hats, rhinestone harnesses), presented as avatars rather than people.
6. **Optical media as ornament** (strong in the image record): CDs, jewel cases, holographic disc rainbows, disc-and-chrome-hardware assemblies on blue fields.
7. **Y2K chrome iconography** — desktop-folder icons, screws, hinges and coils rendered as precious metal objects.
8. **Mirror-symmetric flat vector emblems** — pixel-chevron / circuit-like bilateral patterns in acid green with a tiny wordmark at center.
9. **Glossy 3D ornaments** — hearts, bubbles, soft tubes with iridescent gradients; rhinestone and crystal embellishment on costumes.

### Materials and textures

Chrome and polished metal; holographic foil and iridescent plastic; frozen water and syrup-like liquid; satin and gloss fabric; rhinestones and crystals; airbrushed, poreless skin; clean vector edges; halftone dots. Glitchy digital texture appears as an accent layer (type distortion, pixel sorting) over otherwise clean gloss — the source describes a mix of clean lines and simple logos with glitchy textures, so glitch seasons the dish rather than being the dish.

### Recurring subjects

Product cans and packaging; CDs and jewel cases; stylized pop personas (mostly young women, often East-Asian or European-presenting, in constructed styling); abstract CGI ribbon/scribble fields; symmetric emblem graphics; surreal tableaux of figures on chrome slabs or laptops; maximalist collage posters mixing photos, CJK and Latin type, and brand parody.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Sterile product staging:** object centered on a seamless monochrome or white void, advertisement framing, no context, no cast-shadow realism.
- **Flat symmetric graphics:** bilateral mirror symmetry, emblem at center, tiny type.
- **Floating scatter:** objects suspended in saturated space with shallow depth of field (discs drifting, splash droplets).
- **Maximalist poster:** dense, grid-defying collage of photos, stickers, vertical and warped type.
- **Persona portrait:** centered or slightly off-center bust, direct gaze, flat even studio light or CGI-specular light with crisp highlights; alternatively a pink-violet sunset gradient sky with stylized clouds behind the subject.
- Lighting is always *synthetic-looking*: flat studio evenness, hard specular highlights on metal and liquid, or gradient-sky glow. Naturalistic available light, golden-hour warmth and documentary candor are out of register.

## Mood & Values

Artificiality as a virtue and as a question. The mood is euphoric, playful, glossy and slightly uncanny — perfection that announces itself as constructed. Values encoded in the imagery: consumerism both critiqued and celebrated (the ad for the nonexistent drink), internet culture as native homeland, experimental pop sensibility, and nostalgia aimed specifically at the early digital age rather than at any pre-digital past. Sincerity and irony coexist: the portrait is genuinely beautiful and openly fake at the same time.

## Era & Origins

Emerged 2013 in London, United Kingdom, on SoundCloud, around A. G. Cook and a close-knit group of producers and artists (SOPHIE, Hannah Diamond, Danny L Harle, GFOTY, EasyFun; later mainstreamed through Charli XCX and, as a genre name, Hyperpop). It reacts against both indie authenticity-culture and mainstream pop's pretense of sincerity by exaggerating pop's tropes until they become concept art: the 2014 QT project — a fictional pop star whose single was an advertisement for a nonexistent energy drink — is the archetype. Visual sources are late-1990s/early-2000s corporate branding, advertising, stock photography and cyberculture, filtered through a 2010s conceptual-art sensibility.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build the image as if it were an advertisement for a product, persona or event that may not exist; brand-parody logic over narrative logic.
2. Push saturation to the edge of clipping; choose one dominant field color from the palette rule and let metallic/iridescent accents carry the highlights.
3. Include at least one hyper-real material study: frozen liquid, polished chrome, holographic plastic, or poreless retouched skin.
4. Treat any human as a constructed avatar: stylized, retouched to doll-like smoothness, posed like stock photography or cover art, never candid.
5. Let typography participate: either a tiny clean sans wordmark (minimalist strand) or warped/vertical/collaged type (maximalist web strand). Both strands are in-style.
6. Keep lighting synthetic: flat studio evenness, crisp CGI speculars, or gradient-sky glow. No atmospheric realism.
7. Balance sterile corporate cleanliness with one internet-native "wrong" note — halftone dots, curved Flash type, a sticker, a glitch accent — so the gloss reads as PC Music rather than generic 3D product art.

**Variation axes (what is allowed to vary)**

- *Subject:* drink cans, CDs, chrome icons, symmetric emblems, abstract ribbon renders, persona portraits, surreal cloud/chrome tableaux, collage posters — any of these, or new objects treated the same way (a perfume bottle, a flip phone, a trophy, a snack packet).
- *Composition:* centered product staging, mirror-symmetric flat graphic, floating scatter with depth of field, dense maximalist poster, bust portrait.
- *Lighting:* flat studio, CGI specular, sunset-gradient glow, void-black with rim light.
- *Color handling:* blue-dominant, pink-dominant, lime-dominant, yellow-dominant, chrome-on-void, holographic iridescence over any field.
- *Mood register:* from sterile corporate minimal to chaotic DIY-web maximal.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — saturation, synthetic gloss, ad-parody logic, constructed persona, internet-native typography — not any reference image. An image of, say, a chrome toaster splashing into pink liquid on a #0000FF field with a tiny sans label is perfectly in-style despite matching no reference.

**Avoid (style-breakers)**

- Muted, earthy, desaturated or pastel-faded palettes; matte finishes; beige minimalism.
- Film grain, VHS decay, paper texture, scratched analog damage, vintage printing artifacts.
- Classical sculpture, checkerboard marble floors, palm-tree sunsets, mall interiors (Vaporwave territory).
- Underwater ocean scenes, dolphins, mermaids, aquatic dreamscapes (Seapunk territory).
- Handcrafted/organic materials: wood, linen, clay, plants as sincere props.
- Naturalistic documentary candor, street snapshots, unretouched skin texture.
- Dark moody chiaroscuro, grunge distressing, gothic or heritage serif typography.
- Sincere, unironic luxury glamour with no conceptual/ad-parody layer.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Bubblegum Bling:** shares pink, silver, rhinestones and Y2K gloss, but is celebratory pop glamour without the conceptual layer. PC Music adds sterile CGI product staging, advertisement parody, hyperlink blue and the persona-as-avatar concept; its bling is quoted, not worn sincerely.
- **Neo-Aero:** shares glossy 3D and corporate smoothness, but Neo-Aero is translucent glass, aero gradients and frictionless utopian tech-branding with no irony, no web-clutter typography and no pop personas. PC Music is more saturated, more parodic, and always carries an internet-native "wrong" note.
- **Seapunk:** aquatic teal/aqua/violet palette with marine motifs and dreamy water surfaces. PC Music uses water only as hyper-real splash/liquid material on saturated solid fields, never as an oceanic world.
- **Vaporwave:** desaturated pastels, classical busts, checkerboards, VHS decay, melancholic mall nostalgia. PC Music is hyper-saturated, clean, decay-free, and nostalgic for the Y2K corporate web rather than the 80s/90s mall; its irony is euphoric, not melancholic.
- **Bimbocore:** soft pink, plush, hyper-feminine selfie-culture warmth and faux-naive lifestyle framing. PC Music portraits are colder and more constructed: airbrushed to CGI doll-likeness, placed in advertisement framing, with conceptual distance between subject and viewer.

Where the record is thin: the source text names "glitchy digital textures" as part of the minimal/maximal mix, but the surviving imagery shows gloss dominant with glitch appearing mainly as typographic distortion and halftone/web-clutter layers; treat glitch accordingly, as accent rather than foundation.
