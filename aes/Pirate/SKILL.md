# Pirate (Piratecore / Piratewave)

Pirate is a community-born internet aesthetic that romanticizes the material world of the sea raider — the ship, the sea, the treasure — while deliberately refusing the criminal half of the historical pirate's reputation. What remains is a culture of collection and voyage: shells, rocks, bones, coins and trinkets gathered into jars and cabinets; maps and constellation charts studied by lamplight; coastlines sketched in ink; swords and daggers kept as objects of pride and practice rather than violence. Its visual language fuses Golden Age of Piracy material culture (flintlocks, tricornes, rigging, wax-sealed paper) with the thrifted, layered boho sensibility of its cottagecore / dark academia / adventurecore cousins, and with a curator's eye for the display shelf, the shadowbox and the labelled vial. The result is an aesthetic that feels simultaneously adventurous and domestic: a hold full of wonders, arranged with care.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule: a cool sea family dominates (sea green, turquoise, teal, marine blue), grounded by weathered brown timber and sand, lifted by ivory and bone white, and sparked by antique gold or brass used sparingly as glint — coin, buckle, compass rim, shelf fitting. A muted oxblood or bandana red appears only as a small accent (scarf, coat, velvet lining). Paper artifacts live in sepia and cream ink-on-parchment tones. A storm variant pushes the same family toward slate grey, rust-orange ember skies and near-black sea. Saturation runs from sun-bleached and faded to painterly-rich; it never goes neon, candy-bright or chrome.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how strongly the defining text documents them)

1. **Ships, boats and the open sea** — hulls, rigging, sails, ship wheels, waves, islands, coastal cliffs, seaweed. The anchor of the style; nearly every other motif orbits it.
2. **Treasure trinkets** — old or rusted coins, gold trinkets, pearls, vintage lockets, watches, keys; kept in jars, pockets, velvet-lined shadowboxes rather than scattered like loot.
3. **Collected natural specimens** — shells, corals, fossils, rocks, sand, marine skeletons and skulls; the naturalist's half of the pirate soul, usually sorted, labelled and displayed.
4. **Navigation and voyage lore** — compasses, maps, constellation charts, hourglasses, ship wheels, quills and ink, hand-written letters, sketches of coastlines and horizons.
5. **Blades and powder** — swords, daggers, old gunpowder flintlock guns; carried or mounted as heirlooms and fencing tools, held with pride, not mid-murder.
6. **Coastal land and water forms** — beaches, dunes, grottoes, cliffs, tide lines; the setting for fashion and for contemplation.
7. **Display furniture of collecting** — cabinets of curiosities, glass shelves and domes, specimen vials with hand-written labels, Victorian mirrors and chairs, statues of marine animals, old photographs and sea sketches on the wall.
8. **Garb** — tricornes, justacorps, corsets and corset belts, square belts, oversized blouses, ruffles, lace, flowing maxi dresses, fingerless gloves, heeled boots and sandals, scarves, beads, chains and handmade jewellery.

### Materials and textures

Salt-worn and hand-touched throughout: grained and splintered natural timber; rusted iron and tarnished brass; salt-clouded glass (jars, domes, portholes); wrinkled linen, gauze, lace and worn leather; toothed aged paper, vellum and ink; the matte surfaces of shell, coral, bone and fossil; twisted rope and tarred rigging; damp sand; occasional velvet or oxblood lining inside a case. Nothing is glossy, laminated or factory-fresh. Patina is the signature finish.

### Recurring subjects

Still-life displays of specimens and trinkets; fashion figures posed on rocks, dunes or decks with wind in the layers; seascapes with a vessel small against horizon and sky; flat-lays and shadowbox arrangements of collection objects on wood or velvet; aged sketch-and-map pages with marginalia; interiors that read as a captain's cabin-cum-curiosity room; stylized illustrated vignettes of crew life at sea.

### Composition and lighting

Composition follows a *display logic*: frontal, shelf-like arrangements with objects sorted by size and kinship; orderly clutter rather than chaos; or, outdoors, a full-body figure anchored on stone or sand with fabric and hair blown sideways; or a horizon-dominated vista where sky and water take two-thirds and the ship is a silhouette. Collage and moodboard layouts of sepia fragments are a native format. Lighting ranges (the defining text specifies palette and objects but is silent on light; this range is read from the style's image corpus): sun-bleached midday on sand and dunes; overcast, diffuse storm light with ember-orange breaks in cloud; dramatic shafts of daylight cutting through grotto arches or cabin windows onto turquoise water; warm lamplight or candle glow on brass, paper and glass in interior displays. Shadow is soft and atmospheric, never horror-hard.

### Medium and rendering

The style is medium-agnostic but texture-forward: editorial fashion photography; still-life and interior photography with shallow, natural light; painterly digital illustration with slightly caricatured, storybook proportions (the lineage of Plastic Beach-era album art and voyage video games); ink engraving and naturalist sketching on aged paper; collage and scrapbook assemblage; rendered museum-case displays. Across all of these, grain, brushwork, paper tooth or film softness is preferred over vector cleanness.

## Mood & Values

Curious freedom. The aesthetic expresses longing for the open ocean and the unknown, balanced by the domestic pleasure of arranging what the tide brings in. Its stated values are freedom, fairness, curiosity, balance and loyalty, bound by a personal moral code; it believes in hard work and strength of heart and mind. Thrift ethos ("one man's trash is another man's treasure") makes every object feel earned and storied. There is melancholy under the adventure — distance, waiting, weather — and a communal joy borrowed from the shanty tradition: songs sung together, letters written by hand, fences practiced at dusk. Crucially, the style does not value stealing or thieving; treasure appears as wonder and inheritance, weapons as craft and pride, never as instruments of cruelty.

## Era & Origins

Subject era: the 16th–18th centuries, with the Golden Age of Piracy (roughly 1650–1720, Atlantic and Caribbean sea lanes) as the gravitational center — flintlocks, tricornes, justacorps, sail rigging, paper charts. The aesthetic itself is a modern internet micro-aesthetic (Piratecore, Piratewave), emerged from the same community soil as Cottagecore, Dark Academia and Adventurecore with boho / hippie inflections, and fed by a long media lineage: *Treasure Island* and *Robinson Crusoe* in literature, *Pirates of the Caribbean*, *Black Sails*, *Our Flag Means Death*, *One Piece*, *Monkey Island*, *Sea of Thieves* and *Assassin's Creed IV* in screen and game form, and Gorillaz's *Plastic Beach* in the illustrated branch. Its music scene — traditional shanties, folk-punk, folk-metal, indie folk — keeps the tone communal and rowdy-sentimental. The style reacts against landlocked, disposable modern routine by reclaiming the pirate as a figure of code, loyalty and self-made freedom rather than crime.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Weather everything: salt, rust, sun-fade, mended hems, hand-written labels. Patina over polish.
- Use display logic: objects sorted, jarred, shelved, labelled, layered — the collector's eye, not the loot pile.
- Keep the palette sea-locked: green-turquoise-blue water family, brown timber, ivory cloth, gold glint; red only as a small accent.
- Let paper carry narrative: maps, charts, sketches, letters, marginalia, ink lines.
- Put wind and water in motion: blown fabric, rigged rope, choppy sea, birds working the sky.
- Mix thrifted eras gently: a Victorian mirror beside a flintlock beside a labelled bone vial reads correct; matched costume-shop sets do not.
- Light as atmosphere, not effect: grotto shafts, storm diffusion, ember dusk, lamplight on brass.
- Keep the human touch visible: hands, handwriting, mending, practice stances, arranged shelves.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range across specimen still-life, fashion portrait, seascape, map-or-sketch page, cabin interior, shadowbox flat-lay, or stylized illustrated crew vignette — including the style's tolerated anachronistic branch, in which painterly illustration mixes modern maritime industry (working ships, submarines, lighthouses) with pirate sensibility and muted storm color. Composition may be frontal display, cluttered flat-lay, full-body figure in landscape, horizon vista, or collage vignette. Lighting may be bleached noon, overcast storm, grotto shaft, ember dusk, or warm interior lamplight. Color handling may be faded photographic or painterly-rich within the stated family, with sepia reserved for paper. Mood may slide from whimsical curiosity to melancholic longing to swaggering adventure to communal joy. Rendering may be photograph, painterly illustration, engraving, or collage. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a cabinet of labelled shells on mint-green shelves and a storm-lit carrier horizon are equally valid pirates if they obey palette, patina and display logic.

**Avoid**

- Neon, candy or holographic color; chrome, gloss plastic, vector flatness.
- Crisp new props: unstressed linen, unweathered wood, polished stainless steel, factory-perfect rope.
- Modern tech or sportswear in hero roles (phones, sneakers, branding); clean minimalist or corporate interiors.
- Horror gore, drowned ghosts, occult ritual, funerary black as the dominant register.
- Crisp resort-nautical language as the main voice: breton stripes, navy-white-red poster cleanness, postcard lighthouses.
- High fantasy physics: merfolk, tridents, spell-glow, bioluminescent magic as focal point.
- Steampunk goggles and gear clusters; safari khaki jungle gear; desert, forest or city settings with no coastal or collection tie.
- Glorified theft or cruelty: no mid-robbery violence, no prisoners; weapons as heirloom and practice only.
- Pure monochrome noir or desaturated cold dread throughout.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs. Crowcore:** Crowcore is corvid and occult — black feathers, mourning Victorian dress, bones as omen. Pirate bones are naturalist specimens in labelled jars; its palette is sea-green and gold, its mood curious and sun-or-storm-lit, never funerary.
- **Vs. Dark Nautical:** Dark Nautical keeps the sea but makes it a threat — ghost ships, drowning, cold near-monochrome dread. Pirate storms are backdrop to adventure; the style retains warm gold glints, curiosity-cabinet charm, living community and humor.
- **Vs. Nautical:** General Nautical is crisp and orderly — navy/white/red, breton stripes, polished brass, resort tidiness. Pirate swaps crispness for weathering: rust, thrifted clutter, corseted boho layers, aged paper, fossils and sand.
- **Vs. Adventurecore:** Adventurecore explores inland — khaki, satchels, binoculars, jungle and mountain trails. Pirate is tide-locked: shells, salt crust, rigging, charts, ship wheels; its green is sea green, not safari olive.
- **Vs. Nautical Fantasy:** Nautical Fantasy leans on merfolk, sea monsters and magical glow. Pirate stays anchored in historical material culture — flintlock, tricorne, wax seal, compass — even when stylized or cartoonish; myth appears at most as a sketch in a map margin, never as the physics of the scene.

**One-line recognition test:** weathered timber, sea-green water, ivory layers and a gold glint, arranged or worn with a collector's care and a voyager's longing — curiosity, not cruelty, is the tell.
