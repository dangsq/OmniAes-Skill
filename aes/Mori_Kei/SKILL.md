# Mori Kei (Mori Girl / Mori Boy)

Mori Kei is a Japanese fashion and lifestyle aesthetic that imagines how a person living in the forest would dress. In Japan it is called Mori Girl (森ガール) or Mori Boy (森ボーイ); "Mori Kei" is the international label. It is neither costume nor historical reenactment but a street-fashion coordinate system: a grammar of loose layering, natural fibre and muted earth colour, worn with the unbothered calm of someone who spends her day picking herbs and reading beside a window. The style peaked around 2009–2012 in a burst of magazines and brands, faded in Japan, and survives through international enthusiasts and subgenres (Natural Kei, Dark Mori, Bright Mori, Hama Kei).

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
The core scheme is an off-white base washed with earth tones, plus optional floral pastels used in small doses. Saturation stays low to moderate — colours look milked, greyed, or sun-faded; value sits mid-to-high for bases. Pure white and pure black are avoided in the core style; nothing neon or electric ever appears.

### Silhouette and layering (the core grammar)
The defining rule is *many loose layers that hide the body shape* — the "yurufuwa" (fluffy-soft) effect. Expect three or more visible strata: a long dress or skirt over a blouse, topped by a cardigan or vest, topped by a knit scarf, shawl or poncho; hems land midi to maxi, sleeves run long or gently ballooned, and nothing cinches the waist hard. The outline reads soft, columnar or A-line, slightly rumpled, never fitted. Lace-up leather boots, opaque tights or legwarmers, and a carried basket or bag complete the coordinate.

### Motifs and objects, ranked by textual prominence
1. **Forest and greenery** — trees, woodland paths, mossy ground; the definitional setting.
2. **Flowers** — worn as crowns or sprigs in braids, printed on fabric, dried and pressed.
3. **White dresses and the layered garments themselves** — the wardrobe is the motif.
4. **Hanging herbs and herbalism** — bundles drying from beams, gathered sprigs.
5. **Cottages and simple wooden interiors** — stools, stumps, linen curtains.
6. **Deer and baby animals** — gentle fauna as companions or illustrations.
7. **Moss, ferns, undergrowth** as texture everywhere.
8. **Fairy-tale narrative props** — picture books, teacups, baskets of foraged goods; Grimm, Andersen, Potter as imagined source world.
Secondary, consistent wardrobe objects: knit shawls and scarves, cardigans, vests, berets or soft hats, canvas tote bags, lace-up boots, tights, woven baskets. Quiet-hobby props (a book, a cup of tea, pressed leaves) are always in key.

### Materials and textures
Matte natural fibres only: cotton and linen voile, wool knits with visible stitch, crocheted lace, embroidered panels, gauze, flannel, felted wool, fur trim in small doses. Textures should look hand-touched — cable knit, eyelet lace, patchwork, darned seams — and slightly worn-soft rather than crisp new. No satin shine, no synthetics that glint.

### Hair and makeup
Hair is natural-toned and softly arranged: loose waves, low braids threaded with flowers or ribbon, bobs with headbands, flower crowns of fresh or dried blooms. Makeup is minimal and matte: beige, gold or pink eye shadow barely there; blush placed high and round on the cheek; thick, natural eyebrows; lips tinted, never glossy-red. The face reads fresh and a little sleepy, not made-up.

### Subjects, composition, lighting
Subjects range from full-body coordinate portraits (female or male wearers), to hairstyle and detail studies, to lifestyle stills of tea, books and herbs, to woodland scenes with deer or cottage exteriors. Composition tends to full- or three-quarter-length standing figures with inward, gentle posture — weight on one leg, hands holding a bag or touching a prop, gaze soft, downcast or slightly off-camera. Two legitimate staging modes exist: real forest, park or cottage locations, and bare plain-wall studios dressed with a single cluster of natural props (fallen leaves, a tree stump, hanging dried herbs). Magazine-derived layouts with handwritten or serif text blocks and tiny bird-and-flower illustrations are a recognized variant, not a requirement. Lighting is diffused and shadowless: overcast daylight, open shade under canopy, or soft window light indoors. Tonality is matte, low-contrast, gently faded like a film scan, with a warm-neutral cast.

## Mood & Values
Quiet, slow, contemplative. Mori Kei values love of the forest, quiet hobbies, reading, drinking tea, herbalism and a natural lifestyle — an anti-glamorous, anti-hurried stance. The mood is tender whimsy grounded in mud and wool: a fairy tale told in a whisper, cozy rather than theatrical, nostalgic without being ornate. "Yurufuwa" softness is both tactile and emotional: everything looks like it would feel warm to touch.

## Era & Origins
Born in 2006 when Choco founded the "Mori Girl" community on Mixi, named after a friend's remark — "you look like you live in the forest." The community hit 35,000 members in 2009, igniting a boom of magazines (Mori Girl Lesson, Choco's Mori Girl Book) and brands such as Wonder Rocket, and spreading overseas under the name Mori Kei. It declined in Japan in the early 2010s (the Mixi group was deleted by 2019; Wonder Rocket's Harajuku store closed in 2017) but persists internationally. Its deeper lineage runs through Natural Kei and the 1980s PINK HOUSE prairie-layering tradition, and its imagination feeds on Studio Ghibli films, Tasha Tudor, and folk and dream pop.

## Subgenre range (part of the variation space)
- **Natural Kei**: simpler, more casual; fewer layers, solid colours, prairie and 1970s Gunne Sax / Laura Ashley influence; pinafores and layered skirts.
- **Dark Mori / Mori Goth**: the same layering and silhouette rules in charcoal, black and deep brown; hooded capes, corset belts over layered skirts; gothic mood, Helena Bonham Carter as muse; still matte and layered, distinct from Strega.
- **Bright Mori**: pastel and kawaii-inflected, borrowing from Fairy Kei, Cult Party Kei and Dolly Kei; the only branch where unnaturally coloured hair is normal; obscure.
- **Hama Kei**: seashore adaptation — lighter, fewer layers, blue/aqua/beige/white, denim, sailor stripes; thinly documented.
- **Yama Kei**: practical mountain wear in bright colours; adjacent rather than core.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Layer first: build three or more loose strata that erase the body's outline; soft rumple beats crisp fit.
2. Keep the palette muted: off-white base plus earth tones, floral pastels only as accents.
3. Mix matte natural textures — knit, lace, embroidery, linen, wool — in one coordinate.
4. Ground the figure in forest logic: greenery, flowers, herbs, baskets, boots, tights; props from the ranked motif list.
5. Light softly: overcast, canopy shade, or window light; matte faded tonality, low contrast.
6. Face and hair natural: minimal makeup, high round blush, thick brows; braids, waves, or flower crowns.
7. Mood before drama: quiet, gentle, unhurried; nothing poses hard or sells hard.

**Variation axes.** Subject: wearers of any gender within the style, hairstyle/detail studies, cottage interiors, tea-and-book still lifes, woodland scenes with animals, editorial pages. Composition: full-body, three-quarter, seated, detail crops, multi-angle plates, prop-dressed studio or real forest. Lighting: cool spring overcast, warm autumn shade, dim interior window light. Colour handling: greener, browner, creamier, seasonal rusts or powders; Dark Mori monochrome-earth; Bright Mori pastel. Season and weather: spring blossom to autumn leaf-fall to soft winter knits. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Bodycon shapes, miniskirts, crop tops, hard waist-cinching, sharp tailoring and power shoulders; petticoat bell silhouettes, fitted boned bodices and giant head bows (Lolita architecture); grunge distressing, ripped fishnets, band tees, combat hardware; glam makeup (smoky eyes, contour, glossy lips, lashes) and neon hair in the core style; satin shine, sequins, latex, vinyl, metallics; neon or saturated primary colours; phones, headphones, cars, logos, sportswear as focal points; hard direct flash, HDR crispness, glossy commercial lighting; sun-baked golden-hour saturation; sleek minimalist interiors of chrome and glass.
