# Gulf Futurism

Gulf Futurism is a hypermodern art movement from the Arabian Gulf states that imagines the region's technological future through its petrochemical present: skyscrapers, refineries, desalination plants, master-planned capitals and off-road machine culture, all filtered through exaggeration and surrealism. It is doubly coded — at once a celebration of cultural confidence and a deadpan critique of rampant consumerism, urban expansion and the alienation of the individual inside them. Its signature move is fusion without nostalgia: traditional Gulf ornament (radial stripes, geometric lattice screens, repetitive modular pattern) applied as surface skin to hypermodern engineering, so that a water tower reads as a monument and a sculpture reads as alien machinery recovered from the sand.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

The palette is a **bleached neutral field plus at most one saturated intervention**. The field is desert: bone, sand, dust, pale washed sky, or — in industrial register — an amber dust monochrome. Into that field goes exactly one of: (a) a bold ornamental accent (stripe blue, cream, coral red, olive-sage), (b) a single iridescent petrochemical object shifting magenta–violet–teal like an oil slick, or (c) glowing practical lamps inside dust gloom. Never a full spectrum; wealth here is expressed as scale and gloss, not color abundance.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the movement's writings document them)

1. **Skyscrapers and master-planned megastructure** — glittering towers, perforated lattice-skinned blocks, linear mirror cities, empty new capitals with bouquets of identical towers on a flat plain.
2. **Petroleum industry and heavy machine** — flare stacks, pipes, crawlers, harvesters, tanks; infrastructure so large it becomes landscape; also petro-materials sublimated into glossy sculpture.
3. **Traditional Gulf ornament as engineering skin** — radial stripe patterns flaring over funnel forms, geometric lattice / mashrabiya-style perforated façades, checkerboard and banded borders, obsessive module repetition.
4. **Off-road vehicles and hyper-masculine machine culture** — documented in the texts; thin in the surviving visual record, so treat as monumental vehicle, convoy or treaded chassis rather than portraiture.
5. **Baroque-style ostentatious interiors** — text-documented, image-thin: gilded, chandeliered, over-decorated rooms rendered sterile and empty.
6. **Imported consumer iconography, estranged** — diners, malls, condiment caddies, wall-mounted screens playing something else; globalization's props staged in rooms with no people.
7. **Desert stage furniture** — sand, dry median grass, scrubby trees, perimeter walls, streetlight masts, empty asphalt; the horizon is always near and flat.
8. **The absent or tiny human** — a single figure at the base of a machine, or nobody at all; alienation is the default population.

### Materials and textures

Poured concrete wearing painted stripe ornament; perforated metal or stone lattice screens; anodized, iridescent oil-slick coatings with violet-to-green shift; chrome trim, vinyl booths and glossy ceramic tile bands in interiors; dust-matte rusted steel on exterior machinery; mirrored glass reflecting sky and sand. Surfaces are either **petro-gloss** (wet-looking, reflective) or **dust-matte** (chalky, sanded, hazed) — never soft Scandinavian matte, never wet rain sheen.

### Composition and lighting

Frontal symmetry or strict one-point perspective aimed at the megastructure; low horizon with the monument filling the upper frame; aerial rendering views of linear cities trenching through desert; module repetition (tower clusters, booth rows, spike rings) as rhythm; vast empty foregrounds of sand, checkered floor or median grass; occasionally a centered screen or artifact inserted like recovered alien technology. Lighting is harsh flat noon under a washed pale sky, or amber dust-scatter gloom with practical lamps glowing through haze, or sterile even fluorescent interior light; a sun flare at the vanishing point is idiomatic. Shadows are hard and short or swallowed by haze.

## Mood & Values

Hypermodernity, the paradox of prosperity, globalization, surrealism. The feeling is awe with a hollow center: wealth made visible as colossal infrastructure, yet emotionally uninhabited. Exaggeration is the method — too many towers, too big a machine, too empty a room — producing deadpan surrealism rather than spectacle-for-its-own-sake. Underneath the irony sits genuine cultural confidence: tradition is not mourned but upgraded, worn as pattern on the future's hardware.

## Era & Origins

Born in 2008 with Sophia Al Maria's online video *The Gaze of Sci-Fi Wahabi*; the term was coined jointly by Al Maria and Kuwaiti composer Fatima Al Qadiri in 2012, explicitly inspired by Afrofuturism's method of fusing cultural history with technological imagination. It reacts to the Gulf's petro-funded urbanization boom, consumer saturation and role in globalization, and extends into the rendering culture of actual megaprojects (NEOM, The Line, Egypt's New Administrative Capital) and desert-set science-fiction cinema. Practitioners include Monira Al Qadiri (petro-iridescent sculpture) and Bahar Noorizadeh (speculative video).

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** (1) Monumentality versus human scale: infrastructure dwarfs or replaces people. (2) Ornamented engineering: apply Gulf geometric pattern — radial stripes, lattice perforation, banded borders, module repetition — to functional structures. (3) Desert as stage: sand, haze, pale sky, dry grass, empty road; the horizon stays flat and near. (4) Finish discipline: petro-gloss or dust-matte, bleached field plus one accent. (5) Exaggerate until surreal: one more tower, one size larger machine, one alien object out of place. (6) Keep it empty: prosperity without crowds.

**Variation axes.** Subject: water towers, desalination plants, flare stacks, highway interchanges, mall or diner interiors, oil crawlers, sculpture-in-sand, planned-city renderings, airport halls, fountain plazas, convoy of lifted trucks. Composition: frontal symmetry, one-point monumental, low-angle仰 against sky, aerial rendering, centered-artifact still life. Lighting: flat noon, amber duststorm with glowing lamps, dusk sun-flare, sterile interior fluorescent. Color: bleached neutrals + stripe blue; neutrals + coral/olive interior; full dust-amber monochrome; single iridescent object on sand. Mood: awe, irony, loneliness, satire, elegy-for-nothing-yet.

**A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A striped desalination tank at dusk, an empty gilded majlis with a chrome chandelier, or a lattice-skinned toll plaza in a sandstorm are all valid Gulf Futurism without resembling any known work.

**Avoid:** rain, snow, lush tropical or temperate foliage; bustling crowds, souk street life, any populated scene; rainy neon-night cyberpunk with wet asphalt reflections; painterly gestural brushwork and speed-line dynamism; outer-space scenes with stars, planets or cosmic transcendence; Afro-diasporic or Indigenous cosmological symbols; cozy nostalgia or hygge warmth played straight (imported consumer props must feel estranged, not homey); generic Western chrome sci-fi city with no desert, no haze and no ornament; literal religious iconography (ornament stays geometric).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Afrofuturism** centers Black diasporic liberation, cosmic ancestry and music culture (jazz, funk, chrome tribal-futurist costume); Gulf Futurism centers petro-capital infrastructure, desert master-planning and stripe/lattice ornament, with alienation instead of liberation, and no diaspora narrative.
- **Futurism** (Italian, 1909 onward) is painterly, dynamic, fragmented, glorifying speed and war in European cities; Gulf Futurism is static, monumental, photographic or render-smooth, desert-bound, and ironic about its machines rather than worshipful.
- **Indigenous Futurisms** ground the future in land sovereignty, ancestral ecology and decolonial cosmology; Gulf Futurism's tradition appears as decorative geometry on state-capital concrete and petro-gloss, and its stance includes critique of consumerism rather than ecological reciprocity.
- **Russian Cosmism** reaches upward: stars, resurrection, philosophical sublime, transcendent cosmos; Gulf Futurism stays terrestrial — sand, steel, haze and highway — its future built horizontally and vertically on the ground, funded by oil, inhabited by nobody.
