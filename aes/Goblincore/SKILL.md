# Goblincore

Goblincore is an aesthetic of affectionate scavenging: it prizes the parts of nature usually called ugly — frogs, toads, snails, bugs, mud, moss, rotting logs, fungi — and treats them as treasures worth pocketing, jarring, shelving and hoarding. It takes its name from the goblin of European folklore, but here the goblin is not a villain; it stands for a carefree infatuation with nature's unpredictability, closer to the wild, mischievous Unseelie side of Celtic fae myth than to any monster. Practitioners collect small shiny objects called "shinies," and the aesthetic overlaps Cottagecore, Crowcore and Vulture Culture while refusing their tidiness or morbidity. It is strongly rooted in LGBTQ+ community culture, especially non-binary and transmasculine practitioners, and circulates through mood-board collages, Etsy vendors, zines and comic strips as much as through photography. Alternate names in circulation: Gremlincore, Greedcore, Gobblecore.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** The base is always earth-toned and slightly desaturated, weighted toward shadow: deep moss and fern greens, olive and dark lime, bark and soil browns, near-black loam, and bone or spore cream. Into this muted ground the style allows small, concentrated accents of saturated color — a fly-agaric red cap, pink mushroom gills, a candle flame, a glint of sea glass or amethyst — never large fields of bright color. Navy blue and black appear as clothing and shadow tones. A correct image keeps roughly 80% earth-muted ground and spends its saturation in pockets.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.** First rank: mushrooms and fungi of every kind (caps, fairy rings, shelf fungus); moss covering stone, log and soil; mud and dirt itself; snails; frogs and toads; bones and small skulls; "shinies" — crystals, rings, old tarnished jewelry, buttons, painted rocks and pebbles; clutter and hoards as a compositional subject; forests and woodland streams; candles; tinctures and corked specimen jars; bugs; ferns and wild flora. Second rank (fashion): cozy oversized knit sweaters, corduroy, lived-in jeans and overalls, unique socks, worn converse and boots, clashing patterns and earth-tone layering, clothes visibly dirty from wandering. Third rank (formats): scanned-paper mood-board collages and "starter pack" flat lays, black-and-white zine or comic strips, shelf and table hoard displays, hand-held object portraits.

**Materials and textures.** Wet moss with a petrichor sheen, crumbly bark, loose loam, matte weathered bone, tarnished (never polished) silver and brass, cloudy glass vials with corks, wax candles, wool knit and corduroy, paper scraps, dried flowers, snail-shell spiral, frog-skin dampness. Everything should look touched, weathered, chewed, wilted or repaired. Glossy, new, or factory-perfect surfaces do not belong.

**Recurring subjects and scale.** The style works at pocket scale: a hand holding a skull or a jar, a cluster of caps on a log, snails crossing a wet table, a pile of trinkets sorted by a collector. Larger subjects are forests, overgrown gardens, mossy streams and cluttered cottage interiors — but even these are framed as places to crouch and rummage, not vistas to admire.

**Composition and lighting.** Ground-level or table-level camera heights; shallow depth of field isolating a small specimen against dark leaf litter; receding forest paths and streams pulling the eye into green depth; maximalist arrangements with no negative space — shelves, tables and collages packed edge to edge; asymmetry and accidental-looking placement. Light is diffuse overcast, dappled canopy shafts, blue dusk or near-dark night, and indoor candlelight; contrast stays soft, grain and vignetting are welcome, and studio strobe or high-key brightness never appears.

## Mood & Values

Curiosity, diversity, imperfection, nature, maximalism. The emotional core is tenderness toward the overlooked: a snail is a companion, a broken ring is a relic, mud is proof of a good day. There is mischief and a faint feral edge — the Unseelie wildness — but never horror; bones are keepsakes, not threats. The aesthetic values the worn over the new, the many over the few, and the queer, outsider identity of the collector over any picturesque ideal.

## Era & Origins

A 2020s internet micro-aesthetic, organized through communities like r/Goblincore, Discord servers and Etsy vendors, with a literary and cinematic lineage reaching back through *The Spiderwick Chronicles*, Studio Ghibli films, *Labyrinth*, *The Dark Crystal*, *Over the Garden Wall*, *Frog and Toad* and *Goblin Market*. It reacts against polished, picturesque nature aesthetics by insisting that dirt, rot and "ugly" fauna are the real treasures. The name carries an ongoing community debate: because greedy, hook-nosed goblins have been used as antisemitic caricature, many practitioners prefer Gremlincore or adjacent terms, and the community publishes guidance on drawing goblins responsibly — a constraint that shapes how literal goblin figures may appear.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Choose a small, conventionally unbeautiful natural object or a hoard of them; treat it with tenderness, not disgust.
2. Keep the palette earth-muted and shadow-weighted; spend saturation only in small accents (fungi caps, gems, flame).
3. Favor wet, worn, tarnished, crumbly textures; add grain, soft contrast, vignette.
4. Compose with clutter or intimacy: packed shelves and tables, hand-held specimens, ground-level macro, receding mossy depth.
5. Light with overcast diffusion, canopy shafts, dusk, or candle flame.
6. Let imperfection structure the image: asymmetry, breaks, wilt, dirt smudges.
7. If a goblin creature appears at all, depict it as a soft fae being or imply it only through its hoard; follow community guidance against caricature tropes.

**Variation axes.** Subject may range across fungi, mollusks, amphibians, insects, bones, stones, jars and tinctures, jewelry, fashion flat lays, cluttered interiors, forest streams, paper collages and comic strips. Composition may range from extreme macro to interior wide shot to scanned collage. Lighting may range from bright dappled noon to near-black night to candlelit shelf. Color handling may range from heavily desaturated filmic to moderately saturated fungal accents. Mood may range from cozy and tender to mischievous to faintly eerie. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Pristine minimalism, negative-space styling, glossy plastic, neon or candy-pastel color, high-key studio lighting, new luxury metals and brilliant cut gems, desert or tropical or chrome-urban settings, kawaii sanitization, horror-gore treatment of bones, and — when drawing goblins — hooked warted noses, green-skinned greedy-villain stereotypes or any depiction echoing antisemitic caricature. Also avoid making the image read as tidy domestic pastoral (that is Cottagecore) or as ominous cryptid hunting (that is Cryptidcore).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cottagecore** shares forests and handmade objects but pursues picturesque domesticity: clean linen, baking, tidy gardens, warm cream light. Goblincore swaps linen for mud-stained corduroy and the tidy garden for a hoard of bones and snails.
- **Adventurecore** shares woodland setting but works at expedition scale — wide vistas, maps, functional new gear, optimistic summit light. Goblincore stays crouched at ground scale with worn secondhand clothing and collecting, not traversing, as the goal.
- **Crowcore** shares scavenging and bones but centers corvids, black feathers and a near-monochrome black palette with occult undertones. Goblincore's palette is green-brown earth, its fauna plural (frogs, snails, bugs), its tone playful rather than funereal.
- **Cryptidcore** shares night forests but builds analog-horror tension: flashlit fog, mothman evidence, dread. Goblincore's darkness is cozy or curious; its specimens are real, small and loved.
- **Dragoncore** shares hoarding but imagines wealth: gold piles, gem vaults, reptilian grandeur. Goblincore's shinies are buttons, sea glass and tarnished rings — pocket-scale, worthless to anyone but the collector.
- Neighboring **Mushroomcore** isolates fungi as sole subject; Goblincore always embeds fungi in a wider ecosystem of mud, moss, bones and trinkets. **Earthcore** lacks the clutter and the creaturely fauna; **Witchcore** adds ritual intent where Goblincore keeps only appetite and affection.
