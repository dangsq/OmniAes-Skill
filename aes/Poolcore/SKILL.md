# Poolcore

Poolcore (also called Poolrooms, Liminal Pool or Dreampools) is a subgenre of Liminal Spaces built from images of swimming pools that feel simultaneously eerie and peaceful. It emerged from 2010s internet image culture, closely tied to the Backrooms / liminal-space milieu, and was codified largely through the 3D poolroom renders of artist Jared Pike. Its subjects are indoor pools — municipal, hotel, therapeutic, dreamed — rendered as photographs, digital art or 3D renders. The style's emotional engine is generic familiarity: because every public pool resembles every other, an empty tiled poolroom reads as a place you have already been, perhaps in a dream. Poolcore spans moods from clean-minimalist to nostalgic to quietly unsettling, but every instance keeps the same backbone: tiled walls or floors, contained water, a blue-dominant palette, and the distinctive netted patterns of light refracting through water.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

Blue is always the most prominent hue, even when other colors are present; everything else is a tint, a cast or a small accent. The working range:

| Role | Range |
|---|---|
| Tile white / off-white | `#F4F6F5` → `#E4E8E6` |
| Grout and shadow grey | `#C6CDCA` |
| Caustic highlight / pale cyan light patch | `#D8F2EF` → `#BFE9E6` |
| Shallow water aqua-teal | `#7ED4D0` → `#46BFBF` |
| Mid-pool turquoise | `#2FA8B8` |
| Deep basin blue | `#16698C` → `#0C4A66` |
| Mint-green cast variant (tinted light or green tile) | `#A6D8B0` → `#7CC49A` |
| Vaporwave-adjacent accents (peach-pink, lilac) | `#EFC0C8`, `#B9A0D6` — small areas only |
| Kidcore-crossover accents (cobalt, magenta) | sparse saturated notes inside a blue-dominant frame |

Tile color follows the text: commonly white and square, sometimes navy or lighter blue. Alternate casts (greenish rooms, peachy-pink or purple vaporwave-tinged light) are legitimate poolcore only while blue-teal water and tile still own the largest area of the frame; if the sunset gradient takes over, the image has drifted into vaporwave.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Contained still water** — a pool basin, lap channel, plunge pool or flooded floor. Water is the protagonist; natural bodies (ocean, lake, river) never substitute for it.
2. **Square ceramic tile grids** — small-format glazed tile with visible grout lines, white first, navy or pale blue as documented alternatives. In 3D renders the tile typically climbs the walls as well as lining the basin; in photographs of real pools tile stays inside the basin, since real pools are not tiled wall-to-wall. This render-vs-photo tile rule is a reliable medium tell.
3. **Light refraction in water (caustics)** — the netted, rippling light pattern projected onto submerged tile, walls above the waterline, or ceilings. Some images make this the entire subject: close-ups of reflections on the pool bottom or of the water's surface, where tile fills most of the frame.
4. **Pool architecture**: submerged steps with chrome handrails, ladders, columns standing in water, arches and vaults, low tiled ceilings, clerestory window rows, round porthole windows, tiled corridors adjoining the basin.
5. **Nostalgiacore / Kidcore crossover equipment** — water slides, water playgrounds, painted murals of dolphins, fish or coral, painted sky ceilings: pools originally aimed at children, carrying a brighter, busier nostalgia while keeping water and tile dominant.
6. **Sparse inanimate props** — a pair of loungers at the pool edge, a drift of petals or a float on the water. Props are few and never imply present people.

### Materials and textures

Glazed small square tile and mosaic banding at the waterline; still or faintly rippled water with a caustic net moving across it; polished chrome or stainless steel rails, ladders and grab bars; wet glossy tile floors mirroring light sources; painted plaster or tiled ceilings; occasionally dated municipal fixtures — rockwork walls, tropical murals, fluorescent tube housings — in the nostalgic photographic branch.

### Recurring subjects

Indoor pool halls (public, hotel, therapeutic), tiled corridors and anterooms that open onto water, stairways descending into a basin, water-surface and pool-bottom close-ups, children's water-play pools in the colorful crossover branch, and dreamed or rendered poolrooms whose geometry is plausible but subtly endless.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Emptiness as composition**: no swimmers, no staff, no crowds. The frame is held by architecture and water alone.
- **Water as floor plane**: the waterline often sits as the horizon, with the basin occupying the lower half or the entirety of the frame; high-angle looks down into a basin and water-level close-ups are equally native.
- **One-point or gently angled perspective** down the pool axis, with repetition — columns, arches, window bays, tile courses — supplying rhythm.
- **Three lighting regimes**, all compatible: (a) diffuse daylight through clerestory or side windows laying soft rectangular patches on tile and water; (b) flat fluorescent glow, frequently with tube reflections streaking across the surface; (c) dim volumetric mood with glowing underwater lamps, haze, and a single bright source in a dark vaulted room.
- **Light behaves twice**: every successful image shows both a direct light source and its refracted re-projection through water — caustics on tile, walls or ceiling.

## Mood & Values

Poolcore holds two feelings at once: eeriness and serenity. The eeriness is liminal — the sense of a space between uses, a place remembered from a dream, familiar yet unplaceable. The serenity comes from clean tile, blue-and-white order, and clear still water. Nostalgia here is logical rather than personal: pools all resemble each other, so every pool of one's childhood bleeds into this one generic, half-remembered pool. The style values quiet, cleanliness, threshold states and dream logic; it treats absence of people not as loss but as invitation and unease in equal measure.

## Era & Origins

A 2010s internet aesthetic, credited to Jared Pike, whose 3D renders of white-tiled poolrooms filled with water established the visual grammar. It grows out of the liminal-space and Backrooms culture of found and rendered empty architecture, and overlaps Dreamcore through shared dream-water imagery. It has since fed games such as *The Classrooms* (2022), *Anemoiapolis* (2023) and *Pools* (2024). Aliases in circulation: Poolrooms, Liminal Pool, Dreampools.

## Generation Guidance

### Principles

1. **Keep the invariant triad**: contained water + tile grid + refracted caustic light. Remove any one and the image leaves the style.
2. **Blue dominance**: teal/aqua/blue plus white tile own the largest areas; alternate casts and accents stay subordinate.
3. **Emptiness**: no people. At most a few inanimate props (loungers, a float, petals).
4. **Enclosure**: interior or architecturally framed space. Open sky appears only as painted mural, ceiling painting, or light through windows — not as an outdoor horizon.
5. **Light behaves twice**: pair every direct source with its caustic re-projection through water.
6. **Plausible-dream geometry**: architecture should be buildable but slightly too clean, too quiet, too endless; scale can drift gently toward the dreamlike.
7. **Medium consistency**: choose photographic realism (dated civic fixtures, tile confined to the basin, slight documentary grain) or render realism (tile climbing walls, impossible purity, soft global illumination) — and keep the tile rule consistent with that choice.

### Variation axes

- **Subject**: full pool hall; corridor or anteroom opening onto water; submerged steps and rails; water-surface or pool-bottom close-up; children's water-play pool with slides and murals; therapeutic pool with handrails; vaulted or tunnel-like dreamed poolroom.
- **Composition**: one-point perspective down the pool axis; high angle into the basin; water-level close-up where tile fills the frame; symmetric colonnade or arcade; off-kilter angled view of a corner basin.
- **Lighting**: bright diffuse daylight with window patches; flat fluorescent interior glow; dim volumetric night mood with underwater lamps and haze.
- **Color handling**: pure blue-teal-white; mint-green cast; peach-pink or lilac vaporwave-tinged light; saturated cobalt-and-magenta Kidcore crossover — always blue-dominant overall.
- **Mood**: clean and minimalist; serene and spa-quiet; nostalgic and municipal; eerie and dream-uncanny.
- **Medium**: photograph, digital painting, or 3D render.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A green-cast vaulted cistern-like room, a close-up of caustics on a stair, and a bright children's pool hall are all equally poolcore if the triad, palette rule and emptiness hold.

### Avoid

- People: swimmers, splashes, lifeguards, crowds, pool parties.
- Outdoor backyard, resort or beach pools with open sky, palms, grass or sunlit decking — these read as travel or lifestyle photography.
- Natural water: oceans, lakes, rivers, hot springs without tile enclosure.
- Murky, dirty or drained pools, leaf litter, rust stains, black mold, cracked tile — decay belongs to urbex/ruin imagery; poolcore water is clear.
- Dominant vaporwave apparatus: sunset gradients, Greek busts, chrome grid floors, glitch, palm silhouettes.
- Kidcore clutter (toys, stickers, primary-color chaos) without water and tile dominating.
- Heavy signage, branding, typography, wayfinding graphics.
- Neon cyberpunk or nightclub lighting; colored party LEDs.
- Aquarium biotopes: fish, coral tanks, marine life as subject (painted murals of sea life are fine).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Cleancore**: Cleancore is domestic hygiene order — soap, laundry, sparkling sinks, cozy white tidiness. It lacks the large contained water body, the caustic refraction patterns and the liminal dread; its cleanliness comforts, while poolcore's cleanliness unsettles and soothes at once. Test: is there a tiled basin of still water projecting light nets? If not, not poolcore.
- **Vs Liminal Space**: Liminal Space is the parent genre — empty malls, airports, hallways, play areas. Poolcore is its water-specific child. Test: without pool water, tile and caustics it is generic liminal space; poolcore always contains the basin.
- **Vs Dreamcore**: Dreamcore adds surreal intrusions — floating objects, faceless figures, impossible skies, distorted scale. Poolcore's surrealism is subtractive: emptiness, silence, too-perfect tile, architecture that stays plausible. Test: additive weirdness means Dreamcore; quiet plausibility means poolcore.
- **Vs Kidcore**: Kidcore is primary-color playfulness, toys and childlike clutter everywhere. Poolcore's Kidcore-adjacent branch keeps water, tile and caustics dominant, with slides, murals and water playgrounds as set dressing inside a pool hall rather than as the whole image.
- **Vs Minimalism**: Minimalism reduces form for design's sake with neutral palettes and no narrative mood. Poolcore can look minimal — bare tile planes, single pool — but always carries the caustic light, the tile grid and the nostalgic-uncanny charge; minimalism without water and mood is not poolcore.
- **Vs Vaporwave (edge case)**: Peach-pink and lilac tints over a blue pool remain poolcore while blue-teal water and tile dominate. Once sunset gradients, chrome type or classical busts take the frame, the image has crossed into vaporwave.

**Quick recognition checklist**: contained tiled water + blue-dominant palette + refracted caustic light + total absence of people + indoor enclosure = poolcore.
