# Puppycore (Wancore)

Puppycore is the hyper-feminine, pastel, deliberately exaggerated celebration of puppies and dog paraphernalia, built from the visual grammar of early-2000s Japanese pet merchandise: sticker sheets, poster cards, capsule-toy packaging and stationery where real animal photographs are cut out, blown up, blush-edited and buried under sparkles. It is a nostalgia aesthetic twice over — produced in the 2000s by brands like San-X and Artlist, then resurrected and named in the 2020s on Pinterest, Tumblr and Instagram as a facet of Heisei Retro. Its images behave less like photographs and more like scanned printed matter: flat, bright, grainy with halftone, crowded with clipart, and emotionally single-minded about gentleness. Despite the name, the style tolerates other small animals (hamsters, kittens, rabbits, doves) and even animal-free object compositions, as long as the pastel merchandise grammar holds.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule: a pale, high-value field dominates the canvas, drawn from a soft pastel family, with white or cream as the resting ground. Saturation stays low-to-medium (roughly under 45%) and value high (above ~75%) for any area larger than a hand; stronger candy chroma is permitted only as small punctuation — clipart strawberries, apple-green leaves, magenta starbursts, bubble-letter fills — occupying a minor share of the surface.

- Grounds: white `#FFFDFB`, cream `#FBF3E4`, pale shell pink `#FFE3EC`
- Pink family (the style's center of gravity): sakura to bubblegum, `#FFD6E5` → `#F9A8C9`
- Baby blue: `#CDEBF7` → `#A9D9EF`
- Lavender: `#E8DFF7` → `#CBB8EC` (documented in the style's palette but the sparsest member in surviving material — use lightly)
- Pastel yellow: `#FFF6C9` → `#FFE89A`
- Acceptable extensions seen in period merchandise: mint `#D3F0E4`, peach `#FFDFC9`
- Accent-only chroma: candy red `#E8465A`, apple green `#9BD444`, hot magenta `#F0509E`

Backgrounds are rarely plain: gingham and dotted checks, diagonal candy stripes, scattered star or paw-print repeats, and soft pastel gradients are the typical fields.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Puppies, exaggerated.** Small breeds above all — Corgis, Pugs, Golden Retriever pups, Shih-Tzus — edited for maximum infancy: oversized eyes, digitally airbrushed blush on the cheeks, small bows or ribbons clipped to the ears. This is the core; everything else orbits it.
2. **Haze and sparkle treatment.** Blurry or hazy filters, soft focus, overlaid glitter, digital sparkle bursts, star and heart confetti, fluffy texture veils.
3. **Dog-world secondary motifs:** paw prints, bones, hearts — characteristically rendered as *objects*: iced cookies, collar charms, knitted toys, plastic food bowls.
4. **Punning typography as graphic element:** bold bubble letters with white outlines, highly stylized pixel art type, hand-written marker script, and flowing cursive brand-style logotypes; phrases like "I WOOF YOU," "CUTE!", or invented merch names, sometimes in rounded Japanese kana.
5. **Baby-and-innocence props:** pacifiers, soft cloth toys, plush hoods and costumes, knitted bones and stars, yarn balls.
6. **Other small animals,** "sometimes": hamsters, kittens, rabbits, chicks, doves — treated with identical blush-and-sparkle grammar, often inside fruit-shaped frames.
7. **Merchandise furniture:** die-cut sticker outlines around photo cutouts, scalloped and lace borders, fruit- or heart-shaped photo windows, corner logos, starburst price-tag shapes, daisy chains.

### Materials and textures

Fluffy fur, crochet and knit yarn, felt, plush fleece, icing on sugar cookies, glossy sticker paper, gingham cotton, lace trim. Surface quality matters: period pieces carry printed-matter artifacts — halftone dot grain, slight color misregistration, paper sheen, scan flatness — while contemporary recreations carry digital-overlay artifacts (sparkle PNGs, pixel fonts, airbrush gradients). Either artifact family reads as authentic; clean modern crispness does not.

### Recurring subjects

Puppy portraits and puppy piles; hamsters and kittens as guest subjects; merchandise objects standing alone (collars with bone charms, paw-print cookies, knitted toys, sticker sheets, greeting cards on a rack); capsule-toy and package fronts; poster-style brand layouts with a cursive logotype.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Four layout grammars recur, and any one of them suffices:

- **Sticker-sheet collage:** many die-cut photo cutouts with white keyline outlines scattered or gridded across a patterned ground, with a script logo and corner decorations.
- **Framed portrait:** a single animal centered inside a scalloped, lace, heart- or fruit-shaped window, matted on gingham or stripes, sparkles drifting outside the frame.
- **Merch poster / package front:** centered subject, bubble-letter headline arched above, small logos and starbursts in corners, dense but evenly weighted.
- **Object flat lay:** cookies, collars or knit toys arranged top-down on fur or knit fabric, shadowless and evenly lit.

Lighting is high-key and diffuse: soft studio wash or scan-flat illumination, no directional hard shadows, mild overexposure welcome, plus a bloom or haze layer that melts edges. Period source photography sometimes uses a wide-angle lens close to the muzzle, producing endearing perspective distortion — a documented tendency, subtle in surviving examples. Depth of field, when present, is creamy rather than dramatic.

## Mood & Values

Cuteness, innocence, comfort, playfulness, gentleness, and nostalgia — specifically nostalgia for early-2000s internet culture and the Japanese stationery shelf of that decade. The animals are presented as objects of gentle affection: nobody hunts, guards, or works in Puppycore. The emotional register ranges from candy-bright playfulness (dense sticker collages, pun headlines) to tender, almost lullaby softness (hazy sleeping pups, rainbow-and-cloud backdrops). Sincerity is mandatory; the style never winks ironically at its own sweetness.

## Era & Origins

Originated in 2000s Japan through merchandise lines that popularized photo-manipulated pet imagery — San-X's Wan Wan Wanco and Hamham Chan, Artlist's The Dog, Keith Kimberlin posters, Snap'Art — and was cemented in popular memory by Nintendogs and pet-themed games like Hamster Paradise Pure Heart. The name "Puppycore" (and the earlier punning proposal "Wancore," from *wanko*, "doggy") arrived in the 2020s on image boards and social media, filing the style under the broader Heisei Retro nostalgia movement. Contemporary recreations borrow editing techniques from Cutecore, which is why the two can blur.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build the image like a piece of 2000s pet merchandise, not like a photograph: (1) lay down a pale pastel field — gingham, candy stripe, star repeat, or plain cream; (2) place the animal or object subject with exaggerated infancy — big eyes, blush, ear bow, plush prop — under high-key diffuse light; (3) add a haze/bloom layer and scatter sparkles, hearts, stars, paw prints; (4) set typography as a graphic object: bubble letters, pixel type, or cursive script with a punning phrase or invented brand name; (5) finish with a period artifact pass — die-cut white outlines, scalloped borders, halftone grain, sticker-sheet crowding.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any puppy breed, any small pet (hamster, kitten, rabbit, bird), a plush or costume in place of an animal, or no animal at all — cookies, collars, knit toys, stationery flat lays carry the style alone. Composition: any of the four layout grammars above, from a single centered portrait to a maximal twenty-cutout collage. Lighting: from clean high-key studio to heavy dreamy blur; scan-flat evenness always. Color handling: pink-dominant, blue-dominant, mint, peach or lavender leans are all valid, as long as fields stay pale and chroma concentrates in small clipart and type. Mood: boisterous candy energy at one end, quiet comforting tenderness at the other. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a pigeon on a star field or a bowl of paw-print cookies is as Puppycore as a Corgi portrait, provided the palette, haze, typography and merchandise grammar hold.

**Avoid.** Dark, moody or high-contrast lighting and hard shadows; black, neon, or earth-tone dominant palettes; grunge, grain-of-film-sepia, or 70s warm nostalgia; sharp unedited documentary pet photography with neutral color; aggressive or working-dog portrayal (snarling, guarding, kennel realism); minimalist modern flat design and corporate sans-serif typography; sleek 3D-render gloss; vector character mascots standing in for photographic animals; maximalist clutter of non-kawaii objects; irony, edge, or horror framing.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Puppycore |
|---|---|
| **Clovercore** | Clovercore lives outdoors: grass greens, clovers, frogs, picnics, foraged plants. Puppycore is indoor merchandise pastel — if the green is a lawn and the motifs are flora, it is Clovercore; if the green is an apple-clipart leaf on pink gingham, it is Puppycore. |
| **Heisei Retro** | The umbrella term: 1990s–2000s Japanese nostalgia that also covers flip phones, Tamagotchi, bolder saturated palettes, fashion and city scenes. Puppycore is the pet-cute facet; remove the animals and pet-merch grammar and you have generic Heisei Retro. |
| **Cutecore** | A 2020s digital editing trend, subject-agnostic (food, selfies, any object) with cleaner overlay sparkle. Puppycore shares those techniques but insists on puppies/pet merchandise and printed-stationery artifacts — die-cuts, halftone, brand-style logotypes. |
| **Kawaii** | The whole of Japanese cute culture across decades, including flat saturated vector character design and red-and-white primaries. Puppycore is a pastel, photo-manipulation-first, 2000s-nostalgic subset; broad character-driven kawaii without haze or pet photos is not Puppycore. |
| **Sanriocore** | Built on drawn Sanrio mascots (Hello Kitty, My Melody, Cinnamoroll). Puppycore uses photographs of real animals, cut out and edited; if the faces are illustrated character mascots rather than photo cutouts, it is Sanriocore. |

**Honest gaps:** lavender is named in the style's palette but appears only faintly in surviving material; wide-angle muzzle distortion is documented in period poster photography yet reads subtly in most examples. Treat both as licensed options, not requirements.
