# Clockpunk

Clockpunk (also called Machinepunk) is a subgenre of speculative fiction and its companion visual aesthetic: a world where technology never passed through steam or electricity, but instead perfected the Renaissance art of clockwork. Where Steampunk extrapolates from the Victorian boiler, Clockpunk extrapolates from the spring, the gear and the escapement of 14th–17th century Italy, and pushes them to surreal, impossible sophistication — automatons that think, dragons of brass ribbon, cities whose towers turn like dials. Its patron saint is Leonardo da Vinci: the inventor-artist whose sketchbooks fuse anatomy, ornament and mechanism. The aesthetic's core conviction is that a machine is not merely functional but a work of art, carved and gilded to the standard of a Baroque altarpiece, assembled by hand before industry existed.

## Visual Identity

### Colour palette

The palette rule is a warm metallic core with parchment neutrals and soot-brown shadows; cool colours appear only as small counter-accents.

- **Metallic core:** bronze `#8C6A2F` through brass `#B5A642` to gold `#D4AF37`; copper `#B87333` as the secondary metal; silver/pewter `#9C9A92` used sparingly for blades, chains and pale metalwork.
- **Wood tones:** walnut `#6B4A2F` down to near-black oak `#3E2C1E` for cases, beams, carved panels, leather-adjacent browns.
- **Neutrals:** parchment, plaster and ivory `#EFE6D0`–`#F7F1E3` for robes, porcelain automaton shells, sun-bleached city walls.
- **Shadows:** soot and umber `#2A2118`–`#4A3B2A`, never pure black, never cool grey.
- **Light:** candle amber `#E9B44C` as the characteristic highlight.
- **Cool accents (small doses only):** verdigris green-blue `#6E8F8A` on aged bronze, dusty cerulean `#7FA8C0` in sashes, sky and patina.

The style tolerates two lighting poles within this palette: a **dark pole** of candlelit chambers where amber highlights sit on deep umber ground, and a **pale pole** of hazy, sun-bleached vistas where ivory plaster and pale brass dominate against cream sky. Both are correct as long as the warm metallic core and brown shadow family remain; what is never correct is saturated cool colour, neon, or industrial grey as the dominant register.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Clockwork mechanism itself:** interlocking gears and cogwheels, coiled springs, levers, escapements, winding keys and winding cores. The gear train is the style's skeleton; it is usually *visible*, exposed through openwork plates or glass, rather than hidden in a casing.
2. **Automatons:** clockwork robots and clockwork animals — humanoid servants, birds, insects, spiders, beasts — built of brass segments, riveted plates and articulated joints, sometimes with blown-glass or porcelain bodies.
3. **Ornithopters and clockwork flight:** flying machines with flapping, ribbed membrane wings (brass ribs over leather or canvas), spring-driven rotors, and mechanical creatures aloft; flight in this style beats its wings rather than burning fuel.
4. **Polished lenses and mirrors:** ground glass, telescope barrels, spectacle lenses, mirrored discs — the period's optics rendered as jewellery-bright focal points.
5. **Intricate carvings and elaborate embellishment on machinery:** scrollwork, rosettes, meander (Greek key) bands, engraved foliate patterns, gilded relief applied directly to mechanical parts. Ornament is not added to the machine; the machine is ornament.
6. **Dials and clocks:** Roman-numeral faces, concentric rings, ornate hands, clock towers and pocket-watch medallions; the circular dial recurs as a compositional device as often as a literal object.
7. **Renaissance/Baroque architectural frame:** rounded arches, domes, columns, coffered and carved niches, gilded frames, spired cityscapes laced with bridges and aqueduct-like walkways.
8. **The inventor and the sketch:** the da Vinci figure — robed scholar, masked artificer, apprentice in doublet — plus the paraphernalia of study: blueprints, parchment diagrams, candlelit workbenches.
9. **Alchemy and magic as hidden power (conceptual, subtle):** because a spring stores limited energy, the style permits a fantastical power source — a glowing alchemical orb, arcane inscription, impossible perpetual motion — expressed as a quiet uncanny glow or impossible scale, not as spellcasting spectacle.

### Materials and textures

Wood, brass and bronze are the declared materials, explicitly *not* the industrial iron and copper of Steampunk. Supporting materials: gold leaf and gilding, silver, blown and ground glass, parchment and blueprint paper, leather straps and bellows, porcelain and enamel for pale automaton shells, canvas or membrane for wings. Textures to specify: polished metal with bright edge-wear and patina settled in recesses; hammered or engraved plate with small, jewel-like rivets (never long industrial rivet seams); deep relief carving in wood and stone; gauzy wing membrane stretched over metal ribbing; dust motes in light shafts; candle soot warm rather than grime cold.

### Recurring subjects

Clockwork creatures and humanoid automatons; inventor, scholar, masked or robed figures in Renaissance-cut dress (robes, sashes, doublets, cloaks, ceremonial masks); mechanical interiors — workshops, mansion chambers, candlelit cabinets of curiosities; sprawling pre-industrial cityscapes crowned with gears, dials and flying machines; isolated object studies of a single device or automaton; macro views of gear trains and dial faces. Cartoon, anime, painterly concept-art, 3D game-render and product-photography treatments all occur: the style is medium-agnostic, defined by content and palette rather than by rendering technique.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Circular/medallion logic:** gears, dials and concentric carved arches frame subjects like halos or mandalas; symmetry and ornamental framing are common.
- **The perch:** a machine or creature mounted high on a tower or bracket, overlooking a layered, atmospheric vista of spires, bridges and smoke-thin haze.
- **The niche:** a centered figure standing within a carved, gilded architectural recess.
- **The object study:** a single automaton or device isolated on a plain neutral ground, lit like a museum specimen.
- **Candlelit interior:** one warm flame source, deep umber shadow, glints on brass and glass.
- **Light shafts:** window light cutting through dust in tall chambers; soft hazy daylight with strong atmospheric perspective in exteriors; occasionally pale diffuse overcast for the ivory/porcelain variant.
- Scale play is welcome: gears the size of buildings, dials embedded in city walls — the surrealism the style is noted for.

## Mood & Values

Clockpunk expresses wonder at handmade precision. Its mood ranges from scholarly curiosity (the inventor's bench, the diagram, the lens) to courtly reverence (gilded niches, ceremonial robes, masks) to whimsical surrealism (a clockwork cat, a bulb-bodied spider, a dragon coiled around a bell tower) to candlelit mystery and gentle melancholy. Underlying values: craftsmanship over mass production, ornament over bare utility, curiosity over conquest, the artisan's hand over the factory's stamp. Machines are loved objects here — wound, oiled, engraved and inherited — which is why even ominous clockpunk scenes keep a jeweller's delicacy rather than an engineer's brutality.

## Era & Origins

The aesthetic draws on the Italian Renaissance and Baroque, roughly the 14th through 17th centuries, and on pre-industrial craftsmanship generally. As a named genre it is a modern retrofuturist offshoot of the "punk" family — a sibling of Steampunk that reacts against Steampunk's Victorian London soot and iron by reaching two centuries earlier, to Renaissance Italy and the worldview of da Vinci. It appears across games (*Assassin's Creed II*, *Dishonored 2*, *Thief*, *Syberia*, *Fable II*, the Clockwork City of *Morrowind*/*ESO*), film (*Hugo*, *The Adventures of Baron Munchausen*, *The Three Musketeers*) and television (*Da Vinci's Demons*, *Clockwork Planet*). Because spring power is finite, the genre deliberately keeps one foot in the Renaissance's magical and alchemical imagination, which licenses its surrealist streak.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**

1. **Mechanism first.** Every device must read as spring- and gear-driven: show the gear train, the coiled spring, the winding key, the escapement. Power is stored, wound and released — never burned, never wired.
2. **Ornament is structural.** Engrave, carve, gild and scrollwork the machine. A plain utilitarian box with a dial is not clockpunk; the machine must look like it was finished by a goldsmith.
3. **Material truth.** Brass, bronze, gold, wood, glass, parchment, leather. Polish on edges, patina in recesses, small decorative rivets — not industrial plate.
4. **Era silhouette.** Renaissance/Baroque dress (robes, sashes, doublets, cloaks, ceremonial masks) and architecture (rounded arches, domes, rosettes, meander bands, gilded frames). No Victorian cut, no modern cut.
5. **Light from flame or sky.** Candle, lantern, window shaft, hazy sun. Never electric cool white, never neon.
6. **Surreal allowance.** Impossible scale, clockwork fauna, a quiet alchemical glow as power source — all legitimate; the style is explicitly more surrealist than Steampunk.
7. **Medium-agnostic.** Painterly illustration, 3D render, product photograph of a real object, anime line art, film-still realism: all acceptable if the rules above hold.

**Variation axes.** Subject may run from a macro of a single escapement, to an object-study automaton, to a costumed figure, to a candlelit interior, to a continent-scale clockwork cityscape. Composition may be medallion-circular, niche-symmetrical, specimen-on-neutral-ground, wide atmospheric vista, or diagonal perch. Lighting may span candle chiaroscuro, dust-filled window shafts, hazy golden daylight, or pale overcast ivory. Colour handling may lean dark-amber or pale-porcelain as long as the brass/bronze core and umber shadows persist. Mood may be whimsical, scholarly, reverent or ominous. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a clockwork songbird on a plain table and a gear-crowned cathedral facade are equally valid clockpunk.

**Avoid (style-breaking elements).**

- Steam power: boilers, smokestacks-as-engine, pistons, pressure gauges, driving wheels of locomotives.
- Victorian Steampunk costume defaults: top hats, corsets, bustles, cravats-with-brass-goggles-on-leather.
- Industrial iron and copper plate with long rivet seams; soot-blackened factory grime.
- Electricity and modernity: exposed wiring, sockets, bulbs-as-lighting, LEDs, neon, screens, digital displays, plastics, chrome, carbon fibre, rubber tyres.
- Dieselpunk and cyberpunk registers: rusted scrap metal, matte-black tactical gear, saturated neon accents.
- Dungeonpunk crudeness: spiked iron, chains, torchlit subterranean brutalism.
- Minimalist or flat modern design; airbrushed sci-fi smoothness; unornamented functionalism.
- Fixed-wing propeller aircraft or jets as the flight solution (flight should flap, spin on springs, or drift on rare lighter-than-air silhouettes at most).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Shared ground | How Clockpunk differs |
|---|---|---|
| **Steampunk** | Retrofuturist "punk" gear-fiction, goggles-and-brass surface | Steampunk is Victorian London, steam-powered, iron and copper, sooty and industrial; Clockpunk is Renaissance Italy, spring-powered, brass/bronze/wood, gilded and hand-finished, with dials, winding keys and flapping ornithopters instead of boilers and trains |
| **Italian Renaissance** | Same dress, architecture, palette, carving | Pure Renaissance has no speculative machinery; Clockpunk always inserts anachronistic mechanism — exposed gear trains, automatons, flying devices — into the period frame |
| **Baroque** | Gilding, heavy ornament, chiaroscuro | Baroque drama is devotional and theatrical — swirling drapery, ecstasy, ceiling frescoes — with no machines; Clockpunk redirects that ornament and drama onto devices, making the mechanism the sacred object |
| **Dungeonpunk** | Pre-modinary fantasy technology | Dungeonpunk is crude, grimy, subterranean: raw iron, spikes, chains, torchlit stone, brutal traps; Clockpunk is precise, polished, urban and courtly: lenses, springs, engraved brass, jewel-fine joinery |

**Quick recognition checklist:** exposed gears or springs doing real work; brass/bronze/gold on wood with parchment or ivory neutrals; ornament carved into the machine itself; Roman numerals or concentric dial rings; flapping-wing or clockwork flight; candle or hazy-sun light; Renaissance/Baroque silhouette; zero steam, zero electricity, zero Victoriana. If most of these hold, the image is clockpunk regardless of subject, medium or composition.
