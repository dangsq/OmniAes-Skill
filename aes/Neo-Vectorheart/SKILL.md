# Neo-Vectorheart

Neo-Vectorheart, also called Vectorheart Revival, emerged in the late 2010s as a pseudo-revival of the early-2000s Vectorheart aesthetic, carried in on the same Neo-Y2K wave that resurrected other Y2K-era visual languages. It is a pastiche discipline: designers working in it consciously homage the design firms that built the original Vectorheart look, reproducing that sleek, dynamic vector craft with a colder, darker, more monochrome sensibility. Its native habitats are logo and wordmark systems (its primary category), electronic bass-music cover art (neurofunk, dubstep, industrial techno), esports and video-game branding and interface frames, and technical schematic plates. The style lives on a spectrum between two poles: a restrained minimal pole of a single emblem plus wordmark on black, and a maximalist "gamer-grunge" pole of dense HUD clusters, glitch displacement and grain borrowed from Cyberpunk visuals. Both poles share the same geometry and the same monochrome discipline.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is monochrome first: near-black grounds (`#000000`–`#101010`) and paper-white ink (`#F2F2F2`–`#FFFFFF`), with neutral greys (`#1C1C1C`, `#4A4A4A`, `#8C8C8C`, `#D8D8D8`) as the only permitted mid-tones. Color enters as a single saturated accent, never as a palette: signal red (`#FF1E00`–`#D60000`), acid lime-yellow (`#DFFF00`–`#FFD400`), electric cyan (`#00CFFF`–`#0090E0`), amber-orange (`#FF7A00`), or violet (`#6A00FF`–`#B400FF`); magenta appears only inside glitch stacks. The accent shows up as a slash, a vertical stripe, a small text run, a glitch bar, or a tint on one element. A licensed variant inverts the scheme into a single-hue monochrome field — the whole canvas flooded in one saturated hue (cyan, red duotone, pale lavender) with black and white vector elements on top — which still reads monochrome because hue count stays at one plus neutrals. Bright, high-contrast handling is constant; soft tonal blending is not.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Striking vector shapes** — the core motif: flat, matte, hard-edged polygons with notched and chamfered corners; angular shield, Y- and trident-like emblems; shard clusters and fractured plate forms.
2. **45- and 60-degree diagonals** — every cut, chamfer, slash and tilt obeys these angles: diagonal bands crossing the canvas, type set on a slant, corners clipped at 45°, wedge shapes interrupting wordmarks.
3. **Futuristic typography** — custom glyph-run display type that is deliberately cryptic or illegible (rune-like alphabet specimens), wide-tracked angular sans-serif wordmarks, and microscopic monospace metadata lines of fake or real technical data.
4. **Monochromatic color scheme with lone accent** — as above; this is what separates the revival from its predecessor.
5. **Neubrutalist vernacular** — blunt rectangular panels, stripped utilitarian grids, matter-of-fact labels and credits set plainly ("PODCAST 001", "HOSTED BY"), hard-edged blocks instead of soft cards.
6. **Cyberpunk / gamer-grunge maximalism** — HUD clusters, barcode tick columns, crosshairs and registration marks, leader lines, pixel-sort glitch storms, scanline and grain corruption.
7. **Logo and wordmark lockups** — emblem plus wordmark plus microtext arranged as a complete identity plate; the style's primary output category.
8. **Technical and exploded schematics** — wireframe machinery, exploded assemblies with leader lines to tiny labels, engine diagrams rendered as fine white line on dark grounds.
9. **Interface frames** — loading screens, OS-like window stacks, readout panels and console screens treated as graphic compositions.

### Materials and textures

Matte flat vector fills dominate; there is no gloss, no paper, no organic texture. Over the flatness sits a layer of machine noise: fine film grain on black fields, faint blueprint or grid underlays, glitch displacement and pixel-sorting on type and imagery, scanline banding, and 1-pixel hairline strokes for all schematic work. Where three-dimensional or photographic matter appears it is secondary: dark chrome or gunmetal machine parts rim-lit in a void, wireframe renders, or photography crushed into a single-hue duotone. Evidence for photographic content in this style is thin and always treated graphically — tinted, cropped, subordinated to vector overlay.

### Recurring subjects

Logo and identity systems for collectives, labels and forums; single and EP covers for bass-music artists; esports titles and game branding; game loading screens and HUDs; glyph and type specimen sheets; exploded engine or device diagrams; podcast and event posters. Human presence, when any, is a small silhouette inside a technical environment, never a portrait.

### Composition and lighting

Composition is asymmetric and scale-extreme: a massive emblem or glyph block against 4-point annotation type; micro-marks anchored in corners; registration ticks and rulers hugging the edges; vast structured negative space treated as an active element; or, at the maximalist pole, a centered exploded diagram radiating leader lines into dense marginal data. Diagonal bands or slashes cut the frame to inject motion. Lighting is overwhelmingly non-photographic — flat graphic light with no source. When depth exists it comes from a matte black void with faint ambient grid glow, rim-lit metal in darkness, or luminance emitted by glitch bars and accent stripes. There is no daylight, no softbox, no warm ambient light anywhere in this style.

## Mood & Values

Cold precision, machine romance, underground credibility. Neo-Vectorheart values craft — custom-cut type, exact angles, disciplined hierarchy — and secrecy, via glyph scripts that refuse to be read by outsiders. It expresses gamer and bass-music subcultural identity: sleek enough to signal professional design lineage, grimy enough to signal the underground. Its emotional register is alert and kinetic rather than cozy: everything looks like it is booting, transmitting, or targeting.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s into the early 2020s, online, within the Neo-Y2K movement's revival of Y2K-era vector design. It reacts to its own ancestor: where original Vectorheart was bright, busy and abstract, the revival narrows to monochrome, sharpens into logos and HUDs, and absorbs a decade of Cyberpunk game interfaces and "gamer-grunge." It was embraced by online gamers and by the bass-music cover-art community, and game-side appearances reach into titles such as the Death Stranding series and Arknights: Endfield. The wiki's game corpus is short, so treat game-UI usage as documented but not exhaustive.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build every form from vector geometry cut at 45 or 60 degrees: chamfered shields, notched plates, shard clusters. Let typography carry the image: a cryptic glyph run, a wide-tracked angular wordmark, and at least one block of microscopic monospace data. Hold the palette to black, white, greys and one accent — or flood the canvas in one hue and work monochromatically on top. Add technical scaffolding: hairline leader lines, tick rulers, barcodes, crosshairs, corner registration marks. Contrast scales violently: hero emblem huge, annotations tiny. Finish with machine texture: grain, glitch displacement, scanlines — never smooth perfection. Keep fills matte and edges hard.

**Variation axes.** Subject: identity lockup, music cover, game UI/loading screen, schematic plate, type specimen, event poster — any of these. Composition: centered glyph block, corner-locked lockup, exploded center with radial leaders, diagonal band cut, dense full-bleed HUD. Color handling: pure black/white; black/white plus one accent; single-hue monochrome field; duotone photography under vector overlay. Mood: minimal-cold lockup through maximalist gamer-grunge. Medium: flat vector print, screen interface, wireframe or dark-chrome 3D render. Lighting: flat graphic light, void with ambient grid glow, rim-lit metal, glitch emission. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a poster for a fictional transit authority or a schematic of an imaginary engine can be perfectly Neo-Vectorheart without resembling any known exemplar.

**Avoid.** Rounded corners and soft organic curves; gradients, aurora blends, glossy sheen; pastel or earth-tone palettes; more than one accent hue competing; serif, script or friendly humanist type; hand-drawn marks, paper grain, brush texture; natural daylight, bokeh, soft shadows; photorealistic human portraits as the hero; skeuomorphic buttons and bevels; hexagonal honeycomb tessellation as the structural grid; melted chrome liquid type; cluttered photo collage without vector discipline; warm cozy domestic content.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Against Vectorheart (the predecessor):** original Vectorheart is brighter, multi-hued and more abstract — busy pattern fields of free vector ornament. Neo-Vectorheart is monochrome-dominant, less abstract, logo- and HUD-driven, with grain and glitch grunge the original lacks.
- **Against Acid Design:** Acid Design melts: stretched liquid chrome type, psychedelic color stacks, rave chaos, rounded and warped forms. Neo-Vectorheart never melts; its edges stay hard, its angles exact, its palette restrained to one accent.
- **Against Hexatron:** Hexatron organizes everything on hexagonal and honeycomb grids with corporate tech-blue optimism. Neo-Vectorheart uses 45/60-degree diagonal cuts and shard geometry, darker grounds, and music-scene grit rather than corporate grid futurism.
- **Against Neubrutalism:** Neubrutalism is deliberately unpolished — default sans-serifs, flat primary or pastel blocks, thick black borders, hard offset shadows, web-native anti-design. Neo-Vectorheart borrows only the blunt vernacular; its type is custom-futuristic, its ground dark monochrome, its finish sleek and technically dense.
- **Against Cyberminimalism:** Cyberminimalism is quiet: vast negative space, a single hairline gesture, near-nothingness. Neo-Vectorheart shares the dark field but adds emblematic weight, diagonal dynamism and, at its maximalist pole, dense gamer-grunge detail that Cyberminimalism would delete.

**Quick recognition check:** hard vector emblem with chamfered corners + 45/60° slashes + cryptic futuristic type + black/white field with one accent + micro-monospace data and hairline scaffolding = Neo-Vectorheart.
