# Bloghouse

Bloghouse (Blog House, Bloghaus, Dirty Electro House) is a microgenre of electro house, a party scene, and a fashion trend that lived on the mid-to-late-2000s internet: MP3 blogs like Hype Machine, Gorilla vs. Bear, Big Stereo and Missing Toof, with MySpace as the town square. The name is retrospective — coined in 2008 by the Hipster Runoff blog just as the scene peaked out — and participants simply called it electro or house. Visually it is the photo arm of that DIY network: images shot on cheap digital cameras and early phone flashes in dark clubs, uploaded to Tumblr or MySpace before dawn, plus a graphic-design layer of flyers, blog headers and album art. It inherits from French house, electroclash, eurodisco and nu-rave, and it hands the baton to Indie Sleaze. Its self-image in one line: gritty, hyper-saturated, messy but confident, trashy yet stylish.

## Visual Identity

### Light and medium (the signature)
The single strongest identifier is **direct on-camera flash in a dark room**. Subjects sit one to two metres from the lens; skin and cotton blow out to a glossy glare; the background falls off into black or a starkly lit wall (plywood, brick, wood panelling, club interior). Add the failures of cheap sensors as features: luminance noise, JPEG squish, slight motion blur on dancing limbs, red-eye-adjacent hotspots, sweat sheen reading as specular white. Strobe smear and glowstick light-trails are welcome secondary light events. Nothing should look lit by a softbox or by daylight.

### Color palette
Rule: **saturated neons exist as light sources, plastics and hair dye against flash-black; whatever the flash touches blows toward white.** Acidic triad from the scene's graphics — pink, green, metallic silver — anchors everything.

| Role | Range |
|---|---|
| Flash-blown highlight / skin glare | #FFFFFF – #F2E4D8 |
| Acid pink / magenta | #FF1F9C, #E6007E, #FF4FD8 |
| Chartreuse / acid green | #39FF14, #7FFF00, #00E066 |
| Electric violet / purple | #8A2BE2, #B026FF |
| Hyper-cyan / blue | #00E5FF, #0057FF |
| Highlighter yellow / orange (drinks, glow tubes) | #FFE600, #FF6A00 |
| Metallic silver (chrome type, stickers) | #C9C9D2, #8E8E96 |
| Club void / flash-black shadow | #050505 – #161616 |

Muted, earthy or desaturated handling is out of range; so is smooth airbrushed neon (that reads Swag). Neon may dominate as clothing dye, as practical light, or as a graphic ink — any of those handlings is correct.

### Motifs and objects, ranked by how strongly the style documents them
1. **Chaotic party imagery**: crowds dancing in dark clubs, drinking, posing with reckless confidence; the feeling of being caught mid-movement.
2. **Neon tones and neon lighting**: neon leggings and dye-bright hair, glowsticks, light tubes, strobe.
3. **American Apparel basics**: plain bright tees, hoodies, simple cotton layers as the wardrobe base.
4. **Shutter shades** and **thick black glasses**.
5. **Skinny jeans and cheap leather jackets** (cracked pleather shine).
6. **Graphic-design layer**: flyers, blog headers, album art with bold distorted typography, MS Paint-style cuts and collages, acidic pink/green/silver inks on black.
7. **Kool-Aid** and drink props: plastic cups, cans, juice-coloured cocktails held toward the lens.
8. **Friday the 13th hockey masks**, especially on DJs.

Secondary accents observed in the photographic record (valid, but lower authority): trucker hats and mirrored aviators, cupcake or snack props, corner blog watermarks and party-promo logo stamps, stickered laptop lids at the DJ booth, plywood or brick promo-portrait backdrops.

### Materials and textures
Thin cotton jersey, denim skinny jeans, cracked pleather, polyester hoodie fleece, plastic shutter-shade slats, plastic cups and anodised cans, glowstick plastic, sticker-paper gloss, chrome vinyl type. The image surface itself is a material: low-megapixel grain, compression blocks, flash glare, sweat.

### Subjects and composition
Snapshot candids dominate: cramped frames, bodies piled into the lens, subjects leaning in or kissing a friend's cheek, props thrust forward, tilted horizons, cropped limbs, groups of two to a dozen. DJ-booth angles (decks, laptops, masked or leather-jacketed performers) and staged promo portraits against a stark flash-lit wall are equally in range. Composition may be centred and confrontational or genuinely accidental; both read correctly because the style values immediacy over craft.

## Mood & Values

DIY ethos above all: fans as publicists, bypassing labels and radio, eclecticism born of file-sharing. The mood is immediacy and imperfection — celebration of the unretouched moment, irony paired with opulence (a lineage from Detroit techno's Dopplereffekt and Cybotron), detached sharp vocal cool inherited from electroclash. Reckless confidence, sweat-glazed euphoria, trashy-yet-stylish self-awareness. Honest footnote: the scene's party dynamics have been retrospectively criticised for exploitation; generations should keep the euphoria adult-presenting, consensual and celebratory rather than predatory.

## Era & Origins

Peak roughly 2005–2009, rooted in the American blogosphere with a French-house and electroclash soundtrack (Justice, Daft Punk, Uffie, MSTRKRFT, Crystal Castles, Boys Noize, Yelle). It reacts against gatekeepers — record labels, radio, professional music press — and visually against polished professional photography: the cheap flash *is* the credential. Named in July 2008; predecessor to Indie Sleaze. Period tech cues: compact digicams, flip phones, wired earbuds, MP3 players, stickered laptops.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. One flash, one moment: light as a single on-camera burst in a dark room.
2. Push saturation past truth; let neons clip.
3. Let the medium fail honestly: noise, blur, glare, compression.
4. Crowd or cramped intimacy; bodies overlap the frame edge.
5. Dress code as shorthand: AA basics, skinny jeans, cheap leather, shutter shades or thick black glasses, one neon accent minimum.
6. For collateral (flyer, header, cover): sticker-bomb collage, stretched/distorted type, MS Paint crudeness, pink-green-silver on black.
7. Props in hand: cup, can, glowstick, snack, mask.

**Variation axes.** Subject: club crowd, DJ set, backstage pile-up, warehouse or rooftop afterparty, staged promo portrait against plywood/brick/plain wall, bathtub-or-confetti styled shoot, flyer/album art/blog header. Composition: centred snapshot, cramped group huddle, overhead DJ angle, tilted candid, flat graphic collage. Lighting: direct flash, strobe smear, neon-tube practicals, flash-fill in a dim room. Color handling: neon-on-black, acidic pastels under flash, silver-plus-pink graphic ink, near-monochrome black with a single neon spike. Mood: euphoric, ironic-deadpan, chaotic, tender-candid. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Softbox or studio lighting, golden hour, window daylight as key; polished modern EDM-festival renders (main-stage LEDs, confetti cannons, clean 4K); warm analog film grain and medium-format softness (Indie/Hipster territory); muted earth tones, beige, desaturation; retouched poreless skin; 2010s+ wardrobes (snapback swag poses, hypebeast logos, athleisure); vector-clean minimal typography; modern smartphones, selfie sticks, ring lights; goth black-on-black with no neon; exploitative or sexualised framing of young-looking subjects.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Swag**: early-2010s hip-hop brag, airbrushed smooth neon, snapbacks and gold chains, posed "stuntin'" portraits. Bloghouse keeps sweat, sensor noise, club context and electro wardrobe; it never looks airbrushed.
- **Electroclash**: the colder late-90s/early-2000s ancestor — stagey, retro-futurist chrome-black-red, detached poses, clean vector graphics. Bloghouse is its messy descendant: snapshot not stage, sticker collage not minimal vector.
- **Hipster**: same years, opposite light — daylight, film grain or cross-processed mute, flannel and cardigans, outdoorsy deadpan. If the image could occur at a farmers market, it is not Bloghouse.
- **Indie**: 2000s indie rock earnestness, natural light, warm film palette, band-in-a-room portraits. Bloghouse swaps earnestness for irony and film for cheap digital glare.
- **Indie Sleaze**: closest neighbour and successor; shares flash, parties, American Apparel. Indie Sleaze skews rock-decadent: smeared mascara, cigarettes, heroin-chic, warm analog flash editorial. Bloghouse skews dance-electro: glowsticks, DJ decks, shutter shades, neon leggings, and a sticker-bomb blog-graphic layer with distorted type. Torn-editorial graphics say Indie Sleaze; flyer/blog-header graphics say Bloghouse.
- (Fringe neighbours: New Rave adds UK rave fluoro and smiley motifs; Tecktonik adds French club-kid dance poses — Bloghouse keeps the North American blog irony instead.)
