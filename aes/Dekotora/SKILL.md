# Dekotora

Dekotora (デコトラ, short for "decoration truck"), also called art trucks, are extravagantly decorated Japanese trucks. Workers build them from their own work vehicles for fun; hobbyists build them for special events and shows. Either way the result is a utilitarian machine — flatbed, cargo box, cab-over rig, trailer combination — converted into a mobile festival float and personal monument: bolted steel superstructures stacked above the cab like shrine roofs, hundreds of neon tubes and bulbs turning the vehicle into its own light source, and a cab interior lined in brocade under a crystal chandelier. The style emerged in 1970s Japan and entered the mass imagination through Toei, the film studio credited with creating it, whose truck movies turned a working-class decorating habit into a national aesthetic. Dekotora is maximalism with a chassis: excess that is engineered, symmetrical, luminous and driveable.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: emitted light on dark or mirrored ground

Color in dekotora arrives chiefly as *emission* — tubes, bulbs, backlit panels — so saturated hues sit on near-black night or on mirror metal, never on matte neutral surfaces.

| Role | Hex range |
|---|---|
| Cool neon anchor | teal #00E5C0–#00FFD0, electric green #33FF55–#7CFF6B |
| Warm neon counterpoint | hot pink / magenta #FF2D9B–#FF4FD8, ultraviolet purple #7A2BFF–#B44BFF |
| Bulbs and accents | warm white #FFF6D8, signal red #FF2222, amber #FFB300 |
| Metal neutrals | stainless silver #C8CCD4–#F0F2F5, chrome near-white highlights, gold/brass #D4AF37–#FFD75E |
| Ground | night black #050508–#12121A; overcast grey #9AA0A6 for daylight metalwork studies |

The rule, not the recipe: at least one cool anchor hue (teal or green) plus saturated warm counterpoints, all at neon saturation, against black or polished metal. A given truck may lean green-teal, magenta-purple, or full rainbow mix; extravagant paint and murals add secondary color but never replace the light.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Neon and ultraviolet lighting architecture** — hundreds of neon tubes, LED arrays and matrix panels, rows of colored bulbs, UV lamps; the vehicle illuminates itself "like a maximalist spaceship." Bulb rows line bumpers, roof edges and racks; tubes outline panels, frames and fenders.
2. **Architectural body modifications** — elaborate grille guards, protruding visors and roof spoilers, oversized bumper extensions, customized mirror brackets; above the cab, tiered superstructures of light racks, masts and cantilevered frames stacked like pagoda or shrine roofs, often crowned with star, crest or finial ornaments.
3. **Polished metalwork** — golden chassis parts, chrome plating, gleaming stainless steel; mirror-finish and engraved or embossed steel sheet cladding body panels so the truck reflects and multiplies its own lights.
4. **Signage and lettering** — illuminated marquee panels and nameplates carrying the truck's personal name in kanji or romaji, bordered by bulb rows, star strips and crest medallions. Lettering is personal identity, never commercial advertising.
5. **Decorative fixtures** — custom lamps, mini crystal chandeliers hanging in the cab, hanging ornaments and tassels, high-end brand accessories; stickers and plushies as smaller personal tokens (key motifs of the style, but read as minor accents against the light and steel).
6. **Murals and airbrush art** — airbrushed or hand-painted panels of traditional mythical creatures (dragons, phoenixes, koi), Japanese landscapes and waves, gods, or pop-culture icons, mounted on cargo sides and backlit light-box panels.
7. **Luxurious upholstery** — velvet, brocade and damask, frequently floral, concealing seats, dashboards, door cards and cab ceilings; the cab becomes a brocade-lined shrine room.

### Materials and textures

Mirror-polished stainless and chrome; gold-painted or gilded fittings; etched, embossed and perforated steel sheet; glass neon tubes and bulb reflector cones; backlit acrylic light boxes; airbrush lacquer and candy paint over metal flake; brocade, velvet, fringe and crystal-bead chandeliers. The texture story is a three-way contrast: hard mirror metal, soft plush textile, glowing glass.

### Recurring subjects

The decorated truck as protagonist, in any working-vehicle body style. Secondary subjects: the owner or driver seated in the brocade cab; trucks parked and fully lit at night gatherings and shows; trucks rolling in parades or event processions; close detail studies of a grille guard, mirror bracket, bumper bulb row, chandelier or mural panel.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Frontal, near-symmetrical presentations that stage the full width of the light architecture; three-quarter views exposing tiered height; low angles looking up the light masts against black sky; side profiles reading the truck as a moving lantern. Lighting is self-emitted: the vehicle is the key light of the frame, backgrounds drop to night black, wet asphalt and chrome catch colored reflections, bulbs flare in starbursts. Legitimate alternates exist: overcast or plaza daylight where the chrome and stainless sculpture carries the style with lights muted or off, and warm chandelier glow for interior cab scenes.

## Mood & Values

Dekotora expresses working-class pride turned spectacle: the tool of labor reclaimed as the owner's canvas, name and monument. It carries festival (matsuri) brightness and a votive, shrine-like reverence — ornament piled on the machine the way offerings pile on a altar — while staying secular, personal and often humorous. It values handcraft and hours: every rack, tube and embroidered panel reads as devoted labor. Its excess is generous and outward-facing, a gift of light to onlookers, and a deliberate defiance of utilitarian restraint and minimalist good taste. Community belongs too: these trucks exist to be shown, paraded and photographed together.

## Era & Origins

1970s Japan. The name contracts "decoration truck"; creation is credited to Toei, the film studio whose 1970s truck films nationalized a working-class decorating practice. Because builders start from real work trucks, the style keeps working-vehicle DNA — cab-over cabs, flatbeds, cargo boxes, trailer hitches — beneath the ornament. It reacts against postwar utilitarian vehicle culture and against any ethic of restraint: the mass-produced machine is overwritten with one person's hand, name and taste.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** (1) The vehicle is the canvas and the protagonist; ornament must be bolted to a truck or seen from inside one. (2) Light is a building material: count sources in hundreds — tubes, bulb rows, matrix panels, UV wash — and let the truck light the scene. (3) Added architecture: tiers, racks, guards, visors and bumper extensions beyond any factory body. (4) Metal grammar: stainless, chrome and gold cladding with engraved panels that reflect the lights back. (5) Handmade bricolage, not factory finish: mixed motifs, personal kanji nameplates, asymmetries inside an overall symmetric frame. (6) Night-first lighting logic; if daylight, the metalwork sculpture must carry the style. (7) Density floor: no large empty panel or bare surface; every plane is lit, plated, painted or upholstered.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any commercial vehicle base and scale, from a small work truck to a long trailer rig; interior cab portraits; detail close-ups; event, parade or parking-gathering contexts. Composition: frontal symmetry, three-quarter, side profile, low-angle upward, eye-level interior. Lighting: full night illumination (most canonical), dusk, overcast daylight with lights off, UV-dominant purple washes, warm interior chandelier glow. Color handling: teal-green dominant, magenta-purple dominant, or full rainbow; gold-heavy versus stainless-heavy metalwork. Mood: festive parade, solitary night highway, proud owner portrait, static show display. Mural content: dragons, landscapes, deities, pop icons, or abstract pattern. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Stock, minimalist or fleet-livery trucks; matte wraps and advertising vinyl; corporate logos as signage. Neon without a vehicle (city signscapes, cyberpunk streets). Sleek concept or aerodynamic bodies with integrated, flush light strips — dekotora light is add-on, protruding, countable. American big-rig chrome culture alone (polished stacks and bull bars with no neon tubes, light towers, murals or kanji marquees). Shrine structures or festival floats with no vehicle chassis. Rust, patina, steampunk brass, rat-rod scruff. Pastel palettes, desaturated cinematic grades, or a single accent color on black. Maximalist cluttered rooms with no truck. Holograms, HUD graphics and screen-based sci-fi lighting in place of bulbs, tubes and backlit light boxes.

## Discrimination Cues

**Against Maximalism (closest neighbor).** Maximalism is excess as a free-floating interior and graphic principle: pattern-on-pattern rooms, crowded shelves, collage walls, static and object-based, in any palette under reflected light. Dekotora is excess mounted on a working vehicle, with an automotive and luminous grammar: neon tube arrays, bulb rows, chrome and stainless cladding, tiered light masts, kanji name marquees, brocade cabs. Two quick tests: if the truck were removed, would the image still read? If yes, it is Maximalism. And where does color come from? Dekotora color is emitted light against night; Maximalism color is reflected pigment in room light. Dekotora's clutter is also engineered — bolted steel frames, symmetrical racks, architectural tiers — rather than accumulated objects.

**Secondary neighbors.** American chrome truck culture polishes but does not build light architecture or paint murals. Cyberpunk vehicle design uses integrated, seamless light lines on fictional sleek bodies, lacking handmade bricolage and shrine-like tiering. Bosozoku car style favors stance, exhaust pipes and flags over luminous superstructure. Festival floats and shrine dashi share the ornament but lack the engine, chassis and cab that make dekotora a driving, working machine.
