# Trxsh

Trxsh (pronounced "trash", also called the Trash Gang Aesthetic) is an internet aesthetic of the late 2010s that grew out of the Slovenian YouTube collective Trash Gang, founded in 2017. Its raw material is the anime music video: shōnen and seinen characters — Tokyo Ghoul, Naruto, My Hero Academia, Jujutsu Kaisen, Darling in the Franxx, Future Diary, Hunter x Hunter, Neon Genesis Evangelion — re-edited to the tempo of underground emo rap and trap metal (XXXTentacion, $uicideboy$, Ghostemane, Scarlxrd, Night Lovell, Bones), then re-graded into high contrast and vandalized with digital distortion, tattoos, horns and masks. Fans copied the AMV language onto their own profile pictures, photos and clothing, which made Trxsh a feed-wide look rather than only a video style, and seeded the early E-Boy/E-Girl visual vocabulary. By the early 2020s the centralized channel commercialized and the underground community fragmented, freezing the style as a distinct late-2010s artifact.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is a **monochrome skeleton plus one saturated accent**. The structural field is black (#000000–#1c1c1c), off-white (#eeeeee–#ffffff) and mid-greys (#7a7a7a–#9c9c9c); whole images often sit at full grayscale. Into that field exactly one accent is allowed to bleed: canonically red, from dried blood (#6e0000–#8a0303) to siren crimson (#d40000–#ff1a1a), applied as glowing irises, flame washes, horn tips, poster backgrounds or blackletter side-text. Documented secondary accents — violet (#7d3cff), electric cyan (#00d0ff), magenta-pink (#ff33cc) and hazard yellow (#ffd900) — appear as chromatic-aberration fringes, glitch tints, hair color or stripe patterns, never as a harmonious multi-color scheme. A correct image may run nearly all red-on-black or nearly all white-void-with-black-ink; what must hold is that black and white carry the composition and at most one hue interrupts them.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how the text documents them)

1. **The sharp-toothed smiley / "shark mask"** — the collective's logo and the style's primary motif: a black band or mask across the mouth bearing a row of triangular white zigzag teeth. It appears as a worn cloth mask, as a drawn overlay on an anime face, as a grin floating on a blacked-out silhouette, and even grafted onto vintage cartoon characters.
2. **Devil horns** — small pointed horns, digitally added to hair, hoods or silhouettes; black or red.
3. **Tattoos and facial chains** — scribbled face/hand ink (numbers, short phrases, scratchy logos) and metal facial jewelry added to characters who canonically have none.
4. **Japanese typography overlays and scratchy blackletter/metal logos** — vertical side columns of kanji, bottom subtitle captions, corner watermarks and illegible pen-scrawl band logos treated as graphic elements.
5. **Glowing iris effects** — eyes blanked to luminous white or lit to red as the only light source in a dark face.
6. **Dark streetwear** — oversized black hoodies (often horned hoods), deconstructed Soft Grunge garments, layered chains and heavy metal hardware; deliberately androgynous.
7. **AMV distortion grammar** — scanlines, datamosh bands, chromatic aberration fringes, VHS grain, compression blur, vignettes; the image should look like a frame ripped from a synced edit.
8. **The pure silhouette** — image-supported extension of the high-contrast grade: figures crushed to a flat black shape readable only by horns and grin, set against a pale horizon, fog or floral field.

### Materials and textures

Flat cel-shaded anime color overwritten with ink hatching and pen scribble; grainy grayscale photography of concrete, brick walls, parking garages and bedrooms; fuzzy fleece and cotton hoodie fabric; matte metal chain links; low-bitrate video noise and analog tape softness; scratchy ballpoint-pen texture in the logo tags. Everything reads slightly degraded, copied, re-uploaded.

### Recurring subjects

Edited anime characters; manga-styled original characters with manic grins and face ink; real people in mask-and-hood cosplay (photographed, usually desaturated, often androgynous); horned silhouette demons holding nothing or a blade; co-opted vintage rubber-hose cartoons given the shark grin and a bleak subtitle; occasional 3D-rendered hooded figures with chain pendants.

### Composition and lighting

Portraits and busts dominate: the face or mask is centered and close, the grin the focal point. Typography lives at the margins — vertical text down one edge, caption strips along the bottom, logo stamps in corners. Backgrounds are either voids (blown white, crushed black, flat grey) or blurred, desaturated environments (alley, shelf room, garage, sea horizon). Lighting is a grade, not a source: crushed blacks, clipped whites, eyes or red washes glowing through, or the dim murk of a VHS tape. Collage layouts with ransom-note lettering and butterfly-like pasted elements are an accepted louder variant.

## Mood & Values

Edginess worn over melancholy. The grin is never joy: it is a mask of teeth over numbness, the visual equivalent of a distorted 808 under a lyric about grief — "it's as if nothing's funny anymore." Values are underground allegiance (rap logos and artist tags as community badges), anonymity (the mask erases the face), DIY piracy ethics (re-editing, re-uploading, degrading), and androgynous self-styling as armor. Aggression and sadness are the same gesture here.

## Era & Origins

Emerged 2017 with the Trash Gang YouTube channel, Slovenia-by-way-of-the-internet. It reacts against both polished mainstream anime fandom and clean social-media photography by dragging shōnen imagery into the SoundCloud underground: high-contrast grading, tempo-synced cuts, digital distortion. It fed the early E-Boy/E-Girl look through its contrast artifacts and re-edited anime iconography, and dissolved as a centralized scene by the early 2020s.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build monochrome first: black, white, grey carry the whole frame; then let one accent (red by default) bleed in as glow, text, wash or fringe.
2. Include at least one signature motif, ideally the sharp-toothed grin mask; horns, glowing eyes, face tattoos, chains and Japanese overlays stack on top.
3. Degrade the signal: at least one artifact layer — scanlines, chromatic aberration, tape grain, datamosh band, compression blur, vignette.
4. Treat type as image: scratchy blackletter logos, vertical kanji columns, bottom subtitle captions, corner tags.
5. Grade hard: crush the blacks or blow the whites; let eyes or a red wash be the only light source when dark.
6. Never a wholesome smile: grins are masked zigzag teeth, manic open-mouth snarls, or silhouette grins.
7. Clothed figures follow streetwear logic: oversized black hoodie or deconstructed layers, horned hood, stacked chains.

**Variation axes.** Subject: anime edit, manga-style OC, masked photo portrait, co-opted vintage cartoon, pure silhouette, 3D render — any of these, or any other subject treated with the rules above. Composition: tight bust, full-body streetwear stance, silhouette against a horizon, typography collage, captioned tape still. Lighting: white void, black crush, red flame glow, grey daylight photo, VHS dimness. Color handling: grayscale-plus-red as default, grayscale-plus-violet/cyan/magenta/yellow, full red-on-black poster, or a rare saturated pop variant that still keeps a black-and-white structural skeleton. Mood: menace, numbness, or bleak irony. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image** — a flower field, a duck, a parking garage or an invented character all read as Trxsh once the mask, grade, accent and degradation rules apply.

**Avoid**

- Clean 4K polish with no degradation layer; glossy commercial photography.
- Warm cozy palettes, pastel softness, or multi-hue harmonious schemes.
- Wholesome natural smiles or healthy glossy skin untouched by ink or grade.
- Occult ritual props — candles, sigils, goat skulls, church interiors (that is Devilcore).
- Vaporwave grids, sunset gradients, neon-pink skylines.
- Minimalist sans-serif corporate typography; absent or timid lettering where a tag belongs.
- Preppy, couture or high-fashion styling instead of oversized streetwear.
- Lush full-color nature or daylight documentary scenes without contrast crush.
- Kawaii cuteness with no menacing twist; tearful softness without aggression.
- Any image where black is not a structural color.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | How Trxsh differs |
|---|---|
| Dead Inside | Shares melancholy anime monochrome, but Dead Inside leans on bare manga panels, Cyrillic scrawl and hopeless stillness; Trxsh adds the shark-grin mask, horns, chains, rap-logo tags and tempo-edit glitch energy. |
| Emo Rap | Emo Rap centers real performers, face tattoos and colored hair in photo culture; Trxsh is anime-edit-first, with masked silhouettes and AMV distortion as the frame, not the musician's portrait. |
| Sadboi | Sadboi is soft, tearful, lo-fi blue-lavender gradients and gentle sorrow; Trxsh is high-contrast, toothy and aggressive, its sadness hidden behind a grin rather than worn on the cheek. |
| Devilcore | Devilcore's horns belong to folk-occult ritual — candles, paganism, rural dread in warm dim light; Trxsh horns are digital stickers on hoodies and anime heads, lit by tape noise and red grade, with no ritual context. |
| E-Boy | E-Boy keeps the chains and streetwear but in clean, well-lit TikTok polish with curtain hair and no mask; Trxsh is its degraded ancestor — masked, horned, grayscale, artifacted, grin bared. |

Quick recognition test: monochrome base + one acid accent, a zigzag-tooth grin or horned silhouette, at least one tape/glitch artifact, and marginal Japanese or blackletter type — if three of four hold, it is Trxsh.
