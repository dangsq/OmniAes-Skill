# Traumacore

Traumacore is an internet aesthetic of the photo and video edit, formed on Tumblr in the mid-to-late 2010s and carried onward through Pinterest and TikTok. Its entire meaning sits in one operation: take a soft, pastel, nostalgic image of childhood — a Sanrio-type sticker, a plush, a dollhouse, a frosted cake, an empty pink bedroom — and corrupt it with sorrow, violence, impurity or guilt. The contrast is deliberate and personal: it evokes a corrupted girlhood, lost innocence, and the lingering weight of traumatic experience. For its community the style is not decoration but visual journaling and art therapy, a way to articulate feelings that resist words; participation by people without lived trauma is widely discouraged, and cross-tagging disturbing edits into safe tags like #kawaii is condemned. This ethic is part of the style's DNA: the images are sincere confessions, not shock content and not irony.

## Visual Identity

### The two-layer rule
Every Traumacore image is a base layer of innocence plus a corruption layer. The base supplies pastel childhood nostalgia; the corruption supplies the wound — confessional text, blood, decay, religious guilt, a knife laid on a pie, tears drawn onto a plush, a face scribbled out. If the innocent layer dominates alone, the image slides into Sanriocore; if the dark layer dominates alone with no cute carrier, it slides into gore or grunge territories. The juxtaposition itself is the style.

### Palette
Field colors are soft and girlish, high-key and washed: baby pink and blush (around #FFD6E0, #F8C8DC, #F4C2C2), white and cream (#FFF9F5, #F1E8E0), and pale grey or beige-grey washes (#D9D4CF, #BFB6AC) for faded photographs and institutional corridors. Wound accents are blood red and oxblood (#8E0E00, #B01030, #C41E3A) plus hot magenta-pink for overlaid text (#FF5FA2, #FF007F). Black appears only as censor bars, scribbled-out faces, or a blunt statement line — never as a governing background. Contrast stays low; colors look sun-faded, overexposed or JPEG-dulled. Saturated jewel tones, neon, or brown Victorian sepia are outside the palette.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the style documents them
1. **Overlaid confessional text** — the most prominent feature: handwritten or typed first-person phrases of abuse, abandonment, guilt and fear ("I am broken," "you ruined me," "rotten child," "i should have been safe"). The voice is childlike: lowercase, simple syntax, misspellings, pleading repetition. Text may stack into walls ("im so sorry" repeated until it fills the frame), loop a word ("forever forever forever"), or split into contradictory inner voices arguing in the same image. Lines scatter across the frame, overlap subjects, or clip at edges.
2. **Children's media characters** — Sanrio-type figures (Hello Kitty, My Melody, Cinnamoroll equivalents) as flat stickers, plush toys, or crying variants with drawn-on tear streams.
3. **Dolls and toys** — porcelain or vintage dolls, plush bears, dollhouses, toy rooms; often solitary, small in frame, or taped and hung in odd places.
4. **Angels, cherubs and religious iconography** — haloed sticker angels, crosses, rosaries, saint candles on satin; used to stage purity, violation and fall from grace.
5. **Birthday cakes and sweets** — frosted cakes, candy, lattice pies; corrupted by a knife, red filling bleeding from a cut, or text confessing sickness.
6. **Childhood liminal settings** — empty bedrooms, playrooms, bathrooms, suburban houses, empty playgrounds, hospital or school corridors; low-poly 3D renders of such rooms are a common base image.
7. **Blood, dirt and decay** — stains, rot, mold; red jam or filling standing in for blood; illustrated splatter.
8. **Visceral imagery (guts)** — named among the style's key motifs, but in practice usually stylized or symbolic; photographic clinical gore belongs to the predecessor Morute.

Supporting recurring devices: knives and weapons rendered as cute or pixel-art stickers, censor bars and scribbles over faces in anime screengrabs, glitter-heart clipart frames, satin fabric backdrops, repetitive text walls.

### Materials, textures, medium
The medium is the digital edit: a base photograph with digicam softness and overexposed fade, a low-poly 3D render with flat game-interior lighting, an anime screengrab, a scanned illustration, or a straight photo of an object on grass or floral cloth. Overlays are flat PNG stickers with hard edges, clipart, and typed or handwritten text. Surface quality is deliberately degraded: low resolution, JPEG compression blocks, soft blur, haze, blown highlights. Tactile notes come from plush fur, satin sheen, porcelain gloss, frosting, grass, linoleum. The degradation reads as digital age and wear, not analogue art-grain.

### Composition and lighting
Amateur journaling logic governs layout: the base image fills the frame, stickers and text float where felt rather than on a grid, with no drop shadows, no alignment, no designed hierarchy. Subjects sit as centered still lifes (dollhouse on lawn, cake on a table, plush close-up) or as deadpan straight-on views of rooms and corridors. Human figures are absent, or present only with faces censored by bars or scribbles. Lighting is diffuse and shadowless: washed overexposed daylight, dim indoor tungsten, or the flat ambient light of renders. No chiaroscuro, no golden hour, no studio strobe.

## Mood & Values

The mood is sorrowful intimacy: a child's voice stating unbearable things plainly, tenderness directed at a wounded inner child, loneliness voiced without theatricality. Sincerity is non-negotiable — no punchlines, no wink. The values the style serves are coping, emotional expression, validation and the processing of trauma; it exists to be felt and used, not merely admired. A generator or curator should honor the community ethic: distress is expressed, never instructed or glamorized; faces stay censored; no identifiable real minors appear.

## Era & Origins

Roots in the mid-2010s, crystallizing in the late 2010s on Tumblr, later Pinterest and TikTok. The direct predecessor is Morute, the early-to-mid-2010s Tumblr aesthetic that paired delicate sweetness with explicit gore, medical horror and Southern Gothic visuals; Nicole Dollanganger's sweet-voiced songs about violence supplied thematic groundwork, and Melanie Martinez's *Cry Baby* pastel-goth child metaphors built a bridge. As Morute declined, Traumacore kept the innocent/disturbing juxtaposition but swapped its visual language for contemporary internet aesthetics — Weirdcore's amateur digital uncanny and Sanriocore's character cast. Musical adjacency runs through Dollanganger, Martinez, Kikuo, Jazmin Bean, Soap&Skin.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Build two layers always: a pastel childhood-innocence base and a corruption layer of text, blood, decay, censoring or drawn-on grief.
2. Write the wound in a childlike first-person voice; typed or handwritten, scattered, stacked, or clipped. A textless image is acceptable only when the juxtaposition alone carries the wound (a knife in a pie, a plush taped to a door).
3. Fade the base: low resolution, compression, haze, overexposure, flat diffuse light.
4. Keep palette discipline: pink-white-cream or grey-washed field, red/magenta as wound accent, black only for censoring.
5. Cast cute as the carrier of pain: stickers, plush, dolls, angels, cakes, candy — never cute for its own sake.
6. Empty the room: childhood liminal spaces with no people, or people reduced to censored silhouettes.
7. Stay sincere: confessional register, no irony, no meme punchline.

Variation axes: subject matter may be any childhood-coded object, character, room, food or trinket — dollhouses, gummy candy, satin altars of crosses, bathroom tubs, school corridors, plush piles; masculine-coded variations with cooler, greyer palettes and non-girlish toys are documented as existing. Medium may be photograph, low-poly render, anime screengrab, illustration or scanned collage. Composition may range from centered still life to deadpan interior to close-up plush to dense sticker-and-text collage to sparse text-wall minimalism. Lighting may be washed daylight, dim tungsten, flat render ambient or overexposed flash. Mood intensity may run from quiet numbness to acute crisis. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: crisp high-resolution commercial finish, studio lighting, saturated color grading, designed typography or layout grids; pure cuteness with no dark layer; empty-space photography with no innocence-corruption and no journaling text; surreal uncanny scenes or cryptic impersonal text without a trauma narrative; explicit photographic gore, surgical or medical clinical props, goth-lolita fashion coords; black-dominant goth palettes, neon, jewel tones, Victorian sepia antiquing; ironic meme captions and brand logos; analogue grunge collage and xerox texture; sexualized or identifiable minors, instructional or glorifying self-harm content, uncensored real faces.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Traumacore's tell |
|---|---|---|
| Dreamcore | Surreal dream-logic uncanny, cryptic or absent text, no personal narrative | Personal confessional text and a wounded-childhood story over familiar, not impossible, spaces |
| Liminal Space | Straight photographs of empty transitional space, no stickers, no text, anonymous eeriness | Same empty rooms, but always corrupted with cute stickers, plush, or journaling text making the space personal |
| Morute | Earlier Tumblr predecessor: explicit gore, medical horror, Southern Gothic lace and sepia antiquing | Softer digital pastel field, Sanrio-type stickers, low-poly renders, typed childlike confession; gore symbolic rather than clinical |
| Sanriocore | Celebration of Sanrio characters, pure cute, no dark layer | Same characters, but carrying drawn-on tears, censor bars, or confessional text of abuse and loss |
| Yami Kawaii | Japanese fashion subculture: pastel coords worn on a body with medical props as accessories | Not fashion at all: digital edits of rooms, objects and stickers, with first-person journaling text rather than outfit presentation |
| Weirdcore | Amateur surreal edits with impersonal, cryptic captions | Amateur technique shared, but the text is intimate, first-person, and emotionally specific |

When in doubt, ask one question: is a childlike voice confessing pain through a corrupted cute image? If yes, Traumacore; if the uncanny is impersonal, the cute is uncorrupted, or the darkness is clinical gore or fashion, it is a neighbor.
