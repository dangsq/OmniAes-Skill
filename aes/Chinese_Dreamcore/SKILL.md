# Chinese Dreamcore (中式梦核)

Chinese Dreamcore is the Chinese branch of Dreamcore that traded surrealism for memory. Where Western dreamcore distorts reality with floating objects and impossible rooms, this style photographs — or paints — the utterly ordinary architecture of turn-of-the-millennium China until it feels like a dream of childhood: school corridors, residential slabs, old malls, newsstands, fast-food counters. It is also called, more accurately but less often, Chinese Nostalgiacore. It circulates on Bilibili, Douyin and Rednote and has been codified in indie games such as *千禧梦* (Millennium Dream), *无归* and *无人*. Its core act is a digital bridge between present and past: the spaces it pictures are frequently already demolished in reality, so the image becomes a cyber spiritual shelter, a quiet room entered to escape information overload.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule: warm desaturated neutrals carry the frame, one saturated cool (sky or tinted glass) opens it up, and one faded warm accent (banner red, dusty pink) punctuates it. Everything sits slightly sun-bleached, as if scanned from an old print.

| Role | Hex range | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Tile beige / off-white | `#E9E1D2` – `#CFC4AE` | ceramic tile walls, painted concrete, floors |
| Institutional mint / oil green | `#9DBBA6` – `#4E7A63` | corridor wainscots, window paint, booth kiosks |
| Cobalt / ceramic blue | `#2E5FA3` – `#1E4E8C` | cloudless sky, blue-tinted "Bauhaus" windows |
| Glass teal | `#7FBFAF` – `#5E8F86` | mall curtain walls, balcony glazing |
| Faded banner red | `#C4453A` – `#8E332C` | vinyl signage, couplets, kiddie rides |
| Dusty pink / peach | `#E3B7A6` – `#C99A94` | curtains, wallpaper, blossom in render register |
| Wood veneer brown | `#8B5A3C` – `#5E402C` | combination wall units, doors, furniture |
| Fluorescent white | `#F4F2EA` | blown corridor ends, ceiling tubes |

The photographic register desaturates everything except sky and glass; the digital-paint register tolerates fuller saturation (pink blossoms, gilded domes, orange spheres) but always keeps beige concrete and tile as the ground note.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual documentation)

1. **Liminal passages** — school and hospital corridors with two-tone green wainscots, dim stairwells, glass double doors, elevator lobbies; the empty threshold is the style's heartbeat.
2. **Millennium residential slabs** — Khrushchyovka-type workers' housing: white-tiled facades, zigzag or stacked balconies, walls studded with air-conditioner units and steel security grilles.
3. **"Bauhaus-style blue windows and white walls"** — the style's own shorthand for blue-tinted glazing set in white tile.
4. **Old school buildings** — corridors, classrooms, computer rooms full of bulky CRT monitors, playgrounds.
5. **Old shopping malls** — curved green-glass atria, corridors of shuttered stalls, hanging shop signs.
6. **Fast-food chain counters** — early-2000s interiors: orange counters, green backlit menu boards.
7. **Magazine booths / newsstands** — maximalist clutter of glossy covers, the style's sanctioned density.
8. **Childhood street furniture** — coin-operated kiddie rides, fiberglass playground sculptures, uncanny-cute mascots on lawn posts.
9. **Domestic interiors** — wood-veneer combination wall units housing CRT televisions, floral wallpaper, lace or blue curtains, platform beds, height-chart stickers on doorframes.
10. **Chinese-language signage as texture** — red vinyl banners, shop names, slogans, paper window cuts; text reads as color, not message.

### Materials and textures

White ceramic and mosaic tile, terrazzo and beige floor tile, painted concrete, green oil paint, aluminum and steel window frames, chrome railings, security grilles, vinyl banner cloth, wood-veneer laminate, beige CRT plastic, floral wallpaper, glossy magazine paper, weathered fiberglass. Surfaces are worn but functioning: scuffed, sun-faded, faintly yellowed. Aged, never ruined.

### Light and atmosphere

Two documented registers, each internally consistent:
- **Low-fi photograph**: early-2000s digicam or film-scan softness, mild sensor noise and blur, overexposed windows and blown corridor ends, flat overcast or harsh midday sun, pale hazy sky.
- **Smooth digital paint / game scene**: airbrushed gradients, saturated cobalt sky, clean beige concrete, identical light logic (hard sun outside, flat fluorescent inside).

Interiors run on bare fluorescent tubes; a bright blowout at the far end of a dim corridor is a near-signature. Warm dusk appears; true night exteriors are essentially undocumented, so treat night as outside the core range.

### Composition and framing

Deadpan and frontal. One-point perspective down empty corridors; centered doorways acting as portals; facades stacked until they fill the frame; views half-blocked by a security grille or railing in the foreground; high-angle looks down onto courtyards and paths; snapshot crops with a slight tilt permitted. People are absent, or tiny and distant — two customers at a counter, small figures on a walkway. Architecture is the protagonist; humans survive only as traces: a mop, a posted notice, a laundry pole, stacked magazines.

## Mood & Values

The emotional paradox is the point: eerie yet warm, unsettling yet soothing, fear blended with comfort. Empty rooms feel watched by memory rather than by threat. The style mourns and celebrates at once a childhood that coincided with China's fastest urban expansion, easing present-day anxiety by offering a temporary escape. Its values are collective memory, tenderness for the unfashionable recent past, melancholy without grief, and uncanniness without horror.

## Era & Origins

Coined and codified in the 2020s in China, on Bilibili, Douyin and Rednote, looking backward at urban China of roughly 1998–2010. It is a deliberate correction to Western Dreamcore — less surrealism, more nostalgia — and it extends into indie games (*千禧梦*, *无归*, *无人*). It reacts to two pressures at once: the physical demolition of millennium-era buildings and the psychological load of contemporary information saturation.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Space first, people never central; the building is the subject.
2. Plausibility filter: every element must be buildable in a Chinese city of c. 2000–2010. No impossible geometry.
3. Traces without actors: life recently paused, not life absent — buckets, notices, AC units, AC drip stains, stacked goods.
4. Palette discipline: warm neutral ground + one saturated cool + one faded warm accent.
5. Pick one fidelity register (hazy digicam OR smooth painterly render) and hold it across the whole image.
6. Eeriness emerges from silence, emptiness and slight overexposure — never from menace.
7. Chinese signage is texture: period-plausible shop names and slogans, read as color blocks.

**Variation axes (what may change)**
- *Subject*: any millennium Chinese space — residential courtyards, stairwells, rooftops with water tanks, hospital corridors, bus terminals, basement arcades, karaoke rooms, schoolyards with ping-pong tables, elevator lobbies, factory gates — alongside every documented motif above.
- *Composition*: centered one-point, deadpan frontal, grille-framed, high-angle, snapshot tilt, facade-filling crop, interior corner.
- *Lighting*: flat overcast, midday glare, fluorescent interior, blown window light, warm dusk.
- *Color handling*: desaturated photo haze ↔ saturated render clarity; blossom-pink or gilded accents allowed in the render register.
- *Mood dial*: tender ↔ uncanny, always inside the warm-eerie band.
- *Medium*: digicam photo, film scan, digital painting, game-scene render.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette, emptiness, period plausibility, light logic — not any reference image. No reference composition is canonical.

**Avoid**
- Surreal manipulation: floating objects, melting architecture, impossible staircases, distorted or faceless figures.
- Horror vocabulary: blood, ghosts, paper effigies, funerary objects, talismans, ritual altars.
- Present-day China: 2020s glass supertalls, smartphones, delivery-app scooters, shared bikes, LED megascreens, minimalist café interiors.
- Non-Chinese stand-ins: American diners, European brick terraces, Japanese tatami rooms; Soviet blocs only as Chinese workers' housing with Chinese signage.
- Vaporwave grids, cyberpunk neon, chrome Y2K gloss as the dominant note.
- Ruin porn: rubble, collapsed ceilings, heavy grime, vegetation overgrowth; spaces are aged but intact.
- Crowds, posed families, queues, active commerce, influencer styling.
- Crisp modern HDR, heavy sharpening, neutral studio white balance, studio lighting.
- Overlaid caption text or meme typography (in-scene signage is fine).
- Darkness-dominated frames: keep daylight or fluorescent brightness even at the eeriest.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it |
|---|---|
| **Chinese Folk Horror** | Rural ritual and funerary craft — paper effigies, red candles, altars, explicit dread. Chinese Dreamcore is urban-suburban, millennium-dated, warm; its eeriness is absence, never ritual. |
| **Millennial Feng** | Same era, opposite attitude: celebratory Y2K optimism, glossy graphic design, chrome and translucency, populated consumer energy. Chinese Dreamcore subtracts the people and adds haze, silence, melancholy. |
| **Dreamcore (Western)** | Surreal edits: floating objects, replaced skies, distorted faces, caption text. Chinese Dreamcore keeps spaces physically plausible; its maximum surrealism is the mild uncanny of an empty playground sculpture. |
| **Liminal Space** | Placeless, era-less transitional emptiness (any country's airport or mall), affectively cold and anonymous. Chinese Dreamcore is specifically Chinese, specifically c. 2000s, and warm with domestic detail; its emptiness is nostalgic, not neutral. |
| **Nostalgiacore** | Object-driven childhood keepsakes — toys, snacks, bedrooms — of any culture, cozy and cluttered. Chinese Dreamcore is space- and architecture-driven, urban Chinese, with a dreamlike haze over the whole frame. |

Quick recognition check: white tile + blue-tinted glass + faded red Chinese banner + no people + hazy or fluorescent light + a space that feels both familiar and gone = Chinese Dreamcore.
