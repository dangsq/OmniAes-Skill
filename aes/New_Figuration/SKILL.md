# New Figuration (Nueva Figuración, Neofiguration)

New Figuration is the post-Second-World-War return to the human figure in painting, launched in the 1950s and consolidated in the 1960s across Argentina, Mexico, Venezuela, Colombia and Spain. It is a return without nostalgia: the figure comes back damaged. Bodies are distorted, exaggerated, inflated, smeared or monstrified, and the deformation is itself the argument — a cynical, cruel or mocking comment on power, war, marginality and the bourgeoisie. The movement absorbed what it reacted against: the gestural violence of informalism and abstract expressionism survives inside it as scribbled impasto, stain and formless passage, even while a recognizable human silhouette reasserts itself. A New Figuration canvas typically runs two pictorial logics at once — cut-paper flat color planes colliding with greasy smudged flesh, or watery ink wash interrupted by frantic dry scribble — and it always leaves the trace of process, as if, in Francis Bacon's phrase, a human being had passed through the paint like a snail.

## Visual Identity

### Color: two registers, one menace
The palette is documented as "varied, often bold and expressive," and the corpus settles into two working registers; a given work commits to one or deliberately collides them.

- **Saturated plane register** (the Argentine Otra Figuración and Venezuelan line): flat, matte, unmodulated fields of cobalt and ultramarine blue (#2E5FBF–#4A7BD0), leaf and grass green (#3F7A34–#5C9A46), signal red and vermilion (#D93A26–#E4553A), ochre and mustard (#C98A2B–#D9A441), bone cream (#EDE4D2–#F4EEE2), and violet through purple (#5E3E8E–#7E57B0). Rule: brightness is never cheer — anchor the composition with at least one wedge or band of deep aubergine or near-black so the color reads as pressure, not decoration.
- **Mourning register** (the postwar, Baconian line): near-black (#141010–#241C18), umber and brown (#4A3526–#6B4A32), aubergine and deep purple (#2A1B2E–#3E2A44), bruised grey (#6E6A66–#8C8680), punctured by raw flesh pink (#D9A08C–#E8BBA6) and occasional acid accents — a magenta contour line (#E0508C), an acid-yellow band (#E8C619), a lavender halo (#B9A6D9).
- **Paper register** (the Mexican ink line): off-white and beige paper (#E9E2D6–#F2EDE3) carrying black and sepia ink, grey washes, and sparse stained color.

Harmony is never the goal; adjacent hues should argue. Purple, black and brown are the movement's documented mourning tones, and when bright color appears it appears flat and loud, like painted placards.

### Signature motifs, ranked by documentary prominence
1. **The distorted human figure** — the movement's core. Exaggerated, monstrous, split or eroded bodies; faces smeared into masks, bruised blurs, or skull-like planes. The figure stays legible as human; the damage is the message.
2. **Abstraction inside figuration** — formless informalist passages, dramatic brushstrokes, color fields that refuse to describe anything, coexisting with the body in one frame.
3. **Social critique and satire** — mocking or cruel representations of officials, clergy, bourgeoisie, family units and crowds; the portrait as indictment.
4. **Suffering and war** — meat, carcasses, wounds, caged or crucified bodies; the dark tonality of postwar aftermath.
5. **Collage logic** — patchwork and quilted grounds, printed or painted lattice and patterned wallpaper, taped seams, fragments of different pictorial styles spliced into one canvas.
6. **Regional variants** (documented but narrower): Botero's inflated voluminous bodies; Cuevas's ink-and-watercolor figures of alienation and marginalization; Borges's vibrant bourgeois critiques; Arango's assertive satirical brushstroke portraits.
7. **The isolated figure in a box** — Baconian cage-like spatial frames, a single body sealed in dark space with only a trace of presence.

### Materials and surface
Oil on canvas dominates, but the surface is never unified: matte hard-edged planes sit beside greasy smudged, wiped or airbrush-blurred flesh (the smeared face is a fingerprint of the style); watery washes and stains bloom across paper or raw canvas; dry frantic scribble — charcoal-like, heavy-ink — knots into impasto clots; thin contour lines, often pink or magenta, trace shapes the paint itself refuses to hold. Collage elements and patterned grounds appear as quoted material. Shape edges are either cut sharp like paper silhouettes or bled and eroded.

### Recurring subjects
Seated or standing isolated figures; self-portraits and portraits with masked, split or skull-like faces; family or group portraits with eerie uniformity of stance; couples and embracing fragments; enthroned, caged or crucified bodies; hanging meat and carcasses beside living sitters; satirical officials and religious figures; children rendered with adult menace; chaotic crowd and landscape friezes where figures tangle with vegetation and debris.

### Composition and space
Space is shallow and contested. Recurring structures (any one suffices): the figure as central emblem on colliding flat planes; a patchwork or quilted ground behind a single sitter; a horizontal frieze crowded with tangled figures; a vast pale empty field holding one smeared mass or stain, the figure kept marginal by informalist restraint; an interior box or cage framing the body. Anatomy is assembled from fragments — a head from one pictorial logic, a torso from another — so the body reads as a site of collision rather than an organism, and scale jumps abruptly between fragments.

### Light
Light is anti-naturalistic. In the plane register there is no light source at all: color fields carry even, ambient, shadowless illumination. In the mourning register, theatrical single-source gloom isolates the body against blackness, flesh pink glowing like a wound. Smudged faces appear lit from within by bruise. Cast shadows, atmospheric perspective and impressionist light effects are absent.

## Mood & Values
Cynical, cruel, mocking; underneath, grief and existential angst. Alienation and marginalization are felt subjects, and chaos is accepted as a condition rather than resolved. The movement's stated values — social critique, freedom of expression, individuality — appear visually as refusal: refusal of finish, of good taste, of intact anatomy, of neutral tone. Even the bright works are ironic; even the dark ones are loud. The overall effect should be a human trace left under pressure.

## Era & Origins
Emerged in the 1950s, peaked in the 1960s; the term was first used by the French critic Michel Ragon. Strongest in Argentina (Otra Figuración: Ernesto Deira, Rómulo Macció, Luis Felipe Noé, Jorge de la Vega), Mexico (José Luis Cuevas, reacting against the nationalist doctrine of Mexican Muralism), Venezuela (Jacobo Borges, after the fall of the military regime in 1958), Colombia (Fernando Botero, Débora Arango, answering La Violencia), and Spain. Francis Bacon, unaffiliated, is the movement's gravitational north for the distorted isolated figure. New Figuration reacts simultaneously to the dominance of abstract expressionism and informalism (reclaiming the figure while keeping their gestural violence) and to exhausted traditional figuration (refusing academic intactness), all under the shadow of WWII and continuous political turbulence in Europe and the Americas.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Figure first, figure damaged. Every image needs a legible human presence and a legible injury to that presence: smear, split, inflate, erode, mask or fragment the body, but never let it dissolve into pure abstraction.
- Run two pictorial logics at once. Pair flat hard-edged color planes with gestural scribble, or wash with impasto, or patterned lattice with stain. A single uniform handling drifts into another style.
- Color as assertion, not description. Commit to the saturated plane register, the mourning register, or the paper register; let adjacent hues argue; anchor bright palettes with a dark wedge and dark palettes with flesh pink or an acid accent.
- Leave the trace of process. Wiped smears, scribble, stains, raw edges, collage seams — the surface should record events, like Bacon's snail trail.
- Embed the critique. The image must read as commentary — mockery, menace, or mourning — never as neutral depiction.

**Variation axes**
- Subject: any human situation is in range — official portrait, family group, lovers, children, crowd, butcher's shop, protest, interior, self-portrait, allegorical figure.
- Composition: central emblem, patchwork ground, horizontal frieze, marginal mass on empty field, caged box interior.
- Lighting: shadowless even ambient; theatrical single-source gloom; or no modeled light at all.
- Color handling: full saturated planes; near-monochrome mourning with flesh accents; ink-and-wash paper with sparse color.
- Mood: satirical grotesque, existential dread, chaotic vitality, cold alienation.
- Medium look: matte oil plane, greasy smudge, watery wash, dry scribble, impasto clot, collage seam — in any combination.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A still life of smeared meat beside an empty chair, a crowd frieze in acid color, or a single masked child on a beige wash can all be perfect New Figuration without resembling any documented canvas.

**Avoid**
- Photorealistic or academic polish; seamless digital smoothness; clean vector flatness with no painterly trace.
- Intact idealized anatomy, symmetrical heroic bodies.
- Pure abstraction with no human trace.
- Decorative, harmonious or comforting color; pastel prettiness; cute or kawaii distortion.
- Impressionistic or naturalistic light, cast shadows, atmospheric perspective.
- Socialist-realist or muralist optimism and heroic narrative — the movement explicitly reacted against these.
- Surrealist dreamscapes with illusionistic finish; oneiric symbol tableaux.
- Comic-strip anecdote with speech-bubble legibility (that lane belongs to Figuration Narrative).
- Neutral documentary tone; illustration that describes instead of indicting.

## Discrimination Cues
- **Vs. Figuration Narrative:** FN tells anecdotes — legible everyday scenes, illustrative detail, narrative clarity, a cooler reportorial eye. New Figuration deforms the body itself and foregrounds the paint event; its critique rides on distortion and surface violence, not on a readable story.
- **Vs. Cubism:** Cubism fractures the figure into analytic geometric facets, holds multiple viewpoints in intellectual equilibrium, and favors muted ochre-grey tonality. New Figuration's distortion is organic and fleshy — smeared, inflated, wounded — with saturated or mournful color and an emotionally raw, gestural surface.
- **Vs. Expressionism:** Expressionism distorts for inner feeling with jagged continuous brushwork and a unified handling. New Figuration adds postwar syntax: colliding flat planes, collage seams, patterned grounds, smudged mask-faces, and an ironic satirical program aimed at society rather than pure subjective angst.
- **Vs. Surrealism:** Surrealism paints dreams precisely — illusionistic finish, uncanny but carefully rendered symbolic tableaux. New Figuration is materialist and immediate: paint as trace, body as social fact, distortion expressive or satirical rather than oneiric; no dreamscapes, no polished uncanny.

**Evidence note:** the surviving visual record spans flat matte plane-painting, ink-and-wash paper, and heavy impasto, and lighting is consistently anti-naturalistic across all of them; the Spanish branch of the movement is thinly represented in the available images, so its specific visual fingerprints are under-documented here and should not be over-specified.
