# Claymorphism

## Overview

Claymorphism is a user-interface and illustration design trend of the 2020s in which every element is modeled as if sculpted from clay, Play-Doh, or an inflated soft toy: smooth blob-like geometry, chunky thickness, no sharp edges, and a surface that catches light like matte plasticine. The term was coined in 2021 by designer Michał Malewicz as a portmanteau of "clay" and "skeuomorphism." The style exists to make digital surfaces feel welcoming and tactile to general audiences — a deliberate antidote to the "cold" neutrality of 2010s Flat Design — and is read as the three-dimensional evolution of flat illustration trends such as Corporate Memphis, carrying their friendly figurative language into modeled volume with greater detail and tactility. Elements look "fluffy" or "inflated," like stuffed toys or balloons, and each card, button, icon or character sits on top of the background as a discrete modeled 3D surface rather than being printed into it. It gained mainstream traction as a way to humanize tech branding: mascot rebrands, 3D emoji sets, app onboarding screens, and advertising illustration are its native habitats.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is **solid, vibrant pastel per element, set against a separate paler plane** — a flat pastel field or a smooth soft gradient (pink melting into lavender into periwinkle is the characteristic blend). The style explicitly rejects monochrome-on-monochrome: color is a functional cue that separates interactive elements, so buttons, cards and tiles each carry their own confident hue.

- **Pastel core range:** pastel blue and periwinkle (#8AB4F8–#A5B4FC, #7B8FF7), lavender and lilac (#CDB4F6–#E6E0F8), candy pink and magenta (#FF8FC8–#F472B6), peach (#FFC999), butter yellow (#FFD666), mint (#A8E6CF), clean white (#FFFFFF).
- **Background range:** pale gray-blue (#E8ECF3), soft lilac-pink washes, vertical or diagonal pastel gradients (#FF9ED2 → #C9B8F0, #8F9BFF → #6E7BF2), sometimes populated by oversized cloud-like forms in the same clay material.
- **Saturated accent range:** one or two candy-bright notes for focal or interactive cues — vivid azure (#2E6BFF), mascot green (#3DDC84), signal orange-red (#FF4500), glossy orb gradients of purple-orange-green. A saturated field with a white inflated hero (a white puffy butterfly on pure blue, say) is a legitimate inversion of the pastel-field rule.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Inflated 3D geometry.** Objects look pumped with air or molded by hand: chunky, softly sculpted, edgeless. This is the defining trait; everything else serves it.
2. **The two-shadow system.** A deep, soft *outer* shadow beneath or behind each element proves it floats above the background; a subtle *inner* shadow along the lower inner rim and in crevices proves it has thickness and puffiness. Both together are the style's fingerprint.
3. **Discrete modeled surfaces.** Cards, buttons, tiles and badges are separate solid objects hovering over the backdrop, never extrusions of it.
4. **Rounded corners everywhere.** Large corner radii on rectangles, pill shapes, capsules, spheres, rounded cubes; sharp corners simply do not occur.
5. **Pastel color cues and soft gradients** differentiating elements (see palette).
6. **Diverse people and everyday scenarios**, chiefly in advertising illustration: friendly non-photorealistic 3D avatars with chunky proportions, matte skin shells, simple hair forms — varied skin tones, hairstyles, glasses, headscarves, ages — caught in ordinary moments (holding a plant, lounging in an armchair with a phone, waiting, eating, pointing).
7. **Clay mascots, 3D emoji and icon sets.** Brand mascots re-sculpted in clay, and whole glyph families (emoji, office app icons) rendered as puffy collectible objects.
8. **Minimal typography.** Clean, bold, geometric or rounded sans-serif; small labels and pill badges; type stays restrained and professional to balance the whimsy of the visuals.

### Materials and textures

Matte-to-satin clay, Play-Doh, soft vinyl inflatable, stuffed-toy plushness. Surfaces are smooth, poreless and uniform in color; a faint broad specular sheen on curved tops is acceptable (the glossier emoji-and-orb variant of the style), but the dominant finish is soft satin, not mirror. Ambient occlusion gathers gently in creases and under overlaps. There is no grain, no brushwork, no photographic micro-texture, no glass.

### Recurring subjects

UI mockups (weather dashboards with puffy clouds and suns, onboarding screens, food-ordering cards, recap screens); logo and mascot sculptures; emoji and app-icon sets; advertising illustrations with characters in everyday scenes; and a floating prop vocabulary — spheres, capsules and pills, rounded cubes, megaphones, pointing hands, coins, clouds, suns, raindrops — scattered as orbital garnish around a hero element or screen.

### Composition and lighting

Composition keeps depth shallow and separation legible: a single centered mascot on a plain field; grids or stacks of rounded cards and phone screens; a hero screen orbited by scattered props; or a fuller scene with characters and props over a gradient sky. Margins are generous; backgrounds stay quiet so the floating solids read clearly. Views are front-facing or slight three-quarter. Lighting is a soft, diffuse studio key, usually from top-front or top-left, with broad gentle falloff: low overall contrast, no harsh rim light, no hard-edged cast shadows, no night or dramatic lighting. The shadow under each object is soft, deep and slightly blurred — proof of float, not of sunlight.

## Mood & Values

Claymorphism expresses **friendliness, accessibility, playfulness and tactility**. It wants technology to feel approachable enough to touch — modeled on toys and stop-motion charm rather than machinery — and it treats clear color contrast and legible minimal type as part of that kindness. The emotional register is warm, whimsical and optimistic, yet deliberately kept usable and professional: the candy volume is balanced by orderly layouts and restrained typography.

## Era & Origins

Emergent 2021–present, named and categorized by Michał Malewicz in 2021. It reacts against two things: the emotional coldness of 2010s Flat Design, and the accessibility failure of Neumorphism, whose low-contrast monochrome extrusions made interfaces hard to read. Claymorphism keeps Neumorphism's tactile 3D appeal but adds strong color cues and distinctly floating layers to restore usability. Artistically it descends from stop-motion animation (the Wallace and Gromit tradition of handled, thumbprint-soft material) and from Amrit Pal Singh's "Toy Faces" series, which popularized the friendly non-photorealistic 3D avatar. Mainstream confirmation came quickly: the 2023 Android rebrand re-sculpted the Bugdroid mascot in clay, Reddit gave Snoo a 3D clay treatment across its logo and recap campaigns, and Windows 11's "Fluency" emoji set replaced flat icons with puffy clay-textured versions; copilot advertising, office icon sets, a puffy white butterfly app icon and glossy orb broadcast logos all sit in the same family.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Model first, decorate never: every element — card, button, character, cloud, letterform glyph — is a solid inflated volume with a chunky rounded silhouette.
2. Apply the two-shadow rule without exception: soft deep outer shadow for float, subtle inner shadow for puffiness and thickness.
3. Keep elements opaque and solid; the background is a separate plane (flat pastel or smooth gradient), never a material the elements are carved from.
4. Use color as a cue: one confident pastel per element, one or two candy-saturated accents for focal or interactive notes.
5. Light with a single soft diffuse studio key from top-front or top-left; low contrast; matte-satin skin with at most a faint sheen.
6. Round every corner; favor pills, capsules, spheres, rounded cubes; ban sharp edges and thin strokes.
7. Set type clean, bold and minimal — geometric or rounded sans, small labels, pill badges — so the whimsy stays professional.
8. When people appear, make them stylized clay avatars: chunky proportions, matte shell skin, simple hair and clothing forms, diverse identities, ordinary activities.

**Variation axes.** *Subject:* mascot sculpture, avatar bust group, emoji or icon set, single UI card, full multi-screen mockup, landing page, advertising scene, abstract prop arrangement. *Composition:* centered solo object, card grid, stacked screens, orbital prop scatter, full-bleed character scene. *Palette:* pink-lilac candy; periwinkle-violet sky; mint-peach; pale neutral gray-blue carrying one saturated hero (green, azure, orange); or a saturated field carrying a white hero. *Lighting:* fully matte diffuse through to a slight satin gloss on curved tops. *Mood:* exuberant marketing playfulness through calm dashboard professionalism. *Figure diversity:* any skin tones, ages, hair, glasses, headscarves, body types.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A generated result may look nothing like the weather dashboards, mascots or app screens that document the style and still be perfectly claymorphic, so long as the inflated geometry, two-shadow physics, solid pastel color cues and soft studio light hold.

**Avoid.**
- Flat 2D vector shapes with no modeled volume (that is Corporate Memphis).
- Frosted transparency, background blur, semi-transparent white borders (Glassmorphism).
- Monochrome elements extruded from a same-hue background with paired light/dark edge shadows (Neumorphism).
- Realistic material imitation: wood grain, brushed metal, leather, stitched fabric, paper (Skeuomorphism).
- Sharp corners, hard edges, thin outlines, stroke-based illustration.
- Harsh direct light, hard-edged cast shadows, high contrast, noir or dark moody palettes, film grain or noise.
- Photorealistic humans, photoreal materials, or photographic depth of field beyond a shallow soft blur.
- Dense serif, script or decorative display typography.
- Low-poly, faceted, wireframe or hard-surface geometry.
- Elements that reveal the background through themselves.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Claymorphism |
|---|---|
| **Glassmorphism** | Glass elements are semi-transparent with background blur and thin light borders; clay elements are fully opaque solids with inner-shadow puffiness. If you can see the wallpaper through a card, it is not clay. |
| **Corporate Memphis** | Memphis is flat 2D vector: exaggerated limbs, offset proportions, no volume, no shadows of thickness. Clay keeps the friendly figures but gives them modeled depth, chunky inflation and float shadows. |
| **Neumorphism** | Neumorphic elements are extruded *from* the background in the same monochrome hue, using one light and one dark edge shadow. Clay elements float *on* the background in distinct vibrant pastels, with deep outer drop shadows plus inner shadows. |
| **Skeuomorphism** | Skeuomorphism imitates real-world materials (leather, metal, wood, paper) with photographic fidelity. Clay imitates a toy material — plasticine, inflatable vinyl — with stylized, poreless, candy-colored surfaces. |
| **Blob World** | Blob-style abstraction uses flat or gradient-filled organic shapes with no thickness, no consistent light source and no shadow physics. Clay blobs are solids: they have volume, a single soft key light, occlusion in crevices and a drop shadow proving separation from the backdrop. |

Quick field test: squint at the image. If the shapes still read as puffy discrete objects hovering over a quieter pastel plane, each carrying its own color and its own soft shadow halo, with inner shading that says "squeeze me" — you are looking at Claymorphism.
