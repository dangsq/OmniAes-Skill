# Neubrutalism

Neubrutalism is a design aesthetic built on a single conviction: that breaking the traditional conventions of design is itself the point. Where most commercial design seeks harmony, Neubrutalism seeks productive collision — complementary colors slammed together, type stretched past comfort, cards tilted off-grid, shadows that behave like solid blocks of ink. It is fundamentally a digital-native style, born in UI/UX and web design, and it carries the memory of two eras at once: the chunky chrome of 1985–2000 computing (window title bars, pixel grids, fat cursors) and the confetti geometry of Memphis Design. Despite the name, it shares almost nothing visually with architectural Brutalism's raw concrete monochrome; what it inherits is the *value* — functionality and honesty expressed in the most basic shapes and forms, regardless of taste.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette (rule, not recipe).** Flat, saturated, unmixed color fields chosen to clash productively. The documented key colour is simply *rainbow*, and the working rule is: pick three to five high-chroma hues plus one neutral ground (paper cream or pure white), and include at least one deliberately off-putting pair (blue against orange, hot pink against mint, yellow against purple). Representative range: paper cream `#FFFDF5`–`#FDF6EC`, ink black `#000000`–`#141414`, electric blue `#2400FF`–`#3A5BFF`, cyan `#00C8FF`, signal yellow `#FFD400`–`#FFC700`, orange `#FF6B35`–`#FF8A00`, red `#FF3B30`–`#E4572E`, hot pink `#FF6EC7`–`#FF2E88`, magenta `#E6007E`, violet `#7C4DFF`–`#6C4AB6`, mint `#98E9C5`–`#A0E8AF`, kelly green `#00A651`–`#35C759`. Rainbow stripe bands (parallel hard-edged stripes of the full spectrum) are a licensed accent. Softer pastel fields (peach, baby pink, pale mint) are an acceptable background register *only* when bold outlined shapes and at least one clashing hue keep the tension alive. Gradients are forbidden; color changes happen at hard edges.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.**
1. *Dark outlines* — a uniform heavy stroke (black or near-black) around cards, buttons, shapes, characters, type stickers.
2. *Hard 45° angled shadows* — solid, unfeathered offset blocks behind every raised element, usually black but sometimes a contrasting palette hue; the shadow reads as a second shape, not as lighting.
3. *Contrasting, off-putting color palettes* — as above.
4. *Unusual and grotesque typography* — heavy grotesque sans serifs, uppercase lettering, stretched/extended or compressed widths, warped arcs, words set inside outlined pill stickers; incongruity is the criterion, so an occasional misfit serif or pixel/monospace face is on-style as deliberate dissonance.
5. *Geometric blocks and pseudo-3D objects* — flat-facet isometric extrusions, stacked slabs, pixel-grid boards; depth implied by geometry alone.
6. *Oversized UI/UX* — giant buttons, giant cursor arrows, browser windows, tabs, scrollbars, folder and pin icons used as composition elements; window-within-window framing.
7. *Random or inconsistent layout* — tilted stickers, overlapping cards, off-grid placement, collage chaos; or a strict color-block grid punctured by one unruly element.
8. *Graph paper grids, zig-zag lines, starbursts* — the Memphis/Webcore nostalgia kit.
9. *1930s-style cartoon characters* — rubber-hose mascots with gloved hands and pie eyes, often anthropomorphized objects (a walking computer, a waving appliance).
10. *Realistic outline illustrations, empty and/or unshaded* — proportionate figures and objects drawn in line only, or with a single flat fill: crowds, anatomical hearts, bicycles, hands.
11. *Absence of gradients or other visual effects* — the negative motif that polices all the others.

**Materials, textures, surfaces.** The style is vector-flat: matte digital fills, cream "paper" grounds, graph-paper ruling, pixel grids inherited from early GUI screens. There is no grain, no gloss, no ambient occlusion, no brushwork. Photography, when it appears at all, is demoted to collage insert — cropped into an outlined frame, duotoned or halftoned, sitting flush in the flat field like a pasted clipping.

**Recurring subjects.** Website hero screens and long scroll pages, app and phone mockups, brand identity boards, posters and event key visuals, infographics, icon sets, sticker sheets, product packaging, editorial spreads, merch. The subject matter is overwhelmingly *design about design*: interfaces, announcements, tools, communities, playful commerce.

**Composition and lighting.** Composition modes range from a single centered outlined card on a saturated field, to scattered confetti/sticker fields, to rigid color-block grids, to dense window-collage stacks. Lighting does not exist in the photographic sense: illumination is uniform and flat, and the only "light source" is the implied diagonal of the hard shadow, which may point down-right, down-left or up at 45° but never feathers, blurs or fades.

## Mood & Values

Playful defiance. Neubrutalism feels loud, honest and a little rude on purpose — the visual equivalent of speaking plainly in a room full of euphemisms. Its stated key value is breaking the conventions of design, and its off-putting combinations function as an authenticity signal: nothing here is airbrushed, so nothing here is hiding. Beneath the noise sits a genuinely functionalist ethic (the style shares Flat Design's goal of accessibility and usability) and a warm nostalgia for the handmade early web — zines, GeoPages-era personal sites, shareware boxes. The mood ranges from carnival optimism to deadpan dryness, but never to slickness.

## Era & Origins

Coined by Lin Duncan and crystallized in the late 2010s from early/mid-2010s experiments, Neubrutalism went mainstream in UI/UX, advertising and web design around 2022–2023. Its name descends from architectural Brutalism (value lineage, not visual lineage), and its motif kit was prefigured almost entirely by Microsoft Windows 1.0 in 1985: hard 45° shadows in Reversi and Calculator, pseudo-3D boards, dark outlines, clashing menu and scrollbar colors, uppercase MS-DOS Executive type, freely rearrangeable windows. Its emotional fuel is 1990s/early-2000s nostalgia via Memphis Design and Webcore, with secondary pulls from Y2K Futurism, Bauhaus, De Stijl and the International Typographic Style.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Every raised element gets two things: a uniform dark outline and a solid offset shadow at 45°. No feathering, ever.
2. Fill every surface with flat color or flat paper; color changes only at hard edges.
3. Build from basic geometry: rectangles, slabs, pills, starbursts, zig-zags, isometric extrusions with flat facets.
4. Set type loud: heavy grotesque, uppercase, stretched or warped, often inside outlined stickers; let headlines occupy a third of the frame.
5. Frame content in the vocabulary of old computing: browser chrome, title bars, tabs, cursors, folder icons, graph paper, pixel grids.
6. Keep layout deliberately imperfect — tilt, overlap, misalign — or make the grid so rigid that one chaotic element reads as rebellion.
7. People and objects drawn as figures appear either as 1930s rubber-hose cartoons or as realistic unshaded line illustrations; never as softly modeled vector humans.
8. Treat any photograph as a flat pasted insert, never as the lit hero of the image.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be anything within the style's domain — a poster for a bakery, a phone app, a concert key visual, an infographic about rivers, a sticker sheet, a packaging wrapper, a scrollable site. Composition may run from one centered card on a quiet cream field to full confetti scatter to strict color-block mosaic. Color handling may be paper-dominant with rainbow accents, a single saturated field with contrasting cards, a pastel field with bold blobs, or a dark field carrying neon outlined panels. Shadow direction and outline color may vary; mood may run carnival or deadpan. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Smooth gradients of any kind; soft, blurred or glowing shadows; glassmorphism, frosted blur, transparency stacks; realistic 3D rendering with soft global illumination or physical materials; photographic lighting as the dominant treatment; muted single-hue corporate palettes with gray support; delicate serif-led editorial minimalism without clash; hand-painted brush texture, grain or paper fiber as the main surface; ornamental curvilinear styles (Art Nouveau, groovy 70s florals) without outlines and hard shadows; skeuomorphic depth; vaporwave chrome and neon-on-black glow; perfectly balanced classical symmetry with no disruptive element.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Flat Design** shares flatness, no gradients and a functionality ethic, but *conforms*: harmonious limited palette, no outlines, no offset shadows, orderly grid, restrained type. Neubrutalism keeps the flatness and adds outlines, hard shadows, clashing color and unruly layout. Remove the convention-breaking and you have Flat Design.
- **Acid Design** runs on psychedelia: chrome metal, liquid distortion, glitch, neon glow on black. Neubrutalism forbids gradients, glow and distortion-of-material; its chaos is geometric and matte, not chemical.
- **Avant Basic** is soft pastel groovy: cream, lilac, wavy checkerboards, florals, curvy type, no black outlines and no hard shadows. Neubrutalism may borrow pastels but always re-arms them with ink outlines, solid shadows and grotesque uppercase.
- **Corporate Memphis** uses flat vector humans with impossible limbs, smooth curves, harmonious brights, and no outlines or shadows. Neubrutalism's figures are rubber-hose cartoons or unshaded realistic line art, and everything carries a stroke and an offset shadow.
- **Gen X Soft Club** is cozy, muted, earth-toned, soft-focus and serif-driven with gentle grain. Neubrutalism is hard-edged, saturated, digital-loud and anti-nostalgic-soft; its nostalgia is for pixel chrome, not for beige photographs.
- Quick test: outline + hard 45° shadow + clashing flat color + loud grotesque type. Present together, Neubrutalism; drop the outlines and shadows and it slides toward Flat Design or Corporate Memphis; drop the flat matte color for glow and gradient and it slides toward Acid Design.
