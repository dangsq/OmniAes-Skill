# Boujee

Boujee takes its name from "bourgeoisie" but inverts the old meaning: it is not the middle class, it is aspirational or achieved upper-class status performed out loud. Wealth here is not inherited quietly, it is showcased — through social media, through partying, through self-care, through objects that carry price tags on their surface. The alias "Night Luxe" names its native hour: the aesthetic lives after dark, in flash-lit rooms, cars, balconies and club corners, though it also licenses daylight strides and serene spa mornings. Its key values are wealth, pageantry, beauty and status; its emotional register is confidence, personal enjoyment and a degree of unapologetic entitlement. Where Old Money whispers, Boujee posts.

## Visual Identity

### Color

The rule: metallic neutrals carry the image, black supplies the night contrast, jewel tones appear as saturated accents, and sparkle behaves as a texture layer laid over any hue rather than a hue itself.

The source text names jewel tones among the key colours, but sampled imagery skews heavily toward metallic neutrals, black and blush; treat jewel tones as a licensed accent, not an obligation. Full black-and-white is a licensed reduction — as long as gloss, patent and sparkle contrast survive the desaturation, a monochrome night image still reads Boujee.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Champagne apparatus** — the single most repeated motif cluster in the source: bottles popping, champagne towers, fountains, showers, flutes raised in toast, coupe glasses at black-tie events.
2. **Designer fashion and logo hardware** — Gucci belts, designer handbags, heels with visible logo buckles; the brand roll call runs Hermès, Gucci, Chanel, Armani, Alexander McQueen, Louis Vuitton, Dior, Cartier, Versace, Tom Ford, BOSS, Paco Rabanne, Balenciaga. Clothing traces to haute couture: hand-finished, extravagant, flashy.
3. **Jewels and watches** — diamonds, pearls, gold chains, chandelier chokers, jewel-set rings, arm bands, luxury watches of the Rolex type.
4. **Fur and plush texture** — white and blush stoles and coats wrapped around bare skin; plush furniture, deeply detailed rugs.
5. **High interiors and views** — mansion and penthouse vistas, hotel corridors, crystal chandeliers, marble accents, gold and silver gildings, intricate wallpaper, four-poster beds, large TVs.
6. **Mobility of wealth** — Porsche, Ferrari, Lamborghini; private jets, yacht parties, luxury resorts, private islands; being driven, stepping out of a car door.
7. **Table of excess** — caviar, oysters, lobster, steak, salmon; expensive wines in crystal glassware.
8. **Party infrastructure** — exclusive parties, VIP lounges, velvet ropes, red carpet events, high-end bars and nightclubs, gold and silver confetti in the air.
9. **Glamour grooming** — dramatically done eyelashes, perfectly contoured cheeks, glossed or matte lips in rich natural or gold colors, fake nails, big curls or super flat-ironed hair, impressive buns and braids; alternatively the flawless "no-makeup" makeup look; towel-wrapped hair and terry robes as the self-care variant.
10. **Accessories of concealment and shine** — designer sunglasses (including rhinestone-encrusted pairs), brand-new smartphones held up as mirror-selfie tools, lap dogs.

Exotic animals appear in the source list but come with the source's own ethical caution; treat them as optional at best. The lap dog is the safe animal signifier.

### Materials and textures

Sequins and rhinestones that catch flash as starbursts; satin and silk with liquid pooled sheen; velvet; fur pile in white and blush; patent leather with mirror gloss; marble veining; gilded metal; crystal refraction from chandeliers, glassware and stones; pearls; glitter and confetti suspended in air; terry cloth as the soft counterpoint in spa and self-care scenes. If a surface cannot catch a highlight, it does not belong in the frame.

### Recurring subjects

Women in cocktail and bodycon dresses on heels; men in evening black tie (dinner jacket, black silk bow tie, waistcoat or cummerbund, white shirt, patent shoes); beauty macro of lips, nails and neck jewelry; hands toasting with flutes; mirror-selfie pairs; figures walking away down gilded corridors; silhouettes at penthouse windows overlooking city lights; street-stride subjects in tailored coats and sunglasses; still lifes of a flute, a handbag, a single heel.

### Composition and lighting

Native framings: vertical social crop; mirror selfie with phone-flash starburst; cropped anonymous detail where the face leaves frame and the object becomes the proof; low-angle trophy juxtaposition of a luxury object against a landmark or skyline; rear-view procession down a symmetrical luxe interior; shallow-depth macro; candid street stride. Lighting default is direct on-camera flash at night, burning specular highlights off sequins, patent and crystal while ambient falls to black. Licensed variants: candle-warm interior glow, soft diffused studio beauty light, bright flat daylight for paparazzi and editorial street shots, and high-contrast monochrome night. Sparkle overlays, lens flares and glitter bokeh are legitimate post-layers, not errors.

## Mood & Values

Wealth, pageantry, beauty, status. Confidence and personal enjoyment first, with a shade of perceived entitlement that the culture both celebrates and critiques as materialism. The image is meant to be posted, envied, double-tapped — much of the canon is first-person: the subject holds the phone, catches her own flash in the mirror, documents the robe-on-balcony pause. That self-documentation intimacy is what separates Boujee wealth from advertised wealth. Excess is played with a wink as often as with sincerity: rhinestone sunglasses, a handbag encrusted with banknotes, a lollipop held like a jewel — kitsch glamour is in-family, never off-limits.

## Era & Origins

The term descends from the French bourgeoisie; modern slang flips it into aspirational or achieved upper-class status inside capitalist consumer culture. Its fashion lineage is long: nineteenth-century haute couture, hand-built custom clothing in expensive unusual fabric, and the nineteenth-century British-American evening black tie code. Its cultural crystallization is recent: 2000s–2010s reality television (The Hills, Gossip Girl, Keeping Up with the Kardashians, The City, Dynasty) and luxury-lyric hip-hop, R&B, pop and EDM (Jay-Z, Ye, Drake, Cardi B, Beyoncé, The Weeknd, Rihanna, Lady Gaga, Dua Lipa, Calvin Harris, David Guetta), with jazz and swing (Ella Fitzgerald, Duke Ellington) as its elegant underscore. Cinematic ancestors run from Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961) through The Wolf of Wall Street and The Great Gatsby (2013). Boujee defines itself against understated Old Money quiet luxury: it is the loud cousin, aligned with New Money overtness but filtered through smartphone nightlife and glam self-documentation.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:

1. **Proof, not catalogue.** Include at least one unmistakable wealth signifier — champagne ritual, crystal, fur, marble, logo hardware, jewel — but embed it in a lived moment: a toast, a stride, a robe-on-balcony pause. Never a flat product lineup.
2. **Specular first.** Light the scene so sequins starburst, satin pools, patent mirrors, crystal refracts. If nothing in frame catches a highlight, it is not Boujee.
3. **Flash-forward night as default**, with warm interior glow, soft studio light, daylight candid and high-contrast monochrome as licensed variants.
4. **Self-documentation stance.** The subject may hold the camera (mirror selfie, phone flash), or the frame may arrive as paparazzi catch or glam editorial plate — all three are native registers.
5. **Grooming is luxury.** Lashes, contour, glossed rich-natural or gold lips, manicured nails, sculpted hair — or the flawless no-makeup face; towel-wrap and terry robe count as self-care luxe.
6. **Layered abundance.** Combine at least two luxe textures (fur over sequin, satin with pearls, marble under crystal) and keep the palette inside the metallic / champagne / black / blush / jewel range so excess reads curated rather than chaotic.
7. **Post-ready format.** Vertical crop, sparkle and lens-flare accents welcome.

Variation axes:

- **Subject:** person of any gender; object still life; interior; car, jet or yacht; travel vista; beauty macro; gourmet plate; lap dog.
- **Composition:** full-figure, cropped detail, mirror selfie, rear view, low-angle trophy juxtaposition, symmetrical interior vista, macro, street candid.
- **Lighting:** night flash, candle-warm interior, soft studio, bright daylight, monochrome high-contrast.
- **Color handling:** champagne-gold lead, silver-platinum lead, black-tie monochrome, blush-pearl softness, jewel-tone accent, full black-and-white reduction.
- **Mood:** celebratory party, serene self-care, confident stride, vintage old-Hollywood glamour, wink-kitsch.
- **Medium:** grainy phone snapshot, film-grain flash photo, clean editorial plate, monochrome film.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A personless still life of flutes on marble and a daylight street stride with no champagne both qualify, provided the surface language, palette and proof-logic hold.

Avoid:

- Old-money quiet luxury: unbranded cashmere, matte heritage tweed, beige minimalism, restraint with no sparkle and no logo.
- Matte earthy boho or cottagecore: terracotta, rattan, wrinkled linen, macramé, dried florals, rustic farmhouse wood and mason jars.
- Gritty documentary realism: ordinary clutter, utilitarian plastic, worn public transit, flat overcast despair.
- Neon sci-fi or cyberpunk glow: magenta-cyan neon sources, holographic UI, chrome futurism (iridescent sheen on fabric is fine; neon lamps are not).
- Gothic or noir austerity: matte black without gloss, smoke-and-shadow plotting, mourning mood.
- Head-to-toe athleisure or grunge: sweat sets, flannel, distressed denim as the statement.
- Shadowless e-commerce catalogue lighting that strips every glamour cue.
- Gambling props as subject — dice, cards, roulette wheels borrow Casino's identity.
- Animal-print dominance — a leopard coat as the loudest element borrows Mob Wife's identity.
- Clean-Girl minimalism or pastel kawaii: slicked bun, bare beige set, no jewels, no flash.
