# Acid House

Acid House is simultaneously a music genre (the squelching Roland TB-303 bassline born in Chicago clubs around 1985–86) and a youth counterculture that exploded in Britain in 1988–89 as the "Second Summer of Love." Its visual identity was forged in two places at once: the cheap, urgent **flyer** — the scene's only mass medium before the internet, carrying club names, addresses, smileys and borrowed comic-book or 1960s psychedelic imagery — and the **amateur flash photograph** of the party itself: fog, crowded bodies, raised arms, and yellow smileys pinned, printed and patched onto every surface of baggy clothing. The style is deliberately unpolished, euphoric and communal: litho print on cream paper, plastic pin badges, cotton jersey, 35mm grain, and a single saturated yellow circle doing the work of a logo, a flag and a faith.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The palette operates in two modes that may coexist in one image:

- **Emblem mode (dominant):** acid yellow against ink black. Yellow sits in the range `#FFD500`–`#F8E21A` (flat or with a slight litho gradient), black in `#0A0A0A`–`#141414`. This two-color punch carries posters, flyer backgrounds and type.
- **Neon/psychedelic mode (accent or full-field):** hot pink/magenta `#FF1F8E`–`#FF4FD8`, neon green `#52E800`–`#00FF85`, orange `#FF7A00`–`#FFA300`, electric cyan/blue `#00C8F0`–`#0066FF`, deployed as tie-dye, concentric swirl rings, bubble lettering or all-over print.
- **Substrate and ambient tones:** cream/off-white flyer paper `#F2EFE6`–`#F7F3E8`; in photographs, deep saturated club shadows (blue-teal walls, black interiors) lit to white-hot highlights by direct flash; silver-grey plastic and metal for synthesizer hardware.

Rule of thumb: yellow is never a muted mustard and never shares the frame on equal terms with earth tones. Either black holds the yellow, or neon holds the black — brightness is the point.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them

1. **The smiley face.** The ubiquitous core symbol: a plain yellow circle, two eyes, one curved smile. Canonical variants: the *wink* (one eye closed to a dash), the *ecstatic/distorted* expression (the Shoom flyer's modified, three-dimensional smiley read as a drug allusion), the *damaged* smiley (cracked or exploding crown), and the *multiplied* smiley (all-over badge prints, rows of small faces along a flyer footer, a big patch on a jacket back). The smiley may scale from a 25mm pin badge to a wall-sized banner without losing its geometry.
2. **The flyer / poster.** The scene's defining artifact. Two families: (a) *basic* — cream or black stock, hand-drawn or Letraset-style type, club name, date, address, ticket price, smileys scattered or stacked; (b) *full-color* — 1960s-derived psychedelia: concentric rainbow swirls, bubble lettering, comic-book figures, futuristic landscapes, symmetrical mandala layouts with an eye or smiley at center. Bold condensed sans-serif capitals, often stretched or letter-repeated ("ACIIIIID", "CLASS OF 88"), lock top and bottom of the sheet.
3. **Baggy utilitarian fashion.** Oversized dungarees, baggy T-shirts (frequently printed with a giant smiley or the words "TRIP" / "ACID"), bucket hats, bandanas (with a borrowed outlaw charge), sunglasses worn indoors and at night, trainers (Adidas Torsion, Nike Cortez) and European/American sportswear labels (Chipie, Lacoste, Naf Naf, Russell Athletic, Champion). Silhouette first: everything loose enough to dance in for eight hours.
4. **Psychedelic pattern.** Tie-dye, swirl rings, halftone dots, comic linework — always in neon print ink, never in earthy hippie dye.
5. **The machines.** The Roland TB-303 Bass Line (silver-grey faceplate, rows of small knobs, orange/black sequencer buttons) and TR-808 as still-life subjects or props; vinyl records and sleeves as scene furniture.
6. **The party space.** Warehouses and club interiors: fog/haze, low ceilings, painted or hung smiley banners, dense crowds, hands and arms overhead.
7. **Smiley-badge accumulation.** Clothing treated as a pinboard: dozens of button badges clustered on sweaters and jackets, occasionally with one off-color (pink/red) badge breaking the yellow field.

### Materials and textures

Offset/litho print on uncoated cream paper with visible halftone dots and slight misregistration; plastic-and-metal pin badges with glossy highlights; cotton jersey, denim, corduroy dungaree straps, tie-dyed fabric; 35mm compact-camera film — grain, color shift, red-eye, motion blur on hands; airborne haze catching a single light source; brushed metal and molded plastic of analog synthesizers. Nothing in this style is vector-clean, glassy or rendered: every surface shows print, fabric, film or fog.

### Recurring subjects

Dancers mid-euphoria with arms raised; crowds shot from inside or from behind (a jacket-back smiley patch facing camera); groups of friends posing with badges and hand-painted signs; a single flyer or a grid collage of many flyers; hardware product shots; daytime street casuals in bucket hats and graphic tees (a minor, documentary variant).

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Graphic register:** centered emblem (smiley or eye) with type locked above/below; radial or bilaterally symmetrical psychedelic fields; all-over repeat patterns; dense informational type blocks (line-ups, addresses, prices) treated as design elements; collage grids of multiple flyers.
- **Photographic register:** waist-up or full-figure centered subject; on-camera direct flash with brutal falloff into black or a saturated flat wall; candid framing from within the crowd; slight tilt, crop accidents and motion blur accepted as authenticity; haze-diffused single-source light in club interiors; flat even light (scanner-like) when reproducing flyers.
- A peripheral documentary variant exists in monochrome or overcast daylight; the text does not codify it, so treat it as optional seasoning, never the default.

## Mood & Values

Euphoria, unity, hedonism, escapism, peace and love — the smiley is a value statement, not a decoration. The style communicates cheekiness (the wink), openness (inclusive crowds, broken sartorial barriers), and a gentle outlaw pride (illegal warehouses, bandanas, flyers that pretend to legitimacy). It is anti-prestige: joy expressed through cheap print, borrowed symbols and sweat-soaked cotton rather than craft or luxury. Underneath the grin runs hypnotic repetition — the visual equivalent of a 303 loop: one motif, repeated until it becomes trance.

## Era & Origins

Chicago, USA, mid-1980s (Phuture, DJ Pierre, "Acid Tracks", the Music Box), transplanted to the United Kingdom from 1987 (London's Shoom, then Trip, then illegal warehouse raves) and peaking in the Second Summer of Love, 1988–89, with Manchester's Haçienda as temple; a Belgian crossover with EBM produced the darker New Beat sibling. The style reacts against polished disco/hi-NRG professionalism and against mainstream club licensing law alike — its visuals are DIY by necessity and by ethos, and they laid the template for 1990s global rave culture.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Start from the smiley grammar: circle + two eyes + one curved smile, in acid yellow. Modify its expression (wink, ecstasy, damage, dimension) or multiply it, but never complicate its geometry.
2. Hold the two-color contract: black grounds yellow, or yellow grounds black; add neon pink/green/orange/cyan only as accent or as a full psychedelic field.
3. Choose one register and commit: flat litho flyer/poster (paper, halftone, condensed caps, informational type blocks) OR flash-lit film snapshot (grain, falloff, haze, motion blur). Hybrids are allowed only as collage (flyers photographed/pinned in a scene).
4. Dress every figure in the loose silhouette: oversized dungarees or baggy jeans, baggy tee with smiley or "TRIP"/"ACID" text, bucket hat or bandana, trainers, sunglasses at night.
5. Let typography behave period-correct: all-caps condensed sans, stretched or letter-repeated words, hand-drawn marker type, or plain utilitarian listing of club, date, address, price.
6. Put the party in the air: fog, sweat, crowded frames, raised arms — or, in graphic work, put the trance in the pattern: swirls, repeats, radial symmetry.

**Variation axes (what may change)**

- *Subject:* flyer, poster, badge sheet, vinyl sleeve, crowd, solo dancer, fashion portrait, jacket-back detail, TB-303 still life, flyer collage grid, warehouse exterior queue, daytime street casual.
- *Composition:* centered emblem, radial mandala, snapshot candid, from-behind crowd view, all-over pattern, informational type-heavy layout, grid collage.
- *Lighting:* direct on-camera flash; haze-lit single source in darkness; flat scan light for print; overcast daylight as a minor documentary variant.
- *Color handling:* strict black/yellow; black/yellow plus one neon accent; full neon psychedelic multi-color; cream-paper basic flyer; rare monochrome documentary frame.
- *Mood:* peak euphoria, tender camaraderie, cheeky graphic humor, clandestine tension (illegal rave, police lights at the edge of frame).

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — smiley grammar, black/yellow contract, loose sportswear silhouette, print-or-film texture — not any reference image. A still life of badges on cream paper and a foggy warehouse crowd are equally valid Acid House.

**Avoid**

- Muted earth tones, beige minimalism, Scandinavian calm, matte pastel grading.
- Modern EDM-festival spectacle: LED walls, confetti cannons, laser grids, chrome mainstages, digital screen gloss.
- Clean vector perfection or CGI smoothness in the smiley; the symbol must feel printed, pinned, patched or photocopied.
- 1960s hippie authenticity: paisley wool, flower crowns, hand-dyed browns, van-life props (psychedelia here is neon revival, not origin).
- Grunge/punk signifiers: flannel, safety pins, ripped denim, distressed monochrome.
- Luxury streetwear codes: logo monograms, tailored hype drops, studio product lighting.
- Dark industrial techno/EBM austerity: all-black leather, stern grid typography, red/black only (that is New Beat's door, not this one).
- Synthwave/retro-futurist chrome, purple sunsets and perspective grids.
- Sad or ironic detachment: the smiley may be warped but the scene believes in it.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Madchester:** same era, same city overlap (Haçienda), same baggy silhouette and bucket hats — but Madchester is guitar-band casual: paisley, mod-ish sportswear, indie tees, earthier primaries, daylight street swagger, and crucially *no smiley system and no neon-on-black flyer language*. Smiley ubiquity plus flash-lit club night tips any baggy-clothed image into Acid House.
- **New Beat:** the Belgian EBM crossover slows the tempo and darkens the room: black/red/white industrial graphics, stern poses, heavy minimal type. Acid House keeps yellow, grin and haze where New Beat keeps black, glare and grid.
- **Acid Design:** the detached descendant — smiley and squelch typography applied as pure graphic vocabulary to any product or era, often vector-clean and context-free. Acid House artifacts are period-bound and scene-bound: dates and addresses on flyers, film grain, sweat, badges; if the smiley floats free of the 1987–92 party world in polished modern form, it is Acid Design.
- **Groovival:** revival of 1960s–70s groove/disco warmth — browns, oranges, creams, flares, platform soles, wood-and-brass texture. Acid House rejects warm retro comfort for neon print on black and sportswear looseness.
- **Psychedelia (original 1960s):** organic hand-painted lettering, liquid light, paisley and earth-saturated rainbow, no smiley icon, no flash snapshot, no sportswear. Acid House *quotes* psychedelia (swirl rings, bubble type, comic eyes) but filters it through litho neon ink, the smiley emblem and the compact-camera party photo; the presence of the smiley or the flash-lit clubber is the deciding cue.
