# Cybergoth

Cybergoth is a club-born subculture of the late 1990s, fused from the German and Austrian rave scene and the industrial/EBM music scene — its precursors were called "Gravers" (gothic ravers). Despite the suffix it is not a branch of gothic rock culture: its soundtrack is EBM, Aggrotech, Futurepop, electro-industrial and power noise (VNV Nation, Combichrist, Hocico, Suicide Commando), and its natural habitat is the dark dancefloor, the industrial-dance stomp, and festivals like Wave-Gotik-Treffen. Commercially it was shaped by London's Cyberdog (founded 1994) and footwear brands like Demonia; socially by venues like London's Slimelight and early online communities. The look is deliberately artificial, dystopian and DIY-futurist: everything synthetic, everything black except one screaming neon color.

## Visual Identity

### Color rule: black ground, one neon signal

The palette is a discipline, not a mix. Black occupies the majority of the figure and frame — garments, boots, mask, goggle straps — while a **single** UV-reactive accent color is repeated across hair falls, leg warmers, arm sleeves, mask filters or hardware details. The text names green, pink, blue and orange; the photographic record also admits violet and blood-red as accepted extensions of the same rule. Metallic gunmetal/silver hardware (buckles, chains, O-rings, goggle rims) is the only neutral permitted beside black.

| Role | Range |
|---|---|
| Base black (PVC, rubber, fishnet) | `#050505` – `#1A1A1A` |
| Neon green (most frequent accent) | `#00FF41` – `#39FF14` |
| Acid pink / magenta | `#FF10F0` – `#FF2E97` |
| Electric cyan / blue | `#00E5FF` – `#1F51FF` |
| Neon orange | `#FF6B00` – `#FF9500` |
| UV violet (documented extension) | `#9D00FF` – `#BF00FF` |
| Hardware steel / gunmetal | `#8C8C8C` – `#D9D9D9` |

Under UV/blacklight the accent must read as *fluorescing* — glowing against a black that swallows all other detail. In daylight the same accent reads as dyed synthetic fiber: saturated but matte. Both are correct; the accent never becomes an all-over print or a rainbow scatter.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Cyberlox** — synthetic dreadfalls of yarn, foam strips and tubular crinoline; the text calls hair "the most defining feature." Falls hang past the shoulders, often two-toned (black plus accent), segmented and tubular, clearly plastic/fiber rather than human hair.
2. **Goggles** — large aviator-style goggles, *typically pushed up on the forehead* or slung at the neck/upper arm, rarely over the eyes.
3. **Platform boots** — heavy, tall, Demonia-type soles; footwear is "almost exclusively" this.
4. **Black PVC / vinyl / matte rubber clothing** — tight pants, vests, cropped or ripped tops, fishnet panels; shiny wet-look specular highlights are a texture signature.
5. **Gas masks and shiny PVC medical-style masks** — half-masks covering nose and mouth with round filter cartridges; eyes left visible and heavily lined.
6. **Fluffies** — shaggy faux-fur leg warmers over boots, in black or the accent color.
7. **Biohazard symbols and circuit-board patterns** — printed on masks, tops, goggles; note these are text-documented but appear less often than lox or goggles in surviving photography, so treat them as reinforcing accents, not requirements.
8. **Medical/fetish-futurist hardware** — harnesses, straps, buckles, chains, O-rings, fingerless or paw gloves, arm warmers, bandanas and headbands.
9. **Glow sticks and light trails** — documented in the industrial-dance context; an optional motion motif in club scenes.

### Materials and textures

Wet-look PVC and vinyl with hard specular highlights; matte rubber and reflective striping; open fishnet and ripped jersey; shaggy faux fur; smooth segmented tubular falls; brushed metal hardware; rubberized mask bodies with plastic lenses. The style rejects natural fiber visibly: no linen, no wool knits, no suede, no denim as a primary surface.

### Subjects, composition, lighting

Subjects are androgynous club dancers and festival-goers, solo or in pairs, any gender — shirtless torsos under strap harnesses and cropped ripped tops are as canonical as skirts over fishnets. Composition tends vertical and figure-centered: full-body or three-quarter portraits that keep boots and hair falls in frame, because the silhouette (volume at head and feet, narrow shiny torso) carries the style. Poses range from confrontational static stances to mid-industrial-dance stomps with raised or flailing arms, hands gripping lox, crouches, and seated floor poses that display platform soles. Environments are gritty or dark: graffiti walls, concrete stairs, harbors and industrial ruins, club interiors, locker rooms and server corridors, festival streets, or plain studio seamless washed in a colored gel matching the accent.

Four lighting regimes all belong: (a) UV/blacklight club darkness where neons fluoresce and skin goes pale; (b) hard on-camera flash snapshot against graffiti or concrete, with harsh shadows and saturated color; (c) flat overcast daylight at outdoor festivals; (d) studio portraiture with a single colored gel wash. High-contrast black-and-white treatment exists as a minor variant, in which case the accent survives as tonal contrast and the motifs must carry recognition alone.

## Mood & Values

Futurism, technology, dystopian fantasy, electronic music, club culture and individuality. The mood is aggressive playfulness: post-apocalyptic survival gear worn to dance, menace softened by carnival color. Artificiality is a virtue — synthetic hair, plastic shine, medical equipment as jewelry — and the body is treated as a modifiable machine. There is euphoria (dance, glow sticks, paired friends) and confrontation (masks, stares, stomping boots) in equal measure, but never romance, nostalgia or pastoral calm.

## Era & Origins

Term coined incidentally by Games Workshop in 1988 for *Dark Future*, but the subculture emerged independently in the late 1990s from German/Austrian rave meeting industrial clubs; solidified 1998–2005 via Cyberdog, Slimelight, Usenet's alt.gothic.cybergoth and scenes in the UK, Germany and the US. Mainstream visibility faded with the late-2000s club downturn; the style persists in dedicated club nights and festivals. Visual era signal: compact-camera flash aesthetics and early-digital color, though a clean modern render is equally valid if the rules hold.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build black-first, then place one neon accent and repeat it in at least two locations (falls plus fluffies, sleeves, mask filters or hardware). Keep the silhouette vertical and top/bottom-heavy: lox volume above, platform mass below, tight shiny torso between. Put face hardware on the forehead (goggles) or the mouth (mask), leaving the eyes readable and heavily made up. Make every surface synthetic: shine, rubber, fur, mesh, metal. Set the figure in a dark, gritty or club environment, lit by UV, hard flash, overcast daylight or a single gel.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo dancer, couple, crowd, DJ, festival pedestrian, studio portrait. Composition: full-body, three-quarter, close portrait, dynamic dance frame, crouch, seated. Lighting: any of the four regimes above. Color handling: any single accent in the neon range, occasional two-tone split falls, rare monochrome variant. Mood: euphoric, confrontational, or melancholy-dystopian. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Earth tones, pastels, heritage palettes and all-over rainbow or tie-dye color; natural human-hair dreadlocks standing in for synthetic lox; steampunk brass-and-leather goggles; Victorian/romantic goth lace, velvet and rosary iconography; military camouflage dominance; minimalist all-black techwear or Berghain-style understatement; kandi beads, PLUR gear and glitter EDM-festival styling; bondage-latex fetish framing with no neon or lox; sleek white utopian sci-fi chrome; softbox beauty lighting, domestic or pastoral settings; cotton streetwear as the primary garment.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Cybergoth's counter-tell |
|---|---|---|
| Aggrotech | Plain black band tees, tactical straps, concert-crowd utility; little costume | Neon accent discipline, cyberlox, fluffies, platforms |
| Brostep / EDM rave | All-over bright prints, kandi, glitter, no black ground | Black-dominant base, goth-lineage hardware, masks |
| Fetish Goth | Latex/bondage monochrome, black+red, sexualized maturity | Club playfulness, UV neon, lox and goggles |
| Rivethead | Military surplus, camo, dog tags, combat boots, olive/khaki | Synthetic falls, platform soles, fluorescing accent |
| Berlin Techno Fashion | Minimal functional black, sheer tops, no costume elements | Maximalist costume: lox, masks, fluffies, biohazard prints |

The decisive combination is always: black artificial base + one UV neon accent + synthetic dreadfalls + forehead goggles or respirator mask + platform boots. Remove the neon and it drifts to rivethead or techno; remove the lox and platforms and it drifts to fetish or aggrotech; remove the black ground and it becomes generic rave.
