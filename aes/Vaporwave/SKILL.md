# Vaporwave

Vaporwave is an internet-born music microgenre, visual aesthetic and meme of the early 2010s. Its sound — chopped, slowed, reverb-drowned samples of 1980s–90s smooth jazz, R&B and elevator muzak — has a direct visual counterpart: a collage language assembled from the "dead" design of a recent past, mall and lobby interiors, retro commercials, obsolete brand logos, early CGI, 1990s web design and Japan's bubble-economy imagery. The stance is deliberately ambiguous: part satire of consumer capitalism, part sincere mourning for the futures it promised (the name derives from "vaporware," software announced but never shipped). Everything below describes the visual half of that project.

## Visual Identity

### Color language (rule, not recipe)
Vaporwave color works in two registers plus a global cast. The **field register** is desaturated pastel: powder pink, baby cyan, lilac, mint, cream (roughly `#F7C8D8`, `#AEE6EF`, `#CBB9EA`, `#BFE9D9`, `#F6E7C9`). The **accent register** is neon used in gradients and outlines: hot pink, electric cyan, violet, mint (`#FF71CE`, `#01CDFE`, `#B967FF`, `#05FFA1`), often blended into sunset ramps (peach → magenta → purple). Over everything sits a **global unnatural hue shift** — a magenta or cyan cast — and **lifted, milky blacks** (dark values settle around `#241F2E`, never crushed to pure black). Duotone treatments (an entire bust in lilac, a wave in pink-and-cyan) and near-monochrome pastel fields are fully in range, as is the louder Future Funk mode of saturated neon on black. What is constant: faded, washed, chemically-off color, never neutral color science.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the source documents them)
1. **Glitch and degradation** — the style's declared key motif: scanlines, dither and halftone dots, pixel sorting, VHS tracking error, JPEG smear, corrupted hue shifts.
2. **Retro computing and UI** — "central" per the source: Windows 95 / MS-DOS-era dialog boxes, error windows, desktop icons, beige hardware, early low-poly CGI renders, 1990s web design, and physical media (jewel cases, cassettes, MiniDiscs, floppies).
3. **Classical antiquity** — Greco-Roman busts (Apollo Belvedere type), plaster casts, columns, frequently tinted a single pastel.
4. **Consumer branding** — period or obsolete logos and packaging (cola, bottled water, sportswear, operating systems), retro commercials, corporate training-video pictograms.
5. **Tropical vacation symbols** — palm trees, dolphins (a lineage traced to the 1992 game *Ecco the Dolphin*), swimming pools, ocean horizons, perpetual sunsets.
6. **Mall and lobby interiors** — tiled or terrazzo floors, glass brick, coffered ceilings, atriums, Memphis Lite patterns, empty corridors (the Mallsoft territory).
7. **Period anime and cartoons** — 1970s–90s cel-anime figures and city-pop-era illustration, especially in the upbeat Future Funk vein.
8. **Typography as image** — gratuitous Japanese kanji/katakana and full-width Latin characters (ＦＵＴＵＲＥ), chrome or gradient corporate serif, manual-cover type; text is often decorative, nonsensical, or brand-like.
9. **Memphis Design geometry** — checkerboards, triangles, squiggles, confetti shapes and grids, inherited from the 1980 Milan Memphis Group.
10. **Skylines and liminal space** — night city windows, deserted plazas, hotel lobbies, the "Nobody here" emptiness.

Surreal insertions consistent with the source's "surreal, altered reality" framing — smiling clouds, x-ray skeletons, floating fruit, mirrored doubles — appear in collage practice and are acceptable spice, never the core.

### Materials and textures
Marble and plaster; glossy ceramic tile and terrazzo; glass brick; chrome trim; beige plastic computer cases; CRT phosphor and scanlines; VHS tape noise; dither and halftone dots; flat vertex-lit low-poly meshes; airbrushed gradient skies; laminated paper manual covers; checkerboard vinyl; still pool water. Surfaces should feel like *copies of copies*: scanned, taped, tracked, compressed.

### Recurring subjects
Statues and busts, palm trees, dolphins, bottled water and soda cans, CRT monitors and OS windows, jewel cases, anime figures, pools and coastlines, mall atria and boardroom-lobbies, city skylines, training-video stick pictograms, gradient suns and prisms over horizons.

### Composition and lighting
Collage stacking with hard cut edges; a single centered icon on a flat pastel field; one-point perspective down an empty corridor or atrium; horizon-line seascapes and skyscapes; mirrored or kaleidoscopic symmetry; UI windows floating over unrelated scenery; text blocks placed like advertising; 4:3 CRT or letterboxed VHS frames, sometimes with subtitle captions. Lighting is flat and shadowless (scan or render light), fluorescent mall glow, sourceless gradient sunset, night bokeh, or washed-overexposed daylight. There is no chiaroscuro and no dramatic key light; depth is suggested by perspective and layering, not by modeling.

## Mood & Values
Nostalgia is the declared key value, but it is nostalgia for something that never existed: melancholy, uncanny, hauntological. Empty rooms hum with muzak; corporate optimism curdles into eeriness; the irony dial sits permanently between celebration and critique, and the ambiguity is the point. Stasis over action: figures wait, pose, or are absent entirely.

## Era & Origins
Emerged 2010–2011 from online communities (Turntable.fm, then Tumblr, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, 4chan) out of plunderphonics and hypnagogic pop — Lopatin's *Eccojams*, Ferraro's *Far Side Virtual*, Macintosh Plus's *Floral Shoppe*. Its source material spans the 1970s to early 2000s, core 1980s–90s corporate and consumer culture, filtered through influences like Memphis Design (Milan, 1980), *Ecco the Dolphin* (1992), *Miami Vice*, and 1990s educational-software videos. It reacts to late-20th-century technological utopianism by lovingly rotting it.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Begin from a period artifact, not a scene: imagine a 1983–1999 advertisement, manual cover, UI screenshot, VHS frame or mall postcard, then process it.
2. Apply the two-register palette plus one global cast; lift the blacks.
3. Stack at least two motif families (bust + OS dialog; palm + checkerboard; anime + kanji).
4. Treat type as a graphic element: Japanese strings, full-width Latin, period corporate serif.
5. Degrade deliberately — dither, scanlines, pixel sort, compression smear — but keep the image legible.
6. Keep humans peripheral, passive, or absent; never heroic, never mid-action.
7. Flatten the light: diffuse, fluorescent, or sourceless sunset.
8. Commit to a position on the irony dial (sincere dream vs. corporate parody); both are valid.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be anything within the 1970s–2000s consumer/tech/classical range listed above. Composition may run from dense collage to a single centered object on a flat field to strict one-point emptiness. Lighting may be scan-flat, fluorescent, sunset-gradient, or night-bokeh. Color handling may be full pastel wash, duotone, monochrome lilac, or saturated neon-on-black. Mood may slide across subgenre dials: Mallsoft (vast, empty, eerie), Future Funk (vivid, anime, upbeat), Signalwave (broken television transmission), Dreampunk (cinematic dusk), Slushwave (hazy, over-reverbed). **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**
- Deep pure blacks with glowing neon grid horizons, chrome type and sports cars (Synthwave/Outrun earnestness).
- Pristine modern 4K realism with neutral color science and no degradation or cast.
- Contemporary devices, flat-design UI, or post-2010 brands and logos.
- Glitch as the sole subject with no period content (that is pure Glitch Art).
- Aquatic early-CG sea life with no consumer/corporate layer (Seapunk).
- Airbrushed nightclub neon on black with dance-floor energy (Italo Disco).
- Hyperclean pastel minimalism with no retro residue (Cleancore).
- Gritty cyberpunk rain-noir, warm 1970s film-grain nostalgia alone, handmade craft textures, crowded candid street life, heroic poses.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cassette Futurism** shares retro-tech nostalgia but is analog-lab: beige and amber CRT terminals, chunky switches, utilitarian sci-fi optimism, cream/orange/olive palette. Vaporwave adds pastel pink-cyan, consumer branding, classical sculpture, tropical motifs, and irony instead of engineering sincerity.
- **Cleancore** is hyper-clean pastel minimalism with whitespace and hygiene-soft products. Vaporwave uses similar pastels but always carries retro residue: glitch, period type, branding, uncanny emptiness.
- **Demoscene** is virtuosic real-time computer graphics: sharp vectors, saturated primary Amiga color, technical showmanship. Vaporwave's CGI is deliberately *low*-fi early-3D, wrapped in consumer parody rather than technical pride.
- **Glitch Art** makes corruption the subject, in any palette, often abstract. Vaporwave uses glitch as seasoning over legible period subjects and a pastel-neon palette; remove the glitch and a vaporwave image still reads vaporwave.
- **Italo Disco** is 1980s club-sleeve design: airbrushed neon, chrome lettering, saturated magenta-blue on black, party energy. Vaporwave is faded rather than glossy, corporate-consumer rather than nightclub, and includes UI windows, busts and malls that Italo Disco never touches.
- Nearest of all, **Synthwave**: high contrast, deep blacks, wireframe grids, chrome, earnest 80s heroics. Vaporwave is soft, faded, sampled, and hauntological — muzak where Synthwave is anthem.
