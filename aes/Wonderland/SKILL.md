# Wonderland

## Overview

Wonderland is the aesthetic of being lost on purpose. It takes the visual vocabulary of Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* — the 1865 novel, Tenniel's engravings, and a century of film, poster and stop-motion adaptations — and generalizes it into a mood: a dreamlike world of paradox, curiosity and strange enchantment where fairytale brightness carries a darker, uncanny undertow. It is nostalgic, mysterious and playful at once, and it lives equally in fashion photography, still-life tableau, collage journals, vintage illustration and cinematic set pieces. Its defining trick is **duality**: order and chaos, light and dark, structure and whimsy coexist in every frame — a neatly laid tea table in a tangled root forest, a symmetrical corridor guarded by absurd costumed figures, pristine white tights standing in wild undergrowth.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The ground is always **muted vintage**: parchment cream (`#F2EAD9`–`#D9C9A8`), sepia and light brown (`#B99B74`–`#8A6F4D`), faded paper white (`#F7F3EA`), and ink black (`#171412`). Onto this ground the style allows **at most one or two saturated pops**, drawn from a fixed short list: cardinal / rose red (`#A31621`–`#7E2A33`), alice / teal blue (`#6E93A8`–`#2F4A56`), and hard black-and-white contrast (checker floors, stockings, card suits). Environmental mid-tones of moss and hedge green (`#5A6B4A`–`#33402F`) bridge garden scenes. Dusty lilac, blush pink and antique gold may appear as secondary accents in fashion, poster and Lolita-inflected variants, but never carry the image. Shadows and highlights are used deliberately to suggest hidden depth — pools of light, long gloom, candlelit falloff.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Threshold and passage objects** — locked doors, twisted doors, keyholes, locks and keys, mirrors, rabbit holes, corridors: the text flags these as the symbolic core (transition, discovery, hidden passage).
2. **Game and time props** — playing cards (scattered, oversized, costumed as guards), checkered floors, pocket watches and clocks running at wrong times.
3. **Tea party apparatus** — teacups, ornate saucers, pots, abandoned or impossible table settings.
4. **Creatures** — white rabbits, anthropomorphic or masked animal figures, grinning cats; flamingos are named in the text but rare in practice, so treat them as optional.
5. **Flora and terrain** — oversized mushrooms (fly agaric above all), roses (painted red, worn in hair), gardens, hedge mazes, dark forests with twisted trunks.
6. **Books and paper culture** — open vintage books, tags and handwritten labels ("drink me"), fashion plates, posters, collage and journal pages; curiosity-cabinet displays (specimen boxes, pinned butterflies) belong to this paper-and-cabinet lineage.
7. **Costume** — pinafore dresses, lace-trimmed blouses, knee-length skirts, petticoats and doll-like Lolita silhouettes, white tights and striped stockings, Mary Jane shoes and ankle boots, bow ties, top hats with tucked cards, key- and clock-motif jewelry.

### Materials and textures

Paper grain, foxing and sepia fade; brass watch cases, wound chains and iron keys; porcelain china with floral transfer print; lace, cotton voile, velvet ribbon; wood doors and table legs gone root-like; moss, hedge leaf and mushroom flesh; marble or painted checker tile; candle wax and flame; pinned specimen wings. Surfaces should feel handled, aged or slightly wrong — never glossy new.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Recurring structures, any of which may carry an image: one-point symmetrical corridors and hallways; top-down POV of feet or objects on ground cover; flat-lay grids and collage moodboards of small square studies; centered still-life tableaus on improbable supports; a tiny figure dwarfed by forest or architecture; macro close-ups of watch, key or cup; cut-paper silhouettes against muted fields; selective desaturation leaving a single hue alive (a red rose, a pink bow). Lighting splits between **soft overcast daylight** (gardens, fashion detail, flora) and **low-key candlelit or moonlit gloom** with strong chiaroscuro (interiors, forests, tea rooms). Both poles are canonical; the constant is contrast that hides as much as it reveals.

## Mood & Values

Curiosity, disorientation, escape, childlike imagination, duality — and beneath them the infobox values: the feeling of being lost, confidence, knowledge, nonsense. A Wonderland image should feel like a page from a book that is reading you back: whimsical on first glance, faintly disquieting on second. Pure cuteness without strangeness fails the style; pure horror without whimsy fails it too.

## Era & Origins

Rooted in the 1860s (Carroll, 1865; Tenniel's engravings), filtered through Victorian fashion and manners, then re-layered by 20th-century illustrated editions, poster art, and film adaptations from 1933 through the 1988 stop-motion *Alice* and beyond. As an internet aesthetic it resurfaces in fashion, interiors, journaling and lifestyle content as nostalgic fantasy and surreal escapism — and carries acknowledged criticism: romanticized escapism, rose-tinted Victorianism, and a tension between its pastel "cute" reception and its gothic, maximalist roots. Both the pastel pole and the gothic pole are legitimate; the style lives in the span between them.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build every image on the muted-ground-plus-pops palette rule; restraint in the base, precision in the accent.
2. Include at least one disorientation device: a threshold (door, keyhole, mirror, hole, corridor), a scale paradox (oversized mushroom, tiny chair, giant card), or a nonsense juxtaposition (tea set on root legs, carrot dangled over a rabbit hole).
3. Pair order with chaos: symmetry, grids and neat settings against tangle, spill and overgrowth.
4. Age the surface: paper grain, sepia fade, film grain, candle soot, worn lace.
5. Keep creatures uncanny — masked, costumed, silhouetted or stop-motion stiff — never mascot-cute.
6. Let Victorian storybook logic govern props: brass, porcelain, paper, wood, wax. No anachronisms.

**Variation axes.** Subject: fashion detail shots, still-life tableaus, interiors and corridors, gardens/forests/hedges, collage or journal spreads, poster and fashion-plate illustration, stop-motion or film stills, specimen cabinets, masked portraits. Composition: symmetry, top-down POV, flat-lay grid, centered tableau, dwarfed figure, macro. Lighting: overcast daylight, candlelit chiaroscuro, cool moonlight, spotlight pools, faded vintage exposure. Color handling: full sepia mute, monochrome with a single pop, pastel Lolita, deep gothic teal-and-black. Mood: playful nonsense through eerie uncanny. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Neon or fully saturated modern palettes; glossy CGI sheen; clean digital minimalism.
- Modern technology, logos, sneakers, contemporary streetwear.
- Checkered floors read as 1950s diner Americana rather than chessboard logic.
- Steampunk gear-and-goggle industrialism (Wonderland time is brass pocket-watch, not machine).
- Cozy warmth with no uncanny or disorienting element (slides into Cottagecore).
- Empty liminal haze with no narrative props (slides into Dreamcore).
- Glitch, compression artifacts, crude snapshot dread (slides into Weirdcore).
- Gore or horror-movie menace; the dark here is a shiver, not a wound.
- Cuteness alone: pastel frills with zero strangeness abandon the style's duality.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Chaotic Academia** shares sepia, books and clutter, but its objects are scholarly — annotated stacks, tweed, desks, libraries — with no nonsense, no thresholds, no cards or tea. Wonderland's paper culture is game-and-dream props, always with a passage motif or scale paradox.
- **Cottagecore** shares gardens, tea and vintage dress, but its world is coherent, laboring and wholesome in warm golden light. Wonderland warps the same props: hedges become mazes, tea tables go impossible, mushrooms overscale, and the light turns muted or gloomy. Cottagecore never shows a checker floor or a rabbit in a waistcoat.
- **Dreamcore** shares disorientation, but expresses it as airbrushed, soft-focus *emptiness* — liminal malls, pools, playgrounds, faceless haze. Wonderland is object-rich and narrative: period costume, storybook iconography, crafted tableau.
- **Weirdcore** shares unease, but delivers it through lo-fi compression, amateur flash, glitch and overlaid dread-text. Wonderland keeps composed, crafted framing and vintage texture; its fear is storybook uncanny, whiskered and grinning, not existential static.
