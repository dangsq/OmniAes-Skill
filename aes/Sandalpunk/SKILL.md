# Sandalpunk (Bronzepunk)

Sandalpunk, also called Bronzepunk, is a retro-futurist subgenre of cyberpunk relocated to antiquity: an alternate history in which Bronze- and Iron-Age civilizations possess advanced fantastic technology, producing a Greece that was never conquered or a Roman Empire that never fell. Its defining tension is exactly one anachronistic layer: unmistakably ancient dress, armor and architecture carrying unmistakably impossible machines. Crucially, the technology is rare and artisanal — the one-off miracle of a genius philosopher or the guarded trade secret of an artificer workshop — so machines appear as singular wonders, indistinguishable from magic or divine gift, never as mass-produced infrastructure. The visual lineage runs through the stop-motion mechanical wonders of *Jason and the Argonauts* (1963) and *Clash of the Titans* (1981), the *God of War* series, and the deliberately anachronistic Imperial-Rome-meets-1930s-monumentalism of Julie Taymor's *Titus* (1999): a world with motor vehicles, radios and simple firearms, where war is still waged by armor-clad troops with swords and spears.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is *sun-bleached stone plus worked metal plus southern sea and sky*. Ground tones are white and warm off-white marble (#F5F1E6 to #E4DCC8); metals run from bronze and gold (#8C6A3F, #B08D57, #C9A227, highlights near #E3C567) through neutral steel and iron grays (#55585C to #8E9296); the cool counterweight is Aegean blue-green and pale sky blue (#1F4E5F, #2E7D8A, #9FC3DC). Accents are limited and symbolic: Roman crimson and ox-blood red on banners, cloaks and plumes (#7E2A2A to #A33B2A), and desert ochre/sand (#C8A165) for exterior ground planes. Shadows tend toward cool teal-blue in daylight scenes and near-black warm brown in studio-lit armor portraits. Saturation is moderate; nothing glows neon. A correct image may lean warm (bronze-and-sand dominance) or cool (marble-and-blue dominance) and still read as Sandalpunk, so long as stone, bronze/steel and blue remain the structural triad.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Greco-Roman architecture** — the style's first key motif: fluted columns and colonnades, pediments, entablatures, marble staircases, vaulted basilica interiors, triumphal arches; monumental, processional, symmetrical space.
2. **Advanced technology derived from real ancient engineering** — the second key motif: gear trains and dial mechanisms in the family of the Antikythera device, watermills scaled into power plants, bronze automata and mechanical colossi, flying vessels and suspended structures rigged with radial fans, propellers, sails and ropes; machines ornamented with engraving, laurel, meander and figural relief rather than left bare.
3. **Greek and Roman arms and armor with "advanced" tells** — crested Corinthian/Attic-style helmets with tall plumes, muscled cuirasses, pteruges straps, greaves, round shields (aspis/hoplon), gladii and spears, Roman vexilla and signa banners; the advanced tell is precision: flush panel lines, articulated laminated plates, inset lenses or glowing cores, impossibly clean machining on hand-hammered bronze.
4. **Divine or magical charge around technology** — light treated as miracle: god-rays, haloed glow at a machine's core, votive atmosphere; magic given by the gods is a legitimate power source.
5. **Anachronistic everyday tech in antique guise** — chariot-derived motor vehicles, radios in bronze housings, simple firearms; always subordinate to the sword-and-spear war economy.
6. **Imperial Roman monumentalism with 20th-century echo** — vast processional axes, ranked banners, austere stone grandeur recalling the *Titus* blend of Imperial Rome and Fascist-era classicism.
7. **Colossal scale figures** — bronze automatons, talos-like guardians, mecha-duellists from the mythic canon (Achilles vs. Mecha-Hector), dwarfing human figures.

### Materials and textures

Hammered and polished bronze, patinated steel, engraved gold leaf; veined marble, limestone dust, sun-warmed plaster; oiled leather straps and pteruges; wool and linen chitons, cloaks and tunics in crimson, saffron and undyed white; rope, sailcloth and olive-wood on machines; occasional feather plumes and laurel. Surfaces show craft: hammer marks, chase work, rivets, solder seams, wear at edges — never injection-molded plastic, never mirror chrome.

### Recurring subjects

Armored warriors (hoplite, legionary, centurion, champion) in portrait or full-figure; artificer workshops and mechanism close-ups; automata and colossi; flying or floating vessels over classical cities; sunlit temple interiors and colonnades; chariots and antique-derived vehicles; banners, standards and civic processions; desert or coastal exteriors with pyramidal or temple silhouettes. Diagrammatic presentation — character-design sheets, callout breakdowns of a helmet or shield mechanism — is a documented presentation mode in this culture, not a subject requirement.

### Composition and lighting

Composition favors monumental contrast: small human figures against colossal columns, gates or machines; strong axial symmetry and processional recession in architecture; frieze-like horizontal staging; or a single centered armored figure isolated against darkness for armor studies. Lighting splits into two documented regimes: (a) bright Mediterranean daylight — hard sun, atmospheric haze, shafts of light raking through colonnade interiors, dust motes in god-rays; (b) museum-dark chiaroscuro — a single warm key raking across bronze to describe form, background sunk to near-black. Bronze and marble both take strong specular highlights; shadows stay cool or warm-brown, never colored neon.

## Mood & Values

Wonder held in reverence. Technology is miraculous precisely because it is scarce, hand-made and secret — the philosopher's engine, the workshop's trade secret — so machines are framed like relics or idols, not appliances. The style expresses civic pride and imperial permanence (an empire that never fell), heroic idealism of the human body in disciplined armor, and the Greek conviction of light as reason: sunlit marble, rational geometry, ordered colonnades. Against that order sits the forge-shadow: the eerie, almost sacred dread of a bronze giant waking. Craftsmanship is the core value — every gear is engraved, every plate hammered — and human scale matters: swords and spears remain the weapons of war however marvelous the machines.

## Era & Origins

Sandalpunk is a named internet-aesthetic consolidation of a much older visual tradition. Its roots are mid-20th-century cinematic myth-making — the stop-motion mechanical wonders of Ray Harryhausen's *Jason and the Argonauts* (1963) and *Clash of the Titans* (1981) — filtered through modern video-game antiquity (*God of War*, *Assassin's Creed: Odyssey*, *Hades*, *Age of Mythology*'s Atlanteans, *Ryse: Son of Rome*) and anachronistic stage/film classicism (*Titus*, 1999). The "-punk" suffix deliberately rhymes with Steampunk, relocating the retro-futurist impulse from Victorian steam to Bronze- and Iron-Age mechanics; as a subgenre of cyberpunk it inherits the "high tech, old power" theme but inverts the gloom into sunlit marble. It neighbors and borrows from the Hellenic aesthetic while refusing pure historicity.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Anchor antiquity first: silhouette, architecture and dress must read Greek or Roman before any technology appears (crested helmet, cuirass, pteruges, chiton, toga, colonnade, pediment).
2. Add exactly one layer of impossible technology, and make it hand-crafted: bronze gear trains, dial mechanisms, automata, fan-rigged flying barges, watermill dynamos — ornamented, engraved, singular.
3. Treat the machine as miracle: give it reverence in framing and light (god-rays, votive glow at the core), and keep it rare within the scene.
4. Keep the palette structural: marble white, bronze/gold, steel gray, Aegean blue; crimson and ochre as accents only.
5. Light like the Mediterranean or like a dark museum: hard sun with haze and colonnade shafts, or a single warm key on bronze against darkness.
6. Show craft on every surface: hammer marks, rivets, chase work, wear; marble veined and dusted, never sterile.
7. Scale the human against the monumental: columns, gates, colossi and machines dwarf figures.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range from an armor portrait, to an automaton, to an environment (temple interior, harbor, desert plateau, floating city), to a vehicle, to a design-sheet diagram. Composition may be axial and processional, diagonally dynamic, or a centered vignette. Lighting may be full noon, raking interior shafts, dusk, or studio chiaroscuro. Color handling may lean warm bronze-and-sand or cool marble-and-teal within the stated palette. Mood may be triumphal, scholarly, eerie or devotional. Medium may be painterly digital concept art (the dominant register), clean 3D render, or line diagram. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — no reference composition, figure or framing is canonical.

**Avoid.**
- Victorian/industrial steampunk hardware: steam boilers, smokestacks, exposed copper piping, goggles, top hats, corsets, coal soot as the dominant texture.
- Cyberpunk neon: holograms, LED strips, glowing signage, saturated magenta/cyan night lighting.
- Modern or space-age materials: chrome, glass curtain walls, polymers, carbon fiber, sleek minimalist sci-fi forms.
- Medieval or Gothic vocabulary: plate armor of the 15th century, castles, cathedrals, heraldic knights.
- Pure historicity with no anachronistic machine or advanced tell (that is Hellenic / peplum, not Sandalpunk).
- Mass-produced infrastructure: fleets of identical machines, power grids, factories; the tech must stay singular and artisanal.
- Diesel-punk grime, rust-bucket decay, or post-apocalyptic ruin as the default state; this world is prosperous and permanent.
- East Asian, Egyptian-only or non-Mediterranean architectural dominance (Egyptian motifs may appear as contact/tribute, not as the base culture).

## Discrimination Cues

| Cue | Sandalpunk | Hellenic | Steampunk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technology | Impossible but hand-crafted antique tech: gears, automata, flying barges, glowing cores | None; pure classical antiquity | Victorian steam: boilers, pistons, brass pipes, goggles |
| Architecture | Marble colonnades *plus* machine structures, suspended wonders | Marble colonnades, temples, agoras only | Brick, ironwork, factories, Victorian streets |
| Palette | Marble white, bronze/gold, steel gray, Aegean blue | Marble white, olive, terracotta, sea blue | Brown leather, copper, soot, amber gaslight |
| Silhouette | Crested helmet, cuirass, pteruges — with machined tells | Crested helmet, chiton, himation — historic | Top hat, coat, corset, goggles |
| Mood | Miracle-tech reverence, imperial permanence | Philosophical calm, pastoral ideal | Inventor's clutter, industrial adventure |

Fast tells: if the marble is intact but a bronze gear train or automaton occupies the temple, it is Sandalpunk; if the same scene contains no machine at all, it is Hellenic; if the machine runs on visible steam and the dress is 19th-century, it is Steampunk. Against plain sword-and-sandal peplum, the decisive element is the anachronistic advanced detail — precision machining, inset lenses, powered cores — on otherwise ancient arms. Against cyberpunk proper, the decisive element is scarcity and craft: one miraculous engine, engraved and revered, instead of ubiquitous chrome-and-neon infrastructure.

*Honest gaps:* the documented corpus is strongly visual-concept-art in medium (painterly character and environment studies, design sheets, game renders); typography, graphic design and music are thinly evidenced and should not be treated as defining. Diagrammatic callout sheets appear frequently but are a presentation convention of concept culture, not a required content element.
