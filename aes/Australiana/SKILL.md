# Australiana

Australiana is a contemporary aesthetic built from the bric-a-brac, kitsch and commercial imagery of twentieth-century Australian homes, suburbs and tourist shops, from the industrializing 1960s onward through the confident 1980s boom. It fuses three strands that rarely meet elsewhere: stylized native wildlife and flora, abstract patterns descended from Aboriginal painting (as championed by the Pwerle family and absorbed into mainstream design), and suburban-tourist vernacular — cream brick veneer, Queenslanders on stilts, terrazzo and mosaic highway murals, postcard typography shouting place names like Gold Coast and Surfers Paradise. Irony is central: the style understands the contradictions of Australian culture, paying homage to a mocked past ("cultural cringe") while optimistically remixing it. Nothing in it is tasteful by conventional rules, and that is the point.

## Visual Identity

### Colour language

The governing rule is *deliberate discord*: "colours that don't go together," contrasting schemes, opalescent and iridescent accents, and bright primaries splashed onto stark white. Four compatible registers exist, and an image may sit in any one or blend them:

| Register | Anchors (approx.) | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Clash-on-white | white `#F6F4EF` ground with magenta `#E8447A`, cobalt `#2A6BD4`, emerald `#3FA34D`, canary `#FFD34E`, tangerine `#F5A01E` | sweatshirt graphics, tea towels, fashion prints |
| Neon colour-block | purple `#7B4FC9`, hot pink `#FF4F87`, lime `#8DC63F`, black `#1A1A1A` panels | spray jackets, windbreakers, 80s sportswear |
| Earth-dash field | brick `#B5452C`, ochre `#C97B2D`, olive `#8A9A5B`, pale sky `#A8C8E8`, cream `#F4E9D5`, dotted white | Aboriginal-derived painting, mosaic murals, terrazzo |
| Sun-bleached coast & suburb | cyan sea `#29B8D8`, yellow sand `#EFD98A`, cream brick `#E8DCC0`, gum grey-green `#9AA88C`, red dirt `#A0522D` | postcards, aerials, street and beach snapshots |

Opalescence — pearly, shifting pink-green-blue — appears as an accent note (opal-inspired fashion), never as the whole palette. Saturation stays high in every register; even the earthy field is punched up with pastel blues and whites rather than muted to boho beige.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the tradition documents them

1. **Native wildlife**: koalas, kangaroos, galahs — stylized, printed, embroidered, never documentary-naturalistic.
2. **Native flora**: eucalyptus and gum leaves, scribbly gum bark, waratahs, hibiscus — the default print vocabulary.
3. **Abiginal-derived abstract pattern**: rhythmic parallel dashes, dot fields, tessellated colour blocks evoking bush-medicine leaf painting; treated as respectful abstraction, not copied sacred iconography.
4. **Landscape**: red dirt plains, yellow sand beaches, vast horizontal expanses; aerial coastline views.
5. **Suburban architecture**: low-set cream brick homes, Queenslanders on stilts; older Brunswick, Burleigh, Bondi streetscapes.
6. **Retro design surfaces**: terrazzo patterns, mosaic highway and shopping-centre murals, grout-lined tile grids.
7. **Kitsch consumer culture**: tourist postcard borders and chunky display type, advertisement imagery, children's television references, corner-store and 7-Eleven signage, fairy bread, "sangas," chips with chicken salt.
8. **Fashion objects**: Coogi-style acrylic knits, Ken Done-style sweatshirts, Country Road logo sweats, neon spray jackets, terry towelling or flower-motif bucket hats, wide-brim hats, fanny packs, loafers, Rip Curl surfwear; buttery-yellow blonde hair with dark regrowth as a grooming cue.
9. **National emblematics as kitsch**: "AUSTRALIA" lettering, sun-and-eye hand-painted marks, flag and map silhouettes on garments and postcards — secondary devices, always played with a wink.

### Materials and textures

Terry towelling pile; chunky acrylic knit loops and zigzag intarsia; glossy nylon sheen on spray jackets; glazed mosaic tile and grout lines; terrazzo chip; acrylic paint laid in fat dashed strokes on canvas; glossy postcard print with visible colour saturation and border ink; cream brick mortar; weathered timber of stilted Queenslander verandahs; eucalyptus bark shedding in cream-and-grey patches.

### Recurring subjects

Beachgoers and street candids in painted or printed bodysuits and knits; boutique rails and mannequins dense with clashing prints; suburban lounge rooms overstuffed with gaudy soft furnishings; mural walls beside highways and mall plazas; garment flat-lays or hanging product shots on plain white; aerial coastlines framed like souvenir postcards; painted canvas fields of dashed colour.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Postcard logic**: bordered frames, oversized rounded display type overlaid on panoramic or aerial views, place names as graphic elements.
- **All-over print**: motif repeated edge-to-edge with no resting focal point — textile, mural, or painting read as a continuous field.
- **Mosaic grid**: tessellated rectangles and dashes organizing colour, from canvas to plaza floor.
- **Casual snapshot**: slightly off-kilter street or beach framing, figures mid-stride, signage and palms intruding at the edges.
- **Flat product staging**: single garment centred on white, graphic isolated with generous negative space.
Lighting runs to hard vertical midday sun with saturated cyan sky and short shadows, or even shadowless studio light for garment graphics, or dim warm interior light for domestic kitsch scenes. Blowouts and gloss are acceptable; murk is not.

## Mood & Values

Nostalgia with a raised eyebrow. The style is fun, laidback and irreverent, channeling the "larrikin" spirit — occasionally iconoclastic, always self-aware. It celebrates what was once mocked: the gaudy knit, the fairy bread, the mosaic mall mural. Beneath the joke sits genuine affection and an optimistic, collaborative forward-look, plus respect for the Aboriginal artistic strand it incorporates. The emotional temperature is loud warmth: a barbecue, a road trip, a souvenir shop treated as a gallery.

## Era & Origins

Roots in 1960s–70s suburban kitsch and early Flamingo Park designs (Jenny Kee and Linda Jackson painting gum bark and opal colour onto fashion in the 1970s); crystallized in the 1980s through Ken Done's graphics, Coogi knitwear, Expo '88 souvenir clothing and the Fashion Design Council of Australia (1983–1993), which rejected European conventions in favour of the local party scene. Fell out of favour by the mid-1990s under "cultural cringe," then revived from the late 2010s as Australiana 2.0, with independent designers and boutiques reinterpreting the symbols sincerely-ironically. Visual memory therefore spans 1970s hand-painted fashion, 1980s neon and knitwear, 1990s mosaic murals and television kitsch, and contemporary digital-age remix.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Clash on purpose: pair hues conventional taste forbids; keep saturation high; let white ground amplify the splashes, or eliminate ground entirely with all-over pattern.
2. Speak Australian fluently: fauna, flora, suburbia, surf-coast tourism and Aboriginal-derived abstraction are the vocabulary — combine at least two strands in one image.
3. Keep the wink: exaggerate slightly (gaudier knit, louder type, brighter mural) so irony reads; sincerity alone tips into plain documentary.
4. Honour texture: name the material — terry, knit, nylon, mosaic, acrylic dash, brick — and let light catch it.
5. Treat Aboriginal-derived pattern as abstract rhythm of dashes and dots in earth-plus-pastel blocks; invent within that grammar rather than reproducing ceremonial symbols.
6. Let typography behave like a motif: chunky rounded display caps, hand-painted script, place names and slang ("ta") integrated into the composition.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- *Subject*: wildlife portrait, beach candid, suburban street, lounge-room interior, mural wall, plaza aerial, garment product shot, painted canvas, boutique display, food still life (fairy bread, sanga, chicken-salt chips), postcard collage.
- *Composition*: flat graphic on white, edge-to-edge print field, mosaic grid, bordered postcard with type, loose snapshot, symmetrical product staging, high aerial.
- *Lighting*: hard noon sun with cyan sky, flat even studio light, warm dim interior, glossy overexposed flash snapshot.
- *Colour handling*: clash-on-white, neon colour-block, earth-dash field, sun-bleached coast — or a deliberate hybrid (e.g., neon knit against cream brick).
- *Mood*: warm sincerity, broad comedy, cool ironic detachment, celebratory national pride played straight enough to be suspect.

**Avoid**

- Muted minimalist palettes, Scandinavian beige, quiet-luxury neutrals, monochrome or duotone schemes.
- Sleek modern gradients, glassmorphism, clean corporate vector flatness.
- Generic American retro (diners, Route 66, chrome), British seaside, Japanese city-pop neon nightscapes.
- Tropical tiki/Hawaiian print logic (hibiscus is allowed only inside the Australian flora mix, never as Polynesian kitsch).
- Desaturated film-fade washes that drain the primaries; murk, fog, moody low-key lighting.
- Documentary-realist wildlife photography or respectful-nationalist monumentality without irony.
- Copying sacred Aboriginal iconography; keep to abstract dash/dot field grammar.
- Surf-gloss bronze bodies or dad-holiday understatement as the *point* of the image (see below).

## Discrimination Cues

**Vs Surf Crush**: both love Australian beaches and share brands like Rip Curl, but Surf Crush centers the surf body — boards, wax, bronze skin, wave spray, sun-flare sensuality — in a cohesive tonal or dusk-neon palette. Australiana centers national kitsch symbols instead: koalas and galahs, cream brick and Queenslanders, mosaic murals, postcard type, Aboriginal-derived dash fields, clashing primaries on white. If the image could exist without any beach, it can still be Australiana; Surf Crush cannot leave the water.

**Vs Vacation Dadcore**: Dadcore is the unselfconscious holiday wardrobe — beige, navy, white, socks in sandals, muted resort snapshots, earnest uncool. Australiana borrows the bucket hat and the casual resort setting but pushes volume: neon spray jackets, Coogi zigzags, terry towelling florals, ironic national lettering. Dadcore never clashes on purpose and never references Aboriginal art, suburbia kitsch or fairy bread; Australiana's irony and loudness are the tell. Where Dadcore fades, Australiana saturates.
