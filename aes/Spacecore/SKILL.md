# Spacecore (Astrocore)

Spacecore is a contemporary internet aesthetic of the 2020s built on the romanticization and artistic stylization of the cosmos. Stars, planets and galaxies are used as abstract, highly saturated decoration for digital art, fashion and home décor rather than as scientific subjects. The style is explicitly escapist and fantastical: it defines itself against hard science, military sci-fi and historical Space Age realism, and instead embraces dreamlike compositions, impossible planetary colors, and introspective questions addressed to the sky ("Are we alone?", "The stars know everything"). The cosmos here is a bedroom, a diary and a comfort object — a place to float away to, not a frontier to conquer.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: the void/light contract

Every Spacecore image honors one color rule: a light-swallowing dark ground set against self-luminous, intensely saturated celestial light. The background is typically solid black or deep indigo/navy; all color arrives as glowing bodies, nebular swirls and glitter. Planets are routinely rendered in impossible colors — rainbow bands, oil-slick pastels, candy magenta — never in realistic ochre, rust or grey.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how firmly the text documents them)

1. **Stylized galaxies and nebulae** — spiral swirls, soft vortex clouds, heart- or wreath-shaped star clusters; abstracted, never telescopically accurate.
2. **Rainbow- and pastel-colored planets** — banded gas giants in iridescent oil-slick color, frequently ringed; rings may themselves be rainbows or ribbons.
3. **Pink/purple celestial bodies and moons** — cratered moons tinted magenta or lavender, hovering large in frame.
4. **Meteor showers and shooting stars** — streaks and star-trails representing motion or emotion; trails of stars climbing like stairways or winding like roads into space.
5. **Glowing star-shaped lights as decor** — star lamps, string lights, star wands and scepters; the cosmos brought into the bedroom. (Strongly named in the text; in imagery it surfaces as star wands, star trails and sparkle overlays.)
6. **Dreamlike floating imagery** — bodies suspended with no orbital logic; hands reaching out of windows toward falling stars; figures sitting on asteroids as if on bedroom floors.
7. **Existential text overlays** — short captions in serif, handwritten or retro type: lonely, homesick, wondering one-liners placed over starfields.
8. **Glitter, shimmer, iridescence** — sparkle grain scattered across the whole surface as a material, not an effect.
9. **Cosmos inside silhouettes / double exposure** — starfields filling chest X-rays, portraits veiled in glitter and stars; the universe located inside the body.
10. **Magical-girl / anime inflection** — star wands, tiaras, pastel scepters and cel-shaded hands, inherited from the franchise lineage (Sailor Moon, Star☆Twinkle Pretty Cure).

### Materials and textures

Digital glow and bloom halos around every light source; four- or six-point cross flares on bright stars; airbrush softness inherited from 70s–80s paperback cover art; stippled glitter grain and dense pin-star fields; occasional VHS softness, grain and faded contrast on photographic layers; holographic/iridescent sheen. In fashion (text-documented; imagery shows little of it): high-resolution galaxy and constellation prints on fabric, translucent overlays, iridescent and silver materials, glitter, bold fantasy silhouettes with knee-high or combat boots.

### Subjects, composition, lighting

Recurring subjects: lone planets or moons in void; horizon landscapes with a planet hovering over silhouetted hills; cosmic cloud banks tinted lilac; pattern/wallpaper repeats of stars and painted planets; quote cards; double-exposed portraits; retro paperback-cover scenes; a window opening onto a starfall. Humans, when present, are tiny silhouettes, reaching hands, or figures dissolved into star overlay — the celestial bodies are the protagonists.

Composition tends to center a single glowing body in the void, or stack a low horizon beneath a dominating sky, or cut the frame with a diagonal meteor trail or rainbow ribbon; all-over decorative repeats and centered text blocks are equally valid. Lighting is always emissive: objects glow from within, bloom bleeds into the dark, sparkle flares punctuate the brightest points. There is no single directional sun, no hard shadow, no daylight.

## Mood & Values

Escapism, wonder, fantasy, existential searching, individuality, dreaminess. Beneath the glitter runs a tender melancholy: loneliness, homesickness, quiet questions asked to an empty sky. The style values feeling over accuracy — motion as emotion, color as mood — and treats vastness as comforting rather than threatening.

## Era & Origins

A 2020s internet aesthetic (aesthetic-wiki culture), also called Astrocore. It inherits Tumblr-era galaxy prints and vaporwave-adjacent dreaminess, blends with Harajuku's Uchuu Kei, and is fed by magical-girl and animated space fantasy (Sailor Moon, Pretty Cure, Voltron, Treasure Planet, Super Mario Galaxy's rainbow orbits) and by ethereal electronic/dream-pop sound (Air, Duster, Windows 96, Kalafina, LOONA). It reacts against hard science, military sci-fi and historical Space Age aesthetics; a retro strand within it airbrushes surreal spacescapes that romanticize — or gently parody — Space Age promises, such as a road winding up into orbit.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Honor the void/light contract: dark ground, luminous saturated bodies.
2. Color the impossible: planets in violet, magenta, pastel or rainbow; never true-color rock and gas.
3. Glow, glitter, flare: bloom halos, cross-sparkles, stippled shimmer on every light.
4. Decorate, don't simulate: no orbital mechanics, no instrumentation, no realism checks.
5. Let motion be emotion: meteor trails, star stairways, ribbons and roads into space.
6. Keep humans small, silhouetted, overlaid, or absent.
7. Optionally whisper a caption: one short existential line in serif, script or retro type.
8. Keep the medium soft: photo-bash composite, airbrush illustration, anime cel, glitter photography, pattern design, paperback cover.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any celestial or celestial-touched thing — planet, moon, galaxy, nebula, comet, star decor, bedroom corner, portrait with star overlay, X-ray or silhouette filled with cosmos, pattern repeat, retro cover, hand reaching from a window. Composition: centered floating body, horizon-and-hovering-planet, diagonal trail, all-over repeat, text-centered quote card, double exposure, window view. Lighting: full neon bloom on black, dim VHS dreaminess, soft pastel ambient glow. Color handling: saturated neon-on-black, lavender pastel dream, rainbow-iridescent accent, near-monochrome violet mood. Mood: wonder, melancholy, loneliness, comfort, playfulness. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a composition unlike every sample here can still be perfect Spacecore if it keeps the void/light contract, impossible color, glitter-glow and escapist tenderness.

**Avoid**

- True-color NASA-style photography: grey/ochre planets, white sunlight, black-and-white mission archives.
- Hard sci-fi: technical spacecraft, HUDs, realistic suits and helmets, orbital diagrams, military insignia.
- Historical Space Age kitsch played straight: chrome rockets, mission patches, sepia documentation.
- Aliens, UFOs, abduction tropes, acid-green slime palettes.
- Flat merchandise-only fabric prints with no scene, glow or mood.
- Fandom clutter: shelves of collectibles, consoles, desks, reference-heavy props.
- Daylight, blue sky with white clouds, warm earthy palettes (beige, terracotta), hard shadows.
- Hard-edged vector minimalism or corporate flat gradients.
- Cosmic horror: threatening voids, dread, monstrosity — Spacecore is tender.
- Neon perspective grids and scanline suns (that is Signalwave, a neighbor, not this style).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Its tell | Spacecore's counter-tell |
|---|---|---|
| Aliencore | Alien creatures, UFOs, green/acid palette, kitschy abduction narrative | No lifeforms needed; the bodies themselves are the subject; violet/magenta glow |
| Fantasy Astronomy | Plausible or real astronomical rendering, telescopic framing, Hubble-like teal/orange, reverent mythic-science tone | Impossible candy colors, glitter grain, captions, décor logic over accuracy |
| Galaxy Print | Surface pattern applied to products (leggings, hoodies, cases); repeat without narrative | Compositional scenes with bloom, mood and optional caption; prints appear only as an ingredient |
| Uchuu Kei | Harajuku street-fashion coords: full-body outfit photos, space-motif garments, decora accessories | Cosmos-first digital art, décor and quote cards; fashion is one output, not the frame |
| Nerd | Fandom merchandise, tech clutter, referential props, arbitrary palette | Locked violet-void palette, dreamy non-referential mood, no merchandise clutter |

Quick test: if the image reads as "the universe as a soft, glittering place to escape to, colored however feeling demands," it is Spacecore; if it reads as science, merchandise, fashion coord, alien kitsch or fandom collection, it is one of the neighbors.
