# Bon Chic, Bon Genre

Bon Chic, Bon Genre — "good style, good class," abbreviated BCBG or Bécebège — is the lifestyle aesthetic of the traditional French high bourgeoisie. It is not merely a wardrobe but a code: manners, private-school education, Catholic traditionalism, old French given names, rallye dance parties, and a self-assured inheritance of taste. Visually, however, it compresses into one of the most legible sartorial languages in Europe: conservative tailoring in quiet neutrals, heritage accessories worn small and correct, and an air of relaxed propriety. It is the French expression of Old Money — less clubhouse and more arrondissement, less crest and more scarf.

## Visual Identity

### Colour palette

The palette is a rule, not a swatch: **neutrals dominate the frame, and any saturation arrives small and deliberate.** The textual core is navy blue, beige, grey, white and cream, with occasional muted pastels. The photographic record extends this with camel/caramel wool and grounding black, and permits a single punctuation of saturated colour — a scarlet beret, a bordeaux handbag, a rust skirt, a red silk bow — occupying a minor fraction of the frame. Pastels, when present, stay powdery and desaturated.

Never let the accent lead: if red covers more than a beret, a bow, a bag or a single garment among neutrals, the frame has drifted off-style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual documentation

1. **Silk scarves** (Hermès, Burberry type) — knotted at the neck, tied at the collar of a blouse, or carried folded over the forearm; the style's single most cited object. Pussy-bow blouses read as the same gesture.
2. **Trench coats** and tailored wool coats (camel, beige, grey) — belted or open over a tailored layer.
3. **Pearl necklaces** — a single discreet strand; strongly documented in text even when the photographic sample favours scarves and bows at the neckline.
4. **Tailored suits and blazers** — double- or single-breasted, nipped but not tight; navy blazer with grey or cream trousers for men; skirt suits and belted waists for women.
5. **Cashmere sweaters** — V-necks and cardigans layered over button-down shirts or polo shirts; fine-gauge, never chunky.
6. **Loafers**, plus ballet flats and riding boots — closed, polished, low-heeled; block heels and pumps appear as the dressier edge.
7. **Classic leather handbags** — structured top-handle or small crossbody boxes in smooth calfskin; tartan or plain, always compact and rigid, never slouchy.

Secondary staples documented in the fashion code: tweed jackets, silk blouses, tailored chinos and cigarette pants, midi and knee-length skirts. Observed in the imagery but not canonized by the text, and therefore allowable variation rather than core: the breton marinière stripe as a casual layer, the red beret, and small tartan accessories.

### Materials and textures

Wool gabardine and tweed (herringbone, houndstooth), cashmere and fine merino knit, silk twill for scarves and blouses, crisp cotton poplin and oxford for shirts, smooth box calfskin for bags and loafers, pearl lustre at the throat, a tan leather belt as the one visible hardware note. Textures should read expensive to the touch: matte, dense, woven. Shiny synthetics, sequins, distressed fabric and technical sportswear materials are foreign to this world.

### Recurring subjects and settings

Well-groomed bourgeois figures of any age: women in tailored coats, men in navy blazers and ties, children in smart knitwear (the style's childrenswear brands are part of its identity), couples arm-in-arm, small groups of friends or siblings walking or posing together. Settings cluster around inherited urbanity and domestic heritage: Haussmannian facades and arcades, stone steps and heavy panelled doors, wrought-iron railings, leaf-strewn residential streets in autumn, church parvises and private-school gates, train cabins and warm apartment interiors. A neutral studio seamless (grey, taupe, or a draped curtain) is equally valid — much of the record is lookbook and editorial photography, including annotated outfit-diagram layouts.

### Composition and framing

Outfit-first framing: full-body or three-quarter vertical compositions that leave the silhouette readable from collar to shoe, the figure centred or slightly off against a simplified ground. Group shots stand figures side by side or walking in loose formation; candid street snaps allow laughter and linked arms, but grooming never slips. Poses are poised and mannerly — hands in coat pockets, a bag held in the crook of the elbow, a scarf adjusted mid-gesture. Eye contact is calm or averted; nothing performs for the camera beyond good breeding.

### Light and medium

Soft, even, diffuse light: overcast daylight outdoors, large softbox or window light in studio with gentle falloff into a muted backdrop, warm tungsten glow in interiors. Contrast stays low-to-medium; colour rendering is true and slightly desaturated, with a clean editorial-digital or fine-film finish. No hard flash, no neon spill, no heavy vignettes.

## Mood & Values

Understated elegance, discretion, tradition, good manners, quality craftsmanship, inherited social status. The emotional register is composed cheer: amusement is allowed (laughing couples exist in the record), but effort must never show — everything looks owned for years rather than bought this season. Beneath the polish sits a whiff of elitism and snobbishness, the style's acknowledged criticism: this is taste as class boundary, nostalgia for Ancien Régime social codes worn on a modern street.

## Era & Origins

The phrase crystallized in the affluent circles of Paris and Versailles in the late 1970s and early 1980s and was codified by Thierry Mantoux's 1985 guide *BCBG: le guide du bon chic bon genre*. Its components — "chic" and "bon genre" — reach back through 19th-century salon society to Ancien Régime codes. The modern subculture formed as a deliberate reaction against Hippie and Bohemian counterculture, offering a traditional, sophisticated alternative inspired by prestigious French schools. Iconic figures: Catherine Deneuve, Inès de La Fressange, Charlotte Gainsbourg. House names that anchor the look: Hermès, Chanel, Charvet, Loro Piana, Burberry, J.M. Weston, Goyard, plus the family wardrobe of Lacoste, Saint James, Petit Bateau, Bonpoint and Jacadi.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Neutrals carry the frame; saturation is a punctuation mark (one scarf, beret, bag or skirt), never a field.
2. Silhouette over statement: tailored, fitted, knee-length or longer hemlines; correct shoulders; trousers creased and cropped clean.
3. Layer in the grammar of the code — shirt or blouse, cashmere V-neck or cardigan, blazer or trench on top, scarf or pearls at the throat.
4. Wealth is implied by fabric and cut, never announced: no visible logos, monograms or hardware branding.
5. Footwear closed and classic: loafers, ballet flats, riding boots, modest block heels.
6. Set the figure in inherited space — stone facades, steps, arcades, leafy autumn streets, warm traditional interiors — or on a neutral studio seamless.
7. Light softly and evenly; grade slightly desaturated; keep the whole outfit in frame.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range from a solo woman or man to children, couples, sibling pairs, walking groups of four, or a diagrammatic outfit layout. Composition may be a striding studio full-length, a seated interior portrait, an arm-in-arm street candid, a lookbook lineup, or a labelled ensemble illustration. Lighting may be overcast daylight, softbox studio with dark falloff, or warm interior glow. Colour handling may sit anywhere within the neutral family — navy-dominant, camel-dominant, cream-dominant, black-and-white — with or without muted pastels, with or without one small saturated accent. Mood may run from composed editorial neutrality to open laughter. Season skews autumn but spring and winter read correctly. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Logomania, monogram prints, conspicuous brand hardware.
- Neon or saturated colour blocking; more than one loud accent.
- Distressed denim, athleisure, sneakers, hoodies, oversized streetwear.
- Bohemian layers, hippie florals, fringing, crochet; grunge flannel, punk studs and leather-jacket hardware.
- Overt sexiness: clubwear cuts, sheer panels, micro-minis with heavy makeup.
- Hard direct flash, high-contrast noir lighting, tropical or beach settings, graffiti or industrial backdrops.
- Chunky knits, slouchy unstructured bags, visible tattoos or extreme hairstyles breaking the groomed norm.
- Maximalist clutter: the BCBG frame, like its manners, stays tidy.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Chic Modernist:** shares restraint but strips ornament — architectural monochrome, minimal jewellery, sparse modern interiors. BCBG keeps heritage ornament (scarf, pearls, tweed) and traditional stone-and-wood settings; its minimalism is social, not spatial.
- **Old Money:** near-synonymous values; BCBG is its French dialect. Tell them apart by signifiers: BCBG shows silk scarves knotted Hermès-style, pussy-bow blouses, berets, breton stripes, Haussmannian stone and Catholic-school formality; Old Money leans Anglo-American — country clubs, sailing, polo and crested blazers, New England estates, khaki and Nantucket red.
- **Preppy:** brighter and sportier — madras, rugby stripes, boat shoes, chino shorts, campus greens, playful colour pops. BCBG is urban, formal, palette-muted, and swaps the crest for the pearl strand.
- **Sprezzatura:** Italian studied carelessness — deliberately rumpled jackets, rolled sleeves, clashing patterns, flamboyant pocket squares. BCBG is correctness itself: neat, discreet, never intentionally askew.
- **Classicism:** a broader timeless ideal drawn from antiquity — draped gowns, columns, statuesque posing. BCBG is a 20th-century social subculture in contemporary tailored staples; its references are boarding schools and salon society, not the antique.
- **Power Dressing** (a listed relative): assertive padded shoulders and boardroom aggression; BCBG tailoring is conservative and soft-shouldered, signalling inheritance rather than ambition.

**Quick recognition check:** neutral tailored layers + one discreet heritage accessory (scarf, pearls, structured bag) + poised grooming + soft light + inherited urban or studio setting = Bon Chic, Bon Genre.
