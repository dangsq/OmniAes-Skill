# 2010s Dark Fandom

2010s Dark Fandom (retrospectively named; also Insanitycore, EXEcore) is the edit-culture aesthetic of the early-to-mid 2010s web: DeviantArt galleries, YouTube AMVs, Google+ circles, Amino and Wattpad pages, Spanish-language Desmotivaciones poster boards, and Flipnote Hatena animation communities. It fuses Western creepypasta folklore (Slenderman, Jeff the Killer) with Eastern anime tropes (the yandere archetype codified by Yuno Gasai, the tragic monsters of Tokyo Ghoul and Elfen Lied) into a single visual grammar of breakdown: a character "snapping" into a violent, manic trance, rendered in black and blood red. Though mocked at the time as a teenage "edgy phase," it functioned as sincere emotional outlet — anger, jealousy, alienation played out through fictional horror proxies — and that sincerity, not irony, is the tone every image in this style must carry.

The style is fundamentally an *editing* style, not a painting style: images behave like layered, over-processed files — splatter photos pasted onto cel-shaded characters, serif captions stamped under black frames, static and compression artifacts left visible as proof of feeling. Even when an image is a clean illustration, it should read as something a passionate amateur made at 2 a.m., not as studio output.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: one warm hue over a void

The rule is monochrome-plus-artery: near-black grounds, bone whites, grey midtones, and a single saturated red family carrying all the warmth and all the violence.

- **Ground:** void black `#050505`–`#141414`; charcoal and ash grey `#2a2a2a`–`#4a4a4a` for shading, fog, static.
- **Red family:** dried blood `#4a0404`–`#7a0a0a`; arterial red `#a00c0c`–`#c1121f` for splatter, clothing, frames; signal red `#e60000`–`#ff1a1a` reserved for glowing eyes, rim light, and text slashes.
- **White:** paper/bone `#e8e8e8`–`#ffffff` for skin, poster captions, flat vector shapes, blown-out highlights.
- **Permitted residue accents:** one desaturated "innocence leftover" hue per image at most — dusty pink hair (`#d9a3b3`), pale teal (`#2ea3a3`), straw blonde (`#e8d8a0`) — always drowned in the red-black field so it reads as something pure being consumed. The Flipnote Hatena lineage additionally licenses a single flat blue (`#1010c0`-range) alongside black and red in flat, shape-based animation-style images.

Value contrast is extreme: blacks crushed, whites blown, midtones sparse. Saturation is suppressed everywhere except the reds. An image with a balanced, full-spectrum palette is not this style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the documentation treats them

1. **The black–red–white monochrome field** itself, often as a splatter-smeared void or scratched backdrop behind a lone figure.
2. **The Evil Variant:** a bright, cheerful childhood-media character (mascot hedgehogs, ponies, vocaloid idols, plumbers' brothers) re-imagined in black and red with hollow or bleeding eyes — innocence explicitly corrupted.
3. **Hyperrealistic blood splatter superimposed on flat 2D anime art** — spatter on cheeks, across white shirts, sprayed diagonally over the whole canvas; the mismatch of photographic gore and cel shading is the intended uncanny effect.
4. **Eye treatments signaling loss of control:** glowing, dilated pupils; pinprick pupils in white voids; or the "shadowed upper face," a hard black band obscuring the eyes entirely. The **Jeff the Killer carved smile** — an ear-to-ear grin sliced into a pale face — is the canonical mouth.
5. **Desmotivaciones (demotivational) poster format:** image set in a thick black frame with a white Times New Roman caption beneath, delivering pseudo-philosophical lines about madness, pain, blood, or "the monster inside."
6. **Broken-data effects:** TV static, scanlines, JPEG block artifacts, chromatic smears, and Zalgo or scratched handwritten text fragments ("leave me," "no more," katakana scraps) scrawled across the composition.
7. **Kitchen knives and cleavers**, held loosely at the side, hidden behind the back, or raised; blade glints are one of the few specular highlights allowed.
8. **Restraint and medical hardware:** chains, bandages, straitjacket-like wrapping, surgical masks and Tokyo Ghoul-derived zipper masks.
9. **Red spider lilies** and other single red flowers (rose in hair, lily in hand) as funeral-flower accents.
10. **Yandere schoolgirl signifiers:** sailor uniforms, hair ribbons, long pink or black hair, cute silhouettes undercut by bloodied hands — the Yuno Gasai shorthand for switching from adorable to homicidal.
11. **Flat limited-palette animation cels** in the Flipnote Hatena manner: hard black shapes, flat red fills, no gradients, melodramatic posed figures.

Motifs observed in surviving imagery but only thinly documented in text, usable as secondary seasoning: checkerboard floors, butterflies as split-personality emblems, small manifesting horns, hooded silhouettes with glowing eye-slits, candle-like single glows in darkness.

### Materials, textures, fidelity

The medium is the compressed image file. Expect JPEG blockiness around high-contrast edges, banding in dark gradients, grain and noise overlays, spray-paint spatter texture, paper-scrap text layers, and soft airbrush shading on characters sitting under hard graphic overlays. Linework stays readable anime/manga illustration — thin clean outlines, large eyes, simplified noses — while everything around it degrades. Some images are painterly with soft red fog; others are vector-flat. Both are valid; neither should look high-fidelity or freshly rendered.

### Subjects

Recurring protagonists: yandere schoolgirls and obsessive lovers; creepypasta entities (bleached-face killers, hooded figures, masked or bandaged stalkers); evil variants of mascots and virtual idols; tragic "monster inside" teens of either gender; corrupted pets and cute bait characters. Figures are usually young, thin, anime-styled, with long hair used as a graphic element — hair spills across the frame like ink. Poses favor stillness with implied violence: arms crossed hiding a blade, crouching on the floor, head tilted in false sweetness, fingers cracked, arms spread in cruciform mania.

### Composition and lighting

Composition is icon-like: a single centered figure staged frontally against a graphic void — splatter field, checkerboard, static, or pure black — like a shrine image or a mugshot of the soul. Bust crops and face close-ups are common; so are full-body poses on empty symbolic ground. Poster frames with caption blocks, diptychs splitting a "cute self" and "dark self," and collage layers of text scraps are standard structures. Symmetry is often broken by one diagonal splatter streak or slash of red.

Lighting is non-naturalistic and dramatic: eyes as literal light sources; red rim light cutting a silhouette out of black; vignettes closing in; hard shadow bands across the upper face; occasionally a single small practical glow (candle, screen) in an otherwise black room. Flat, shadowless graphic lighting in the Flipnote manner is equally correct. Never warm, ambient, or sunny.

## Mood & Values

The style's values are madness as power, trauma as identity, emotional release, rebellion against innocence, and the deliberate "corruption" of childhood media. The mood is melodramatic and *sincere*: manic glee (wide grin, tilted head), cold dissociation (empty glowing eyes), grief-stricken tenderness (bloodied hands clasped like prayer), or panicked self-loathing in scratched text. There is always duality — cute surface, monstrous interior — and the image should let both layers show at once. Irony, parody, and polish are alien to it; the perceived "cringe" intensity is the point.

## Era & Origins

Emerged from the unfiltered Web 2.0 "Wild West," peaking roughly 2010–2016 on YouTube, DeviantArt, Facebook-era poster sites, Amino, Wattpad, Quotev, and Flipnote Hatena, whose three-color palette (black, red, blue) pushed whole communities toward the high-contrast look. It feeds on creepypasta copy-paste folklore, the 2011 yandere boom, Tokyo Ghoul iconography, dark Vocaloid songs consumed as sped-up Nightcore AMVs, and shock-bait games that weaponize cuteness. It reacts against two things: sanitized childhood franchise media (by corrupting it) and restrained, tasteful web design (by over-processing it). Decline came via the 2014 Slenderman moral panic, ~2016 cringe culture, and the 2017 YouTube Adpocalypse, which demonetized gore-heavy edits — so anything reading as late-2010s polish or ironic detachment is out of period.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Lock the palette first: black ground, one arterial red, bone white; desaturate everything else; allow at most one "innocence residue" hue.
2. Build duality: start from a cute or innocent base (idol, schoolgirl, mascot, pet) and add exactly the signifiers that corrupt it — blood on the cheek, knife behind the back, carved or too-wide smile, hollow glowing eyes.
3. Treat the file as an artifact: add compression blockiness, static, scanlines, scratched or Zalgo text, splatter overlays that ignore the illustration's perspective.
4. Let text be a graphic object: black-framed serif captions, lyric fragments, scrawled confessions about madness and the monster inside.
5. Stage the figure like an icon: centered, frontal, alone against a graphic void; break symmetry with one red diagonal.
6. Put the narrative in the eyes: glow them, dilate them, or bury them in a shadow band.
7. Keep amateur sincerity: blunt cropping, excessive layering, and slightly awkward composition are authentic, not errors.

**Variation axes.** Subject: yandere student, creepypasta killer, hooded/masked/bandaged entity, corrupted mascot or virtual idol, tragic monster teen, deceived virtual pet — any figure that can read as innocence corrupted. Composition: full-body shrine pose, crouch, bust close-up, framed poster with caption, cute/dark diptych, text-heavy collage. Lighting: flat graphic, red vignette glow, eye-glow, upper-face shadow, single candle glow. Color handling: pure black-red-white; grey-dominant with red accents; one residue hue against monochrome; flat Flipnote black-red-blue. Mood: manic glee, cold dissociation, melodramatic grief, threatening tenderness, panicked self-address. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Pastel or full-spectrum palettes, warm sunlight, blue skies, cozy domestic light.
- Polished modern 3D renders, ray-traced lighting, clean studio gradients, crisp vector minimalism without distress.
- Photographic anatomical gore: guts, wound close-ups, forensic viscera. Blood here is a graphic-design layer over stylized art, never medical documentation.
- Elegant gothic ornament: baroque frames, lace, candelabra, Victorian mourning props.
- Neon cyberpunk, vaporwave, holographic chrome hues.
- Ironic meme macros and modern caption fonts; text must be earnest serif or handwritten scrawl.
- Crowd scenes or busy narrative backgrounds; the figure stands alone.
- Confident professional typography or balanced layout design.
- Wholesome, untroubled characters with no corrupting signifier whatsoever.

One-glance test: if it looks like a compressed, over-feeling fan edit from 2013 — black void, arterial splatter, one glowing-eyed anime figure, a knife, and a serif caption about the monster inside — it is 2010s Dark Fandom.
