# American Thanksgiving

American Thanksgiving is the visual culture of the American harvest holiday held on the fourth Thursday of November: a large home-cooked feast eaten with relatives and friends, surrounded by a decoration vocabulary so established that retailers, greeting-card printers, parade broadcasters and family snapshot albums all reproduce it reliably each year. The style is therefore both domestic and civic at once — it lives at the crowded dinner table and on the parade street, in the postcard drawer and in the overhead food photograph. Its emotional contract is gratitude expressed as abundance: more dishes than fit the frame, more gourds than fit the bowl, more places set than chairs visible.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The written record fixes the key colours to orange, red, brown and yellow; the visual record shows how those four hues are distributed. The rule: every correct image sits inside the warm quadrant of the colour wheel — golden yellow through pumpkin orange to brick red — grounded in wood-and-crust browns and lifted by cream or ivory. Cool hues may enter only as small food or china accents (the olive of green beans, cobalt flecks in a transferware floral), never as field colour. Saturation is a dial, not a law: folk-bright lithograph colour, faded vintage-film colour, and a neutral linen ground carrying orange only as accent all remain inside the range.

Shadows run brown and umber, never blue-black; highlights run honey, never cool white.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Fall harvest** — the infobox's first motif and the introduction's binding of the holiday to Autumn: heaped gourds and pumpkins, corn shocks and tied husk bundles, wheat and dried grasses, scattered oak and maple leaves.
2. **Turkey** — the roasted bird on its platter at the feast's centre; equally licensed as an inflated parade balloon or a cartoon bird on a plate. The style's protagonist protein.
3. **Pie** — pumpkin pie in a fluted white tin, lattice-topped apple or nut pies, crumb-topped sweet-potato pies; a cut slice with cream or ice cream. Pie is the dessert emblem and the sanctioned single-hero object.
4. **Family** — multiple place settings, hands reaching with serving spoons, pulled-out chairs, guests seated at an outdoor long table; presence may be implied by traces rather than bodies.
5. **Cornucopia** — the woven horn of plenty spilling apples, grapes, gourds and leaves; beside it the bundle of multicoloured flint corn as the harvest's compact emblem.
6. **Pumpkins** — whole field pumpkins with stems intact, cream and striped heirloom varieties, mini gourds used like confetti between dishes.

Secondary motifs documented in the prose introduction: parade spectacle (giant balloons over an autumn city street, banner signage, marching rows), the decorated table as home-and-retailer ritual (brass candlesticks, taper flames, linen runners, stacked china), and the vintage greeting card with script blessing over a lithographed harvest vignette. A cartoon register — a certain beagle chef and his yellow bird from the 1973 animated special — is a sanctioned media branch. Football viewing and charity work are named in the text but have almost no visual canon in the record; treat them as background narrative (a TV glow at the room's edge, a donation box by the door), not required motifs. The written "Visuals" section itself is unpublished, so motif authority rests at infobox and introduction level; texture and lighting grammar below come from the visual record.

### Materials and textures

Flaky crimped lard crust; glossy jellied cranberry and gravy sheen; matte bloom on gourd and pumpkin skin; glassy flint-corn kernels; papery dry husks and straw; woven wicker, rush and cornucopia braid; patinated brass candlesticks; linen, eyelet cotton and damask; floral transferware and speckled stoneware; weathered grey and oiled oak planks; yellowed printed postcard stock; vinyl sheen on parade balloons; film grain over every snapshot register.

### Recurring subjects and compositions

Subject range: the maximalist feast table shot straight down with dishes cropping at every edge; the reverent single-hero still life (one pie, one pumpkin) on a dark ground with props at its base; the baking-prep tableau (filling in an oval dish, halved fruit, flour bowls, rolling pin); the set table before or after the meal seen down its axial length; the harvest bundle or cornucopia as compact still life; the parade street in vertical format; the outdoor long-table dinner under autumn trees at dusk; the centred greeting-card vignette with lettering; the animation still. Density defaults to horror vacui — abundance is the argument — with the dark single-hero still life as the sanctioned minimalist exception. The two dominant camera attitudes are the 90-degree overhead and the intimate eye-level; symmetrical axial perspective covers the table-setting branch.

### Lighting

Four sanctioned modes, all warm: candle or flame low-key chiaroscuro with deep umber shadow for hero still lifes; bright diffuse daylight (north window or open shade) for airy flat lays on white cloth; analog snapshot light — direct on-camera flash or flat overcast daylight, with grain and slight colour cast — for the family-table register; late golden hour for outdoor dinners and window-lit rooms. The common law: warm colour temperature, soft highlight rolloff, honey highlights, brown shadows. Cool studio strobe and blue fill light sit outside the style.

## Mood & Values

Gratefulness performed as plenitude. Values named by the record — American tradition, family, domesticity, gratefulness — appear visually as domestic labour honoured (hands serving, flour on the board, spoons left standing in dishes), generational continuity (heirloom china, old postcards, brass passed down), civic belonging (parade balloons, banner signage), and generosity (extra place settings, bowls filled past need). Nostalgia is structural rather than optional: film grain, yellowed card stock and mid-century parade photographs all assert that this has happened before and will happen again. Warmth and controlled clutter beat sleekness; a lived-in table is evidence of care.

## Era & Origins

The holiday dates itself to the November 1621 Plymouth harvest feast, learned in part from neighbouring Native peoples' corn and foodways, and becomes national under Abraham Lincoln's proclamation. Its visual canon is layered, not single-era: late-19th and early-20th-century postcard and print lithography (corn shocks, script greetings), mid-20th-century colour snapshots and televised parades, the 1973 animated special that licensed the cartoon branch, and 2010s overhead flat-lay food photography that rearranged the feast into a grid. The style borrows folk-print vocabulary from Americana and Historical Americana and seasonality from Autumn, but its centre of gravity remains the table and the harvest emblem.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- **Abundance first.** Fill the frame generously; if you go sparse, go sparse by candlelit reverence (one pie on a dark ground), never by studio sterility.
- **Edible realism.** Food shows cooking evidence: browned crust edges, a carved bird, crumbs, a serving spoon left in the dish, steam or melted butter sheen.
- **Human trace.** Hands, pulled chairs, half-poured drinks, a bracelet at the frame edge; presence may be implied when bodies are absent.
- **Harvest signifier minimum.** Include at least one of: gourd or pumpkin, flint corn or corn shock, wheat or dried grass, autumn leaf.
- **Warm-light law.** Honey highlights, brown shadows, warm colour temperature in every register.
- **Folk ornament permission.** Cornucopia, brass tapers, transferware, script lettering on paper goods are all in-dialect.
- **Medium honesty.** Pick one register — film snapshot, styled editorial, postcard lithograph, animation still — and keep its grain or line logic consistent throughout.

Variation axes:
- **Subject:** full feast table ↔ single pie or pumpkin hero ↔ baking prep ↔ unfed table setting ↔ parade street ↔ outdoor harvest dinner ↔ greeting card ↔ cartoon scene.
- **Composition:** 90-degree overhead maximalist ↔ eye-level still life ↔ axial table perspective ↔ environmental wide ↔ centred card vignette.
- **Lighting:** candle chiaroscuro ↔ bright diffuse daylight ↔ analog flash ↔ golden hour.
- **Colour handling:** folk-bright saturation ↔ faded vintage film ↔ neutral linen ground with orange-red accents only.
- **Mood:** lively family chaos ↔ quiet reverence ↔ civic parade festivity ↔ sentimental card nostalgia.
- **Era register:** contemporary digital ↔ 1970s–90s film ↔ early-1900s lithograph ↔ 1970s animation.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — warm-quadrant palette, harvest signifiers, edible realism, warm light, abundance — not any reference image. A table never photographed in the references, in a room never seen, still reads as Thanksgiving if it obeys these rules.

Avoid:
- Carved jack-o'-lanterns, ghosts, skeletons, candy-corn heaps, trick-or-treaters (Halloween leakage); pumpkins here are whole, heirloom, or in pies.
- Snow, evergreen wreaths, tinsel, Santa, wrapped gifts (Christmas creep); parade imagery stays on autumn streets in fall foliage.
- Cool field colours: blue-grey seamless backdrops, neon, pastel candy palettes, stark monochrome minimalism.
- An oatmeal-and-cream-only palette with latte-and-knit intimacy and no feast or harvest emblem (drifts to Christian Girl Autumn).
- Palette-first terracotta-and-sage minimal interiors or abstract forms with no food and no holiday narrative (drifts to Earth Tones).
- Sleek hotel tableware: chrome, clear glass, unpatterned white porcelain in sparse modern settings.
- CGI-perfect food: plastic gloss, uniform symmetry, no crumbs, no serving wear.
- Fine-dining plating: tweezers-work, microgreens, smear sauces, tropical or summer produce.

## Discrimination Cues

**Against Christian Girl Autumn.** The overlap is real — candles, pumpkins, beige linen — but Christian Girl Autumn is wardrobe-and-beverage intimacy: chunky knits, plaid scarves, foam latte art, porch softness, a palette capped at oatmeal, cream and caramel with pumpkin only as accent, and a subject of solitary comfort. American Thanksgiving is organised around feeding a group: roasted bird, pies in tins, cornucopia, crowded serving dishes, multiple place settings, plus civic imagery (parade balloons, script greeting cards) that Christian Girl Autumn never contains. Test: strip the food out; if the image still reads as one person's cosy autumn, it is Christian Girl Autumn; if the frame collapses without the feast and harvest emblems, it is Thanksgiving.

**Against Earth Tones.** Earth Tones is a substrate-agnostic palette minimalism — matte terracotta, sage, sand and mud applied to interiors, fashion or abstract geometry, often with no food at all, low contrast and austere calm. Thanksgiving is narrative maximalism: glossy cranberry, flaky crust, brass shine, saturated brick red and pumpkin orange that Earth Tones deliberately mutes out, plus lettering, crowds and feast clutter. Test: if the composition is spare and the subject could be any object in a terracotta room, it is Earth Tones; if it is cluttered, edible and holiday-specific, it is Thanksgiving.

**Against generic Autumn and Halloween.** Season-wide Autumn lives outdoors in forests, fog and leaf-peeping; Thanksgiving pulls indoors to the table and the parade route, and carries Americana's folk-print layer. Halloween shares gourds but adds carved faces, candy and spookiness; Thanksgiving's pumpkins stay whole, its mood grateful rather than mischievous.
