# Wizardcore

Wizardcore is the aesthetic of the working wizard: magic treated as a craft, a discipline and a hobby rather than a spectacle. Coined on the 2010s aesthetic-wiki scene (credited to remleeeee), it builds on folklore's magician — Merlin above all — filtered through twentieth-century fantasy fiction and the personal-prop culture of tabletop roleplaying, fandom and meme humor. The text positions it as Witchcore's more masculine-coded counterpart and as a cousin of the Academia aesthetics: the drive is knowledge and experimentation, the taste for discovery and exploration. Its pictures therefore favor the practitioner's scale — a desk, a shelf, a cloak, a staff in hand — over epic landscapes. A strand of deliberate silliness runs through it (the "Wizardposting" lineage of pointed-hat memes), so cartoon frogs in purple robes and glitter-painted dragon figurines sit inside the style alongside solemn candlelit grimoires.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule: a dominant base of dark, muted neutrals (soot black, charcoal, stone gray, leather and parchment brown), pierced by one or two saturated arcane accents (deep violet, indigo-navy, moss or forest green, oxblood dark red), with a small area of light doing the "magic" — candle amber, brass gold, silver-white star print, or a spectral teal/magenta glow from flame, orb or smoke. Approximate ranges: neutrals `#101014`–`#2b2b33`, stone `#6e6a66`, browns `#4b3621`–`#7a5a3a`; violet `#3d2b52`–`#6b3fa0`; navy-indigo `#1b2440`–`#33406b`; greens `#2f4a35`–`#4f6b4a`; dark reds `#5e1f24`–`#8a3033`; glow and metal `#d99a3d`–`#f0c070` (candle), `#b08d3f` (brass), `#d8dce6` (star silver), `#6fd6c8` / `#c857b8` (spectral). Desaturated overcast gray is an accepted alternate base for architecture and costume. Nothing in the style is bright, candy-pastel or neon.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Staffs and walking sticks** — the text's headline motif; commissioned or self-made, gnarled wood, sometimes capped with crystal or metal.
2. **Books, scrolls, notebooks** — leather-bound, old, tattered; open grimoires with stones or crystals resting on the page are the classic tableau.
3. **Cloaks, robes, pointed hats** — plus scarfs, shawls, belts with pouches, trench coats, ankle-length skirts, boots; fashion is "fantastical," wise-but-eccentric.
4. **Glass baubles and trinkets, gems and stones** — crystal balls on stands, orb inclusions of stars or smoke, amethysts, loose stones; the most photographed tier of the style.
5. **Candles, maps and blueprints (weathered), old/Victorian mirrors, hourglasses, globes** — the furniture of study and scrying.
6. **Brooches and celestial jewelry** — moon and star metalwork, layered chains; in the image record this extends to silver star-and-moon embroidery and prints on navy velvet and skirt fabric, and celestial-patterned card backs.
7. **Potion bottles and potion-making** (an activity in the text): apothecary glass in green and amber with ornate labels.
8. **Rustic items, succulents, familiars** — the homely counterweight; a cat keeping company behind a scrying sphere is fully in style. Note honestly: succulents, hourglasses, globes and mirrors are text-listed but scarce in the sampled images; treat them as licensed, not required.

### Materials and textures

Aged and cracked leather, vellum and foxed paper, velvet and heavy wool, tarnished brass, gold and pewter, thick glass and crystal (including glitter suspensions), poured candle wax, gnarled staff wood, rough stone, embroidered metallic thread. A handmade craft stratum is legitimate: glitter, craft paint, resin figurines, sewn celestial fabric — the style tolerates visible amateur making as devotion.

### Recurring subjects

The wizard figure in any register (bearded sage over a glowing page, ordinary modern practitioner, robed silhouette, cartoon animal wizard); the cluttered worktable seen from above or three-quarters; the curiosity shelf or altar of orbs, candles and statuettes; the apothecary shelf; the familiar; castle or academy stonework under gray sky; the casting hand wreathed in glowing flame or sparks; the fashion plate in celestial velvet; the spread of cards or the open book as still-life subject; humorous printed ephemera defending wizardry.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends to crowded still-life accumulation (objects layered edge to edge), shelf and cabinet elevations, macro close-ups on a single orb or embroidered panel, full-length costume poses, silhouettes against a bright window, and community moodboard grids; illustrative modes place a small robed figure against vast starry cloud. Lighting is either a single practical source inside darkness (candle flame, glowing orb, magical fire casting cool light on a hand), diffuse overcast gray for stone and costume, faded snapshot flash for the domestic register, or flat saturated color with bold outlines in the cartoon register. Glow always reads as magic, never as studio lighting.

## Mood & Values

Intelligence, wisdom, ambition, creativity, control, determination, devotion, peacefulness, whimsy. The feeling is studious wonder and cozy secrecy: power held quietly in hands and objects, earned through reading and practice. Eccentricity is virtuous; silliness is devotion, not irony-free kitsch. Even at its darkest the style stays peaceful and curious rather than horrific.

## Era & Origins

Named in the 2010s online; rooted in global magician folklore (Merlin), the pulp Sword-and-Sorcery magazines that cast wizards as antagonists, the 1960s–1980s Tolkien-driven fantasy boom that fixed the cloak-and-pointed-hat silhouette surrounded by energy, and the 1990s onward toning-down of wizards into ordinary people (Harry Potter), which licensed the domestic, everyday-practitioner image. It reacts against nothing aggressively; it gathers — folklore, fantasy media, tabletop games, fandom craft — into a personal iconography of study.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** (1) Dark neutral base first, arcane accent second, small glow third. (2) Pair a knowledge object (book, scroll, map, notebook) with a magic object (staff, orb, potion, candle) whenever objects appear. (3) Age everything: tattered pages, worn leather, tarnish, hand-made imperfection. (4) Let one practical or spectral light source carry the mood. (5) Celestial metalwork and embroidery are accents on fabric and jewelry, not the subject. (6) Whimsy is allowed at any solemnity level, from reverent chiaroscuro to cartoon frog.

**Variation axes.** Subject: sage, modern practitioner, creature or familiar, costume without a face, or no figure at all (desk, shelf, bottles, cards, castle). Composition: overhead clutter, shelf elevation, macro, full-body fashion, silhouette, moodboard grid, figure-small-in-vast-sky illustration. Lighting: candle chiaroscuro, spectral glow on black, overcast gray, faded snapshot, flat cartoon color. Color handling: navy-dominant, violet-dominant, green-gray-dominant, or brown-leather-dominant bases; glow in amber, teal or magenta. Medium: photograph, painterly illustration, flat cartoon, craft photography of handmade objects. Mood: solemn study to playful meme. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Neon cyberpunk or vaporwave gradients, chrome, holograms, glossy modern tech; minimalist white interiors and Scandinavian furniture; pastel kawaii sweetness and fairy-tale dewdrop sparkle; pristine brand-new props; epic widescreen battle spectacle with no practitioner intimacy; skies and constellations as the sole subject with no wizardly object; tweed-and-plaid secular scholarship with zero magic props; modern student clutter of laptops, coffee cups and highlighters; rustic woodland coziness with nothing arcane; cauldron-and-herb kitchen ritual as the only content; gore, dread and haunted decay; bright primary superhero costuming.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Wizardcore |
|---|---|
| Fantasy | Fantasy is the epic parent: battles, quests, vast landscapes. Wizardcore stays at the practitioner's scale — desks, props, cloaks, study — and includes the modern craft-and-meme layer. |
| Fantasy Astronomy | There the sky, stars and cosmos are the subject. Wizardcore borrows stars and moons only as ornament on velvet, metal and card backs, always beside staffs, books, orbs and candles. |
| Cabincore | Shared wood and rustic warmth, but Cabincore has no magic: no grimoires, staves, scrying spheres or celestial prints. |
| Chaotic Academia | Shared mess, different materials: Chaotic Academia is laptops, paperbacks, coffee stains; Wizardcore's clutter is crystals on vellum, wax, potion glass. |
| Dark Academia | Shared reverence for books, but Dark Academia is secular, tweed-and-classical-architecture, lamplit without spectral glow; Wizardcore insists on overt supernatural props and arcane purple-navy color. |

Secondary neighbors: Witchcore shares magic but codes feminine and domestic (herbs, cauldrons, kitchen ritual) where Wizardcore centers staffs, robes and scholarly apparatus; Whimsigoth shares the celestial velvet fashion but drops the practitioner's tools and the study narrative. If an image holds both the costume and the craft — the cloak and the open book — it is Wizardcore.
