# Kogal

Kogal is the uniform-wing of Gyaru: the look of Japanese middle- and high-school girls who took regulation dress code and bent it into self-assertion. The name contracts *kōkōsei* ("high school student"), and the style is defined by that tension — institutional cloth (plaid pleats, sailor collars, beige knits) subjected to gyaru intervention (shortened hems, slouched socks, added bows, bleached hair, decorated everything). It peaked around 1994–1995, inheriting the confident, fun-first attitude of adult Bodikon gals while staying anchored to the school day and the street outside it. Adults wearing the same uniform-as-fashion are technically "Nanchatte Kogal" or "Gyaru Seifuku"; visually the code is identical, so the look reads as Kogal regardless of the wearer's actual age.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** A neutral schoolcloth base — cream and beige knits (`#F0E6D2`–`#D8C49A`), shirt white (`#FBF8F2`), ink black and dark brown for loafers and bags (`#191512`, `#4B2E1E`) — crossed by a plaid field in grey (`#9AA0A6`), navy (`#2C3A55`), lavender-purple (`#8E7CC3`), muted red (`#B4433A`) or green (`#3E5C48`), with pastel accents (pink `#F6C9D8`, baby blue `#BFD8EA`, mint, lemon) arriving through bows, decoden crusts and accessories. Hair supplies the warm counterpoint: bleach blonde (`#E9CE8F`), honey (`#D9A75E`), orange-blonde (`#D78C4A`), light brown (`#A87C4F`), occasionally streaked pink or blue. Skin sits on a dial: deep tan (`#C08552`–`#A96F44`) for the classic 1994–95 register, fairer tones for later and revival versions. Loose socks are always a warm off-white (`#F6F1E6`), never crisp optical white.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the tradition documents them.**
1. **The modified uniform:** plaid or serafuku skirt worn short; beige or cream sweater/cardigan, often oversized, over a white shirt; extra uniforms from other schools worn off-campus.
2. **Loose socks (rūzu sokkusu):** bulky slouched tubes of cotton gathered in folds around calf and ankle, stacked over loafers or platform shoes.
3. **Dyed hair and heavy make-up:** lightened shades against dark uniform neutrals; lined eyes, defined brows, pale or glossy lips; tanned skin in the classic era.
4. **Decoden:** phone cases, iPods, mirrors, lighters, hairbrushes, laptop cases and trinket boxes crusted with resin pieces, pearls, cabochons, bows, studs and whipped-cream glue ridges.
5. **Added neckwear:** bows or ties clipped on even when the uniform doesn't issue them; ribbon colors lean red, pink, navy.
6. **Brand and bag culture:** Burberry check, Louis Vuitton monogram, Liz Lisa, Ank Rouge, Cecil McBee, MA*RS accents; shoulder bags weighed down with straps and charms.
7. **The group snap:** friends in clusters or rows — stairs, crosswalks, sidewalks — arms linked, heads tilted, peace signs raised; the social frame is as signature as any garment.
8. **Magazine-scan gloss:** the Egg/Ranzuki register of saturated print, flash glare, handwritten overlays, katakana callouts, arrows and hearts framing the figures.

**Materials and textures.** Acrylic and wool knit with visible stitch; crisp cotton shirting; polyester pleats holding a knife edge; the doughy collapsed bundle of slouched sock cotton; patent or smooth leather loafers; vinyl and leather school bags; the hard candy crust of decoden — pearl spheres, faceted cabochons, piped glue ridges drying glossy; printed magazine paper grain and flash hotspot; flat cel paint in animated interpretations.

**Recurring subjects.** Teenage girls in modified uniform, solo or in packs; decorated objects as still-life protagonists (a flip phone, a crystal-ball-like ornament crusted with hearts and pearls); classroom and street backdrops; anime and manga characters rendered in the same code.

**Composition and lighting.** Vertical full-body framing that guarantees the sock stack and footwear stay in shot; eye-level street snapshot geometry; diagonal rows descending stairs; tight macro on a decorated object resting on cloth; bright diffuse daylight or on-camera flash with slight overexposure; printed-page saturation when in magazine register; even, shadowless cel lighting with classroom props (desks, chalkboards, cushions) when in anime register.

## Mood & Values

The stated value is simple: *have fun and look cute*. Everything visual serves it — modification as cheeky rebellion against the dress code, decoration density as joy, the group pose as belonging. Kogal projects trend-conscious confidence borrowed from adult Bodikon self-assertion but rerouted through schoolgirl props; it is playful maximalism on small surfaces (phones, mirrors, nails) held inside an otherwise restrained uniform palette. Later manga and anime revivals add a layer of warm nostalgia to the same code.

## Era & Origins

Emergent around 1993 in Japan among high- and middle-school girls emulating Bodikon gals; broadcast to a mass audience by the 1993 TV special *Za Kogyaru Naito*; mainstream by early 1995 with peak concentration 1994–1995. Namie Amuro's 1994 rise cemented platform shoes and tanning as kogal staples. The parent Gyaru culture peaked later (late 1990s–early 2000s), and a post-2016 wave of romance-comedy manga and anime keeps the visual grammar in circulation, which is why flat cel interpretations are legitimate renderings of the style.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Every uniform element must show at least one gyaru intervention: hem height, sock slouch, added bow, oversized knit, charm load. An untouched uniform is not this style.
- Sock geometry is non-negotiable: loose socks are collapsed, folded, bulky at the ankle — never smooth tube socks or neat knee-highs.
- Lightened hair against neutral cloth is the core contrast; keep make-up visibly present.
- Ornament concentrates on small handheld surfaces; large garment areas stay in the neutral/plaid palette.
- Multiple figures behave as friends: touching, leaning, synchronized gestures.
- Pick one medium register — glossy street snap, magazine scan with typography, macro object photo, or flat anime cel — and keep lighting, grain and texture consistent with it.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a solo portrait, a duo or group snap, an object still-life of decoden gear, a classroom or street scene, an anime still, or a magazine spread. Setting may range across school gates, exterior stairs, crosswalks, sidewalks with parked bicycles, karaoke booths, purikura arcades, convenience-store fronts and after-hours classrooms; daytime urban Japan dominates the record, and interior or night scenes are thinly documented, so treat them as extrapolation. Uniform colorway may shift across grey, navy, lavender, red or green plaids and sailor-collar variants. The era dial moves from classic 1994–95 (deep tan, orange-blonde hair, heavy slouch, flip phone) through fairer-skinned revival versions to animated interpretations. Composition may be full-body vertical, three-quarter, group row, overhead object macro, or text-overlaid spread. Lighting may be diffuse daylight, flash gloss, printed saturation or cel flatness. Mood may run cheeky playfulness to wistful nostalgia. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.**
- Pristine regulation uniform with natural black hair, neat knee socks and regulation hem — that is plain seifuku/JK, not Kogal.
- Sleek futurism: chrome, neon, holography, techwear, cables, visors; decorated tech here is candy-crusted, never minimalist.
- Adult nightlife glamour: fur stoles, sequined or bodycon dresses, gold chain jewelry, hostess smoky eyes.
- Lolita bonnets and petticoats, gothic black lace, mori-girl layering, wabi-sabi earth tones, Americana vintage denim and tweed.
- Monochrome capsule-wardrobe minimalism, matte Scandinavian interiors, grunge, sportswear or sneaker-streetwear coding.
- Below-knee skirts paired with opaque tights; ankle socks as the only sock statement.
- Office attire, business contexts, or adult professional framing.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cyber Doll** shares youth and ornament but lives in metal, neon and matte black techwear; Kogal stays in analog schoolcloth, plaids and pearl crusts, and its only "tech" is a flip phone buried under resin hearts.
- **Nanchatte Seifuku** is uniform-as-fashion without gyaru body markers: natural hair, neat socks, regulation-ish hems, light make-up, no tan, no decoden. Kogal always carries at least two gyaru markers (bleach, slouch, heavy make-up, decorated phone).
- **Agejo** is adult hostess-club gyaru: black/gold/red palette, fur, rhinestones, cocktail dresses, nighttime interiors; no uniform, no loose socks, no daylight street.
- **Amekaji** is American-casual vintage: denim, chinos, tweed, earth tones, sneakers and boots; absent pleats, slouch socks and decoden, it is not Kogal.
- **Bodikon**, the parent attitude: tight bandage or one-piece dresses, gold jewelry, club interiors, adult women of the late 1980s–early 1990s. Kogal inherits the tan and the confidence but swaps bodycon silhouettes for pleats, knits and slouched socks on daytime sidewalks.
- **Gyaru at large:** remove the uniform and you have Gyaru; the school uniform and its modification are what make it Kogal.
