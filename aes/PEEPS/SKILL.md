# PEEPS (Gengarcore)

PEEPS is a Japanese street fashion born in the thrift stores of Ura-Harajuku in the late 2010s, built around imported American 90s sportswear restyled into a casual, sporty goth silhouette inspired by ghost Pokémon trainers. It is deliberately anti-brand: outfits are thrashed together from secondhand fleece, jersey and workwear, then upcycled with cut-outs, straps and embroidery. The emotional core is fellowship — the name comes from English slang for "friends," coined by an Instagram magazine launched in 2018 — so images carry a zine-like, coord-sharing, community energy rather than luxury or glamour. By 2020 it was counted among Japan's major youth trends alongside Jirai Kei.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette rule is a two-tone voltage system: a black base carrying neon purple as the charged accent, with monochrome pattern interrupts and metal glints.

- **Base black:** near-black ink and charcoal, roughly `#0B0B0F` to `#1E1E24`. Black should occupy the largest share of any frame — garments, backgrounds, voids.
- **Purple charge:** from deep grape violet (`#5B2A86`, `#6A3FA0`) through mid orchid (`#8A5CC9`) up to electric neon violet (`#9D4EDD`, `#B026FF`). Neon purple is the signature; at least one element (top, socks, plush, print, duotone wash) should sit at the saturated end.
- **Pattern white:** cool off-white (`#EDEDF2`) used only inside black-white patterns — checkerboard, zebra stripe — never as a clean minimal base.
- **Green spark:** acid/neon green (`#39FF14` to `#7FFF00`) as a rare micro-accent — a print flash, a ghost-glow, an embroidery thread. Documented as a key colour but subordinate to purple; use sparingly.
- **Metal:** bright silver (`#C0C0C0` to `#E8E8E8`) for chains, buckles, rings. Gold hardware breaks the style.
- **Optional purple prints:** purple-dominant camo or flame tribal in violet-on-black or violet-on-white.

Allowed handling: purple-dominant outfits on black grounds, or black-dominant outfits with a single loud purple pop, or full purple duotone grades over editorial photography. A correct image may sit anywhere in this range.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Black + neon purple casual sportswear combination** — the defining feature; oversized sweatshirts, cropped tanks, jersey sleeves, never costumed or formal.
2. **Chains and chain belts** — silver chain belts slung low on short bottoms, wallet chains, harness straps with O-rings and buckles, chain necklaces.
3. **Ghost Pokémon, above all Gengar** — plush crossbody bags, grinning mascot prints, stickers, embroideries; Pokémon imagery generally as playful garnish.
4. **Checkerboard pattern** — black-white checks as backgrounds, garment panels, bucket hats, graphic frames.
5. **Zebra stripe** — animal print in strict black-white: bucket hats, belts, pants, socks.
6. **Tribal motifs** — tattoo-style flame and scroll embroidery on sweatbands, detached sleeves and chest prints.
7. **Butterflies** — small white or purple embroidered butterflies on beanies, tanks, prints.
8. **3D kanji** — chunky rendered Japanese characters on socks, prints and editorial layouts.
9. **Swirly face censors / distortions** — spiral scribbles or glitch smears over faces in photos and zine spreads, a privacy-play graphic tic.
10. **Layered-illusion tops** — single garments constructed to look layered: cut-out shoulders, detached sleeves, peeking straps and slivers of skin.
11. **Short bottoms with hardware** — shorts and mini skirts (often pleated) loaded with belts, chains, garter straps and snap buttons.
12. **Silver jewelry** — chain necklaces and oversized rings; silver only.
13. **Socks as the leg statement** — crew socks, zebra socks, kanji socks with sneakers or chunky buckled shoes; a sporty leg, explicitly **not** fishnets.
14. **Sweatbands with tribal embroidery** — occasional sporty head/wrist gear.

### Materials and textures

Thrifted cotton fleece and jersey with a softly worn hand; nylon webbing straps and harness tape; metal chain, buckle and ring hardware with cold shine; pleated suiting fabric on skirts; plush toy pile on mascot bags; raised chain-stitch embroidery (tribal flames, butterflies, gothic lettering); printed canvas and paper for zine spreads. Everything should look handled and reassembled, not pristine or luxury-finished.

### Recurring subjects

Coord flat lays arranged on checkerboard grounds; full-body outfit snaps against plain walls or in shop interiors; accessory close-ups centered on waist chains and harnesses; magazine-style collages mixing photos, character art, kanji blocks and checker frames; chibi or anime character illustrations of PEEPS girls on pure black voids; still lifes of thrifted pieces plus plush and phone cases.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- Flat lays shot straight overhead, garments splayed symmetrically with props (plush, socks, shoes, beanie) orbiting the top.
- Upright full-body frames, face often cropped out or censored with a swirl; emphasis falls on the outfit silhouette.
- Collage grids: one hero figure surrounded by detail insets, text stickers and checker borders.
- Illustrations isolated on black voids with no environment at all.
- Lighting: flat, even, diffuse light for flat lays and snaps; occasional direct flash snap harshness; editorial pages washed in purple duotone; illustrations lit only by their own flat cel shading. No golden-hour, no studio glamour sweep.

## Mood & Values

Playful spookiness meets sporty confidence. PEEPS wants to look like a ghost Pokémon trainer who shops secondhand: edgy hardware and void-black cloth softened by grinning plush, cartoon butterflies and friend-zine warmth. Values are thrift ingenuity, upcycling, anti-brand individuality, and fellowship — clothes worn with confidence among peers. The mood is mischievous and aloof at once: pale, deadpan poses carrying loud purple energy.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s Tokyo, specifically the Ura-Harajuku thrift scene importing American 90s sportswear, with the store Ohpearl as its pilgrimage site. Named in 2018 by an Instagram-born magazine seeking community among its wearers; recognized by 2020 as a major trend beside Jirai Kei and Korean fashion. It reacts against brand-logo Japanese fashion by making secondhand reconstruction the virtue, and against purely dark goth by keeping the silhouette casual, sporty and toy-haunted.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build the outfit grammar first: black casual sportswear base, one loud neon-purple charge, silver chain hardware at the waist or neck, short bottom with straps, socks and sneakers for a sporty leg. Keep it casual — oversized, worn, thrifted — never costumed or couture. Add at most one black-white pattern family (checker or zebra) and at most one purple print (flame tribal or camo) so patterns interrupt rather than swamp. Include a ghostly play element (Gengar plush or grin, butterfly, swirl censor, 3D kanji) in most but not all images. Treat graphic design as part of the style: checker frames, kanji blocks and spiral censors belong on editorial images. Light flatly or flash-harshly; grade editorial work toward purple duotone; let illustrations float in black voids.

**Variation axes.** Subject: flat lay coord, street or shop snap, accessory macro, zine collage, poster with censored face, chibi character sheet, plush still life. Composition: overhead symmetric flat lay, cropped full-body, waist-level hardware close-up, multi-inset collage, single figure on void. Lighting: diffuse even, direct flash, purple duotone wash, unlit black void. Color handling: purple-dominant on black, black-dominant with one purple pop, full duotone purple, green micro-spark optional. Mood: deadpan cool, mischievous grin, community-zine warmth. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.**
- Fishnets in any form — the style explicitly rejects them in favor of socks.
- Frills, bows, lace, Lolita or maid silhouettes; Jirai Kei's pink-black medical ribbons.
- Pastel sickness motifs of Yami Kawaii: bandages, syringes, hearts, pill prints, baby pink bases.
- Heavy biker leather, studs, spikes, band-tee monochrome of Goth Punk.
- Luxury logos, pristine new-looking garments, tailored formalwear; the style is thrifted and upcycled.
- Warm earth tones, beige minimalism, cream bohemia.
- Rainbow multicolor neon; only purple (and rarely green) may go neon against black.
- Gold hardware; PEEPS metal is silver.
- Glamour beauty lighting, sunsets, soft bokeh romance.
- Tan gyaru glam: bleached curls, sparkle makeup, beach-bright palette.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Goth Punk:** shares black and chains, but Goth Punk is leather-, stud- and band-merch monochrome with aggressive posture; PEEPS keeps fleece sportswear, neon purple, plush toys and a casual sporty leg.
- **Gyaru:** Gyaru is tanned, bleached, glam-feminine and sparkle-loaded; PEEPS is pale, dark-based, sporty and ghost-cute with zero glam tan.
- **Jirai Kei:** Jirai Kei pairs black with pink, frills, ribbons and medical-melancholy makeup; PEEPS has no frills or pink, substitutes checker/zebra and sportswear cut-outs, and wears socks where Jirai Kei might bare or net the leg.
- **Yami Kawaii:** Yami Kawaii fuses pastel kawaii with sick-cute medical symbols; PEEPS keeps saturation high (neon purple on black) and its cute element is ghost Pokémon, not illness iconography.
- **E-Girl:** E-Girl overlaps in chains and purple hair streaks but is makeup- and internet-centric (heavy blush, striped underlayers, skate-grunge mix); PEEPS is a thrift-driven Japanese street fashion whose signatures are checker/zebra prints, tribal embroidery, chain-belted short bottoms, sock-based sporty legs and Gengar mascots.
