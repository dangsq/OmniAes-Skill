# Urbancore

Urbancore is imagery of the city as it is actually lived in: streets, rooftops, subways, parking decks, tagged walls, and the dressed bodies of the young people who move through them. It is deliberately present-tense — almost always set in recent decades, in real modern society — and deliberately broad, spanning cityscapes and architecture, graffiti and vandalism, night skate parks, urban fashion, even a picnic on a plaza or rooftop. Aliases circle the same core: Urbanism, Streetcore, Citycore, Ghettocore, Homiecore, Urbex, Metropolitan, High-End Street Style, Streetwear. Like High School Dream, it carries a youthful, somewhat rebellious nostalgia for being a teen in a particular moment; its music lineage is hip-hop with jazz, funk and disco roots, from boom-bap to the late-night-drive calm of Adult R&B.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is a **neutral substrate pierced by a paint/signage accent layer**. Grays, blacks and beiges should hold the majority of surface area in the frame; saturation arrives in small, hard hits — spray paint, road markings, signage, socks, a lamp shade.

| Role | Hex range | Where it lives |

A fog-dawn variant desaturates everything toward pastel gray-blue (#B9C4CC skies, silhouetted towers); this is inside the palette, not outside it.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the text documents them

1. **Cityscape and concrete architecture as protagonist**: skyscrapers, rooftops, alleyways and the gaps between buildings, train stations, worn-down or empty buildings, raw concrete.
2. **Graffiti and vandalism**: tags, throw-ups and burners on rooftop structures, walls, subway cars — occasionally every surface of a room, bed included.
3. **Parking lots and parking garages**, open decks and covered ramps.
4. **Streets and street fixtures**: benches, streetlamps, road signs.
5. **Subways and underpasses**, platforms and tunnels.
6. **Urban exploration**: unpopulated warehouses, barren concrete stairwells.
7. **Dirt and wear**: garbage clutter, dumpsters, concrete rubble.
8. **Streetwear and brand flex**: the dressed body in frame; brand vocabulary from Nike, Jordan, Adidas, Vans, Supreme, Off-White, Balenciaga, Avirex, Schott NYC, Chrome Hearts.

Fashion grammar (text staples): baggy pants or sweatpants, sometimes patched and textile-decorated; oversized brand or vintage brand tees; dad hats and caps; fanny packs; short bubble/puffer jackets; cargo pants; overalls; flannels; plaid skirts; sweaters over collared shirts; sports bras; brightly colored socks; chains and pants chains; belts and lots of zippers; Nike/Jordan sneakers or flat-bottomed skate shoes.

### Materials and textures

Poured concrete with form marks and water staining; spray paint over brick and peeling plaster; rusted metal, chain-link, fire escapes, exterior AC units and hung laundry on facades; asphalt with cracked painted lines; glass curtain wall; puddles, damp rubble, sticker and poster residue. Everything reads touched, weathered, added-to.

### Recurring subjects

Two poles with a middle: (a) the city with no people but never without human trace; (b) the dressed youth in the city — full-body fit shots in empty decks, mirror selfies in stairwells and rooms, candid walks and leans; between them, vernacular interiors such as graffiti-covered bedrooms and tagged subway cars, and rooftop gatherings.

### Composition and lighting

High-angle rooftop vistas across tagged roofscapes toward a skyline; one-point perspective down underpasses, stairwells, alleys, platforms; vertical squeezes between building walls; eye-level snapshots of fixtures and clutter; full-body fashion framing with generous concrete negative space; the mirror-selfie frame. Lighting ranges: hard midday sun under a saturated blue sky; flat overcast scatter; night fluorescents and sodium in garages, platforms and underpasses; direct camera flash indoors; dawn fog softening a skyline. All are native to the style.

### Medium

Predominantly photography — snapshot, film-grade, flash selfie — but the style survives translation into flat vector illustration and stylized 3D/game-like render of the same motifs; a line-clean rendered alley or tram platform still reads Urbancore when palette and motifs hold.

## Mood & Values

Youthful, rebellious, nostalgic: the city as playground, backdrop and proof of presence. Values named by the culture are urban life itself and materialism — expensive clothes and logo fluency — though the aesthetic explicitly does not require wealth, only belonging. Wear is beauty: vandalism is decoration, decay is texture, clutter is evidence. A late-night-drive calm coexists with upbeat rhythmic energy; even staged fits should feel witnessed rather than advertised.

## Era & Origins

Emerges from the 1990s. Its streetwear branch grew from Californian surf and skate culture, absorbing sportswear, hip-hop, punk and Japanese street fashion before going global; its music branch from hip-hop's jazz-funk-disco lineage. It reacts against pastoral and couture fantasies by insisting on the present-tense metropolis, and sits adjacent to Grunge, Hip-Hop, Punk, Skater, Techwear, Brutalism, Industrial Decay, Doomer, Drain, Hypebeast, Baddie and Boujee.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. **Neutral ground, paint accents** — keep most surface in gray/black/beige; let saturation enter as spray paint, signage, road lines, small garment hits.
2. **Human trace always** — tags, trash, chains, puddles, wear; the city is used, never mint.
3. **Real world, recent decades** — ordinary modern infrastructure only.
4. **Snapshot directness** — casual framing, slight tilt, flash blowout, accidental crops welcome; polish only at the high-end-streetwear pole, and even then keep concrete context.
5. **Clothing grammar** — oversized top, baggy bottom, headwear, sneakers; layers, chains, zippers; logos plausible, not mandatory.
6. **Hour and weather are free** — noon blue, overcast, fog dawn, fluorescent night.

Variation axes: subject (pure architecture ↔ fashion portrait ↔ vernacular interior ↔ illustrated render; rooftops, alleys, stations, warehouses, stairwells, parking decks, night skate parks, even a rooftop picnic); composition (vista, corridor one-point, vertical squeeze, eye-level snapshot, mirror frame); lighting (as above); color handling (neutral-dominant with micro-accents ↔ accent-heavy graffiti wall ↔ fog-desaturated ↔ night fluorescent/sodium); mood (rebellious play, nostalgic calm, flex confidence, late-night solitude). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — an empty tagged rooftop, a stairwell mirror selfie, a vector tram platform and a fog-bound skyline are equally valid Urbancore.

Avoid: pastoral or wild nature as subject (greenery only as weeds or ivy on concrete); pristine minimal or showroom interiors; picturesque pre-modern architecture as focus; fantasy or sci-fi elements (magic, creatures, hovering tech, holograms); cyberpunk neon-rain grading and signage walls; couture, formal or business attire replacing the oversized streetwear silhouette; sepia rural or cottagecore softness; sterile stock cityscapes with no wear, tags or litter; traceless uncanny emptiness; studio product flat-lays detached from urban context.

## Discrimination Cues

- **After Hours** is hour-first: night, neon glow, rain-slick solitude, cinematic melancholy. Urbancore is hour-agnostic; daylight concrete and graffiti carry it, and its night scenes use flat fluorescent/sodium light plus street-culture marks rather than neon bokeh.
- **Hypebeast** makes fashion the sole subject — drops, logos, clean or studio backdrops, body as mannequin. Urbancore shares the frame with the city: tagged walls, decks, subway cars; wear and layering over pristine product.
- **Liminal Space** stages empty transitional non-places as eerie and traceless. Urbancore's emptiness is full of evidence — tags, garbage, chains, puddles — and often actual people; lived-in, not uncanny.
- **Shibuya Punk** is Tokyo-specific: Japanese signage density, electric saturated night, punk-collage Harajuku layering. Urbancore is any-city, neutral-dominant, graffiti-and-concrete over signage neon, with day scenes common.
- **Urban Fantasy** inserts the supernatural into the city — runes, creatures, impossible geometry. Urbancore is strictly mundane realism; its most "magical" element is a lit-window pattern or a fog bank.
- Borderline notes: unlike Industrial Decay, decay here is backdrop to youth culture rather than the subject; unlike Brutalism, concrete is lived-in, not celebrated as pure form.
