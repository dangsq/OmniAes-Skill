# Bardcore

Bardcore, also called Tavernwave, is the visual half of a music microgenre: modern pop songs rearranged for lute, harp, flute and drum, and an aesthetic that dresses the whole internet in homespun medieval clothes to match. It is not a reconstruction of the Middle Ages but a loving, memetic forgery of them — manuscript illuminations that dance to cover songs, blackletter mastheads announcing "Ye Olde" versions of contemporary hits, and performers in laced bodices and poet shirts strumming lutes by candlelight. Everything in the style is filtered through age: paper yellowed, pigment muted, leather worn soft, humor worn lightly.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is desaturation-by-age. Every hue behaves as if it were ground pigment on fiber paper or fabric faded by centuries: mid-saturation at most, never neon, never glossy. Grounds lean to parchment cream and sepia; accents come from a small family of muted madder reds, dusty blues and verdigris teals, ochre golds, moss greens and leather browns. Ink is a warm near-black, never pure black; highlights are cream, never pure white. Either a unifying sepia cast covers the whole image, or flat parchment serves as the ground against which the muted polychrome sits.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the style documents them

1. **Medieval musical instruments.** The lute above all, then harp, flute/recorder, frame drum and tambourine, lyre. Instruments appear played, held, or laid out as still-life objects; they are the style's anchor and its most reliable recognition cue.
2. **Manuscript, woodcut and tapestry illustration idioms, with blackletter type.** Illustrations mimic illuminated manuscripts and woodcuts: flat perspective-less space, heavy outlines, figures on bare parchment. Old English / blackletter lettering is the style's house typography for cover art and in-image text, often paired with archaic spellings and phrasing.
3. **Peasant and historical garb.** Simple tunics and poet shirts, laced bodices and corset belts, kirtles and doublets, cloaks, hose, wide-brimmed or feathered hats; wide leather belts, baldrics, pouches, charm necklaces on cords, functional boots. Rugged, comfortable, earthy — reenactment and ren-faire clothing rather than couture.
4. **Humorous anachronism.** Modern pop culture translated into the medieval idiom: meme subjects drawn as manuscript figures, contemporary song titles set in blackletter, modern objects or attitudes smuggled into period scenes. The joke is affectionate, never cynical.
5. **Rustic and tavern-like settings.** Wooden rooms, candlelight and string-light glow, stone courtyards, pastoral outdoor spots; instruments "presented in rustic or natural settings."
6. **Aged surfaces.** Sepia-like filters, paper grain, foxing and stain, woodcut hatching, weave texture — anything that persuades the eye the image is old.
7. **Collage and flat-lay presentation.** Cut-out garments, instruments and trinkets arranged on a parchment or plain ground around a blackletter masthead, often with scattered stars or florals; outfit flat-lays listing a costume piece by piece. This is the community's Pinterest/TikTok presentation mode and a common cover-art format.

### Materials and textures

Linen and wool weaves, brocade and damask on gowns, worn vegetable-tanned leather (belts, baldrics, pouches, boot shafts), wood grain and gut strings on instrument bodies, metal charms and tassels on cord necklaces. In illustrated work: paper tooth, woodcut line hatching, tapestry weave (documented in text, rarer in surviving imagery than manuscript and woodcut modes). In photography: low-fi grain, soft candle flare, the slight murk of a dim room.

### Recurring subjects

Minstrels and bards mid-performance; instrument still-lifes and close crops of hands on fretboards; costumed portraits and mirror or selfie shots of creators in their bard outfits; outfit flat-lays and moodboard collages; manuscript scenes of singing, dancing or processional figures; tavern gatherings; outdoor performers against stone or greenery. Costume photography in ordinary modern bedrooms and halls is a documented community branch — the period clothing carries the style even when the room does not.

### Composition and lighting

Two compositional families. Illustrated work uses flat, perspective-less space: figures standing on bare parchment or a narrow checkered or grassy strip, arranged as friezes, pairs or scattered vignettes with generous empty ground; collages scatter cut-outs around a dominant blackletter masthead; flat-lays grid their garments. Photographic work centers the costume or the instrument — torso crops that linger on belt, lacing and charm necklaces, or full figures in character. Lighting likewise splits: scanned-paper flatness with no shadow at all for illustration, and warm low-key candlelight or tungsten glow for interiors, soft window or courtyard daylight for costume shots. Both regimes lean warm.

## Mood & Values

Bardcore values humor, nostalgia for a fictionalized past, escapism, creative reinterpretation and online community participation. The feeling is cozy, handmade and unpolished — a tavern session rather than a concert hall. Its nostalgia is knowingly false and happily so: the past it longs for never existed, and the style's warmth comes from everyone agreeing to pretend together. The humor is existential-gen-Z winkery, not parody-with-contempt; the medieval frame elevates and cuddles the modern song at the same time.

## Era & Origins

A 2020s internet native. Isolated medieval covers of pop songs existed earlier on YouTube, but the style crystallized on 20 April 2020, during COVID-19 lockdowns, with Cornelius Link's medieval instrumental of "Astronomia," soon followed by vocal adaptations from Hildegard von Blingin' and a wave of other creators; mainstream nods (a Wu-Tang Clan repost, BBC radio play) confirmed its reach. It feeds on lockdown ennui, Gen Z meme humor and long-running internet medieval jokes, and borrows stock characters from adjacent media — the wandering bard of Dungeons & Dragons, Jaskier of The Witcher. It is a reaction to the present: contemporary pop absorbed and re-sung through a fictional past.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Begin from the ground: aged parchment, a sepia cast, or warm dim light. The surface must look old before the subject matters.
- Anchor with minstrelsy: an instrument, a performer, a song made visible. If nothing musical appears, lean harder on manuscript idiom and garb.
- Dress civilian, not martial: peasant and ren-faire layers, lacing, leather, cloaks — no plate armor, no courtly couture.
- Treat type as a motif: any in-image lettering is blackletter or archaic serif with archaic spelling; it belongs to the joke and the age.
- Keep pigment aged: the muted red/blue/gold/green/brown family on cream, saturation capped, no pure white or black.
- Permit one wink: a single anachronism rendered faithfully in the period idiom is the style's heartbeat — optional but characteristic.
- Favor texture over polish: paper grain, woodcut line, weave, leather scuff, photo grain.

**Variation axes (allowed range)**

- *Subject*: instrument still-life, performing bard, costume portrait, manuscript dance scene, tavern gathering, pastoral outdoor player, flat-lay, cover collage — any one qualifies.
- *Composition*: flat manuscript frieze or paired vignettes on bare ground; scattered collage around a masthead; flat-lay grid; centered torso or full-figure photo crop.
- *Lighting*: shadowless scanned-paper flatness; candlelit low-key interior; soft daylight for costume and courtyard scenes.
- *Color handling*: full sepia monochrome at one end, muted polychrome on parchment at the other; both are correct.
- *Medium*: illumination, woodcut, tapestry-like illustration, digital collage, grainy phone photo — the style spans all of these.
- *Mood*: wry memetic humor through sincere cozy nostalgia.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A harp alone on stained paper, a woodcut of dancers with no joke at all, or a phone-photo of a laced bodice in a modern kitchen all read as Bardcore when the palette, texture and minstrel logic hold.

**Avoid**

- Neon or high-saturation modern color; cool blue-white studio light; pure white or pure black.
- Glossy modern materials: plastic, chrome, vinyl, latex, synthetic sheen.
- Clean sans-serif or minimalist modern typography for in-image text; the letterform stays archaic.
- Epic high-fantasy apparatus: dragons, glowing magic, elf ears, castle matte paintings, ornate plate armor as the focus.
- Pristine courtly opulence: rococo gilding, ballroom gowns, powdered wigs.
- Grimdark grit: blood, carnage, gothic gloom.
- Steampunk or dungeonpunk machinery: gears, goggles, rivets, pipes.
- Cool-toned commercial studio polish; flat-lays may sit on a plain light ground but the garments and light must stay warm and handmade.
- Untranslated modern clutter (visible phones, sneakers, brand logos) unless the anachronism is the deliberate, period-rendered joke.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Medieval** (historical aesthetic) uses the same visual vocabulary earnestly: real manuscript reproduction, reenactment, documentation. Bardcore adds the meme layer — blackletter cover type, modern pop references, internet collage and flat-lay formats, and a visible wink.
- **Medieval Fantasy** is sincere worldbuilding: quests, heroes, narrative epic scenes. Bardcore is music-first and joke-aware; if the image could plausibly be cover art for a pop song rearranged for lute, it is Bardcore.
- **Fantasy** is the wider umbrella, free to leave Europe and the medieval period entirely (magic systems, invented creatures, non-historical settings). Bardcore never leaves the European minstrel register and its aged-paper treatment.
- **Knightcore** is martial: plate armor, heraldry, swords, chivalric romance. Bardcore is civilian and performative — instruments, peasant garb, taverns; soft goods and strings instead of steel.
- **Dragoncore** orbits dragons, hoards and scales, often plush or whimsical. Bardcore's whimsy is musical and memetic; dragons are not part of its motif set.

Tie-breaker when an image sits between Bardcore and plain Medieval or Medieval Fantasy: look for the instrument or the act of music-making, the blackletter masthead, and the anachronistic joke. Two of the three present, and the image is Bardcore.
