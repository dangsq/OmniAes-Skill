# Electroclash

Electroclash is a music genre and a total visual package that emerged in the late 1990s between New York, Detroit and Berlin (with Munich's International DeeJay Gigolo Records and the Dutch underground as catalysts), peaked in the early 2000s, and was revived nostalgically from around 2022. It was a self-conscious, ironic resurrection of 1980s electro, new wave and no wave, rebuilt with 1990s techno production and a punk DIY sneer — a reaction against techno's perceived rigidity, substituting songwriting, performance art and humor. Its visual blueprint is the 1982 film *Liquid Sky*: avant-garde New York decadence, bright neon, surreal imagery, androgynous glam. In this style the artwork, the makeup and the stage tableau carry as much meaning as the music; artificiality is the point, and "trashy glamour" is a compliment.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Two color regimes coexist, and a correct image uses one of them (or layers them):

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Ground black | #000000–#141414 | Dominant field, club void, silhouette ink |
| Paper white | #F4F4F4–#FFFFFF | Xerox paper, blown flash highlights, flat vector ground |
| Metallic silver | #A9A9B2–#DCDCE2 | Sequins, lamé, visors, monochrome photo tones |
| Hot magenta | #FF0090–#FF3DA6 | The style's loudest accent; fishnet, type, stripe overlays |
| Signal red | #E60023–#FF2A00 | Cut-paper fields, stencil banners |
| Electric yellow | #FFE500–#EFE900 | Brushstroke type, flat poster fields |
| Cyan | #00D5E8–#00F0FF | Gradient ramps, cool counter-accent |
| Acid violet | #7A2FBE–#B400FF | Gradient terminus, duotone ink |
| Photocopy grey | halftone mid-greys | Xerox degradation, never a soft natural grey |

Rule: in the **xerox/flash strand**, restrict yourself to black + white/silver + at most two neons, applied as flat inks or duotones. In the **vector strand**, flat shapes may carry smooth airbrushed ramps (yellow→orange→magenta→violet, or cyan→yellow), but the shapes themselves stay hard-edged and untextured. Earth tones, muted mid-tones and naturalistic multi-hue color never appear.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the source documents them)

1. **Analog machines as props and jewelry.** Vintage synthesizers, the Roland TR-808, vocoder mics, patch cables and sequencers appear on stage, in portraits and even as fashion accessories. A machine held deadpan is the style's strongest single signifier.
2. **Liquid Sky glam.** Androgynous faces, shaved or thinly drawn brows, heavy slap blush, clashing eyeshadow palettes, severe bobs and asymmetrical new-wave haircuts, plastic visors and DIY plastic eyewear obscuring the eyes.
3. **Retrofuturism.** The future as 1982 imagined it: robotic poses, vocoder-stiff gestures, silver laminate, pixel/8-bit glyphs, mannequin stillness.
4. **DIY punk collage.** Xerox grain, cut-paper edges, ransom-note lettering, zine-style layering of photo, shape and type as visibly separate strata.
5. **Performance-art staging.** Theatrical tableaux, parkas over bare legs, simple shirt dresses, printed T-shirts juxtaposed against fishnet and sequins — austere and overtly sexual at once.
6. **Irony devices.** Borrowed kitsch or institutional glyphs (palms, heraldic lions, stars, zigzag lightning bolts) flattened into sarcasm; deadpan faces; type that contradicts the image.
7. **Revival-era variants (post-2022, secondary).** Trucker hats, velour tracksuits, lo-fi distorted-synth flyer energy. Documented, but optional.

### Materials and textures

Fishnet (gloves, stockings, sleeves), sequins and metallic lamé, satin and vinyl, cheap transparent plastic (visors, eyewear), padded white studio walls. Print textures are as important as fabric: photocopy halftone dots, xerox contrast crush, cut-paper silhouettes, flat vector fills, diagonal stripe overlays, and flash-burned glossy photo paper with blown highlights. Nothing is matte-artisanal; everything reads as cheap, shiny, printed or photocopied.

### Subjects and styling

Monotone singers gripping a mic stand with robotic indifference; androgynous performers in sequined jackets or puffer coats over striped tights; duos posed as frozen tableaux; fashion-editorial portraits with visors and green satin; object still-lifes of synths and drum machines; graphic-only covers with no photograph at all. Styling mixes austere utilitarian items (parkas, shirt dresses, plain tees, uniforms) with overt glam (fishnet, chokers, sequins, blush-heavy makeup).

### Composition

- **Visible layering:** photo cutout, flat color shape and type sit as separate hard-edged strata, like paste-up.
- **Diagonal energy:** tilted banners, 30–45° stripe overlays, zigzag and lightning glyphs, constructivist fragmentation.
- **Deadpan center:** subject frontal, expressionless, framed symmetrically against a void or flat field.
- **Flat field + micro-type:** one overwhelming color plane with small clinical sans-serif clusters parked in corners or edges.
- **Silhouette against field:** black cut-paper bodies or skylines on saturated red, pink or yellow ground.

### Light and camera

Three acceptable lighting worlds: (a) harsh direct on-camera flash in beige rooms or black clubs, producing hard wall shadows and blown skin; (b) club strobe against pure black void; (c) no light at all — pure graphic flatness of vector fields. A fourth, colder variant: white seamless studio flash for glam editorials with silver and satin. Soft window light, golden hour and ambient naturalism are absent. Where the source text is thin — it names neon color and androgynous fashion but not camera technique — the flash-and-xerox handling is inferred consistently across the surviving artwork and press photography of the scene.

### Typography and graphic voice

Type behaves as a shape and often clashes with the image: thin outline script logotypes, bold lowercase grotesques, stencil capitals, ransom cut-out letters, hand-painted brushstroke lettering, and tiny data-style sans-serif blocks. Letterforms are stretched, tilted or overlaid with stripe grids; legibility is secondary to impact.

## Mood & Values

Irony first: deadpan wit, monotone delivery, sexual charge delivered flatly. Hedonism paired with dystopian societal critique — the party is also a smirk at the wreckage. Artificiality over authenticity, trash over taste, camp confrontation over polish. DIY individualism and rebellion against both mainstream fashion and rigid techno purism. The mood ranges from cold robotic disdain to garish celebratory camp, but never sincerity, never warmth.

## Era & Origins

Late 1990s to early 2000s; New York, Detroit, Berlin, with Munich and The Hague as supporting scenes. Term popularized by DJ Larry Tee's 2001 Electroclash Festival in New York; key acts include Miss Kittin & The Hacker, Fischerspooner, Peaches, I-F, DJ Hell, ADULT., Chicks on Speed, Tiga, Felix da Housecat, María Daniela y Su Sonido Lasser. Reacts against techno's rigidity by re-arming 1980s new wave, no wave and *Liquid Sky* glamour with punk irony. Precursor to Indie Sleaze and Bloghouse; revived from circa 2022 in fashion and pop.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build the image in visible paste-up layers (photo, flat shape, type). Choose one color regime — neon duotone on black/white, or flat vector with airbrush gradients — and commit. Light with hard flash or not at all. Keep the face deadpan and the body robotic or theatrically staged. Put one analog machine, visor or fishnet element in frame. Degrade honestly: halftone, xerox crush, flash blowout. Let type collide with the subject. Prefer irony to beauty.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be a performer portrait, a live tableau, an object still-life of gear, a fashion editorial page, a collage poster, or a purely graphic cover with no figure. Composition may be deadpan center, scatter collage, flat field with micro-type, or diagonal constructivist. Lighting may be flash, strobe-void, cold studio, or none. Color handling may be magenta-on-black, red-on-white paper, yellow field with pink stripes, silver monochrome with one neon, or a full gradient vector scene. Mood may slide from cold dystopian to camp exuberant. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Warm earth tones, beige bohemia, pastoral or golden-hour light; candid laughing warmth and documentary sincerity; sleek 3D chrome renders, CGI sci-fi realism, vaporwave pastel grids; EDM mainstage glow, confetti and kandi-rave euphoria; gothic lace or sincere New Romantic ruffles; handcrafted textures (knit, watercolor, macramé); muted Scandinavian minimalism and tasteful corporate flatness; polished airbrushed skin retouching; acoustic guitars and band-room naturalism as the focal prop.

| Bloghouse | Flash photos, neon, mid-2000s dance | Electroclash keeps 80s-revival props (synths, vocoders), xerox print grain and staged deadpan tableaux; Bloghouse is glossier, digital-era, chrome vector logos and candid blog-party energy |

| Minimal Wave | Cold analog synth minimalism | Minimal Wave stays grey/beige monochrome with home-video softness and sincere austerity; Electroclash is neon, camp, loud-type ironic |
| New Romantic | Androgynous glam | New Romantic is satin ruffles, painterly soft light, Blitz-club opulence played straight; Electroclash is its ironic re-read via flash, xerox and vector neon |
| New Wave | The 1980s source itself | New Wave spans guitar bands and MTV pastel polish; Electroclash strips it to black-plus-neon duotone, adds techno-era graphic design and intentional lo-fi degradation |

Quick recognition summary: black + one or two stark neons or an airbrushed gradient; paste-up layers of flash photo, flat shape and clashing type; a deadpan androgynous figure with a synthesizer, visor or fishnet; print grain and flash blowout; irony worn as armor.
