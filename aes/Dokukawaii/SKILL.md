# Dokukawaii (毒かわいい / Dokukawa / Neon Gore)

Dokukawaii is a Japanese aesthetic of the 2010s built on a single contradiction: imagery so hyper-saturated it qualifies as "eye poison" (目の毒 — something so colorful it hurts your eyes yet you cannot stop looking), wrapped around subjects that are sick, toxic, or medically traumatized. The term was coined by the artist Miruki to describe her own morbid neon illustration style, though the look already circulated among Japanese net artists, Harajuku fashion vendors and vocaloid-era album art before it had a name. It is routinely confused with Yami Kawaii and Gurokawa; the separator is color temperature and aggression — where those cousins stay pastel and melancholic, Dokukawaii turns every dial to neon and shouts.

## Visual Identity

### Color language (the rule, not a swatch)

Saturation is the doctrine. Build palettes as *clashes*, never as harmonies: pull three to six hues from opposite sides of the wheel and push each to its neon ceiling, then anchor the chaos with solid black outlines and void shapes. Typical working range: hot magenta `#FF0090–#FF5FD7`, electric cyan `#00E5FF–#35C7FF`, acid green `#39FF14–#00FF7F`, violet–purple `#7A00FF–#BF00FF`, acid yellow `#FFE600–#FFF200`, signal orange `#FF6A00`. Blood red `#E60033–#FF0033` appears only as a wound accent (eye-bleeds, drips). White carries hearts, halftone dots and highlight gloss; pale sickly skin tones sit against bruised undertones. Rainbow itself is a legitimate "color": vertical stripe fields, tie-dye swirls and glitch-split gradients count as on-palette. Acceptable variation runs from full rainbow maximalism down to two-neon duotones (magenta/cyan, pink/green) or a dark purple-black ground lit by acid accents — but desaturation never is.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them

1. **Gas masks** — the headline motif, frequently customized cute: pink shells, heart-shaped filters, candy straps, worn by dolls, mannequins or girls.
2. **Slime** — glossy dripping blobs, oozing edges, jelly spheres, eyeball-in-slime candies.
3. **Toxic chemicals / poison** — pill capsules in clashing neon, bottles and canisters with cute labels, spray tanks, and keep-out / "no entry" caution tape as décor.
4. **Bleeding eyes** — black void eyes weeping flat red tears, blank or stitched-over eyes, spiral or mismatched irises.
5. **Fruits** — named by the text as toxicity bait; in practice they surface as candy-bright cherries, strawberries and ice-cream props (evidence in images is lighter here than for masks and pills).
6. **Medical trauma kit** — bandages, straps and buckles, syringes, IV hardware, X-ray skeletons glowing through torsos, skull friends.
7. **Kawaii substrate** — hearts, stars, polka dots, rainbow stripes, chibi faces, animal mascots (unicorns, cats, pandas), dolls and mannequins with cracked porcelain skin.
8. **Slogan text as graphic element** — short Japanese kana/kanji or blunt English phrases, letter-by-letter in mismatched neons with hard offset drop shadows, cornered like sticker bombs.

### Materials and textures

The surface reads as *print*, not paint: flat vector fills, clean anime lineart with cel shading, thick contour lines, halftone/screentone dots, spray splatter, risograph-style misregistration and glitch offsets. In photographic and 3D-rendered work the same logic becomes material: glossy vinyl and plastic sheen, fuzzy fur and harajuku layering in fashion shots, wet slime gloss, cracked doll lacquer, UV/blacklight-even illumination in installation rooms. Absent by definition: paper grain, canvas texture, watercolor bloom, rust, grunge decay.

### Recurring subjects

Anime girls with one sickly cue (void eyes, bandaged limb, mask dangling from one ear); creature mascots and chibi characters; dolls and wigged mannequins; street-fashion looks; cluttered bedrooms and gallery installations drowned in objects; object still-lifes of pills, slime and masks; skeleton/X-ray figures glowing inside dark fields.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Two dominant structures: the **framed poster** — thick border, patterned stripe/heart/polka field, character bust plus cornered slogan — and the **maximalist clutter** — edge-to-edge stacking of toys, pills, skulls and hardware, sometimes isometric rooms with no empty floor. Diagonal energy (floating, falling, lunging figures) beats static symmetry. Lighting is graphic rather than physical: flat fills with no source, hard flat shadow shapes, or in render/photo work an even glossy studio or UV glow. Naturalistic light, golden hour and soft bokeh are foreign to the style.

## Mood & Values

The core value is **toxicity** — poison worn as identity and as affection. The mood is playful menace: candy-coated sickness, ironic self-destruction delivered with a wink, overstimulation as a form of attraction ("it hurts but you can't look away"). Cuteness is the delivery system, not the message; the bleed, the mask or the pill capsule is the message. There is defiance in it — a refusal of soft, safe pastel kawaii — and a net-native deadpan humor in its slogans.

## Era & Origins

2010s Japan. Coined by Miruki for her morbid neon illustration; pre-existing currents in Harajuku street fashion (vendors such as ACDC RAG, Ningen Allergy, Brokendoll Japan, Invasion Club, Nonokisoyo), in vocaloid/UTAU album art and net music (Dj Technorch, Kairiki Bear's recent work, Nekozuna), and in the illustration of Miruki, Mitsume, Project C.K and Yukaman. Echoes reach anime and manga touchpoints (Coppelion, Dandadan, Jashin-chan Dropkick, Zom 100, HYPERSONIC Music Club). It reacts against gentle pastel kawaii by injecting chemical poison into the cute, and against gloom-core gore by refusing to desaturate.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Saturate first: choose 3–6 neons from opposite wheel sides plus a black anchor; harmony is a failure state.
2. Mandatory poison sting: include at least one toxicity/medical motif (gas mask, pills, slime drip, bleeding void-eye, poison bottle, keep-out tape, bandages, X-ray skeleton).
3. Mandatory cute core: the sick element must sit on a kawaii substrate — round faces, hearts, stars, polka dots, mascots, dolls.
4. Keep gore cartoon-flat: blood as glossy red drips, eyes as voids or stitches; never anatomical realism.
5. Treat the surface as print: flat fills, bold outlines, halftone, splatter, misregistration; in 3D/photo modes use glossy vinyl/plastic under even UV-like light.
6. Compose as poster or clutter: bordered frame with slogan corners, or dense object stacking; avoid an empty minimal center.
7. Text is optional but on-style: short JP/EN slogans, mismatched letter colors, hard drop shadows.

**Variation axes (what may change)**

- *Subject*: girls, animal/creature mascots, dolls/mannequins, fashion looks, rooms/installations, object still-lifes, skeleton figures — any of these, or new subjects treated by the same rules.
- *Composition*: framed poster, full-bleed collage, centered character on pattern field, isometric room, diagonal action pose.
- *Color handling*: full rainbow stripes, two-neon duotone clash, tie-dye swirl, glitch split, or dark ground with acid accents.
- *Medium/lighting*: flat vector, cel-shaded anime lineart, riso/glitch print, glossy 3D render, UV-lit photograph.
- *Mood dial*: from candy chaos and deadpan humor to darker void-eyed morbidity — saturation never drops along the way.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A still-life of neon pill capsules, a fashion portrait in a customized gas mask, or an abstract rainbow-slime poster with no character at all can all be perfect Dokukawaii.

**Avoid**

- Pastel-only or desaturated palettes; beige, earth tones, muted film looks (that drifts to Yami Kawaii/Gurokawa/Pastel Gore).
- Naturalistic lighting: golden hour, soft window light, bokeh, realistic shadow gradients.
- Realistic gore: open wounds, anatomical detail, viscera.
- Pure cheerfulness with no sickly element (that is Colorful Pop).
- Minimalism, generous negative space, a single restrained accent.
- Watercolor, painterly brushwork, oil texture, paper grain, vintage decay, rust, grunge.
- All-black goth palettes or monochrome gloom.
- Gold-leaf elegance, gallery-grade restraint, iconic flower fields (Superflat's high-art register).
- Dreamy soft-gradient vaporwave grids and pastel sunsets.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Separator |
|---|---|
| **Colorful Pop** | Same neon saturation, zero sickness. Dokukawaii always carries a toxic/medical sting or morbid ironic slogan; Colorful Pop stays wholly cheerful. |
| **Gurokawa** | Gore-kawaii in soft pastels (mint, cream, baby pink) with cozy-melancholic mood; Dokukawaii is acid neon, chemical-toxic rather than wound-based, poster-loud. |
| **Pastel Gore** | Desaturated pastel washes, soft dreamy light, gentle wound imagery; Dokukawaii keeps hard flat graphic light and maximum chroma. |
| **Superflat** | Gallery-derived flatness with composed negative space, iconic motifs, no medical-toxic cluster, no slogan stickers; Dokukawaii is street/net-born, cluttered, text-heavy, character-driven. |
| **Akibapop** | Celebratory moe merchandise brightness, clean idol/otaku subjects, no poison; Dokukawaii's cuteness is always laced — masks, pills, bleeding eyes. |
| **Yami Kawaii** (frequent conflation) | Pastel medical depression, handwritten self-harm slogans, subdued mood; Dokukawaii is its loud neon sibling — same medical kit, opposite volume. |

Quick recognition test: if the image hurts your eyes with color *and* makes you notice a mask, a pill, a drip or a void-eye while still reading as cute — it is Dokukawaii. Remove the poison and it is Colorful Pop; remove the neon and it is Gurokawa or Yami Kawaii.
