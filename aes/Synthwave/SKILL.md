# Synthwave

Synthwave is an electronic music microgenre and, just as importantly, a complete visual aesthetic that emerged in the mid-2000s out of the French House scene. It deliberately emulates 1980s film soundtracks, video game scores and pop culture, and its visual half — the "Outrun" aesthetic, named after Sega's 1986 arcade game *Out Run* — is a curated, hyper-stylized memory of the decade filtered through science fiction, action cinema and arcade culture rather than any historical documentation. The result is a high-contrast "neon noir" world: nocturnal, glowing, geometric, and sincerely romantic about speed, technology and heroism. Within the community, "Outrun" denotes the driving-focused, arcade-energy core of the style, "Retrowave"/"Dreamwave" its lighter, vocal-led, hazy pop side, and "Darksynth" its aggressive metal-and-horror fringe; the umbrella visual language described here covers all of them, with the nocturnal neon landscape as the common ground.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is strictly curated and nocturnal. Deep black and indigo-blue occupy the sky, the shadows and the negative space; against that base sit two or three emissive neons, never a full rainbow. A warm sun ramp is the classic accent but is optional in night-scene variants.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Night base | `#05010D` – `#120458` | near-black indigo/deep blue for sky, voids, silhouettes |
| Magenta / neon pink | `#F20089`, `#FF2E88`, `#FF71CE` | dominant glow: grids, suns, haze, type |
| Violet / purple | `#6A0DAD`, `#8C1EFF`, `#B026FF` | sky gradients, mid-tones, mountain bodies |
| Cyan / electric blue | `#00FFF9`, `#05D9E8`, `#01CDFE` | counter-glow: grids, wireframes, rim light, trails |
| Sun ramp | `#FFD319` → `#FF9E00` → `#FF2975` → `#F20089` | bright yellow core grading to deep magenta rim |
| Chrome | `#F5F7FA` → `#9AA5B1` | metallic type and trim, reflecting pink/cyan environment |
| Blood red / black | `#FF003C` on black | Darksynth subgenre edge only |

Rule of thumb: black plus two neons plus an optional sun ramp. An image that adds daylight whites, earth browns or pastel washes has left the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **The Retrosun.** The genre's most popular symbol: a large setting sun composed of horizontal stripes, gradienting from bright yellow at the core to deep magenta at the rim, seated on or just above the horizon. Accepted variations in practice include a smooth unstriped disc wrapped in concentric halo bands, and the inverted case — a dark eclipsed disc with a glowing rim — but the striped gradient sun is the canonical form.
2. **The laser grid.** A glowing neon perspective plane of square cells receding to a vanishing point, reading as digital landscape or cyberspace. It may lie flat as a ground plane, bend into undulating terrain, or wrap into tunnels and corridors.
3. **Wireframe vector geometry.** Mountains, terrain and objects rendered as glowing line meshes, low-poly faceted surfaces, or topographic contour lines — sharp vector graphics over realism.
4. **1980s supercars.** The Ferrari Testarossa, Lamborghini Countach and DMC DeLorean are standard fixtures, typically seen from behind or in rear three-quarter view, speeding away down a neon-drenched road with taillight trails, or drawn as pure neon outline.
5. **Palm trees.** Silhouetted or neon-edged palms lining coastal highways and horizon edges.
6. **Chrome typography.** Beveled metallic lettering and airbrushed script fonts borrowed from 1980s Italo Disco album covers; also neon-tube lettering. Titles like brand logos, single words ("ACCELERATE", "OUT DRIVE") or band names sit over the scene or on flat black.
7. **Night city and starfield.** Distant skyline silhouettes with lit windows, star noise, nebula washes and horizon glow filling the upper sky.
8. **VHS/CRT accents.** Tracking static, scanlines and slight chromatic fringing as a *sparingly* overlaid layer. Note the tension: the core finish is polished and high-definition; analog degradation is a garnish, not the substrate.

### Materials and textures

Chrome and polished metal; glassy neon tube; glossy car paint; wet-look reflective ground planes that mirror the sun and grid; airbrush-smooth sky gradients; crisp vector line work; low-poly facets; fine star grain and optional scanline overlay. Everything reads as either emissive light or mirror-bright surface — matte, porous or organic materials are absent.

### Recurring subjects

Empty grid landscapes with a sun (the most common composition of all); a car on a road toward the horizon; coastal or desert highway with palms; night cityscape at the end of the grid; mountain passes of wireframe or faceted rock; emblem/badge compositions (a geometric device centered on flat black); typography-led covers; occasionally a lone driver figure or arcade/CRT object. The subject hierarchy is landscape first, machine second, human a distant third.

### Composition and lighting

One-point perspective aimed at a vanishing point on a low horizon, leaving most of the frame to sky; centered or near-centered sun; strong bilateral symmetry or a single road diagonal; foreground grid rushing toward the viewer; silhouetted mid-ground bands (mountains, palms, skyline); starfield or gradient sky above. Lighting is entirely emissive: the sun, the grid, neon signs and taillights are the light sources. There is no external key light, no softbox, no daylight. Expect bloom and atmospheric haze around every emitter, rim light carving silhouettes, specular streaks on chrome, and mirror reflections on the ground plane. The time of day is permanently dusk-to-midnight.

## Mood & Values

Nostalgia, retrofuturism, escapism and cinematic atmosphere are the style's declared values. Synthwave is an *earnest* celebration: it romanticizes the "cool" of the 1980s — heroes, speed, technology — and aims for cinematic escapism rather than irony or critique. The emotional register is the solitary night drive: urgency and forward motion (the visual equivalent of a rolling sixteenth-note bassline) combined with a melancholic, romantic glow. Even its dreamier Dreamwave edge keeps the sincerity, swapping adrenaline for haze. Coolness, polish and heroism are virtues here; grime, sarcasm and decay are not.

## Era & Origins

Synthwave originated in mid-2000s France as an offshoot of the French House scene, with pioneers Kavinsky ("Testarossa Autodrive", 2006), David Grellier (College) and the Valerie Collective, alongside Justice's circle, blending Italo Disco with the cinematic scores of John Carpenter, Vangelis and Tangerine Dream. The visual language reaches back through the 1986 Sega arcade game *Out Run* and 1980s action/sci-fi cinema. Mainstream breakthrough came with the film *Drive* (2011) — cementing the neon-noir driving imagery — and the game *Hotline Miami* (2012), which pushed the darker Darksynth edge; later permeation includes *Stranger Things*, *Kung Fury*, *Far Cry 3: Blood Dragon* and The Weeknd's "Blinding Lights". Subgenres span Outrun, Retrowave/Dreamwave, Darksynth, Chillsynth and Sovietwave (the last two are musically defined; their distinct visual identities are thinly documented).

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Build the world from the horizon up: gradient night sky, sun or starfield above, glowing perspective plane below.
2. Light only from emitters — sun, grid, neon, taillights, screens. Add bloom and haze around each.
3. Keep palette discipline: black base, two or three neons, optional yellow-to-magenta sun ramp, chrome neutrals.
4. Prefer geometry over realism: wireframe, low-poly facets, contour lines, sharp vector edges.
5. Finish polished and high-definition: smooth airbrush gradients, crisp lines, clean reflections. Analog static is an accent layer at most.
6. Imply motion with light: taillight streaks, speed lines, perspective rush toward the vanishing point.
7. If type appears, make it chrome-beveled or neon-tube, in airbrush script or wide geometric capitals.
8. Keep the mood earnest and cinematic — a romanticized memory, never a parody.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range from pure empty landscape, to car-and-road, to cityscape, to emblem-on-black, to typography cover, to non-automotive subjects (a wireframe animal, a lighthouse, a train, a portrait) rendered in the same grammar. Sun treatment may be striped, smooth with halo rings, eclipsed dark disc with rim glow, half-hidden behind mountains, or absent in a pure starfield night. Grids may be magenta, cyan, violet or blue; flat, undulating, tunneled, or replaced by contour-line terrain. Composition may be symmetric one-point, diagonal road, centered badge on black, or type-led; crops may be wide landscape or vertical poster. Lighting may sit anywhere from pink dusk to deep starlit midnight; mood may tilt energetic (Outrun), hazy-romantic (Dreamwave) or, at the subgenre edge, red-black aggressive (Darksynth). Medium may be 3D render, flat vector illustration, album sleeve or CRT-styled frame. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image** — a wireframe whale cruising a magenta grid beneath a striped sun is fully synthwave even though no reference shows it.

**Avoid.**
- Daylight, overcast skies, naturalistic white sun, weather realism.
- Pastel faded palettes, glitch corruption, marble busts, Windows 95 UI, ironic mall/consumer clutter (that is Vaporwave).
- Beige or olive CRT machines, chunky analog consoles, paper printouts, utilitarian spaceship interiors (Cassette Futurism).
- Rain-slick street-level megacity grime, crowds, corporate billboards, cybernetic implants, dystopian critique (Cyberpunk).
- Memphis squiggles, terrazzo patterns, primary-color plastic shapes on cream or white backgrounds.
- Earth tones, organic textures, foliage detail, wood, stone, hand-painted brushwork, watercolor.
- Modern technology: smartphones, flat UI, white LED lighting, contemporary car designs.
- Heavy VHS degradation that destroys the polished finish; static and scanlines only as light overlay.
- Occult pentagrams and slasher gore unless deliberately invoking the Darksynth subgenre.
- Neutral studio backdrops, softbox lighting, photographic matte surfaces.

## Discrimination Cues

**Cassette Futurism** shares retro-tech nostalgia but lives indoors among utilitarian hardware: beige/olive cathode monitors, chunky buttons, analog readouts, lived-in spaceship panels. Synthwave lives outdoors at night on glowing grids with chrome and neons; if the frame is full of cream-colored consoles and printed labels instead of horizon and sun, it is Cassette Futurism.

**Cyberpunk** is also neon-at-night, but street-level, dense and dirty: rain, crowds, megacorp signage, implants, social decay. Synthwave is open horizon, empty road, clean vector geometry and sincere romance — no grime, no critique, no crowd.

**Italo Disco** is the genuine 1980s source: airbrushed album covers with human performers, beach and club scenes, warmer pastel daylight or studio light, chrome script included. Synthwave borrows the chrome script but replaces the photo-studio warmth with nocturnal digital grids, wireframes and a post-2000s render finish.

**Laser Grid** as a standalone aesthetic isolates the glowing grid plane itself — often monochrome, stage-laser or pure geometric field, in any color, without narrative props. Synthwave always embeds the grid in a curated nostalgia package: sun, horizon, palms, cars or chrome type, in the magenta-cyan-violet family.

**Memphis Design** is 1980s postmodern surface design: flat bright primaries, black-and-white squiggles, terrazzo and laminate patterns on products, furniture and posters. It has no night, no glow, no perspective horizon — exactly the daylight playfulness synthwave's neon noir excludes.

**Vaporwave** (the most common confusion) differs in intent and finish: pastel and faded rather than high-contrast neon; lo-fi, glitched and ironic rather than polished and earnest; marble busts, mall muzak ephemera and Windows 95 UI rather than wireframes, chrome and supercars. Where synthwave celebrates, vaporwave satirizes or mourns.

**Quick recognition check:** black night sky + emissive magenta/cyan grid or wireframe + horizon sun (striped or haloed) + chrome/neon type or wedge supercar + polished cinematic bloom = Synthwave. Remove the night and the glow and the style collapses.
