# Fantasy

Fantasy is a literary genre turned visual culture: imagined worlds drawn from mythology and folklore, most famously in its epic high-fantasy mode with medieval European inflections, but broad enough to hold low, urban, nautical and medieval subgenres alike. As a visual aesthetic it aggregates the look of that tradition — Tolkien and Narnia bookshelves, Dungeons & Dragons tables, Zelda and Elder Scrolls screens, Ghibli and Disney frames — plus straight nature photography of the kind of woods, cliffs and ruins where such stories could happen. Its practice is participatory: reading under a tree, writing original tales, archery, horse riding, embroidery, tabletop sessions till midnight. The visual language therefore spans two mediums comfortably — soft atmospheric photography and painterly digital art — united by one conviction: the natural world is alive, old, and slightly enchanted.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** The base is living green and old stone: moss and pine greens (roughly #2E4B3A to #6E8B5A), lichen grey-greens, mist whites and fog greys (#C9D2CC to #E6EBE6), weathered stone (#6F6A5E), and earth browns of leather, cork, corduroy and book cloth (#4E3A28 to #7A5C3E). Over this base sits one warm light family — sunrise amber, sunset gold, candle glow (#D99A3E to #F2C879, deepening to dusk browns like #8A5A33) — or, in the more saturated painterly register, jewel-tone accents for crystals, water and magic light (teal #3F7F7A, amethyst #6D4C7D, rose #C46A7A). Parchment cream (#E9DDBC) bridges paper and light. Both a muted, naturalistic photographic treatment and a vivid storybook-painterly treatment are correct, provided distance desaturates into haze: aerial perspective is non-negotiable.

**Light.** Volumetric and directional-soft: mist swallowing a cliff castle, god-rays slanting through tall tronto forest floors, backlit silhouettes against a burning gold sky, sunlit glades with floating pollen or fairies, low overcast diffusion on ruins. Object scenes borrow candle-warm low light. Shadow exists for depth and mystery, never as menace.

**Motifs, ranked by how strongly the text documents them.** First, enchanted nature: sunlit landscapes, forests, waterfalls, mist, sunrise and sunset. Second, built heritage: castles, towers, ruins of old buildings, treehouses. Third, portable magic — the small-object kit: potions and poisons, tiny potion bottles, interesting keys, books old and new, parchment scrolls, old maps, crystals and gemstones, handmade wands, tiny chests, swords and daggers. Fourth, creatures: dragons above all, then horses, wolves and animal companions generally (stags, crows, pond life). Fifth, costumed figures: long cloaks, loose hooded coats with inner pockets, white poet shirts with puff sleeves, linen and princess dresses, ball gowns, corsets, crowns and tiaras, leather belts and brass accessories, combat or leather boots, knives at the hip. Note honestly: crystals, wands and maps are text-listed but under-represented in the sampled imagery, which favours forests, castles, creatures and the bottle-and-key still life; treat the object kit as canonical even when a given sample lacks it.

**Materials and textures.** Moss on stone, lichen on bark, wet leaf litter, worn leather grain, patinated brass, frayed cloth book spines, parchment fibre, cork, linen weave, translucent wing membrane, keratin scale and spike, fog itself as a texture that erases edges.

**Composition.** Scale and humility: a small figure before a vast glade, giant tree or cliff; vertical thrust for towers, cliffs and waterfalls; receding forest perspective terminating in light; silhouette against a luminous sky; intimate foreground still life laid on mossy ground; macro texture studies of scale or spines. Depth is always staged in layers — near texture, mid subject, far haze.

## Mood & Values

Wonder, yearning and freespirit: the sense that a road leads somewhere impossible. The stated values are adventure, creativity, ambition, companionship and freespirit, and the images honour them — travelling riders, howling pairs, dancing fairies, a reader's shelf of worn volumes. Nature is an ally and a character, not a backdrop; craft is virtuous (embroidery, weaving, handmade wands); storytelling is sacred, descending from oral tradition. The emotional register is hopeful escapism: melancholy is allowed (ruins, mist, dusk), despair is not.

## Era & Origins

Rooted in oral tradition, mythology and folklore, codified into written literature and then into the twentieth century's epic high fantasy; disseminated globally through film trilogies, Disney and Ghibli animation, tabletop roleplaying (Dungeons & Dragons), and RPG video games from Zelda and Final Fantasy to The Witcher and Elden Ring. As an internet aesthetic it reacts against industrial modernity by re-enchanting the world: every image is an argument that magic survived somewhere off the map.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Atmosphere before subject: include a volumetric medium — mist, god-rays, haze, golden backlight — in nearly every frame.
2. Pre-industrial materiality only: hand-worked leather, linen, brass, parchment, mossy stone, cork, iron.
3. One numinous element: a dragon, a fairy glow, an impossible tower, an uncanny animal — or at minimum an implied spell (potion, wand, key, scroll).
4. Layered depth: crisp near texture, soft far distance.
5. Scale humility: humans small, absent, or dwarfed by tree, cliff or beast.
6. Cool base, warm light: green/stone ground answered by amber/gold illumination or a jewel accent.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a sweeping vista, a ruin study, a creature portrait, an object still life, a costumed figure, a mounted archer, a fairy glade, or even an urban-fantasy intrusion (a dragon on a domed rooftop) since urban fantasy is a documented subgenre. Composition may be vertical-monumental, wide-vista, intimate-macro or silhouette. Lighting may be overcast mist, dawn rays, sunset backlight or candle glow. Colour handling may range from muted photographic naturalism to saturated painterly storybook. Mood may be serene wonder, heroic epic, cosy craft or ruin-melancholy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Modern technology and materials (cars, power lines, phones, plastic, chrome, neon signage, contemporary sportswear); flat even studio or flash lighting; airless vacuum-sharp renders with no haze; neon, cyber or vaporwave palettes; gore, rot and oppressive dread (that crosses into Dark Fantasy); near-monochrome grim desaturation; sci-fi hardware (lasers, spacecraft); clean minimal vector flatness; glossy modern interiors and suburban sprawl; anachronistic branding.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Bardcore** centres on minstrelsy — lutes, taverns, illuminated manuscripts, merry rustic medieval domesticity. Fantasy may contain a bard, but its heart is wild nature, magic objects and creatures; remove the music and Fantasy survives, remove the magic and Bardcore remains.
- **Dark Fantasy** uses the same kit (cloaks, castles, mist) but lit by dread: desaturated near-monochrome, corruption, grotesque monsters, shadow as threat. In core Fantasy the mist glows with promise, gold breaks through, and wonder outweighs menace.
- **Dragoncore** makes dragons the sole obsession — scales, eggs, hoards filling the frame. Fantasy treats the dragon as one citizen among forests, books and potions; if the image still reads correctly with the dragon removed, it is Fantasy.
- **Knightcore** is armour, heraldry, chivalric ritual and the castle as garrison. Fantasy's castle is enchanted home or romantic ruin; its sword-bearer serves a magical story, not a martial code, and arcane elements are present.
- **Wizardcore** is scholarly interior magic — robes, stacked grimoires, candlelit studies, staves, arcane diagrams. Fantasy takes magic outdoors into glades, waterfalls and wild woods; Wizardcore stays library-bound.
