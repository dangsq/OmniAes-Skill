# Midwest Emo

Midwest Emo (Second Wave Emo, Midwestern Emo, Emo Revival) is the aesthetic of a DIY music scene: born in the 1990s American Midwest as a melodic, introspective answer to Washington D.C. emotional hardcore, and resurrected in the early 2010s "fourth wave" revival. Because the scene prized comfort, accessibility and raw feeling over promotion and polish, its visual culture is the look of *unstyled life documented cheaply*: flannel and khakis, friends piled onto a couch or a curb, suburban houses at dusk, trains and gravel and parking lots, walls papered with show flyers, guitars held like furniture. The low-budget ethos is the aesthetic: word-of-mouth audiences, basement shows, faded prints, awkward framing. Nothing here is meant to look like an advertisement, and that is exactly why it reads as authentic.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule: an earth-tone base, faded. Every hue sits 25–40% desaturated, as if printed on cheap paper or scanned from an old photograph. Saturated color is permitted only as small incidental *sources* — a poster, a taillight, a sodium lamp — never as the governing tone of clothing or sky (the single exception being dusk rose-violet skies).

Black appears as ordinary clothing black (a graphic tee, sneaker rubber), never as a head-to-toe uniform.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how firmly the culture documents them)

1. **Suburban domesticity** — vinyl-sided houses, shingle roofs, wooden fences, lawns, driveways, cul-de-sacs, power lines, dusk skies over rooftops. The suburb is the stage and often the subject even with no people in frame.
2. **Friendship clusters** — groups of friends in a row against a wall, piled on a couch, sitting on porch steps or a curb; arms over shoulders; shared cans and drinks. Friendship and acceptance are the scene's stated values.
3. **Instruments and DIY music infrastructure** — acoustic and electric guitars (Fender lineage), amps, practice spaces, walls plastered edge-to-edge with show flyers, posters and handwritten set lists; zine and flyer print matter.
4. **Sports and skate culture** — basketball shorts, baseball caps, backyard footballs, skateboards; sport as recreation, never as stadium spectacle.
5. **College and everyday routines** — dorm rooms, campus walks, laundry rooms, cheap apartment kitchens.
6. **Nostalgia artifacts** — faded prints, white photo borders, soft early-digital or film resolution, childhood suburbs revisited.
7. **Weed and drinks, understated** — a haze, a can, nothing glamorized.
8. **Solitary melancholy** — one person on railroad ballast, an empty room, a lit window at dusk; failed romance and introspection.
9. **Wardrobe signifiers** — plaid or tartan flannel and madras shirts (unbuttoned over a plain or graphic tee, rarely tucked), polos, hoodies and sweaters, blue jeans and chinos cuffed at the ankle, khaki or basketball shorts, dad hats, beanies, baseball caps, prescription glasses worn plainly, white or black Converse, Vans and New Balance sneakers (checkered Vans acceptable), casual boots.

Note honestly: sports themes, college life and skate culture are strongly documented in the scene's writing but appear only thinly in surviving imagery; treat them as licensed subjects, not required props. Brass instruments (trumpets, trombones) belong to the revival's "jazzy" wing and are a legitimate, if rarer, motif.

### Materials and textures

Brushed flannel and madras cotton, worn denim, wool-knit beanies, canvas-and-rubber sneakers, spruce guitar tops, paper flyers held by tape and thumbtacks, gravel ballast, asphalt, chain-link, weathered wood fence, vinyl siding, corduroy couch upholstery, basement concrete. Photographic texture is part of the material palette: film grain or early-CCD noise, motion blur, vignetting, slight over- or underexposure, flash falloff into dark rooms, scanned-print softness.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Snapshot awkwardness**: off-center subjects, cropped limbs, straight-on deadpan group rows, candid mid-motion blur; competence is not the goal, presence is.
- **Deadpan staged portraits**: friends arranged on a couch or against a plain wall with flat, unfussy staging — the band-photo mode.
- **Empty-landscape frames**: houses, tracks, parking lots and yards shot straight, horizon high or low, people optional.
- **Cluttered interiors**: flyer walls, basements and bedrooms filled edge-to-edge.
- **Lighting**: overcast flat daylight; direct on-camera flash at night or indoors with hard falloff; dusk and blue-hour ambient punctuated by sodium lamps, lit windows and taillights; dim warm window light. No golden-hour glow, no studio strobe glamour (the plain-backdrop promo look is the one controlled exception).

## Mood & Values

Sincerity over irony, comfort over style, feeling over production. The mood is bittersweet: melancholy held inside warmth — sadness among friends, nostalgia for places that were never impressive to begin with, introspection delivered with a twinkle rather than a scream. The scene values friendship, acceptance and DIY self-sufficiency; it is unfashionable and inoffensive *by design*, relegating artistic expression to the music while the visuals stay deliberately plain. Even the revival's surrealist streak is gentle absurdity, not cynicism.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in the 1990s across the Midwestern United States (Chicago, Milwaukee, Madison, Minneapolis axes) with sibling scenes in Texas, the Pacific Northwest and the East Coast; founded by bands such as Cap'n Jazz, Mineral, The Promise Ring and American Football as a melodic, "twinkly" arpeggiated response to D.C. emo-core's abrasion. Sidelined in the 2000s by label-favored "mall emo," it returned in the early 2010s revival (fourth wave) with math-rock riffs, extended chords, brass and surrealist lyrics. Visually this yields two valid period flavors: 1990s film snapshots and 2010s lo-fi early-digital revival imagery — both belong to the style.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Start from the mundane: a suburb, basement, backyard, rail line, dorm, parking lot.
2. Dress people for comfort, not effect: plaid over a tee, cuffed jeans, canvas sneakers, beanie or dad hat, glasses.
3. Fade everything: earth-tone base, desaturated, with at most one small saturated light source or printed object.
4. Shoot like an amateur: point-and-shoot framing, flash or overcast light, grain, slight blur, imperfect crops.
5. Put feeling in the frame: friends present, or an absence that aches. Sincerity is non-negotiable.
6. Leave one trace of music or DIY life — a guitar, flyer, amp, skateboard — unless the image is a pure suburban landscape.
7. Stay anti-aspirational: no luxury, no grooming gloss, no styling.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: solo figure; group of friends; no people at all (house, tracks, yard, lot); still life (guitar on a couch, flyer wall, cans on a table); a dog in a yard; a trumpet on a porch step.
- *Composition*: candid snapshot; deadpan row or couch portrait; empty landscape; cluttered interior; motion-blurred candid; centered or off-center, level or slightly tilted.
- *Lighting*: overcast day; on-camera flash at night; dusk blue hour; sodium streetlamp; dim interior window light.
- *Color handling*: fully faded film; flash-lit warm foreground against cool dark; twilight violet-rose sky over teal shadow — always muted overall.
- *Mood*: warm camaraderie at one end, solitary ache at the other, gentle surreal juxtaposition allowed in between.
- *Medium*: 35mm print scan, early digital point-and-shoot, zine or flyer print layer.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Third-wave "mall emo" signifiers: black skinny jeans, studded belts, eyeliner, dyed side-swept fringes, head-to-toe band tees.
- Glossy saturated commercial color, HDR crispness, lens-flare sun, golden-hour romance.
- Urban ruin, brutalist blocks, anonymous hooded isolation, total gray nihilism.
- Meme text overlays, ironic edgelord signage, deliberate ugliness as a joke.
- Curated vintage chic, symmetrical pastel framing, minimalist café interiors.
- Catalog-clean anonymity with no emotional or musical stakes.
- Luxury brands, manicured grooming, fashion-editorial posing.
- Neon cyberpunk, tropical vacation settings, epic drone vistas, stadium sports spectacle.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Emo (third wave)**: Emo is black-on-black, studs, eyeliner, night clubs and malls, glossy hair. Midwest Emo is daylight suburbia, plaid and khaki, bare faces, matte faded color; its darkness is dusk, not mascara.
- **Vs Doomer**: Doomer is monochrome urban desolation, a lone hooded figure, hopeless night. Midwest Emo keeps faded *warmth* — khaki, brick, amber windows — and keeps friends, or their trace; bittersweet, not nihilistic.
- **Vs Incelcore**: Incelcore weaponizes squalor with ironic hate-meme overlays and provocation. Midwest Emo's clutter is lived-in and tender; it has no meme text and no cruelty.
- **Vs Indie**: Indie curates its vintage — clean compositions, tasteful pastel grading, intentional retro. Midwest Emo's vintage is accidental: awkward frames, unfashionable clothes, feeling outranking taste.
- **Vs Normcore**: Normcore shares the wardrobe shell (plain tees, jeans, New Balance) but stays blank, commercial and anonymous. Midwest Emo adds the soul: plaid, flyers, instruments, suburban specificity, and visible emotion. Same clothes, different heartbeat.

**Quick recognition hit**: plaid flannel + cuffed jeans + canvas sneakers, a dusk suburb or flyer-covered wall, film grain and sincere awkwardness — that combination is Midwest Emo and nothing else.
