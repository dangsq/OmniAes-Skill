# Cryptidcore

Cryptidcore romanticizes the amateur investigator: the friend group with a map, a Polaroid camera and a thermos, driving out to the state forest because something in the local paper said the lights came down behind the ridge. It grew out of cryptozoology fandom and the kitschy American tourist-trap culture that sells Bigfoot keychains at the gas station, and it was named on Tumblr in 2014 out of the moodboard culture surrounding *Twin Peaks*, *The X-Files*, *Gravity Falls*, *Unsolved Mysteries* and *Welcome to Night Vale*. Its visual memory is deliberately anachronistic: it dresses in the 1970s–90s of tabloid sightings, VHS recordings and *Kolchak: The Night Stalker*, even when made today. The stance is not terror but curious, cozy dread — the watcher in the trees is half frightening, half friend; some fans would rather be the cryptid than hunt it.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette (rule, not recipe).** The base is always muted, desaturated, analog-faded: bark and leather browns (`#4A3826`–`#7A5C3E`), khaki and dry-grass tans (`#B9A47C`–`#D6C69A`), moss and pine greens (`#4C5A3A`–`#2E3A2C`), overcast fog grays and gray-blues (`#8C97A2`–`#B9C0C7`), dusk lavender-gray (`#9A93AC`), newsprint off-white (`#E8E2D4`), and night blue-blacks (`#101418`–`#1C2430`). Into this earthy field goes exactly one register of uncanny accent, kept small and local: glow-in-the-dark phosphor green (`#86FF9E`, `#B8FF4D`), marker magenta (`#E0559A`), tabloid rust-red (`#A33B2A`), candle or neon amber (`#FFB347`), or the cyan/red fringing of VHS chromatic aberration. Accents appear as emissive points (glowing eyes, orbs, a neon sign, a candle) or as printed ink (marker X on a map, a sticker, a screen print) — never as an overall wash. A sanctioned sub-mode exists for illustrated ephemera: flat, faded pastel postcard palettes (mint, butter yellow, dusty rose) for kitschy souvenir graphics, always reading as printed matter inside an otherwise muted world.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the tradition documents them.**
1. Cryptids and, above all, their *kitschy representations*: souvenir-shop screen prints, embroidered patches, postcard illustrations, roadside fiberglass cutouts and statues of Bigfoot, Mothman, lake monsters, chupacabra. The creature itself is implied more often than shown — a black silhouette with two luminous eyes at the tree line.
2. Dark forests and woods at night; bare branches; fog between trunks; wheat and dry-grass fields under a night sky.
3. Abandoned buildings: peeling interiors, empty corridors, sheds, attics, ruined houses.
4. Research ephemera: yellowed newspaper sighting clippings, typewritten pages, cryptozoology field diaries, hand-annotated road maps with marker Xes, corkboard murder boards, sticky notes, string-and-pin connections.
5. Analog capture media: Polaroid cameras and white-bordered prints, vintage film cameras on straps, VHS tapes, cassette tapes, old sci-fi film stills, tabloids.
6. Supernatural tourist traps and roadside signage: warning signs with saucer silhouettes, cryptid crossing signs, sticker-covered highway markers, desert diners and motels, water towers, power lines under enormous skies.
7. Glow-in-the-dark iconography, alien imagery, tin foil hats, Ouija boards.
8. Moths and moth-wing dust — the Mothman lineage: moths at windows, in hands, pinned, illustrated.
9. Vintage field fashion: corduroy and plaid jackets, flannel, big sweaters, trench coats, iron-on patches, lanyards with badges, backpacks, worn Converse and combat boots, earthy tones throughout.

**Materials and textures.** Film grain and slight overexposure; Polaroid and snapshot softness; VHS scan lines, tracking noise and color bleed for the analog-horror leaning; paper grain of newsprint, photocopies and typewriter ink; embroidered thread, corduroy ribs, denim, worn leather straps; natural surfaces of dry grass, moss, fallen leaves, bark, moth wings. Everything feels handled, thrifted, filed, or pinned up — nothing feels factory-new.

**Composition and lighting.** Four recurring compositional families: (a) the found snapshot — candid, slightly off-center, flash-lit or dusk-lit, subject small against landscape; (b) the document close-up — a map, clipping or sign filling the frame, hand-lettering or marker annotation visible; (c) the flat lay / outfit collage / moodboard grid — gear, ephemera and photos arranged or gridded as evidence or fandom moodboard; (d) the centered roadside monument — a sign, diner or water tower isolated against empty sky or field. Lighting is diffuse overcast, blue-hour dusk, or night carried by a single practical source: flashlight, candle, neon, phosphor glow, window light. Contrast is low, blacks lifted and faded, highlights blown softly like old film.

## Mood & Values

Adventure, mystery, curiosity. The feeling is eager paranoia: notes kept, radios scanned, woods entered anyway. It values community investigation (the mystery solved with friends), empathy for the monster, whimsy inside dread, and nostalgia for an analog era of unsolved things. Humor is allowed and characteristic — the tin foil hat worn sincerely-ish, the souvenir shirt bought ironically then loved genuinely.

## Era & Origins

Coined 2014 on Tumblr from fandom moodboard culture; visual DNA borrowed from the 1970s–90s tabloid and occult-detective era (*Kolchak: The Night Stalker*, *In Search of...*, *Unsolved Mysteries*, *The X-Files*) and from the 2015 Regional Gothic trend's deer-in-darkness, forests and diners. It reacts against polished digital modernity by insisting on tape hiss, paper files and places without cell service.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Muted analog base plus one small uncanny accent; never a saturated or neon-dominated frame.
2. Frame the image as *evidence or artifact*: snapshot, souvenir, document, field photo, moodboard — not a studio render.
3. Let kitsch be sincere: screen-printed saucers, embroidered society patches, fiberglass cryptids are welcome at face value.
4. Put the uncanny at the edge: silhouette, glow, shadow, implication outrank revealed monster anatomy.
5. Age the medium: grain, fade, flash flare, VHS bleed, paper yellowing.
6. If text appears, it should be hand marker, typewriter, newsprint, or souvenir-shop lettering, and it should hint at a cover-up, a sighting, or a warning.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any cryptid or local legend (moth-thing, lake serpent, desert phenomenon, swamp monster), any region's woods, swamps, deserts, suburbs, diners, attics, state parks. Composition: any of the four families above, from tight document macro to vast landscape with a tiny figure. Lighting: overcast day, golden dusk, blue hour, full night with one light source, or flash-in-darkness. Color handling: earthy daytime, blue-black night, or faded-pastel printed ephemera. Mood: playful kitsch, quiet uncanny, wistful nostalgia, or cold analog-horror dread. Medium: 35mm, Polaroid, VHS still, scanned document, flat lay, gridded collage, outfit board. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A sunlit swamp boardwalk with a hand-painted rougarou sign and a film camera on the rail is as valid as a night forest with glowing eyes.

**Avoid.** Glossy HDR digital sharpness and saturated cyberpunk neon; sleek contemporary tech as hero props (smartphones, drones, clean UI); gore, jump scares, or fully rendered realistic monster close-ups; pristine heroic wilderness without an eerie or kitschy layer; occult ritual décor (altars, tarot, crystal grids) as the subject; hoarded mossy trinket clutter without an investigative narrative; manicured-domestic uncanny as the main stage; Victorian black-and-white gothic; kawaii pastels used as the overall palette rather than as printed ephemera; minimalist empty-room liminal shots with no cryptid trace.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Aliencore** centers space-age futurism — chrome, holographics, Y2K optimism, iridescent black-silver-neon. Cryptidcore keeps aliens only as earthbound kitsch (saucer signs, tabloid clippings) inside muddy analog folklore; its palette is soil, not space.
- **Goblincore** hoards natural clutter (bones, moss, mushrooms, shiny trash) for gremlin delight. Cryptidcore's clutter is *evidentiary* — maps, clippings, photos pinned in argument — and its nature is watched, not collected.
- **Midwest Gothic** shares silos, diners and empty fields but presses human hardship and oppressive rural decay. Cryptidcore borrows the roadside and lightens it with curiosity, souvenir kitsch and explicit supernatural signage.
- **Suburban Gothic** hides dread behind manicured lawns and domestic interiors. Cryptidcore stages the uncanny in wild, roadside and abandoned spaces, and in paper artifacts.
- **Witchcore** practices: candles, herbs, tarot, moon altars, black lace. Cryptidcore investigates rather than conjures; its moon appears on an embroidered patch, its ritual is a research board.
- **Analog Horror**, its closest technical cousin, uses the same VHS decay but colder, institutional and threat-first; Cryptidcore keeps warmth, whimsy and the invitation to go looking.
