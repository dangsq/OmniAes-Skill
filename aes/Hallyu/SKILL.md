# Hallyu (Korean Wave)

Hallyu is less a single look than the visual surface of an entire media ecosystem: K-pop idols, K-drama romance, K-fashion and the fandom that surrounds them. The wiki is explicit that this is "more of a fashion and media aesthetic" than a fixed scene type — its center of gravity is the idol industry, where stylists rebuild each act's look per song concept, and the peripheral gravity is the everyday life that orbits it: bedrooms, cafés, street snaps, accessory macros, fan rooms papered with ephemera. What holds all of these registers together is a shared finish — milky, desaturated color; groomed, polished subjects; soft diffused light; and an undercurrent of romance and devotion aimed at the female gaze.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette (rule, not recipe).** The default ground is milky and low-saturation with near-white highlights: ivory and cream (`#FAF6EF`–`#F1E8DA`), oat and warm beige (`#E3D5C2`–`#C9B79E`), blush and sakura pink (`#F4D7DC`–`#E9BFC9`), peach (`#F6DCC8`–`#EFB9A0`), lilac and lavender (`#D9CCF0`–`#B7A3DE`), powder blue and muted sea teal (`#BFD3D8`–`#8FB0B8`), pale butter yellow (`#F2E4B8`). Two licensed saturation spikes exist: kawaii product/café styling in bubblegum pink (`#F7B8CC`–`#F49AB4`), and idol stage/concept work in violet-magenta gels (`#6C4BC7`–`#C43E8E`) against idol-promo black (`#17141A`). An image should sit in the milky range unless it is explicitly depicting a stage concept or a cute branded product; even then the spike reads as lighting or packaging, never as grunge contrast.

**Signature motifs, ranked by textual documentation.**
1. *The idol figure and the kkonminam (flower boy)*: pretty, androgynous young men shaped by shoujo and bishounen conventions — perfectly styled or dyed hair, light makeup, small jewelry (usually a singular earring), variations on suit styling.
2. *K-fashion coords*: kawaii inflections, pastel themes, long skirts and dresses, many layered accessories, Korean beauty trends — berets, oversized knits, pleated check skirts, cream shirt-jackets, canvas totes.
3. *Idol stage outfits*: concept-driven ensembles assembled by stylists, which may borrow from nearly any fashion aesthetic for a single comeback.
4. *K-drama romance*: tender, wistful narrative beats — a figure gazing at the sea, walking an empty street, holding a single flower.
5. *Fandom and media artifacts*: taped poster and photocard walls, album shelves, tinted collage grids, neon hand-heart signs.
6. *A J-pop / visual-kei trace* (the Gackt lineage) surfacing in glamorer stage styling.

Image-corroborated recurring objects: pearl earrings and pearl hair clips, heart-shaped metal drops, fine gold chains and rings, satin drapery, sheer cotton voile curtains, low pallet beds with cream linen, macramé hangings, dried pampas grass, cherry blossom branches and fallen petals, vintage compact cameras, faced kawaii drink cups and macarons.

**Materials and textures.** Pearl luster, polished fine gold, satin and silk folds, cream wool knit, sheer voile, matte paper (postcards, photocards, taped notes), pale wood and rattan, dried grasses, blossom petals, glossy plastic cup walls. Everything reads touchable and clean; wear and distress are absent.

**Recurring subjects.** Idols solo or in group formation; young women and fans in K-fashion layers; anonymous figures seen from behind or with the face hidden by a phone or camera; jewelry and beauty macros cropped to ear, hair or hand; bedrooms and fan rooms; café and product styling; sunlit street snaps; concert stages with a small figure inside a vast lit set; blossom-lined landscapes.

**Composition and lighting.** Centered full-body coord against plain ground; from-behind walking or gazing poses; mirror selfies with the phone masking the face; high-key, near-overexposed product shots on draped cloth; wall grids of taped ephemera; multi-panel tinted collages; intimate crops for accessories. Light is diffused overcast or sheer-curtain window light, warm low sunset pouring peach onto cream walls, or deliberately blown-out product light — always with gentle film-like desaturation, slight haze and shallow depth of field. The exception is stage work: saturated violet, magenta and blue gel lighting over darkness.

## Mood & Values

Romance, tenderness and aspirational intimacy. Grooming is treated as care — perfect hair, light makeup and a single earring signal respect for the viewer's gaze, which the style explicitly addresses as female. Fandom devotion is identity: the poster wall and photocard grid are honors, not clutter. Softness outranks rawness; the everyday is curated until it carries media sheen. A wistful nostalgia runs beneath it — film grain, vintage cameras, empty streets under blossoms.

## Era & Origins

The term Hallyu was popularized in the 2000s, replacing *gayo* for domestic pop; the modern musical form dates to Seo Taiji and Boys in 1992, whose genre experimentation reshaped Korean pop. Visually the style inherits from shoujo and bishounen manga conventions and from J-pop glam (Gackt), and it globalized in the 2010s–2020s through streaming, Netflix K-dramas and internationally praised film. Its concept system reacts against single-genre pop stardom: each comeback restyles the idol from scratch, which is why the aesthetic can absorb almost any fashion while keeping its polish.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Build from a milky, desaturated base; push highlights toward near-white and keep shadows soft and warm.
- Groom every figure: styled or dyed hair, light makeup, small deliberate jewelry, coordinated layers. No undone subjects.
- Let at least one marker place the image inside Korean pop media culture — an idol's styling, a fandom artifact, a K-fashion coord, a drama-romance gesture, a stage gel — unless the image is a pure lifestyle/product piece, where pearl, cream and blossom carry the signal.
- Light diffusely: sheer curtains, overcast blossom light, peach sunset on cream walls, or high-key product blowout. Add slight haze and film softness.
- Reserve saturated violet/magenta and black for stage or promo-concept frames; reserve bubblegum pink for kawaii product and café frames.

**Variation axes.** Subject: idol portrait or group promo, fan room, bedroom interior, café product styling, accessory macro, street snap, blossom landscape, concert stage, drama-romance scene. Composition: centered coord, from-behind, mirror selfie, flat-lay or card-mounted product, ephemera wall grid, tinted collage, tight jewelry crop, wide stage vista. Lighting: overcast soft, window sheer, sunset peach, high-key, stage gel. Color handling: any point in the milky range, kawaii pink mode, or stage violet mode. Mood: dreamy romance, playful kawaii, wistful film nostalgia, electric concert energy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Harsh direct flash, grunge contrast or distressed textures; leather, studs, safety pins, graffiti and punk signage; unstyled hair, heavy smoky makeup or absent grooming; candy-saturated Western sticker-clutter pastels; lo-fi indie bedroom mess with thrift-store grime; dark chiaroscuro moody portraiture; cyberpunk neon cityscapes; documentary grit or warreportage realism; luxury gold-marble opulence. The style is polished, soft and commercial — rawness breaks it.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Girl Crush** is one K-pop concept *inside* Hallyu's range: fierce confidence, black leather and red, sharp winged liner, confrontational stare. Hallyu's default is soft gaze or hidden face, cream and pearl, romantic narrative; when Hallyu borrows girl-crush styling it still frames it as idol promo (group formation, stylist polish, floral or stage backdrop).
- **Kkonminam** is the flower-boy figure type itself — the androgynous pretty young man as sole subject. Hallyu contains kkonminam but is wider: interiors, fandom rooms, product styling, drama romance, group and media contexts.
- **Rebels** trades polish for protest: distress, denim wear, studs, graffiti, snapshot flash. Hallyu never shows wear; its rebellion is at most a stylist's concept choice.
- **Soft Boy** is Western indie melancholy — thrifted corduroy and grandpa knits, grainy bedrooms, no idol grooming. A Hallyu male subject keeps dyed styled hair, light makeup, a singular earring and Korean media markers even at his softest.
- **Soft Girl** is Western cutesy maximalism: cloud and rainbow stickers, blush-heavy makeup, pastel hair clips in space buns, saturated candy pastels. Hallyu's pastels are milky and desaturated, its cuteness carried by pearl and fine gold, berets and pleated skirts, cream minimal interiors and fan-culture ephemera rather than sticker chaos.

*Note on evidence:* the wiki documents idol grooming, fashion and concept styling in detail; the interior, product-macro and fan-room registers are lifestyle extensions corroborated by the image corpus, and the stage-gel register, while textually grounded in idol stage outfits, appears in only a minority of frames.
