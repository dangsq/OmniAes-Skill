# Arcadecore

Arcadecore — also called Early 80s Arcade — is the idealized sensory memory of the public video game arcade in its golden age, roughly the late 1970s through the 1990s. It is not a documentary style but an atmosphere style: dim halls where the only light comes from machines, rows of cabinets humming in the dark, chiptune bleeps implied rather than heard, and a crowd of players competing shoulder to shoulder. The aesthetic is deliberately period-loose; modern homages, chiptune music videos and indie pixel games count as fully Arcadecore so long as they reproduce the environment's physics — dark ground, luminous objects, grid-based graphics, and the material culture of the arcade floor.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule: saturated light on near-black ground

Value contrast does the work. The ground plane (walls, carpet base, cabinet vinyl, night sky) stays in black and deep navy; every saturated hue must behave as a light source or as something lit by one — phosphor, neon tube, marquee bulb, blacklight fluorescence.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `#000000`–`#0b0b1a` black; `#10143a`–`#1a1f5c` navy/indigo | walls, carpet base, cabinet wrap, night |
| Emissive primaries | pink/magenta `#ff2ea6`–`#ff71ce`; cyan `#01cdfe`–`#4dfaff`; green `#39ff14`–`#05ff00` | the wiki-named trio; must read as emitted light |
| Accents | yellow `#fffb96`–`#ffd319`; orange `#ff9e00`; violet `#7b2dff`–`#bf5fff` | marquee bulbs, sprite highlights, carpet motifs |
| Phosphor white | `#e8f6ff`–`#ffffff` | vector lines, dot-matrix dots, scanline peaks |

Any hue is admissible if it fluoresces; no hue is admissible as a matte mid-tone. There is no ambient fill light — shadows stay black, and colour arrives as spill, halo and bloom.

### Signature motifs, ranked as the text documents them

1. **Arcade cabinets** — the full anatomy: backlit marquee, bezel framing the CRT, painted side art, control panel, coin slot. Cabinets appear singly as portraits or in rows as architecture.
2. **CRT screens** — curved glass, scanlines, phosphor bloom, sprite graphics burning in the dark.
3. **Pixel art and dot-matrix graphics** — blocky sprites from constrained palettes, pixel typography, and physical LED dot-matrix signs where round epoxy dots stand in for pixels at architectural scale.
4. **Vector graphics** — the Asteroids lineage: sharp, intensely glowing wireframe lines on pure black.
5. **Neon lights and marquee bulbs** — tube signage, chase-light bulb borders, high-score tables in glowing type.
6. **Glowing joysticks and buttons** — ball-top sticks, translucent button caps lit from beneath.
7. **Patterned carpets** — the iconic geometric/cosmic loop-pile: confetti dots, planets, rockets, stars and squiggles in fluorescents on black or navy, also readable as fabric print and all-over pattern fields.
8. **Tokens, tickets, plastic prizes** — the ticket-counter economy: rolled paper tickets, metal tokens, cheap booth prizes.

**Fashion layer** (the wiki describes it as an open blend of Mallgoth, Raver and retro game culture rather than a uniform): graphic tees with arcade logos and characters, denim or leather jackets, phat pants, glowstick jewelry, light-up sneakers, cheap plastic prizes worn as accessories; at its extreme end, translucent iridescent vinyl and UV-reactive accents over dark base clothing.

### Materials and textures

Curved CRT glass, glossy printed vinyl cabinet wrap, painted MDF, loop-pile nylon carpet, injection-molded plastic ball tops and button caps, chrome coin doors, paper ticket rolls, stamped metal tokens, LED epoxy dots, iridescent PVC rainwear, haze in the air catching light.

### Recurring subjects

Players gripping panels mid-game; crowds and competitions under marquees; roller skaters and skateboarders carrying arcade energy out of the hall; cosplayers and stage-lit chiptune performance energy; empty after-hours aisles where the machines still glow; signage and control-panel close-ups; carpet and fabric pattern swatches; and, as an outdoor transplant, night cityscapes where billboard neon and police-car white substitute for marquee glow while the wardrobe stays UV-bright.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

One-point perspective down cabinet-lined aisles receding into black; flat frontal cabinet portraits; macro crops of marquees, panels and dot-matrix signs; all-over pattern fields with no horizon; collages with pixel type overlaid on abstract shape bursts. Lighting is practical-only: CRT glow, neon tube, marquee bulbs, blacklight UV. Expect bloom halos, coloured spill on faces and floors, scanlines and phosphor persistence, and magenta/cyan colour grading. Daylight does not exist here; the closest exterior is neon dusk.

## Mood & Values

Nostalgia, competition, social gathering, escapism, fun. Arcadecore is communal and electric — play as a public ritual, the hum of machines as a social space, high scores as folk honour. It idealizes rather than documents: even an empty aisle should feel paused mid-party, chairs and glowing screens waiting for players. Playfulness outranks cynicism; wonder outranks realism.

## Era & Origins

Rooted in the arcade golden age from Space Invaders (1978) through Pac-Man, Galaga, Q*bert and OutRun to Dance Dance Revolution (1998), with film anchors like Tron and The Wizard and later homages in Stranger Things, Hi Score Girl and chiptune music videos. As a codified internet aesthetic it reacts against isolated home gaming and polished HD realism by resurrecting the public, tactile, glow-in-the-dark play space. Its legacy runs forward into Synthwave's neon grids, Vaporwave's early-computer nostalgia, Glitch Art's error aesthetics and the indie pixel-art revival — Arcadecore is the physical room those digital styles descend from.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Dark ground, luminous figure: black/navy base, everything bright emitted or UV-lit.
2. Give every bright thing a source — phosphor, tube, bulb, blacklight — and show its spill and halo.
3. Grid logic over realism: pixels, dot matrices, scanlines, wireframe vectors; abstraction wins.
4. Period hardware: curved CRTs, chunky bezels, coin slots, painted side art. No flat panels.
5. Carpet is ground truth: interior floors carry the fluorescent cosmic/confetti pattern.
6. Neon trio first (pink, cyan, green), accents second (yellow, orange, violet), phosphor white for lines and dots.
7. Imply sound and crowd: motion, posture, queued players, prizes in hand — the aesthetic is partly auditory, so static images must suggest the chiptune hum.
8. Props of play: tokens, tickets, booth prizes, joysticks, three-initial high-score tables.
9. Typography stays in-world: pixel fonts, dot-matrix letters, bulb-lit marquees, scoreboard type.

**Variation axes.** Subject: interior aisle, single-cabinet portrait, control-panel or marquee macro, carpet/pattern field, fashion portrait, party crowd, skate/roller scene, night-city neon transplant, flat vector illustration, pixel sprite sheet on black, pure vector wireframe scene. Composition: deep one-point perspective, flat frontal, extreme macro, horizon-less all-over pattern, type-over-collage. Lighting: pure UV blacklight, CRT-only gloom, marquee-bulb warmth, hazy bloom, neon dusk exterior. Colour handling: strict pink/cyan/green on black; full rainbow fluorescence; monochrome phosphor-white vector; magenta-graded photography. Mood: crowded competitive energy, dreamy haze, liminal after-hours calm. Medium: photograph, 3D render, flat illustration, pixel art, pattern swatch, music-video still. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — dark ground, emissive neon, pixel/vector logic, arcade material culture — not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Daylight, bright uniform ambient light, sunlit windows, white walls.
- Earth tones, beige, wood, matte pastels, desaturated or faded grading.
- Modern flat-panel monitors, thin bezels, RGB gaming peripherals, esports setups, smartphones.
- Clean corporate sans-serif hero typography and minimalist logo design.
- Minimalist, Scandinavian or concrete-loft interiors.
- Matte Memphis-style terrazzo and squiggles on cream grounds (see discrimination).
- Total glitch corruption as the subject — datamosh and channel-shift dominating the frame.
- Horror decay as the point: rust, rot, gore, ruined-abandonment grime.
- Synthwave outdoor horizon grids and chrome sunsets standing in for the arcade itself.
- Kidcore daylight toy chaos on light backgrounds.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Its tell | Arcadecore's difference |
|---|---|---|
| 8-Bit | The pixel technique is the whole style; sprites can sit on white or any flat ground | Pixels live inside a dark glowing environment of cabinets, carpets and neon; pixel art is one graphic mode among several, vector wireframe equally valid |
| Kidcore | Primary-colour chaos on light grounds, daytime playroom, beads, stickers, matte plastic toys | Same playfulness, opposite physics: black/navy ground, UV and phosphor emission, CRT hardware instead of daylight toy piles |
| Glitch Art | Corruption artifacts — datamosh, channel shift, scan displacement — are the subject | Arcadecore allows incidental scanlines and phosphor noise as period texture; if the error is the point, it is Glitch Art |
| Mallgoth | Mall corridors, black/burgundy/plaid wardrobe, goth lettering, angsty posture | Shares mall space and some wardrobe, but Arcadecore's dark is lit by fluorescent neon and its mood is fun and competition; Mallgoth pieces are ingredients, not the identity |
| Memphis Design | 80s postmodern squiggles, terrazzo and laminate in pastel-plus-primary on cream, matte and studio-lit | Similar geometry, opposite ground: Arcadecore patterns sit on black under blacklight and glow; Memphis is matte and lit, Arcadecore emissive and dark |

Adjacent note: Synthwave borrows arcade neon but stages it outdoors as grids and sunsets; Vaporwave fades early-computer graphics into pale pastel irony. Arcadecore stays saturated, interior and object-bound — the room itself, glowing.

**Quick recognition checklist:** near-black ground; light only from machines and tubes; pink/cyan/green fluorescence; pixel or vector graphics; cabinet anatomy; patterned blacklight carpet; tokens, tickets, prizes; mood of communal, nostalgic play.
