# Raxet

Raxet is a Spanish urban fashion and music-adjacent aesthetic that emerged in the late 2010s from trap culture and marginalized suburban youth scenes. It is closely associated with artists such as La Zowi, Bad Gyal, Ms Nina, Bea Pelea, Tania Chanel, and La Mala Rodríguez. The style deliberately reclaims elements of the “Choni” stereotype—items once dismissed as tacky, vulgar, or unfashionable—and rebuilds them as a defiant, glamorous, self-made identity.

The core visual idea is **high-street streetwear mixed with loud imitation luxury**: Bershka-type fast fashion placed beside bootleg designer signs, athletic basics styled with extravagant accessories, and working-class codes turned into provocation and empowerment. Raxet is not quiet wealth. It is conspicuous, synthetic, accessorized, and intentionally excessive.

## Mood & Values

Raxet’s emotional register is **defiant, provocative, extravagant, and self-empowering**. It expresses:

- Reclamation of working-class or suburban style codes.
- Pride in items previously labeled tacky or ugly.
- Female empowerment and self-expression, especially within urban music scenes.
- Rebellion against respectability and mainstream taste.
- A preference for bold appearance, nails, glamour, and self-making over institutional approval.
- The use of bootleg luxury as a statement, not as an attempt to pass as authentic wealth.

La Zowi’s description is central: a Raxet is a suburban girl combining Bershka clothing with bootleg clothing that imitates expensive brands, feeling excluded from the system, and caring more about beautiful nails than voting. This should be read as a cultural stance: **style becomes a form of agency when other forms of recognition are denied**.

The mood should not be serene, minimalist, nostalgic, or tasteful in a conventional luxury sense. It should feel assertive, glamorous, and slightly confrontational.

Raxet reacts against social exclusion and taste hierarchies. It takes the Choni stereotype—historically mocked or dismissed—and transforms it into a self-chosen aesthetic. It also reacts against authentic luxury gatekeeping by embracing bootleg versions of expensive brands.
