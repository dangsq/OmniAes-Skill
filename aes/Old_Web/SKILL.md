# Old Web (Web 1.0 / GeoCities Aesthetic)

Old Web is not a designed style but a constraint fossil: the visual culture of the first mainstream web (roughly 1994–2004), produced by amateur homepage authors on GeoCities, Angelfire, Tripod and self-hosted servers, under dial-up bandwidth, 256-color monitors and table-only HTML. Its chaos is functional and sincere — tiled backgrounds, spinning mail GIFs, guestbooks, webrings, MIDI autoplay — and its modern appeal is nostalgic mourning for a decentralized, pre-corporate internet of personal shrines. Everything below descends from those limitations: small compressed images, rigid modular grids, system fonts, and a palette chosen to survive any monitor.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
Every color should read as if drawn from the 216-color web-safe cube (RGB components limited to `00, 33, 66, 99, CC, FF`). Transitions between colors appear as checkerboard or ordered dither, never as smooth modern gradients. Anchor values:

| Role | Hex range |
|---|---|
| Web-safe primaries/secondaries (banners, badges, neon text) | `#FF0000`, `#00FF00`, `#0000FF`, `#FFFF00`, `#FF00FF`, `#00FFFF` |
| Hyperlink blue / visited purple | `#0000EE`–`#0000FF` / `#551A8B`–`#800080`, always underlined |
| Interface chrome | `#C0C0C0` face with `#FFFFFF` top-left highlight and `#808080` bottom-right shadow bevels |
| Title bars | navy `#000080` (classic) or blue gradient `#0A246A → #3A93FF` (XP-era) |
| Desktop ground | teal `#008080` |
| Shrine-page ground | starfield black `#000000` with white pixel speckle; neon green/yellow/magenta text on top |
| Pastel fan-page variant | lavender `#CC99FF`, `#E6E6FA`, pink `#FFCCFF` backgrounds |

Saturation is high and harmony is optional: clashing neighbors (red on blue, yellow on magenta) are period-correct. Muted, desaturated or "curated" palettes break the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the tradition documents them
1. **Animated GIF furniture** — spinning email icons, dancing babies, flame and pixel dividers, "under construction" banners, spinning "NEW!" stars. Ubiquitous decoration and page structure alike.
2. **Glitter graphics and word art** — sparkle-speckled text and images; headers in rainbow-gradient or metallic bevel-emboss lettering with hard drop shadows ("Welcome to my Homepage!").
3. **Underlined blue hyperlinks** and default system typography: Times New Roman and Arial body text, bitmap pixel display fonts in badges and headers.
4. **Table/frame layouts** — rigid modular grids, visible gray groove cell borders, split framesets, centered ~640–800px content columns over a tiled background.
5. **Community machinery** — visitor counters at the page foot, guestbook links, webring prev/next nav, "best viewed with Netscape/IE at 800×600" and "Made with Notepad" badges; 88×31 button strips stacked in sidebars or assembled into dense badge walls.
6. **Low-resolution image stock** — pixel art icons, heavily compressed JPEG photos with macroblock smear, off-the-shelf clip art, early low-poly 3D renders; resolutions and provenances deliberately mismatched inside one frame.
7. **Period software chrome** — beveled dialog boxes with tab strips (Display Properties, download progress, file properties), media players and messengers (Winamp visualizer bursts, RealPlayer, MSN-style contact windows), classic desktop with icon column and wallpaper.
8. **Era memes and shrine topics** — dancing baby, "All your base are belong to us", virus hoaxes; planet-and-starfield space shrines, fandom pages, pet and hobby catalogs, personal fan sites.
9. **Beige hardware** (secondary, image-supported) — CRT monitor, tower and keyboard as still-life subject, photographed flat against black.

### Materials and textures
Hard-edged GIF sprites with 1-bit transparency halos (a white or black fringe where a cutout meets a mismatched background); JPEG block artifacts around photo edges; checkerboard dither fields; visible repeat seams of small background tiles (starfields, patterns, marble); chiseled bevel-emboss on text and buttons; groove and ridge fieldset borders; glitter speckle; bitmap pixel-font edges. Surfaces are screen-emissive and flat — no paper grain, no film grain, no lens behavior.

### Composition and lighting
Composition is assembly, not design: a banner header (word art or flame GIF), a badge-stack sidebar, bordered content cells, horizontal-rule or GIF dividers, and a footer of counter + guestbook + webring. Acceptable alternatives: a full-bleed chaotic catalog page with no grid discipline and text packed edge to edge; a calm desktop screenshot (wallpaper, icon column, one open beveled dialog); a wall-grid of dozens of 88×31 badges; a single GIF sprite isolated on black or transparency. Lighting is the flat self-emission of a monitor: no cast shadows except word-art drop shadows and bevel shading; hardware photos use direct flash or neutral catalog light; starfield pages glow only from their pixel stars.

## Mood & Values

Sincere, hospitable, exuberant: "welcome to my page" energy, pride in novelty ("best viewed at 800×600"), generosity of links, handmade imperfection worn as identity. The style values decentralization, amateur creativity, community building (webrings, guestbooks), technological novelty, and — in its modern revival — nostalgia for an internet of people rather than platforms. Clutter signals abundance and care; emptiness signals abandonment. Even the eerie read of an idle desktop dialog is earnest, never ironic.

## Era & Origins

Emerges with the free homepage hosts of the mid-1990s (GeoCities, Angelfire, Tripod) and the dial-up/256-color hardware regime; peaks late 1990s to early 2000s alongside MIDI autoplay, MP3s, RealPlayer, Napster and Winamp skin culture; fades mid-2000s as CSS, Web 2.0 platforms (MySpace, Facebook) and mobile browsing impose standardized responsive design. It reacts to nothing contemporary — it was never chosen — while today's revivals use it as a protest against the centralized, template-driven web.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. **Assemble, don't design.** Build the frame from layers of mismatched provenance: tiled background + table grid + GIF sprites + badge strip + counter. Seams between elements should remain visible.
2. **Web-safe or dither.** Any gradient must be checkerboard-dithered; keep hues inside the cube's saturated primaries/secondaries, neon-on-black, chrome gray, teal, or the pastel fan-page variant.
3. **Mismatch resolutions on purpose.** Place a 32px pixel icon, a blocky JPEG photo, vector-ish clip art and system-font text side by side in one composition.
4. **Treat text as artifact.** Underlined blue links, Times/Arial body, bitmap pixel fonts in badges, one bevel-emboss rainbow word-art header.
5. **Maximalism with modular seams** (or the single-window calm variant). Fill cells; horror vacui is correct; generous whitespace is a failure signal.
6. **Flat emissive lighting only.** The screen is the light source; hardware still lifes use flash or neutral catalog light on black.
7. **Period-correct chrome.** Beveled gray buttons with highlight/shadow edges, blue title bars with square minimize/maximize/close glyphs, tab strips, groove borders, status bars.
8. **Footer furniture completes a page:** counter digits, guestbook link, webring nav, "best viewed" badge.

Variation axes: subject may be any homepage topic (fandom shrine, pet page, space page, hobby catalog, meme archive, hacker manifesto, fan-fiction index), any desktop or dialog artifact, any media-player/messenger window, a badge wall, a meme GIF frame, or a beige-hardware still life. Composition may range from centered column with sidebar, to bordered table grid, to gridless catalog chaos, to one dialog centered on wallpaper, to badge grid. Color handling may range from full rainbow-on-starfield-black to lavender pastels to teal desktop to monochrome neon. Mood may range from joyful hospitality to liminal desktop stillness to chaotic commerce to memorial nostalgia. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

Avoid: smooth modern gradients, glassmorphism, soft ambient shadows, large rounded corners, card grids with whitespace, responsive layouts; modern brand sans-serifs and kerned minimal typography; high-resolution photography with bokeh, HDR or cinematic grading; vector-smooth flat icons and emoji; stylized CRT curvature and scanline overlays as a default filter; ironic vaporwave pastels and statuary; surrealist dream-haze collage; a single consistent pixel grid across the whole frame; placeholder magenta-checker or engine-default graphics; glossy Web 2.0 badges, Flash-player chrome and scene-era profile CSS; Frutiger Aero aqua gloss or Y2K chrome blobs; smartphone frames, flat modern browser tabs, cookie banners.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Pixel UI** crafts deliberate, consistent pixel-grid interface assets (game UI kits, icon sets) with a harmonized palette and one uniform resolution. Old Web pixels are a byproduct of limitation, coexisting in the same frame with JPEG photos, system fonts and beveled chrome; mismatched resolutions and page-level furniture (counters, guestbooks) are the tell.
- **Programmer Art** is utilitarian placeholder graphics made by developers — default engine assets, magenta checkers, untextured primitives — with no decorative intent. Old Web is decorative excess by amateurs: glitter, word art, shrine content, hospitality text.
- **Vaporwave** is ironic and curated: pastel pink-cyan gradients, Greek statuary, mall interiors, smooth airbrushed fades, scanline fetish. Old Web is sincere, uses saturated web-safe primaries, dithered rather than smooth gradients, and never poses at arm's length from its subject.
- **Webcore** reinterprets Old Web elements through a contemporary surrealist lens — dreamlike liminal spaces, impossible or melting UI, soft glow, modern rendering fidelity wearing old-web costume. Old Web stays documentary: correct period chrome, crisp flat emission, functional (if chaotic) layout logic.
- **Cyber Stylin'** (the mid-2000s profile-customization era) keeps glitter but adds Web 2.0 gloss: rounded glossy modules, embedded Flash players, scene/emo typography, heavier personal photography, custom CSS profiles. Old Web predates that gloss — rigid tables, square beveled buttons, default fonts, 88×31 badges, and MIDI rather than Flash audio.
