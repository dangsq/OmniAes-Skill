# Dreampunk

Dreampunk is simultaneously a microgenre of electronic music and the visual world that grew around it on Bandcamp and SoundCloud in the mid-2010s. It began as the atmospheric, sample-free branch of vaporwave — original composition and field recordings of rain and city sound instead of ironic sampling — and took its name as a deliberate play on "cyberpunk," borrowing that genre's obsession with the vast futuristic metropolis while stripping out its heroes and violence. What remains is pure atmosphere: the experience of moving through an enormous neon city at night, in the rain, alone, half-asleep. Visually the style consolidates into album-cover art, music-video stills and promotional graphics: sprawling neon-lit cityscapes, rain-slicked streets, isolated figures dwarfed by architecture, and, from the late 2010s onward, a persistent layer of glitch art reinforcing the digital-dream premise. Its reference pool is cinematic — future-noir like *Blade Runner* and *Ghost in the Shell*, the saturated melancholy of Wong Kar-Wai's East Asian city films — filtered through internet-native decay: compression speckle, scanlines, datamosh smears.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette rule is **dark base, small hot accents**: most of the frame sits in near-black indigo and deep blue (`#04040c`–`#0d1026` base; `#10265c`–`#1b3a7a` blues), with violet and purple carrying the midtones (`#3b1663`–`#7b2fa8`). Into this darkness go one to three neon accents at maximum luminance but small area: hot magenta/pink (`#ff2bd6`–`#ff6ae0`, `#ff4fa0`), cyan/teal (`#00e0ff`–`#17d9c9`), and only occasionally a sliver of acid green (`#46ff9c`) or signal red (`#ff2440`) as a sign fragment. Distance and fog desaturate everything toward a lavender-grey haze (`#9a7cc0` at low opacity), so depth reads as color fading, not as sharp perspective. A legitimate variant dips the *entire* frame in a single hue family — an all-magenta wash, an all-indigo night, a teal-dominated anime still — as long as the black base and the small-area-neon logic survive. Pure white, sunlight yellow and earth tones are outside the palette.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Rain and wetness** — the style's first motif: falling rain, droplet speckle on lens or glass, wet asphalt mirroring signs twice, puddles holding inverted neon.
2. **Neon signage density** — stacked lightboxes and vertical sign columns, very often bearing Japanese or Chinese glyphs; signage is architecture here, not decoration.
3. **The vast night metropolis** — skyscraper canyons, window grids glowing pointillistic, skyline silhouettes punctuated by a spire or broadcast tower, tangles of overhead power lines.
4. **Loneliness** — empty streets, a single small silhouetted pedestrian, or a crowd reduced to an anonymous blurred mass; solitude inside immensity.
5. **Dream and surreal intrusion** — impossible light fields (tunnels or curtains of bokeh dots), floating or falling figures above pixel skylines, haze that dissolves logic; the city as dream content.
6. **Glitch and digital decay** — scanlines, compression speckle, chromatic fringing, datamosh smears at frame edges; a weather layer over the scene, prominent on late-2010s covers.
7. **Cinematic stillness from East Asian film and future-noir** — contemplative, event-free framings; anime-cel stills of rain arriving over a high-tech city belong to this lineage.
8. **Album-cover typography** — vertical CJK text strips, boxed glyph seals in a corner, tiny catalog or URL lines; an optional flat overlay layer inherited from Bandcamp cover culture.

Where the written record is silent — pixel-art skylines, iridescent oil-film puddles, LED dot tunnels — image evidence places these inside the surreal-dream and texture ranges rather than among defining motifs; treat them as licensed variations, not requirements.

### Materials and textures

Wet asphalt and standing water; glass curtain walls and steel tower grids; plastic and paper sign faces; fog and mist as a physical veil; film grain, VHS noise and digital speckle; bokeh dot fields from out-of-focus lights; long-exposure light trails; occasionally the holographic sheen of oil on a puddle. Surfaces are never matte-dry: everything glistens, diffuses or corrupts slightly.

### Recurring subjects

Urban street canyons; skyline overlooks with a tower or spire against a luminous clouded night sky; waterfront or canal cities doubling their neon in water; night roads with light trails; quiet interior or corridor stills; abstract light environments (bokeh tunnels, light curtains); puddle and wet-ground close-ups; pixel-art city silhouettes; collage grids of cover thumbnails. Human presence ranges from none, to one distant figure, to blurred anonymous crowds — never a portrait.

### Composition and lighting

Composition favors **verticality** (sign columns, towers, portrait crops) and **layered recession**: foreground wires or signs, midground buildings, background haze. The square album-cover format with a typographic overlay is common but not mandatory; high-angle overlooks, street-level one-point canyons, distant silhouette skylines, centered symmetric light fields and macro close-ups all occur. Lighting is **artificial only and always diffused**: neon flood, sparse window glow on near-silhouettes, fog-scattered luminance, bokeh, light trails, and violet-glowing night cloud banks. No sunlight, no hard daylight shadows, no studio key light.

## Mood & Values

Atmosphere over event. Every frame feels like a pensive interlude — a city experienced from inside a memory or a dream. The governing feelings are loneliness, introspection, futuristic melancholy, and a tender nostalgia for a future that is imagined rather than lived. The style values **originality and authored world-building** (its music's sample-free ethic translates visually into sincere, constructed scenes rather than ironic appropriation), **narrative suggestion** (each image implies a story it refuses to tell), and **immersion** (haze, rain and sound-implying stillness pull the viewer in). Romance and memory hum underneath: this is a city someone once loved, or dreamed of.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s, United Kingdom, rooted in the Dream Catalogue label founded in January 2014 by HKE (David Russo); the collaborative project 2814 (HKE with t e l e p a t h) and its 2015 album *新しい日の誕生* (*Birth of a New Day*) codified the sound and look. Dreampunk defined itself *against* vaporwave's sample-based irony by committing to original composition, a stance that caused a label schism and a purge of sample-based releases. After 2016 the scene decentralized through netlabels (No Problema Tapes, BLCR Laboratories, PURE LIFE, VILL4IN), took its name fully from an influential video essay, and resurged during COVID-19 lockdowns via virtual music festivals. It reacts to vaporwave by keeping the dream and dropping the joke; it reacts to cyberpunk by keeping the city and dropping the hero.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Night first: set the scene after dark or in deep dusk; artificial light is the only light.
2. Make it wet: rain, mist, reflections or puddles should touch most surfaces.
3. Keep the base dark and the neon small but blazing — darkness is the canvas, accents are the event.
4. Build depth with haze layers; let distance fade toward lavender-grey instead of staying crisp.
5. Keep people absent, tiny, silhouetted or blurred; solitude is the subject's default state.
6. Apply decay *after* composing: grain, speckle, scanlines, mild chromatic fringing or edge smears as a veil, never as the subject.
7. Favor vertical structures and sign density in urban scenes; CJK glyph signage is idiomatic.
8. Frame it like a film still or an album cover: eventless, contemplative, narrative-implying. Typography (vertical CJK strips, boxed glyph seals, tiny URL lines) is an optional overlay layer.

**Variation axes.**

| Axis | Allowable range |
|---|---|
| Subject | street canyon; skyline with spire/tower; canal or waterfront city; night highway with light trails; interior/corridor still; abstract light field (bokeh tunnel, light curtain); puddle macro; pixel-art skyline; surreal dreamspace with a falling or floating figure; collage grid of covers |
| Composition | street-level one-point canyon; high-angle overlook; distant silhouette skyline; centered symmetric light field; macro close-up; square cover with type overlay; vertical portrait crop |
| Lighting | saturated neon flood; sparse window glow on near-silhouette; fog-diffused glow; bokeh dot fields; long-exposure trails; luminous violet night cloud |
| Color handling | poly-neon (magenta + cyan over indigo); monochrome hue wash (all-magenta, all-violet, all-teal); teal-dominant anime still — always with black base and small-area hot accents |
| Medium | night photography / long exposure; anime-cel still; pixel art; 3D render; mixed collage and glitch composite |
| Mood | melancholic loneliness; serene dream stasis; eerie surreal drift; romantic memory |

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A dry rooftop, a pixel skyline, a puddle macro or a light tunnel can all be fully dreampunk without repeating a single known cover.

**Avoid.**
- Daylight, blue skies, golden-hour warmth, crisp dry midday shadows.
- Cozy lo-fi domesticity: bedrooms, houseplants, headphones, mugs, beige/amber warmth.
- Protagonists and action: heroes, cyborgs, techwear close-ups, weapons, posed conflict; cars as heroes (vehicles only as light trails, parked shapes, distant traffic).
- Vaporwave daylight kitsch: classical marble, palms, mall atriums, pastel grids, ironic retro-UI.
- Synthwave sunset grammar: orange-pink horizon sun, chrome type, perspective grid floors.
- Glitch as total destruction — the scene must stay legible beneath the corruption.
- Legible faces, festive crowds, party energy.
- Lunarpunk nature-magic: moonlit forests, wolves, moths, silver filigree, occult props.
- Earth tones, wood, linen, matte organic materials as dominant surfaces.
- Clean corporate flat design, vector minimalism, white backgrounds, studio key lighting.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cyberpunk** shares the neon megacity but keeps actors, gadgets and conflict — cyborgs, techwear, chrome, action poses, hard key light. Dreampunk removes agency: empty or minuscule figures, no gear fetish, violet-shifted palette, ambient stasis, and a haze/glitch veil instead of clean render crispness.
- **Glitch Art** makes corruption the subject; the frame disintegrates into databent abstraction. In dreampunk corruption is weather — speckle, fringing, edge smears — over a scene that remains readable and atmospheric.
- **Lo-fi Art** is warm, domestic and cozy: daylight rooms, plants, relaxed figures, beige and amber. Dreampunk is cold, wet, urban and nocturnal; its comfort is melancholy, not coziness.
- **Lunarpunk** is the night of nature and magic — moonlit woods, silver light, wolves, moths, organic forms. Dreampunk's night is electric and built: neon, glass, concrete, rain on asphalt; any moon sits behind cloud and city glow.
- **Neon Noir**, the closest filmic cousin, retains crime-narrative tension: detectives, chiaroscuro hard shadows, smoky rooms, moral threat, often teal-and-red lighting. Dreampunk dissolves threat into dream: softer fog, purple-dominant palette, glitch veil, dense CJK signage, cover-style typography, and stillness without danger.
- Lineage note: vaporwave (pastel daylight, marble, mall irony) and synthwave (sunset grids, chrome) are ancestors and cousins, but they operate in daylight or sunset modes; dreampunk is strictly the night-and-rain mode of this family.
