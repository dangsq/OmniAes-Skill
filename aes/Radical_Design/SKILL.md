# Radical Design

## Overview

Radical Design (Design Radicale, sometimes Radical Surrealism) was the Italian architecture and design movement that opened with the *Superarchitettura* manifesto and exhibition in Pistoia in 1966 and largely closed with *Italy: The New Domestic Landscape* at MoMA in 1972. Born from Leonardo Savioli's architecture students in Florence, it answered post-war austerity and mid-1960s consumerist minimalism with quirky, ironic, intensely colourful objects of strong visual impact and socio-political undertow: foam furniture shaped like lips, gloves, waves and giant grass; illuminated sculptural mirrors; discotheque interiors; performance sets; and speculative visions of future cities. Studios such as Archizoom, Superstudio, Studio 65 and UFO, with manufacturers like Poltronova, Gufram and Meritalia, made the polemic tangible. The movement is the direct precursor of Memphis, and its DNA resurfaces in fashion (Missoni, Dior, Saint Laurent), in green megastructure architecture, and anywhere design behaves as critique rather than service.

## Visual Identity

### Colour language

The infobox rule is "bold and varied" — there is no fixed palette, only a behaviour: high-saturation hues deployed as flat, monolithic planes or as deliberate clashes, never blended gradients. Anchor hues (approximate): cobalt/electric blue `#1E3FD8`–`#2A2AE0`, fuchsia/magenta `#D92189`, violet `#7B2FBE`, signal red `#E2261F`, grass green `#4C9A57`, coral/tangerine `#FF6F52`; counterweight pastels and grounds: sage/mint `#9DBE9C`, baby pink `#F6C6D0`, peach `#F5B8A0`, cream `#F2EFE9`; plus patent black `#0A0A0F` carrying specular highlights. Typical handling: one saturated hero object isolated on a neutral seamless ground; or two-to-four saturated blocks abutting (fuchsia against cobalt, purple against raspberry); or a pastel architectural shell punctured by one hot accent. Glow counts as colour: neon-soft illuminated edges (the wavy mirror family) and disco colour-washes are in-palette. Matte earth tones, beige greige and desaturated minimalism are out.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Ironic sculptural furniture** — the movement's core document: biomorphic and anthropomorphic seating and objects at disobedient scale. Lips (Bocca sofa), a giant leather glove (Joe chair), a field of oversized upholstered grass blades you lie inside (Pratone), a cuttable foam wave (Superonda), cactus-shaped stands, mushroom stools (Puffo). The object is a joke and a manifesto at once.
2. **Illuminated sculptural mirrors and portal forms** — the Ultrafragola family: soft-edged, wavy or fur-trimmed frames with internal light, reading as a glowing aperture rather than a mirror.
3. **Speculative future-city imagery** — critique of traditional planning and envisionment of future urban environments: megastructures, repetitive modular grids, utopian renderings, and (as later echo) vegetation-clad towers. Note honestly: the movement's built architectural record is thin; this pole lives in drawings, collages and renders, so treat it as a conceptual register, not a fixed look.
4. **Discotheque, performance and set interiors** — theatrical colour-plane rooms, arches, steps, sunburst and scalloped walls, slogan typography ("the future is bright" energy), integrating fashion, photography, lighting and music.
5. **Fashion as carrier** — saturated colour-block tailoring, vinyl and patent pieces, zigzag and chevron knit patterns; a legacy channel rather than a core-era document, but fully in-family when it carries the colour and gloss rules.
6. **Manifesto and advertisement culture** — anti-design publications, photomontage, deadpan ad parody: the absurd object presented with commercial seriousness.

### Materials and textures

Polyurethane foam upholstered in fabric or leather (quilted, tufted, seamed); plush and faux-fur trim; glossy vinyl, PVC and patent leather with hard specular highlights; moulded acrylic and plastic with internal illumination; shag pile and zigzag knits; smooth painted or laminated set walls and floors. Texture contrast is structural: soft matte foam against wet-look vinyl, fur against lacquer. Occasionally a distressed historic shell or antique cabinetry appears as ironic foil behind a glowing pop object.

### Recurring subjects

A reclining or seated figure swallowed by an oversized soft sculpture; mannequins and models in saturated tailoring; still-life assemblies of design icons on neutral ground; single-hero-object product shots; interior vignettes with one absurd gesture; runway figures; utopian city visions and rendered interiors.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Three recurring regimes, all acceptable: (a) **studio advertisement** — object centred on a seamless neutral ground, even diffuse shadowless light, slight print grain, deadpan commercial framing; (b) **theatrical set** — flat colour planes, arches, stairs and symmetry, stage-like frontal framing, saturated washes or neon glow; (c) **runway / frontal figure** — full-body, colour-blocked, glossy. A fourth, **speculative render/collage**, uses axonometric or deep-perspective views of modular megastructures with soft ambient light. Lighting ranges from flat even studio light to internal neon glow to disco drama; what never appears is moody chiaroscuro naturalism or golden-hour lifestyle warmth.

## Mood & Values

Play as protest. Irony, provocation and sensuality in one gesture: the lip sofa is desire, joke and critique of taste simultaneously. Utopian speculation coexists with polemic — questioning consumerism, beauty standards and the very nature of cities ("radical in spirit, not only in the architecture"). Youth culture, rebellion and disco glamour sit atop a serious ideological core: individuality against uniform product, experimentation against norm.

## Era & Origins

Florence and Pistoia, Italy, 1966–c.1972; roots in Savioli's Florence architecture faculty; groups Archizoom, Superstudio, Studio 65, UFO; figures Sottsass, Pesce, Mendini; manufacturers Poltronova, Gufram, Meritalia. A reaction against Modernist functionalism and mid-1960s mass-produced minimalism; fused Pop Art ethos with mass production. Precursor to Memphis Milano (1981); legacy in Critical, Social and Participatory Design, in sustainable megastructure thinking, and in period film atmosphere of the early 1970s.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- One absurd, ironic gesture per image; the object or space must argue, not merely exist.
- Disobedient scale: domestic objects at architectural scale, architectural elements at furniture scale.
- Colour as structure: flat saturated planes and clashes, never decorative gradients.
- Soft sculpture over hard geometry: foam, plush, vinyl gloss, glow.
- Deadpan presentation: commercial-serious staging of the ridiculous.
- Optional period print sensibility: even ad lighting, mild grain, 1970s chroma.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be furniture, interior, discotheque or performance set, fashion look, object still life, manifesto poster, or speculative city render. Composition may be seamless-ground product shot, symmetrical theatrical set, frontal runway, or deep-perspective collage. Lighting may be flat studio, neon-internal, disco wash, or soft render ambient. Colour handling may be single hero hue on neutral ground, two-to-four block clash, pastel shell with hot accent, or jewel-tone head-to-toe. Mood may slide from comic play to utopian awe to sensual glamour to cold polemic. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image** — a foam cloud bed, a chrome-and-fur bar, or a green-clad tower render never seen in the references all qualify if they obey the colour, material, scale and irony rules.

**Avoid**

- Minimalist functionalism, Scandinavian restraint, beige/greige neutrals, wabi-sabi matte earthiness, rustic handcraft.
- Memphis-dominant language: terrazzo and laminate confetti prints, kitsch asymmetric hard-edge geometry as the main event (that is the successor, not this style).
- Pop Art-dominant language: screenprint flatness, ben-day dots, repeated celebrity/consumer icon canvases, gallery-print framing.
- Preschool Pop cues: naive matte primaries, didactic childlike simplicity, rounded friendliness without irony, gloss-free plasticity.
- Straight historic ornament (baroque/rococo played earnestly), corporate glass-box realism without speculative play, matte-black tech minimalism, desaturated or monochrome palettes.
