# Black Metal

Black Metal's visual identity was codified in Norway between roughly 1987 and 1997, when the second wave (Mayhem, Darkthrone, Burzum, Emperor, Satyricon, Ulver) turned low production quality into an ideology and extended it into image-making. Everything about the look is a refusal: refusal of color, of polish, of warmth, of commercial legibility. Album covers and promo photos were made to look photocopied, underexposed and hostile, and the same grammar governs stage dress, merch layouts and scene iconography. The aesthetic is also called Trve Kvlt, and its key motifs per the scene's own documentation are Satanism, dark fantasy, fear, nature, misanthropy and paganism, carried by two colors only: black and white.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

The default register is strict monochrome with threshold contrast: midtones are stripped out so the image resolves into pure black field and blown white marks.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Void black | #000000–#101010 | Dominant; swallows background, clothing, hair |
| Shadow grey | #1a1a1a–#2e2e2e | Only where grain lifts the black slightly |
| Grain grey | #4a4a4a–#7a7a7a | Film grain, fog, snow shadow; never clean |
| Bone white | #d8d8d8–#f2f2f2 | Corpse paint, snow, type, candle-blown highlights |
| Oxblood / faded lapis / parchment (secondary mode) | #6b1f1a–#8a2f24, #2e4a6b, #c9b48a | Medieval-manuscript illustration subrange only |
| Candle amber | #c8862a–#e0a83c | Pinpoint accent only: flames, altar glows |

Rule: color is an exception, never the base. When color appears at all, it belongs to the manuscript subrange — flat medieval miniature tones, muted reds, blues and golds on parchment — or to tiny candle flames inside an otherwise black frame. Saturated modern color does not exist in this style.

### Typography and graphic marks

Old English / blackletter type is a first-class visual element: titles set in white fraktur on black, usually as a bottom band or top banner. Band logotypes are symmetrical, spiky, near-illegible tangles of white linework resembling branches or thorns, placed top-center over the image. Type is compositional, not captioning: it occupies the frame as heraldry.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual documentation

1. **All-black clothing and leather jackets** — named in both the introduction and the visual list; the base condition of every figure.
2. **Corpse paint** — white face base with blackened eyes and mouth; the scene's defining mask.
3. **Low-quality black-and-white photography** — grain, blur, crushed contrast as a deliberate finish.
4. **Forests and winter** — conifer woods, bare branches, snow; nature as cold sanctuary (intensified in the atmospheric black metal branch).
5. **Long black hair, goatees** — documented grooming code; hair often curtains the painted face.
6. **Satanic and pagan sigils** — inverted crosses, pentagrams; worn as pendants, drawn as banners, arranged on altars.
7. **Hardware of dress** — bullet belts, spikes, studs, chains, boots; chainmail armor, cloaks and medieval weaponry as archaic war-gear.
8. **Candles and ritual still-life** — candle rows, altar and stage setups with symmetrical sigil banners.
9. **Medieval manuscript illustration** — secondary, documented range: flat miniature-painting scenes of helmeted warriors and kneeling figures in muted earth tones.
10. **Didactic / diagrammatic layouts** — black field, white blackletter header, annotated figure; an insider meta-format (scene guides, charts) that parodies the dress code while obeying the palette.

### Materials and textures

Film grain and photocopy degradation; motion blur; snow crust, wet bark, needle litter; cracked leather, oxidized studs, chain links; wax and flame; ringmail and rough wool; in manuscript mode, flat parchment and tempera. Surfaces should feel cold, dry or corroded — never glossy.

### Recurring subjects

A solitary painted figure half-lost in forest darkness; a frontal masked or painted portrait holding a guitar or staff; a band promo portrait against grey void; a ritual still-life of candles and sigil banners; an empty winter woodscape; a medieval miniature scene; an annotated black-and-white diagram of the dress code. Solitude is constant: one figure, or a row of identical figures, never a crowd.

### Composition and lighting

Threshold contrast: the figure emerges from black at the edge of legibility. Centered, frontal, static poses — confrontational rather than active. Environment-dominant frames where a small figure is swallowed by trees. Single motivated light sources: candle flame, harsh on-camera flash against snow or night, or flat black field with no light at all for graphic layouts. Symmetrical heraldic arrangement for sigils, banners and altar setups. Vertical cover format with spiky logo above and blackletter title below is the classic container, but landscape promo shots and square layouts are equally valid.

## Mood & Values

Cold, funereal, misanthropic. Dread is expressed through stillness and starvation of detail, not through action. Lo-fi finish is a truth-claim: polish equals compromise, so grain and blur signal authenticity and underground allegiance. Nature is sacred and Christian imagery is an enemy to be inverted. The scene's self-description is austere to the point of parody — no smiling, no fun — and even its humor arrives as deadpan insider documentation, never as warmth or color.

## Era & Origins

First wave (1981–1987, England and Sweden: Venom, Bathory, Celtic Frost, Hellhammer) supplied satanic themes and low fidelity; the second wave (1987–1997, Norway) fixed the visual code of corpse paint, black-and-white forest photography and occult heraldry, reacting against both polished mainstream metal and Christian Scandinavian culture. Later branches stretch the palette: atmospheric black metal foregrounds forest mysticism over Satanism; DSBM pushes bleak suffering; unblack metal reuses the format with Christian symbols; blackgaze and post-black metal admit shoegaze haze and softened color. The core skill remains the second-wave monochrome.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Monochrome first; if color is used, restrict it to manuscript earth tones or candle points.
2. Degrade the image: grain, blur, crushed blacks, blown whites — it should read as a photocopied cassette insert.
3. Keep the figure solitary, frontal, and partially consumed by darkness or trees.
4. Treat sigils as belief, not decoration: inverted crosses, pentagrams and candles placed with ritual seriousness.
5. Let type belong to the image: blackletter titles, spiky symmetrical logos.
6. Keep temperature cold: snow, bare branches, night air; warmth only as flame.
7. Prefer static dread over spectacle: no action, no crowd energy.

Variation axes: subject (musician portrait, forest ritual, winter landscape, altar still-life, manuscript miniature, diagram layout); composition (centered figure, environment-swallowed figure, flat graphic field, symmetrical heraldry); lighting (candle, flash, overcast snow, total black); color handling (strict mono through manuscript muted tones); mood (satanic aggression, nature mysticism, depressive bleakness); format (cover, promo photo, guide page). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: saturated or modern color, red-dominant palettes, neon and digital gradients; glossy polish, clean studio lighting, crisp HDR detail; gory anatomical illustration and fleshy textures; cozy colorful fantasy castles and knights; cartoonish Halloween grotesquerie in orange and green; smiling figures, crowds, mosh pits; modern streetwear or bright brand logos; glossy vector-clean occult clipart; warm daylight domesticity.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Death Metal** shares extremity but not temperature: it favors color, gory anatomy, muscular demonic bodies and dripping ornate logos. Black metal stays monochrome, grainy, photographic or crude, with painted human faces instead of rendered flesh.
- **Dungeon Synth** inherits blackletter and medievalism from the same scene but paints warm, colorful, nostalgic fantasy — castles, knights, parchment glow — and lacks corpse paint, grain and hostility. Black metal's medievalism is austere and armed, not storybook.
- **Keller Synth** (evidence thinner here) is basement cassette DIY: hand-drawn or airbrushed amateur fantasy, xeroxed color, cozy cellar romanticism. It lacks black metal's photographic monochrome, corpse paint and winter forest.
- **Devilcore** uses satanic symbols as glossy internet graphic design — saturated reds, digital pentagrams, horns as fashion accessory, meme energy. Black metal's sigils are lo-fi, ritualistic and ideologically cold.
- **Haunted Mound** is comedic horror: rubber masks, chunky cartoon gore, pumpkin orange and slime green, playful chaos. Black metal never plays for laughs in color; its only humor is deadpan, black-and-white insider documentation.
