# Mod

Mod (short for Modernist) is a style of self-conscious, aspirational modernism: working-class London youth who answered postwar austerity with continental tailoring, American soul music, Italian scooters and an obsession with detail. It is a graphic style before it is a decorative one — clothing and objects behave like printed shapes on a flat field — and it carries its rebellion coolly, through precision rather than distress. The look peaks in the "Swinging London" mid-1960s and reads instantly through silhouette: narrow, short, columnar, androgynous.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette.** The governing rule is contrast plus primaries: hard black against off-white, then saturated blocks of red, blue and yellow, with orange and turquoise as the licensed accents. Approximate ranges: black `#0B0B0C`, off-white `#F4F2EC`, signal red `#D8232A`, royal/cobalt blue `#1E4FAE`, yellow `#F5C400`, orange `#EF6C1A`, turquoise `#17A2A2`. A secondary commercial register exists in mid-60s fashion photography: candy pastels (mint `#9FD8B4`, shell pink `#F2A9B8`, lilac `#BFA3D9`) worn against flat studio backdrops of olive-khaki `#9A8F5B`, chartreuse `#C3CE3C` or lavender `#B4A6D3`. Color always arrives as an unmodulated plane — a whole dress, a whole wall, a whole backdrop — never as shading or gradient.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them.**
1. Bespoke tailored suits: three-button stance, dark wool, extremely narrow lapels, thin ties, pointed-collar shirts.
2. Miniskirts and A-line or geometric shift dresses (the Mondrian-style color-block dress is the type specimen), hem at or above the knee.
3. Italian motor scooters — Vespa, Lambretta — customized with crowds of extra mirrors, lights and flags.
4. Polo shirts (Fred Perry, Ben Sherman) buttoned to the top; the subcultural uniform once tailoring went mass-market.
5. Pop Art and Op Art graphics: color-blocking, polka dots, bold stripes.
6. National and military emblems used as pure graphic: Union Jack, RAF roundel.
7. The fishtail parka (M-51/M-65 military surplus) thrown over an expensive suit.
8. Footwear: Chelsea boots, Clarks desert boots, Mary Janes, white go-go boots, flat or low heels only.
9. Opaque colorful tights; sharp geometric haircuts, above all the Vidal Sassoon five-point bob.
10. Contextual props: flags waved from balustrades, seaside piers and promenades, all-night club interiors (the last is text-documented but visually thin in surviving imagery).

**Materials and textures.** Pressed mohair and wool suiting, crisp cotton piqué, matte shift-dress fabric against patent leather shoes, opaque matte tights, waxed cotton parka, and the hard gloss of the machine age: chrome, enamel paint, mirror glass, headlamp bezels. Texture contrast is deliberate — soft matte cloth set against cold polished metal.

**Recurring subjects.** Young women in shifts and bobs posed full-length; young men in narrow suits or parkas; couples and trios in matched graphic outfits; scooters ridden, parked, or photographed as mirror-covered shrines; street and seaside crowds; band and fashion promo groups arranged in rows.

**Composition and lighting.** Two registers dominate. The studio register: seamless single-color paper backdrop, flat frontal even light with minimal shadow, full-body framing, angular graphic poses — wide stances, arms held away from the body, tilted chins, deadpan faces — or models lined up in symmetrical rows. The street/press register: grainy black-and-white, candid diagonals, motion (riding, climbing, flag-waving), overcast daylight or hard flash. A third, smaller register is the color snapshot of scooter detail. In all registers the image behaves like layout design: figures as shapes, background as field.

## Mood & Values

Cool, self-possessed, fastidious. Mod expresses modernism, sophistication, attention to detail, consumerism, androgyny and a rebellion conducted through spending and style rather than shouting. The emotional temperature is deadpan wit or contained exuberance — never sentimentality, never sloppiness. Everything is chosen; nothing is accidental.

## Era & Origins

London, late 1950s: a small circle of working-class "modernists" named for their taste in modern jazz, rejecting the greaser rock-and-roll of the previous generation. It spreads across Britain in the early 1960s, peaks mid-decade with Swinging London, Mary Quant, Twiggy and Jean Shrimpton, The Who and the Small Faces, and the 1964 seaside clashes with Rockers at Brighton and Margate. By the late 1960s the original movement splinters — some toward the hippie scene, harder street mods toward early skinhead culture. The 1979 film *Quadrophenia* later sparks a separate Mod Revival wave.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Build from geometry: straight lines, circles (dots, roundels, mirrors), rectangles (color-blocks). Hemlines at or above the knee; silhouettes columnar and narrow.
- Ornament is minimal and placed: contrast buttons, piped trim, pocket flaps, a three-button stance, a collar buttoned to the top.
- Color behaves as flat print ink, not as modeling light; patterns are graphic (dots, stripes, blocks, flags), never painterly.
- Youth and androgyny: boyish flat line for women, slim cropped neatness for men; no hourglass emphasis, no musculature.
- Let machines gleam: chrome and enamel against matte wool and cotton.
- Light is flat and even (studio) or hard and documentary (street); shadows short or absent; no romantic glow, no chiaroscuro.
- Period medium: 1960s color-print saturation with slight fade, or silver-gelatin black-and-white grain.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a studio fashion plate, street candid, scooter run, club dance floor, seaside bank-holiday crowd, band promo, headshot, or object still-life (parka, polo shirt, scooter mirror bank). Composition may be symmetrical full-body, row of models, dynamic diagonal candid, or tight object crop. Lighting may be seamless studio flat, overcast daylight, hard on-camera flash, or club spot. Color handling may be full primary punch, strict monochrome, duotone, or pastel candy against a neutral field. Mood may run from deadpan cool to exuberant dance to cheeky mob energy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.**
- Florals, paisley, frills, lace, prairie or romantic dress; earth-tone hemp and distressed denim.
- Flared or bell-bottom trousers, wide lapels, platform shoes, long hippie hair, beards, headbands.
- Leather biker jackets, greased quiffs, heavyweight motorcycles (Rocker code).
- Satin, velvet, ruffles, cravats, peacock florals on menswear (Peacock Revolution).
- Cinched hourglass waists, full circle skirts, stiletto heels (1950s feminine code).
- Two-Tone checkerboard, punk crops, heavy boot stance, late-1970s color cast (Mod Revival).
- Baggy sportswear vests and athletic dance-blur as the primary subject (Northern Soul).
- HDR over-sharpness, cinematic teal-orange grading, grunge texture, bokeh-heavy digital look.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Mod |
|---|---|
| Peacock Revolution | Peacock menswear flaunts satin, velvet, ruffles, florals and widening silhouettes; Mod menswear stays narrow, dark, restrained, lapel-thin, hair cropped. |
| Dolly Girl | Dolly Girl is soft, romantic, long-haired, frilled, ingénue-sexual; Mod women are geometric, boyish, short-haired, flat-shod, deadpan. |
| Mod Revival | Same symbol kit (parka, scooter, polo) but inflected late-1970s: Two-Tone checkerboard, punk-adjacent hair, heavier boots, 1979-onward photo color; original Mod is suit-first, sixties film stock, chrome exuberance. |
| Northern Soul | Northern Soul is a 1970s dance-floor culture: baggy high-waisted trousers, vests, sewn-on patches, spinning motion; Mod is tailored, posed, graphic, static-cool. |
| Rockers | Rockers: leather jackets, motorcycles, greased quiffs, rock and roll; Mod: parkas over suits, scooters with mirror arrays, geometric hair, soul and jazz. |

Mod also brushes Pop Art and Space Age: it borrows their graphics but keeps them wearable — wool, cotton, chrome — where Space Age drifts toward metallics and futuristic helmet silhouettes that Mod never adopts.
