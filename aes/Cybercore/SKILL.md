# Cybercore

Cybercore is a revivalist internet aesthetic that emerged around 2020 on Pinterest and TikTok. It is not the original Y2K wave but a modern, introspective remix of its artifacts: a highly curated time capsule of early web graphics, obsolete hardware, celestial imagery and 1990s–2000s Japanese anime, edited until only the ethereal and melancholic residue remains. Where the historical Y2K era was commercially optimistic, Cybercore deliberately strips that optimism away, keeping the glow, the loneliness and the nostalgia — "anemoia," longing for a technological era the curator was too young to experience. Its touchstones are *Neon Genesis Evangelion* (Rei Ayanami functions as the unofficial mascot), *Serial Experiments Lain*, and early virtual-idol culture such as Hatsune Miku, plus sanrio-cute pixel sprites like Hello Kitty.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

One cold hue family owns the frame. Saturated blue is the default owner of at least two-thirds of the pixels; deep indigo-violet handles shadows and gradient tails; chrome silver and harsh white supply metal, line art and text; pastel pink and lilac appear only as accents — hearts, stickers, plush, keypad fills — and never co-lead. A second sanctioned mode is the void edit: near-black ground with luminous blue line art and white sparkles. Warm hues (orange, brown, sepia, cream) are effectively absent.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Early internet UI** — Windows-95-style dialogue and error boxes, pixelated cursors, menu bars, keypad grids, profile-page layouts; the single most documented marker.
2. **Retro anime and virtual idols** — sleek minimalist late-90s/2000s character designs: pale skin, bob or long straight hair, red or blue eyes, plugsuit-like or chrome bodysuits; low-poly 3D renders in the same idiom.
3. **Obsolete hardware** — flip phones, early digital cameras, CRT monitors and TVs, beige keyboards with Japanese keycaps, arcade cabinets, CD-ROMs, vending machines; hardware is photographed or rendered as relic, never as current tech.
4. **Celestial imagery** — ringed planets, moons, stars, and the four-point sparkle glyph scattered across dark fields.
5. **Butterflies, angels, angel wings** — organic/ethereal symbols juxtaposed against dead technology. The text ranks them high; sampled imagery shows them more as wing-like light rays and soft organic silhouettes, so treat them as sanctioned symbolism rather than mandatory props.
6. **Hearts** — pixel hearts, chrome hearts, heart stickers.
7. **Cute character sprites** — Hello Kitty and sanrio-type icons rendered as pixel menu items or plush charms bathed in blue.
8. **Text fragments** — tiny lyric lines (often stacked in offset echo repeats), Japanese glyph blocks, spec-sheet panels, error messages, small signature-like watermarks.

### Materials and textures

CRT phosphor scanlines and screen curvature; pixel halftone dot patterns; dithered gradients; chrome and brushed metal; holographic/iridescent sheen; low-poly meshes and wireframe waves; glossy plastic of keycaps, phone shells and soda bottles; plush fabric and rippling water softened under a blue wash. Everything reads as either *screen* or *relic photographed through a screen*.

### Recurring subjects

Anime girls and android idols in frontal, icon-like poses; hardware still lifes (keyboards, phones, CRTs, arcade cabinets, vending machines, bottled drinks); celestial scenes; plush and charm arrangements; collaged web pages and desktop screenshots; chrome mecha close-ups with HUD spec text; dreamlike water or sky surfaces holding a single small object.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends to layering and framing-as-interface: UI windows and menu grids floating over portraits; wallpaper-style border frames with hexagonal panels and Japanese spec text; centered sprite-on-flat-field layouts; fragmented panel collages of a face split into scanlined rectangles; corner-anchored micro-text. Full-bleed monochrome-tinted photography of mundane objects is equally canonical. Lighting is self-emissive, never solar: screen glow, neon edge light on line art, lens flares and starburst sparkles, soft bloom, or a flat cold wash as if the whole scene were dipped in blue. Dark voids with luminous subjects and pale lavender high-key are both acceptable poles.

## Mood & Values

Ethereal, slightly melancholic, introspective. Cybercore values nostalgia without lived memory, digital escapism, and retro-futurism drained of marketing cheer. Loneliness is affectionate: lyric fragments about unrequited love, empty arcades glowing at night, a plush adrift on water. The style prizes curation and editing — the past as a tended shrine, cold and synthetic yet tender.

## Era & Origins

Visual source material: late 1990s to early 2000s Japan — anime, virtual idols, Windows-era web design, flip-phone photography. Actual emergence: early 2020s, on Pinterest and TikTok, as a reaction to and deviation from Y2K Futurism. A younger generation archived early-web graphics and anime screenshots, then intentionally stripped the commercial optimism, keeping only isolated ethereal and melancholic aspects. Cybercore is therefore a 2020s remix, not a historical continuation; note that the term is sometimes misused as a synonym for generic Y2K, which this skill does not cover.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Let one cold hue family dominate; reserve pink/lilac for accents.
2. Add at least one legacy-digital stratum: scanlines, halftone dots, dithered gradients, UI chrome, pixel type, Japanese glyph blocks.
3. Juxtapose obsolete hardware with organic or celestial symbolism (phone + butterfly, CRT + angel wings, keyboard + planet).
4. Light the scene from screens and glows, not from the sun; allow lens flares and four-point sparkles.
5. Include small textuality — lyric fragments, error messages, spec panels — set tiny, often in offset echo stacks.
6. Finish edited, not documentary: bloom, gradients, tint washes; the image should feel curated and slightly sad.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be any of: anime/idol portrait, hardware still life, celestial scene, plush or charm arrangement, UI/desktop collage, chrome mecha or abstract light tunnel, water/sky dream surface. Composition may range from full-bleed tinted photograph to framed wallpaper layout, desktop collage, centered sprite field, or fragmented panel montage. Lighting may range from black-void neon line glow to pale lavender high-key to mid-blue screen-lit interior. Color handling may run from near-monochrome blue to blue-plus-pink accents to silver-chrome dominance. Mood may slide from lonely to dreamy-tender but stays ethereal. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a blue-tinted photo of a laundromat with scanlines and a pixel cursor is as valid as an anime portrait.

**Avoid**

- Warm palettes: orange, brown, beige, sepia, golden sunlight.
- Corruption as subject: datamoshing, compression wreckage, violent RGB splitting, shattered pixels (tidy scanline filters are fine; destruction is not).
- Street fashion and club energy: techwear, chunky sneakers, bling, crowds, DJ booths, graffiti.
- Optimistic Y2K pop gloss: bubblegum multicolor, chrome blob logos, franchise cheer, commercial shine.
- Modern interfaces: smartphones, glassmorphism, flat contemporary UI, current-gen hardware.
- Gritty cyberpunk cityscapes with rain and orange-teal neon.
- Photorealistic contemporary fashion styling; real-world clothing as the focus.
- Aggressive, sexualized or horror inflections; the mood must remain ethereal and melancholic.
- Organic analog textures: paper grain, film warmth, hand-drawn wobble.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cyber Angel** centers the celestial being itself — wings, halo, pearl iridescence, devotional framing — with technology optional. Cybercore keeps angels as one motif inside a blue-monochrome edit layered with UI and hardware; if the tech stratum disappears and the figure becomes divine portrait, it has crossed into Cyber Angel.
- **Cyber Swag** is fashion-first Y2K street style: confident poses, techwear, bling, saturated multicolor. Cybercore rejects real-world fashion for minimalist anime/virtual-idol looks and quiet interiority.
- **Glitchbreak** makes breakage the subject — datamosh, artifact storms, chaotic fragmentation, aggressive energy. Cybercore's scanlines and pixelation are tidy nostalgic filters over intact, calm compositions.
- **Krushclub** is rave-club coded: hard contrast, acid or pink-black palettes, crowd and DJ imagery, punchy club graphics. Cybercore is bedroom-screen intimacy, slow and melancholic.
- **Neo-Y2K** revives 2000s pop optimism with glossy chrome type, candy color and commercial confidence. Cybercore deliberately removes that optimism, adds 2020s edit filters (scanlines, halftone, lyric text), and keeps only the ethereal, lonely remainder.
- Secondary neighbors: **Mizuiro** shares the soft blue but lacks all tech nostalgia; **Webcore** shares UI obsession but is brighter, clippier and web-naive rather than melancholic; **Animecore** shares anime imagery but without the legacy-software and hardware layers.
