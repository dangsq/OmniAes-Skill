# Lovecore

Lovecore romanticizes the visual culture of love, affection and courtship: not one relationship, but the concept of love itself, filtered through the commercial and nostalgic imagery of Saint Valentine's Day. It is soft, sweet and hyper-feminine, built from valentine cards, boxed chocolates, rose bouquets and an omnipresent heart, and it treats kitsch as sincerity. Born in niche Tumblr circles of the 2010s and amplified by TikTok and Pinterest — with a surge during the COVID-19 pandemic, when its cheerful escapism became a refuge — it was also adopted within LGBTQ+ communities as a soft, positive vocabulary for queer love and joy outside heteronormative framing. Its visual ancestry runs through mid-century paper valentines, Rococo cupids, kawaii and Larme Kei softness, Coquette hyper-femininity, and the pixel-gif valentine banners of the early web.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: the pink–red–white monopoly

The palette is strictly limited to shades of pink, red and white; any hue outside that family is an intrusion. Within it, value and saturation are free to roam, which is what lets one image read as powdered nursery pastel and another as saturated crimson devotion while both remain lovecore:

- **Whites and creams** (paper, breathing space, background walls): `#FFFFFF`, `#FFF5F7`, `#FDEEF1`
- **Powder and blush pink** (satin drapes, faded film grade, knitwear): `#F7D6DA`, `#F2C6CE`, `#EAB6BE`
- **Candy and bubblegum pink** (envelopes, confetti, house paint): `#FFB0C0`, `#FF8FAB`
- **Hot pink and magenta** (neon tube, bokeh, glossy plastic): `#FF5C8A`, `#F23D6D`, `#E75480`
- **Cherry and ruby red** (roses, glazed chocolates, vinyl, wax seals): `#D2042D`, `#C41E3A`, `#E0115F`
- **Deep red, oxblood, burgundy** (moody backgrounds, quote-card fields, shadow): `#8B0000`, `#722F37`, `#5C0A14`
- **Budgeted micro-accents only:** lilac `#C9B6E4` and baby blue `#A6D8E7`, tolerated solely as conversation-candy-heart pastels or a single illustrated accent, never as fields.

Rule of thumb: white functions as paper and air; pink carries the mid-tones; red carries the emphasis and the darkness. A crimson-dominant image with pink highlights and a blush-dominant image with white space are both correct.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **The heart** — the single most important motif, appearing on nearly every object and in every composition: printed, embossed, cut out of paper, shaped into candy and chocolate, outlined in neon tube, strung as fairy lights, scattered as confetti, blurred into bokeh, projected onto walls, stitched into crochet squares, pearl-trimmed onto knitwear, pressed into vinyl records and house shutters.
2. **Love letters and Valentine's cards** — envelopes stacked or scattered, wax or sticker seals, handwritten cursive (including all-over repeated handwriting like a page of "love you"), vintage paper valentines with lacey or pixel borders, italic underlined declarations.
3. **Bouquets, above all roses** — deep red roses massed against plain walls or held in arms.
4. **Chocolates** — heart-shaped glazed chocolates in paper cups, boxed confections.
5. **Kisses and lipstick prints** — kiss marks stamped on mirrors, paper, cups; the kiss as trace rather than act.
6. **Cherubs and cupids** — often quoted from Rococo art, plus their pixel descendants: winged hearts on retro web banners.
7. **Cute animals** — swans, kittens, kept sweet and stylized.

Supporting cast documented in the visual record: neon heart signage and rose-in-heart line drawings, string lights and projected heart glow, conversation candy hearts, pearls and ribbon bows, satin and silk drapery, typographic love-quote cards on flat color fields, pixel-art valentine banners, crochet granny squares, clasped hands and embracing couples (gesture, not narrative), whimsical pink architecture with heart details.

### Materials and textures

Satin and silk with soft pooled folds; lace; pearl beads and trims; glossy plastic, glazed candy, vinyl and pearlescent charms with specular highlights; matte paper (envelopes, confetti, cards, sticker sheets); yarn in crochet and knit; gilt and ornate metal on vintage mirrors and frames; neon glass and luminous projection; grain overlays — film grain, pixel grain, halftone print dot — as nostalgia varnish.

### Recurring subjects

Still lifes of valentine ephemera; fashion details (satin and lace slip dresses, puffed-sleeve blouses, pleated miniskirts, heart patterns and heart-shaped cutouts, heart-shaped sunglasses, hair bows, delicate heart or cherub jewelry); interiors and installations (heart lights on walls, dark rooms lit by projected hearts); typographic quote cards; food (candy hearts, chocolates); mirrors, records and other single objects enthroned on draped cloth; reproductions of paintings of embracing lovers; hands clasped, arms around each other — affection shown as gesture, always chaste and tender.

### Composition and lighting

Composition leans on **repetition and pattern**: all-over prints, scattered flat lays of envelopes or confetti, tight grids of chocolates or crochet squares, decorative borders framing a central message (lace edges, pixel-heart frames), sticker-sheet layering. Equally common is the **centered single object** on draped satin or a plain wall, the **shallow-depth-of-field close-up** dissolving background hearts into bokeh, and the **environmental dark-room scene** where hearts are the light source. Views tend frontal, flat or straight-on, like scanned ephemera or product shots for a valentine shop.

Lighting occupies two registers, both acceptable: (a) **high-key diffused blush** — soft, slightly overexposed, warm-pink faded like scanned film or flash softened through satin; (b) **low-key saturated glow** — neon tube, string lights or projection painting hearts onto deep red shadow, with glossy speculars on plastic, pearl and vinyl. Either register keeps highlights rosy, never grey or muddy.

## Mood & Values

Sweet, sentimental, "gushy" and unapologetically romantic. Lovecore values romantic love, affection, sweetness, nostalgia and positivity; it celebrates crushes and dating rituals with wide-eyed sincerity and deliberately avoids mature or heartbreaking themes. Its cheer is escapist by intent — pandemic-era comfort — and its inclusivity is a value: love in all its forms, queer love and joy rendered soft and bright. Irony, cynicism and angst are foreign bodies here; even its moody crimson register reads as devotion, not despair.

## Era & Origins

Emergent on Tumblr in the 2010s, popularized on TikTok and Pinterest, swollen by pandemic-era escapism. It reacts against nothing aggressively; it simply withdraws into a curated valentine shop of the imagination. Its source imagery is deliberately anachronistic and commercial: mid-20th-century paper valentines and candy hearts, Rococo cupids, 1990s pixel-gif web banners, Japanese kawaii and Larme Kei softness, Coquette femininity. Its sonic cousin is dreamy indie pop, bedroom pop and classic love songs — nostalgic, gentle, crush-themed.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **The heart is grammar, not accent.** If no heart appears anywhere — as shape, print, cutout, seal, light, shadow, confetti or bokeh — the image is not lovecore.
2. **Hold the tri-color monopoly.** Pink, red, white only; cool pastels spend a tiny candy-heart budget.
3. **Sweetness over angst.** Crush energy, tender gesture, sincere declaration; never heartbreak, decay-as-grief, or irony.
4. **Apply one nostalgia varnish.** Faded pink film grade, pixel grain, vintage print dot, or glossy Y2K plastic — choose one era-flavor per image.
5. **Commercial ephemera and handmade craft are equals.** Paper valentines and sticker sheets sit beside crochet squares, pearl-trimmed knitwear and neon signs.
6. **Repeat to compose.** Pattern, scatter, grid, border, stack — repetition is the style's compositional signature.
7. **Let light be the subject when mood turns moody.** Hearts that emit light (neon, projection, string lights, bokeh) carry the low-key register.

**Variation axes**

- **Subject matter:** any bearer of love iconography — food, fashion, furniture, mirrors, records, architecture, typography, light installations, paintings, animals, hands, rooms. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
- **Composition:** flat-lay scatter, tight grid or all-over pattern, centered still life on drapery, framed border with central message, typographic quote card, environmental dark room, macro texture crop, illustrated vignette.
- **Lighting:** high-key diffused blush ↔ direct-flash gloss ↔ low-key neon/projection glow.
- **Color handling:** pastel-dominant with white air ↔ candy mid-tones ↔ crimson/oxblood-dominant moody; saturation may swing from powdered to saturated while the hue family stays fixed.
- **Medium:** photography, pixel art, flat illustration, painting reproduction, photographed craft (crochet, paper), graphic-design card or sticker sheet.
- **Mood register:** playful kitsch ↔ tender sentiment ↔ devotional moody.

A correct image need not echo any single reference; obeying the palette monopoly, the heart grammar and the sweet mood is sufficient.

**Avoid**

- Hue intrusion: dominant greens, yellows, oranges, blues, rainbow stripes, black-dominated goth fields, beige/brown academic neutrals; rose stems may show but foliage never leads.
- Angst and cynicism: torn letters or photos, wilted-rose grief, anti-Valentine irony, sarcastic text, breakup narratives.
- Explicit sexuality or mature themes; lovecore stays chaste and gushy.
- Grunge dirt, dust, desaturated grey or muddy matte grades; lovecore's fade is pink and luminous.
- Productivity function: planner spreads, checkboxes, calendars, habit trackers (that is Bubble Bujo territory).
- Scholarly props: open classics, candles, tweed, library shelves (Romantic Academia).
- Clouds, halos, cathedral light and winged figures without hearts or bows (Angelcore).
- Rainbow flags or pride-parade signage as the primary visual; queer love here speaks valentine grammar (Pridecore owns the rainbow).
- Full street-fashion coordinate shoots in mixed pastels where hearts are optional (Larme Kei).
- Heart-less minimalism: a lone rose can pass, but the style reads strongest when iconography — hearts, cards, kitsch — is present.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Larme Kei:** Japanese magazine street fashion; the image is a model's coordinated outfit in a broader girly palette (cream, lavender, mint, navy accents) with glossy editorial lighting. Lovecore is iconography-first and object-first, locked to pink–red–white, and treats hearts as mandatory rather than one option among many.
- **Pridecore:** rainbow flag palette, protest and parade energy, identity celebration as loud public joy. Lovecore may celebrate queer love but always through valentine vocabulary — hearts, cards, candy — never rainbow stripes as the color story.
- **Romantic Academia:** collegiate interiors, tweed and lace collars, candlelit libraries, poetry books, beige–brown–burgundy with gold. Lovecore's burgundy is candy-shop and neon, its literature is the love letter and the valentine card, and its hearts appear everywhere Romantic Academia keeps them absent.
- **Angelcore:** clouds, halos, feathered wings, sky blue and white, divine shafts of light. Lovecore's winged beings are cupids and cherubs carrying bows and hearts; its light is neon and film-flash, not heaven; its kitsch is commercial, not celestial.
- **Bubble Bujo:** bullet-journal spreads, washi tape, stickers and handwriting organized around productivity and layout function in multi-pastel mint/lilac/yellow. Lovecore shares the sticker-and-pixel cuteness but serves romance, not scheduling; its repetition is decorative pattern, not journal grid.
