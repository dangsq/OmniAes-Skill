# MDLR — Mec de la Rue

MDLR ("Mec de la Rue", "boy from the street") is a contemporary youth subculture of Spain's working-class suburbs, born in the late 2010s and codified by the Spanish-Moroccan rapper Morad, whose anthem "M.D.L.R." turned a French banlieue rap term into a mass identity. Visually it is a sportswear uniform photographed with smartphone honesty: puffer jackets and hooded tracksuits, the riñonera worn crossbody, friends locked shoulder-to-shoulder on a sidewalk of parked sedans and shuttered storefronts. It is the self-aware successor to the mocked Cani stereotype — same brand consciousness, new codes (balaclava instead of cap, electric scooter instead of tuned car) and, crucially, a politics of pride rather than parody.

## Visual Identity

### Palette
The palette works in two layers. The **garment layer** stays in dark and neutral sportswear tones: black `#0B0B0E`, charcoal `#26282C`, slate `#55595E`, heather gray `#B9BCBF`, off-white `#EDEDE9`, navy `#17202B`, with occasional brand color-block pops of royal blue `#1E63D0`, brick red `#C2302B` or sand beige `#C9A98C`. The **environment layer** supplies all saturation: sodium-vapor amber `#E3A23B` on night facades, magenta curtains `#C13378`, painted red walls, gray car paint and sidewalk tile, or — in the poster variant — a single flat ultramarine field `#2A2ED8`. A bright white sneaker `#F5F5F3` anchors nearly every look. Rule: keep clothing neutral or single-block; let walls, night light, or the poster field carry the color.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)
1. **Matching tracksuits** (Nike, Adidas) — the stated staple.
2. **Puffer jackets and hooded sweatshirts** — "essential"; glossy quilted nylon is the fastest read.
3. **The riñonera / fanny pack slung across the chest** — named the most iconic accessory; monogram or checkered designer canvas versions recur.
4. **Headwear code**: hood up, beanie, or balaclava (the balaclava explicitly replaces the Cani's baseball cap).
5. **Electric scooter** as the mode-of-transport motif, replacing the tuned car.
6. **Neighborhood life**: tiled sidewalks, parked hatchbacks and sedans, open trunks, block doorways, balconied facades, corner shops.
7. **Acronym hand gestures** (🤜, 👌, 👆, 🤏 and peace/finger-gun variants) — near-universal in the photographic record.
8. **Brand sneakers with visible air units** ("muelles").

Evidence note: the balaclava and scooter are canonical in the written record but sparse in surviving photos (one masked indoor shot; the scooter appears mainly in poster graphics). Treat them as available motifs, not per-image obligations.

### Materials & textures
Glossy quilted nylon; matte polyester windbreakers; heathered cotton fleece; mesh lining; rubberized air-bubble soles; coated-canvas monogram bag straps; thin chain necklaces; phone glass; dusty car paint.

### Subjects & posing
Young men, teens to twenties, solo or in duos and small crews. Friendship physics: arms over shoulders, bodies leaning into contact, one seated while the other stands. Casual bravado — chin up, deadpan or half-smile — with gestures held at chest or face height. Faces usually visible; masking, when present, is casual rather than threatening.

### Composition & lighting
Snapshot grammar: centered, straight-on, full-body or three-quarter frames; bystanders and car edges cropped without care. Night shots use direct on-camera flash — hard shadow thrown on the wall, hot speculars on nylon. Daylight is flat or overcast; night streets glow sodium amber; indoor shots sit against saturated walls or curtains. The graphic variant is screen-print flat: monochrome halftone portrait cutout on a solid field, white line-art smoke, roses and scooter. Medium is phone-camera realism (mild noise, unretouched) or print flatness — nothing in between.

## Mood & Values
Pride in precarity; the neighborhood as homeland; friendship as fortress; an anti-authoritarian squint toward police and respectability. MDLR images feel like proof of presence — "we are here, from here, together" — granting dignity to marginal life. Playful bravado and guarded anonymity coexist in the same frame.

## Era & Origins
Late 2010s–early 2020s, Spain, with the term imported from French banlieue rap. A direct successor to the 2000s Cani, reacting against its classist mockery and standing opposite the preppy, upper-class Cayetanos; for the first time migrant working-class youth from the periphery set the national trend. Musically bound to Spanish drill and street rap, whose lyric themes (barrio poverty, loyalty, police criticism) color the mood even when invisible in the frame.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Uniform logic first: puffer or hoodie over matching tracksuit or sweat shorts; riñonera diagonal across the chest; air-unit sneakers; hood, beanie or balaclava.
2. The barrio as stage: sidewalk, parked car, doorway, facade, corner shop; scooter as prop or mount.
3. Friendship physics: two or more bodies in contact, or a solo boast with a hand sign.
4. Phone-real light: direct flash at night, flat daylight, or sodium amber; refuse studio polish.
5. Two-layer palette: neutral garments, saturated environment or poster field.
6. Gestures as punctuation at chest height.
7. Medium honesty: smartphone noise or screen-print flatness, never editorial gloss.

**Variation axes**
- *Subject*: solo portrait, duo, crew, scooter rider, passenger framed in a car window, masked indoor figure, poster portrait; any barrio city, any season.
- *Composition*: full-body stand, three-quarter lean, seated couch or curtain shot, car-window frame, centered poster cutout; edge-cropped bystanders allowed.
- *Lighting*: night flash, overcast day, sodium night, saturated indoor ambient, flat graphic.
- *Color*: garment neutrals fixed; environment hue free (blue, red, magenta, amber); poster field any single saturated color.
- *Mood*: playful bravado, warm loyalty, guarded anonymity, defiant deadpan.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A daylight scooter ride, a flash-lit corner pose and an ultramarine poster are equally valid MDLR.

**Avoid**
- Baseball caps as the headwear signature (Cani code); use hood, beanie or balaclava.
- Tuned, lowered or spoilered cars as hero props (Cani/Chav); ordinary parked cars and open trunks are backdrop only.
- Burberry check, flat caps, UK estate brickwork (Chav).
- All-black masked menace, weapon props, desaturated music-video gloom (UK Drill).
- French banlieue tower-block concrete as default setting with monochrome styling and no crossbody bag (Racaille).
- Preppy or formal wear: chinos, polos, loafers (the Cayetano opposite).
- Studio seamless backdrops, softbox beauty light, heavy retouching, cinematic teal-orange grading.
- American skate codes: baggy denim, band tees, skateboards.
- All-over luxury logomania; designer branding only as small bag hardware or monogram accent.
- Rural, beach-resort or upscale boutique settings.

When in doubt, ask: is there a riñonera across the chest, puffer sheen, a hand sign, and a barrio backdrop shot like a phone snapshot? If yes — MDLR.
