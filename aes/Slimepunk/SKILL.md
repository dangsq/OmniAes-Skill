# Slimepunk

Slimepunk is a microgenre of music and, more durably, a visual aesthetic that emerged in the early 2010s inside internet communities, chiefly Tumblr. It is saturated with slime-related pop culture reference: The Toxic Avenger, Slime Time Live, Mountain Dew, toxic waste, and the green gunk dumped on game-show contestants by old-school Nickelodeon. Like vaporwave and seapunk, the aesthetic is the genre's binding agent — artists share visuals more than sound, united by heavy distorted basslines, dripping sound effects, and a post-acid, corrosive ethos. Slimepunk was coined by Zombelle, co-creator of seapunk, and early visuals freely mixed the two; practitioners joked that it was "nice to have a villain." Its cardinal value is DIY: this is a cut-and-paste, snapshot-and-overlay culture, not a designed one.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is **one dominant toxic green over a black or near-black field**, with a short list of licensed accents. Greens run from phosphor glow to chartreuse body-slime: roughly `#00FF41`–`#39FF14` for emission, `#7ED321`–`#ADFF2F` for wet slime bodies, deeper `#4CBB17` for sludge. Black (`#000000`–`#0A0A0A`) is the ground, not merely an outline. Accents, per the style's key colours: purple/magenta (`#8B00FF`, `#FF00FF`) and distressed red (`#CC0000`–`#FF0000`) for type and makeup; hazard yellow (`#FFD700`/`#FFFF00`) only in black-striped warning patterns. Neutral collage fields are allowed and characteristic: the grey-white transparency checkerboard (`#CCCCCC`/`#FFFFFF`), yellowed beige Y2K plastic (`#D8D0B8`), and occasional pastel or cyan-cast snapshot backdrops from cheap digital cameras. A green-dominant image on pastel or on cyan is still slimepunk if the viscosity and motif grammar hold.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the text documents them

1. **Slime itself** — viscous green ooze: drips, globs, strings, coated objects, drip-borders framing an image, dripping display typography.
2. **Radioactive / biohazard symbols** — trefoils as prints, frames, flyer borders, repeating edge patterns.
3. **Green glowing objects** — phosphorescent emission; the slime or object is the light source.
4. **Needles** — syringes, vials, IV drip bags (brand liquids rerouted into clinical delivery gear).
5. **Gas masks**.
6. **Rubber gloves and protective gear**, alongside rave gear.
7. **Mall goth themes/clothing** — chokers with O-rings, mesh, patched denim, striped leggings, platform and lace-up boots, band-style tees rerprinted with slime graphics; a punk/mallgoth base given a "slimier spin."
8. **Toxic waste iconography** — sludge, sewer gas, contamination, barrels.
9. **Nineties kids'-slime pop culture** — Nickelodeon game-show gunk, Mountain Dew "extreme" marketing, Toxic Avenger; brand logos parodied, never endorsed.
10. **DIY web-collage grammar** — documented by the image record and consistent with the style's Tumblr origin and DIY value: literal transparency checkerboards as backgrounds, repeating clip-art grids of beige vintage computers, WordArt-style drip fonts, scan and JPEG artifacts, early chrome 3D renders, rave-flyer layouts with symbol borders, rotated or upside-down pasted elements.

### Materials and textures

Wet gloss first: specular highlights on ooze, gel and foam. Then rubber, latex and PVC (boots, gloves, masks, shiny black rubberwear); mesh, denim, iron-on patches; metallic laminate skirts; yellowed ABS plastic of nineties computers; chrome and clay-like 3D-render surfaces; distressed, eroded print type. Everything reads either *wet*, *rubbery*, or *cheaply reproduced*.

### Recurring subjects

Teen and tween styled portraits and fashion looks; slime-product still lifes (the style sustains a vendor economy of slime shops); collage posters and rave flyers; 3D renders of spiky or dripping objects; runway or lookbook shots in slime-drip prints; lo-fi snapshot photos; grimy game-world moods in the lineage of Sludge Life and Cruelty Squad.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends to maximalist collage: repeating grids, pasted clip-art, drip borders sealing the frame edges, centered snapshot figures, poster layouts with a logo parody as focal point. Deliberate lo-fi layering and misregistration are virtues. Lighting options: phosphorescent glow emitted by the slime in a dark field; harsh direct on-camera flash of point-and-shoot snapshots; colored gel projection (green and magenta washes on skin and walls); flat, scanner-even light for graphic layouts; oversaturated early-digital color casts (cyan/green tint). Polish is suspect; emission and flash are the two honest light sources.

## Mood & Values

Corrosive playfulness. Slimepunk is teen irony aimed at consumer nostalgia: it loves Mountain Dew and Nickelodeon gunk while dripping them into contamination. The ethos is aggressive but atmospheric — noxious sewer gas as a mood, not a threat. DIY above all: the image should look assembled by one person with a mouse, a scanner, and a sticker folder. Contamination is humor; the villain role is a pose adopted with delight.

## Era & Origins

Early 2010s, internet-native (Tumblr), coined circa 2011–2012 by Zombelle from within seapunk; musically post-acid, with acid-tech and acid-house ancestry. Its props are one generation nostalgic: nineties slime TV, Y2K futurism's beige hardware and chrome renders, extreme-sports soda marketing. The lineage persists into 2020s game aesthetics (Sludge Life, Cruelty Squad) and the artisanal slime-vendor scene.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** (1) Viscosity: something drips, oozes or globs; liquids obey slime physics, stringy and slow. (2) Palette dominance: toxic green leads, black grounds, purple/red/hazard-yellow accent only. (3) Symbol grammar: biohazard/radiation trefoils and hazard stripes appear as graphics, prints or frames. (4) Collage DIY: layering, checkerboard transparency, clip-art repetition, drip fonts, visible cheap reproduction. (5) Honest light: glow-from-slime or direct flash or gel wash, never softbox studio neutrality. (6) Fashion code when people appear: mallgoth/punk base plus protective or rave gear, neon-streaked hair, chokers. (7) Clinical-toxic objects: syringes, IV bags, vials carrying green liquid. (8) Brand irony: logos parodied, contaminated, or dripped on — never clean endorsement.

**Variation axes.** Subject: portrait, fashion look, still life, flyer, poster, 3D render, room scene, game-grime environment — any of these. Composition: centered snapshot, maximalist collage, grid repeat, drip-bordered frame, poster hierarchy. Lighting: flash, phosphor glow, gel projection, flat scan light. Color handling: green-on-black, green-on-pastel, green/purple duotone, hazard-yellow framed, cyan-cast snapshot. Mood: aggressive, goofy, nostalgic, eerie-toxic. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A slimepunk image of, say, a dripping vending machine in a black corridor with a biohazard stencil and flash lighting is fully valid though no reference shows it.

**Avoid.** Organic naturality (moss, ferns, mud, fungi, wood, cottage clutter); frozen water (ice, frost, crystals); oceanic gloss (turquoise sea, dolphins, mermaids, shells as lead motif); yellow smiley-face rave minimalism; pristine white/pastel hygiene order with uncontaminated clinical props; clean corporate flat design, matte muted palettes, elegant serifs; cyberpunk/synthwave city neon (magenta-cyan skylines); warm golden-hour light; steampunk brass; blood or gore standing in for slime (red stays a graphic accent); photorealistic sterile product photography without ironic contamination.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Overlap | Slimepunk tell |
|---|---|---|
| Seapunk | Shared Tumblr origin, 3D-render gloss, neon | Seapunk is aquatic: turquoise water, chrome droplets, marine life. Slimepunk swaps water for viscous green on black, adds biohazard symbols, needles, goth gear. |
| Icepunk | Punk microgenre, neon-on-dark | Icepunk is frozen: crystalline facets, white/ice-blue, sharp edges. Slimepunk is wet, stringy, dripping, acid-green. |
| Acid House | Acid-music lineage, rave neon | Acid House leads with yellow smileys and 303 squiggles, euphoric and minimal. Slimepunk leads with toxic green ooze, hazard trefoils, mallgoth dress, collage maximalism. |
| Cleancore | Clinical props: gloves, syringes, bottles | Cleancore is sterile white/pastel order and hygiene purity. Slimepunk contaminates the same props — green liquid in the syringe, stained glove, black ground. |
| Goblincore | Green, hoarded objects, decay humor | Goblincore is earthy: desaturated moss and brown, natural specimens, soft daylight. Slimepunk's green is synthetic phosphor; its materials are plastic, rubber and JPEG. |

Also note vaporwave and Y2K futurism share the checkerboard and beige-computer props, but vaporwave is pastel, calm and classical-rendered; slimepunk is loud, wet and corrosive. When in doubt, ask: does it drip, glow green, and look assembled by a teen with a sticker folder? Then it is slimepunk.
