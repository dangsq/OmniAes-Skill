# Yabi (亚逼)

Yabi is not a look but a practice: an extreme, deliberate eclecticism that raids the global "cultural supermarket" of the internet — Visual Kei, cybergoth, gyaru, kogal, emo, scene, punk, Y2K futurism, modernized hanfu, retro-Chinese kitsch — and stacks the fragments into a single high-contrast collage outfit. Born around 2019 in the underground club scenes of Shanghai and Beijing (collectives such as EVO blending Japanese Visual Kei, Chinese ghost culture and Western techno), it grew out of the pessimistic meme culture of "sang" (mourning) and "tangping" (lie-flat) and turned that defeatism into active visual self-expression. The term began as a vulgar slur for superficial posers and was reappropriated as a badge of pride: a "neo-tribe" gathered by taste rather than ideology, asserting "aesthetic freedom" against gatekeeping, against "involution" (neijuan), and against mainstream "positive energy" narratives. Its home turf is the phone screen — Xiaohongshu, BiliBili, Douyin — so the degraded, filtered, overlaid digital presentation is as constitutive as the clothing itself.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Four color families coexist in constant friction: **ink black**, **neon**, **pastel**, and **metallic silver/chrome**.

| Family | Range | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Black | #0A0A0F – #202024 | Ground layer: ripped tights, mesh tops, boots, goth fragments |
| Neon | hot pink #FF2D95, acid green #B6FF3C, electric blue #2E6CFF, UV purple #8A2BE2 | Hair dye, knitwear, accents; flashes against black |
| Pastel | baby pink #FFD1E3, lavender #C9B6F0, mint #B8F0D8, baby blue #BFE3FF, cream #F5E9E0 | Kawaii layer: skirts, leg warmers, plush, clips |
| Metallic | chrome #C0C4CC – #E8E8EC, holographic sheen | Small sparks: lamé fabric, hardware, stickers |

**Rule:** a Yabi frame holds at least two *conflicting* families at once — matte black pierced by neon, or a candy pastel stack ruptured by one harsh dark or acid accent, with metallic glints scattered through. A single harmonized palette is never the point; the collision is. Near-monochrome outfits are acceptable only when layering and deconstruction (cut-outs, oversized volumes, chrome-on-black) supply the tension.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Mix-and-match layering** — the defining act: multiple subcultural vocabularies worn simultaneously, "more is more" (the "Dean-De-Milli" styling grammar of layered, oversized, accessory-heavy looks).
2. **Maximalist accessories** — stacked bracelets and rings, charm strands, plush keychains hung from bags and skirts, hair clips piled asymmetrically.
3. **Statement legwear** — intricate or ripped tights, cross-laced garters, kogal-style loose socks, mismatched fur or knit leg warmers; the legs carry the color conflict.
4. **Dyed hair as color event** — neon or pastel shades; hime cuts, twin tails, spiky ponytails; butterfly and star clips, bandanas, cat-ear hoods, crochet hats.
5. **Vintage tech as jewelry** — large over-ear headphones worn or hung, Nintendo 3DS-style handheld consoles, the phone itself in mirror selfies.
6. **Oversized, deconstructed silhouettes** — distressed open-knit mesh sweaters, baggy pants, cut-out shirts, volumes that swallow the body.
7. **Cybergoth fragments** — face masks, goggle-like hardware (text-documented; sparse in surviving imagery, treat as optional).
8. **Neo-Chinese and "Too Cool" kitsch fragments** — modernized hanfu pieces, retro headscarves, kitschy Chinese plush and graphics (text-documented; in practice an occasional accent layer rather than a constant).
9. **Chunky platform boots and sandals** with metal hardware — the silhouette anchor under all that legwear.
10. **Plush companions** — stuffed animals on leashes or strapped to bags, deadpan props in an otherwise harsh frame.

### Materials and textures

Distressed open-knit and mesh with visible holes; ripped nylon tights; faux-fur leg warmers; crochet and hand-knit hats; patent and vinyl platforms; chrome lamé and holographic film; plastic beads and charms; cotton bandanas; lace-up ribbon corsetry; matte headphone plastic; plush pile. Everything should read tactile, slightly cheap, and DIY-touched — safety pins, raw hems, hand-added charms — never couture-finished.

### Recurring subjects and poses

Solo outfit documentation in mundane interiors; crouching mirror selfies with the phone masking the face; clusters of friends whose looks deliberately clash with each other; club, gig and arcade scenes; casual street snapshots. Posture is slouched, crouched, sprawled or deadpan — anti-posing. Faces are bored, blank, hidden behind phones, masks, hair or blur; smiles are rare and ironic.

### Composition, lighting and digital surface

- **Lighting:** direct on-camera flash in cramped dark rooms, blowing out skin and throwing a hard shadow on the door or wall behind; club UV purple/magenta washes with motion blur; flat overcast daylight or arcade fluorescent for snapshots. Light is always a bit wrong, harsh, or degraded.
- **Composition:** tilted horizons, distorted wide or high angles, mirror-selfie framing, full-body outfit checks against wardrobes and hallway doors, group clumps filling the frame. Backgrounds are anti-aspirational: bedroom doors, wardrobes, corridors, arcade cabinets, chalkboard walls, club floors.
- **Digital surface:** blurry haze/"piss filter" softness, digital noise and compression artifacts, overexposure, computer-generated overlays — stickers, Chinese text, scanlines, collage cut-outs, sometimes full collage layouts. The image must feel captured on a cheap digicam or phone and edited in-app, never on professional equipment.

## Mood & Values

Spiritual ennui and alienation worn as armor; defiant playfulness; nostalgia for Y2K-era tech and childhood plush; individuality and self-expression asserted through sheer accumulation. The emotional register spans deadpan boredom ↔ chaotic joy ↔ misty melancholy, but never aspirational glamour. Underneath the noise is sincerity: the chaotic look is framed as a "repressed soul" pushing back against involution, conservative mainstream values, and subcultural gatekeeping. Reclaiming an insult is part of the ethos — superficiality is the point, and the point is political.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s, urban China: Shanghai and Beijing underground club circuits around 2018–2019, viral as a label by 2020 after electronic-music community backlash used it as an insult. Lineage runs from early-2010s "sang" and "lie-flat" meme pessimism through club collectives remixing Visual Kei, Chinese ghost culture and Western techno, with South Korean "Dean-De-Milli" layered styling as a fashion grammar. Documentation lives in vlogs ("I Am Yabi", 2021) and platform posts on Xiaohongshu, BiliBili and Douyin. Critics call it a watered-down, commercialized Shamate and accuse adherents of "genfeng" (following the wind); adherents call it resistance.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Collide, don't coordinate: every outfit or frame combines at least two warring subcultural vocabularies (goth black + kawaii pastel, cybergoth + gyaru, hanfu fragment + techno wear).
2. More is more: layer until the silhouette distorts; stack accessories on wrists, neck, hair, bags.
3. Treat legwear as the canvas: ripped tights, loose socks, mismatched leg warmers carry pattern and color conflict.
4. Degrade the image: flash blowout, haze/blur filter, tilt, noise, compression, sticker or text overlays.
5. Set it in the mundane: bedroom, hallway, arcade, club floor, plain street — never a studio seamless or luxury set.
6. Keep the face bored, blank, or obscured; ennui over influencer cheer.
7. Plant nostalgia props: handheld consoles, over-ear headphones, plush toys on leashes or bags.

**Variation axes (what may vary freely)**

- *Subject:* solo close-up selfie, crouching mirror shot, full-body outfit check, duo or group cluster, club/gig crowd, arcade hangout, street snapshot; any gender presentation; friends' looks may clash internally.
- *Color handling:* black-dominant with neon punctures; pastel-dominant with one harsh rupture; acid neon on dark; near-monochrome deconstructed layering with chrome tension — as long as at least two color families or registers conflict.
- *Lighting:* direct flash, club UV wash, flat daylight, arcade fluorescent; intensity from blown-out to murky haze.
- *Composition:* straight-on full body, tilted snapshot, distorted wide angle, mirror crouch, collage/overlay layout.
- *Mood:* deadpan ennui ↔ playful chaos ↔ misty melancholy.
- *Cultural fragments:* any mix drawn from the style's neighbor pool (gyaru, cybergoth, emo, scene, Visual Kei, punk, hippie, kogal, Y2K futurism, Frutiger Aero gloss, hanfu, Too Cool kitsch).

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Single-style coherence: pure lolita, pure gyaru, pure hanfu, pure punk reenactment, or any outfit speaking one subcultural language.
- Polished commercial studio lighting, clean retouching, editorial fashion-campaign finish.
- Muted earth tones, normcore minimalism, quiet luxury, capsule-wardrobe restraint.
- Uniform candy-color accessory overload in one harmonized hue (Decora's systematic plastic candy) without dark, kitsch or cyber fragments clashing against it.
- Shamate's towering backcombed hair spikes as the identity's sole carrier.
- Reverent, historically accurate traditional Chinese dress.
- Tasteful analog film emulation (hipster warmth and grain); Yabi's degradation is digital — flash blowout, app filters, compression.
- Aspirational settings: luxury interiors, scenic travel backdrops, clean cafés.
- Confident smiling influencer posing or fitness/campaign body language.
- Sparse styling: fewer than three accessory layers, bare legs without statement legwear.

## Discrimination Cues

- **vs Decora:** Decora is Harajuku-born, one harmonized candy palette with systematic plastic-clips-and-beads overload and a cheerful pop mood. Yabi clashes heterogeneous fragments (fur, metal, plush, tech, goth black) across warring palettes, adds Chinese kitsch, and degrades the photo with flash, blur and ennui.
- **vs Hipster:** Hipster is understated ironic minimalism — muted earth tones, normcore thrift, warm analog film. Yabi is maximalist, neon-loud, digitally degraded, and emotionally blunt rather than coolly detached.
- **vs Neo-Chinese Style:** Neo-Chinese Style handles modernized hanfu and Chinese motifs with coherence and elegance. Yabi may borrow a hanfu fragment but crashes it irreverently into cybergoth, gyaru or techno gear; there is no reverence and no unity.
- **vs Shamate:** Shamate (2000s China) is defined by enormous backcombed dyed hair spikes and cheap flashy clothing, photographed pre-smartphone. Yabi is post-2019, urban, digital-native: hair is one layer among many, the curation is deliberate, and the image carries app-filter degradation and club-culture props.
- **vs Too Cool (土酷):** Too Cool celebrates kitschy retro-Chinese pop with a coherent ironic retro poster or studio look. Yabi absorbs that kitsch as one ingredient inside a maximalist, club-born collage and adds snapshot degradation and subcultural layering; Too Cool alone lacks the goth/cyber fragments and the ennui.

**Quick test:** count the collisions. If an outfit or frame holds at least two warring subcultural vocabularies, statement legwear or stacked accessories, a degraded digital-flash surface, and a bored or obscured face in a mundane setting, it reads as Yabi.
