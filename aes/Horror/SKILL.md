# Horror

Horror is the umbrella aesthetic of intended fright: imagery engineered to disgust, unsettle, or trip the fight-or-flight response. Its roots are ancient — folklore, mythology and religious texts full of monsters, demons and afterlives — and it formalized as a genre in the late 18th century with Gothic literature, then spread through film (a century old), survival-horror video games, and web-native creepypasta and mascot horror. Its central menace always transgresses a norm — physical, psychological, social, natural — and often stands in for a society's real anxieties: pandemics, nuclear overreach, consumerism, the safety of childhood media. Crucially, horror plays its macabre straight; the moment the skeleton winks, it has left this style for Halloween.

The image record for this style skews toward mascot horror, creepypasta murk and red-black duotone illustration. Treat that skew as one pole of a much wider range: the textual definition equally covers cosmic dread, medical terror, slasher icons, ghosts and haunted rooms.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette.** Two families, both uncomfortable. *Drained*: soot black `#0b0b0b` through ash gray `#4a4a4a` to bone `#d9d6cc`, plus sickly washes of pale green `#7d8a72`, old beige `#c9c2a0` and bruised brown for rot and degraded footage. *Weaponized*: one saturated hue deployed over black — blood red from `#7a0c0c` to `#e60000`, usable as a full red-black duotone handling; or near-total void punctured by pinpoint neon (cyan `#59e0f0`, magenta `#e0c84f`-free cool accents) for glowing eyes and monitor light. A poster pole allows acid pink and violet duotones. The constant rule: high contrast and deep shadow; black is a compositional material, not a backdrop, and no palette combination may read as cozy or harmonious.

| Family | Range | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Drained monochrome | `#0b0b0b` → `#4a4a4a` → `#d9d6cc` | ghosts, framed portraits, fogged memory |
| Sickly wash | pale green, beige, bruised brown | rotted interiors, degraded stills |
| Weaponized red | `#7a0c0c` → `#e60000` over black | blood, duotone ink art, title lettering |
| Void + pinpoint | black with cyan/magenta points | eyes in darkness, animatronics, screens |
| Poster duotone | acid pink, violet | one-sheet art, comedy-horror edge |

**Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence.** (1) Darkness itself as subject and material. (2) Monsters and creature silhouettes — elongated slender humanoids, stitched or antlered beasts. (3) Masks: slasher masks, costume heads, porcelain faces. (4) The uncanny corrupted familiar — children's mascots, dolls, clowns and animatronics turned killer, a motif the source text devotes multiple whole sections to and the image record confirms with worn suit-mascots, rabbit and monkey costumed figures, puppet clowns. (5) Psychological distress: faces pushed past normal, too-wide grins, hollow eyes, weeping extremis. (6) Body horror — distorted limbs, wrong proportions, violated flesh. (7) Gore and blood, as accent or as subject. (8) The unknown: fog, off-frame threat, obscured silhouettes, eyes without a face. Character styling, where present, follows the documented wardrobe: dark layers, graphic tees, oversized sweaters, striped shirts, fishnets, thigh-highs, chokers, chains and harnesses, distressed denim, heavy makeup, fake blood.

**Materials and textures.** Grime, peeling paint, rust, wet plaster, mold; matted plush and stained costume fur with visible seams and torn panels; ink hatching, screentone and heavy black line in illustration; film grain, compression murk and xerox noise in photographic and game-derived material; matte fog; old wood, wallpaper and staircases in domestic spaces.

**Recurring subjects.** Masked killers; pale ghosts and bridal figures; faceless slender humanoids; costumed mascots and animatronics in dim rooms; dolls and rabbit puppets posed where they shouldn't be; clowns; framed photographs and portraits hanging inside the scene; corridors, stairs, basements, barns, nurseries made wrong; eyes glowing out of black.

**Composition and lighting.** Visibility is rationed. A single practical source — spotlight, lamp, monitor glow — carves the figure out of black; vignettes close the frame; the threat sits either confrontationally centered or tiny in a wide dim interior. Frame-within-frame (picture, screen, doorway) recurs, as does the low looming angle and large negative space held as implied threat. Soft blur and shallow focus deny detail. Symmetry is acceptable when it reads stage-like and uncanny, as in mascot lineups. Poster compositions with stark red title lettering are within range.

## Mood & Values

Dread, disgust, paranoia, grief-madness, cosmic smallness. The style values the exploration of fear, mortality, the unknown and societal anxiety, and the transgression of norms; its menace is metaphorical baggage as much as literal threat. Even at its comedy-horror edge, terror stays operative alongside the laugh. Silliness, comfort and reassurance are outside the value set.

## Era & Origins

Ancient folklore and religious myth; formalized late 18th century in Gothic literature (Shelley, Poe, later Stoker and Lovecraft); cinematic from the silent era through the Universal Monster cycle (1925–1950s); slasher icons from 1970s exploitation into the 1980s; the 1980s–90s Satanic Panic feeding possession and cult narratives; survival horror games from 1996; creepypasta and mascot horror codified in the 2010s and after. The style is therefore era-agnostic: any period's materials can carry it, from woodcut-like ink to low-poly game renders.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Ration visibility — decide exactly what light reveals and what black keeps.
2. Corrupt one familiar thing per image: a mascot, a doll, a grin, a family portrait, a nursery shape.
3. Keep the palette uncomfortable: drained or weaponized, never warm and harmonious.
4. Texture every surface: grime, grain, wear, stain; nothing sterile.
5. Let the menace imply an anxiety — illness, consumption, childhood, faith, technology.
6. Play it straight; no wink, no cuteness.

Variation axes (all allowed to vary): subject (ghost, slasher, corrupted mascot, cosmic entity, haunted room, distorted body, ordinary person in extremis); composition (tight face to vast negative space, symmetry to tilt, single frame to poster grid or collage); lighting (single spotlight, flat sickly wash, overcast daylight kept menacing); color handling (grayscale, red-black duotone, desaturated earth plus one red, neon pinpoint on black, saturated poster duotone); medium (ink manga, painterly, photographic, low-poly render, collage, one-sheet); register (quiet empty dread to loud grinning menace). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: Halloween playfulness (pumpkin orange, cartoon bats, cute skeletons); cozy warmth, golden hour, uncorrupted pastel softness; sterile commercial polish with even bright light and clean surfaces; VHS tracking, CRT curvature and emergency-alert graphics as the foreground subject; glitch and datamosh as motif; crucifixes, rosaries, priests and stained glass as central iconography; sunlit pastoral ritual with maypoles and folk effigies; rubber-suit camp with visible zippers and grindhouse day-glo played for kitsch; splatter as celebratory comedy without dread; wholesome mascots presented straight — the corruption (stain, tear, wrong eyes, too many teeth) must be visible.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Its tell | Parent Horror differs |
|---|---|---|
| Analog Horror |Obsolete broadcast media foregrounded: VHS tracking, CRT glow, test cards, alert screens, dated sans-serif | Degradation is atmospheric murk only; no interface or broadcast framing |
| B-Movie Horror | Cheap rubber monsters, overacted poses, saturated cheap color, exclamation-tag poster copy; kitsch audible | Menace stays sincere even with low-fi means |
| Catholic Horror | Crosses, rosaries, church interiors, possession tableaux, cold solemn blue-gray, Latin text | Religious props incidental at most; dread need not be theological |
| Digital Horror | Glitch, pixel sorting, corrupted files, forum/UI framing as the motif | Web lineage shows as subject and murk, not as interface corruption |
| Folk Horror | Daylight, open fields, rural community, wreaths and bone effigies, natural-fiber costume | Defaults to shadow and enclosure; any daylight is overcast and sickly, never golden pastoral |

When several subgenre signals coexist without one dominating, or when dread is carried purely by darkness, mask, monster, corrupted familiar or distressed face, classify as Horror itself.
