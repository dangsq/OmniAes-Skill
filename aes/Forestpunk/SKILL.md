# Forestpunk

Forestpunk romanticizes a wild, self-sufficient, primal relationship with the wilderness. It emerged in the 2010s internet aesthetic milieu (Tumblr collages, polyvore-style flat lays, cryptid-longing text posts) as a darker, more rugged counter-vision to Cottagecore's pastoral fantasy, and as a visual arm of green-anarchist, anti-capitalist, mutual-aid politics. The "punk" is not a music genre but a DIY ethos: bushcraft, foraging, ethical hunting, wood carving, zero-waste living, mended gear, hand-printed protest graphics. Its music adjacency is raw atmospheric folk and neofolk (Blackbraid is the named artist). The resulting image culture is deliberately lo-fi and unpolished — grainy phone snapshots, fog-softened film frames, xeroxed poster ink, collaged outfits captioned with typewritten fragments — because polish would read as consumerist.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is desaturated earth dominance with strictly rationed accents:

- **Canopy and moss greens:** `#1E2A20` → `#3A4A33` (near-black conifer shade to damp moss)
- **Bark and soil browns:** `#3B2F23` → `#6B5744`
- **Charcoal and ash greys:** `#262624` → `#4A4C48`
- **Black:** `#0D0D0C` (coats, crows, burnt trunks, ink)
- **Fog and bone off-whites:** `#B9BDB4` → `#D9D6CA` (mist, skulls, winter sky)
- **Muted reds as accent only:** `#6E2B26` → `#8C3B31` (oxblood flannel stripe, rust, dried berries)

Reds and whites never become field colors; they appear as stripes, berries, bones, print ink. Hand-made print matter may invert the scheme: a single flat ink (pale acid yellow-green near `#C9CE63`, or bone white) on a dark green ground, in rough hand-lettered strokes.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Dense, fog-shrouded forest** — the primary setting: conifer stands or bare winter deciduous woods, trunks repeating as vertical rhythm, deadfall and bracken underfoot.
2. **Wildlife, deer and crows first** — a deer dissolving into mist, a crow on an outstretched hand; wolves or working dogs as secondary fauna.
3. **Bushcraft, foraging, camping** — lean-to shelters and tarps, stacked firewood, axe and knife, baskets of gathered food, drying herbs, snow-bound camps lit by a single flame.
4. **Cabins and bonfires** — rough sheds and cabins with weathered plank walls; fire or lantern glow at dusk rather than bright daylight.
5. **Rivers** — named in the text as a core motif though thin in surviving imagery; misty banks and dark water are faithful extensions.
6. **Bones, skulls, antlers** — collected, not hunted-for-trophy: a deer skull laid on conifer boughs with feathers, antlers raised over a hooded head, moss growing over bone.
7. **DIY/punk artifacts** — hand-lettered protest posters ("up with trees, down with capitalism" energy), enamel pin badges on denim, zine collages, cracked vinyl, patched packs.
8. **The cloaked or hooded figure** — small in a vast bleak landscape, walking away, or centered in a ritual tableau; face hidden, blurred, or irrelevant.
9. **Fashion objects** — striped or flannel sweaters, denim and corduroy overalls, heavy long coats, sturdy non-rubber boots, leather satchels, newsboy caps, minimal beaded or quartz jewelry, faux fur only.

### Materials and textures

Damp moss, lichen, rough bark, dead grass, fallen birch logs, mud, patchy snow; weathered wood, tar-paper roofing, canvas tarp, waxed cotton, coarse wool knit, denim, corduroy, worn leather; bone, antler, feather; paper grain of xerox and zine print; photographic grain, fog softness, crushed shadows.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Scale contrast (tiny figure, enormous bleak woods); paths or trails leading into dark trees; fog acting as a natural diffuser so backgrounds dissolve to grey and light stays flat and shadowless; dim interiors mimicking forest-floor light, with one candle, lantern, or fire as the sole source; occasional harsh direct flash for raw night snapshots; centered symmetrical ritual tableaus (log circles, raised skulls); flat-lay collages on white ground with handwritten captions for fashion. Medium voice: phone camera or expired film, slight blur and muted contrast welcome.

## Mood & Values

Solitude without loneliness; grief and tenderness for the wild; the uncanny wish to run into the woods and return years later covered in moss, growing antlers, speaking a language no one understands. Defiance sits beside reverence: anti-capitalist anger (posters, pins, raised fists sprouting leaves) coexists with quiet acts of care — mended gear, gathered food, planted natives. DIY ethos is the load-bearing value; equality and mutual aid are community tenets, and the aesthetic explicitly rejects discriminatory ideologies.

## Era & Origins

2010s, online. It reacts twice over: against modern consumerist society (hence survivalism, zero-waste, reclaimed urban green space) and against idealized pastel nature aesthetics (hence fog, bone, deadfall instead of meadows). Neighbors in its cluster — Gloomcore, Cryptidcore, Goblincore, Dark Naturalism, Neofolk — share its mood; Forestpunk distinguishes itself by adding *practice and politics* to that mood.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Begin with weather: overcast, fog, or dusk; sunlight never direct. Desaturate toward earth; keep red and white as small accents. Show labor or conviction — chopped wood, a carved stick, a foraged basket, patched gear, a handmade sign, pins on a jacket — so the image reads punk, not just gloomy. Keep nature raw: deadfall, mud, bare branches, bone. If a human appears, they work, walk away, or ritualize; faces stay unseen or indistinct. Choose a lo-fi medium voice: grain, fog-soft contrast, or handmade print/collage construction.

**Variation axes.** Subject: foggy conifer or bare winter landscape, misty riverbank, snow camp, deer/crow/wolf, bone-and-moss still life, foraged basket, lean-to or cabin exterior, dim plant-and-bone interior, worn outfit or flat-lay collage, protest poster or pin close-up, hooded ritual figure, reclaimed urban garden plot. Composition: wide landscape with miniature figure, tight still life, centered tableau, collage flat lay, gear portrait. Lighting: flat overcast, fog glow, dusk firelight, night flash, dim window light. Color handling: full desaturated earth range, near-monochrome grey-black, or dark field with one flat print ink. Mood: bleak desolation, quiet reverence, defiant anger, uncanny longing. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Pastels and candy saturation; golden-hour warmth and lens flare; manicured gardens, flower meadows, picnics, gingham domesticity; cute anthropomorphic mushrooms or kawaii animals; cozy hygge interiors with string lights, cream knit piles, cocoa-as-hero; crisp alpine or fjord travel photography with blue skies and turquoise water; neon, chrome, glass, glossy 3D renders, luxury logos, clean minimalism; high-tech plastic gear in bright safety colors; slaughter gore (bone is fine, blood is not); smiling direct-to-camera influencer posing; tropical or desert flora.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cabincore** centers the cabin's cozy interior — warm lamplight, wool throws, cocoa. Forestpunk's cabin is a working shell in hostile weather; its interiors stay dim, furnished with tools, moss, bones, and houseplants, and the style always carries labor and politics that Cabincore lacks.
- **Mori Kei** is soft, layered, feminine woodland fashion in cream and oat tones with flowing skirts. Forestpunk dress is utilitarian, dark, ungendered, mud-rated: flannel, overalls, heavy coats, broken-in boots.
- **Mushroomcore** makes fungi the whimsical protagonist, warm-capped and often illustrated cutely. Forestpunk mushrooms are damp floor detail among deadfall; bone and antler outweigh cap, and the mood is gloom, not charm.
- **Northerness** offers Nordic/Arctic vastness with cool blue-white light and scenic clarity. Forestpunk is temperate and claustrophobic: fog instead of clarity, brown-green instead of ice-blue, intimate woods instead of panoramic fjords.
- **Cottagecore** is pastoral, domesticated, sunlit — baking, gardens, romance. Forestpunk shares plants and cabins but never the prettiness: overcast sky, raw deadfall, survival skills, and an anti-consumerist stance replace domestic bliss.
- Against **Gloomcore/Cryptidcore**, the tell is practice: where those styles supply mood alone, Forestpunk adds the basket, the axe, the mended patch, the protest pin — nature not just felt but lived off and defended.
