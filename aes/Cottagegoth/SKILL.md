# Cottagegoth

Cottagegoth — also called Cottagegore or Dark Cottagecore — is an internet aesthetic of the early 2020s that keeps Cottagecore's devotion to rural life, nature and traditionalism but withdraws the sunlight. It moves the idyll into autumn and winter, into rain, fog and thunderstorm gloom, and finds beauty in imperfection, decay and danger: overgrown forests, poisonous flora, animal bones, rusted cemeteries. Its central archetype is the solitary witch or hermit living in harmony with a powerful, destructive version of nature, a figure borrowed from dark fairy tales and folklore in which the natural world is not a gentle provider but a formidable entity commanding reverence. Its cultural shelf holds folk-horror cinema (from *The Company of Wolves* through *The Witch* and *Midsommar*), animated melancholy like *Over the Garden Wall*, and the archaic instrumentation of dark folk and neoclassical darkwave such as Dead Can Dance. Visually it is a quiet, photographic style: grainy, damp, desaturated, intimate in scale even when the landscape is vast.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette is muted earth tones only: faded brown, moss green, grey and black, with bone-cream and fog-white as the light end. Colour interest comes from value contrast and texture, never from hue saturation.

| Role | Hex band | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow black | `#141311`–`#22201B` | conifer interiors, wool coats, pond water, cabinet wood |
| Bark / faded brown | `#4A3B2C`–`#6E5B44` | trunks, weathered shingles, old books, soil |
| Leaf-litter rust | `#6B4A33`–`#8A6A45` | forest floor, bracken, rusted iron |
| Moss / lichen green | `#3E4A34`–`#5A6B4A` | bark patterns, logs, grave stones, duckweed |
| Deep conifer | `#24302A`–`#2E3B30` | pine walls, hedge masses |
| Fog / storm grey | `#6E7470`–`#A8ADAA` | mist planes, overcast sky, rain haze |
| Bone / cream / pale bark | `#C9BFA8`–`#DDD6C6` | skulls, antler, mushrooms, birch bark, unbleached linen, white shifts |
| Candle / lamp amber | `#B98A4A`–`#D8A860` | small practical sources only: a bulb, a candle ring |

Rule: keep saturation low (roughly under a quarter); reserve the brightest values for fog, bone, pale bark and linen; allow amber only as a tiny point source inside darkness. A single subdued floral accent — a pale rose on a dark shed, white petals on black water — is permissible precisely because everything around it is drained.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the style documents them

1. **Weather as subject** — fog, rain, thunderstorm gloom. Mist is not background; it is the compositional material that erases distance and silhouettes trees and deer.
2. **Dark forests and dense woods** — conifer walls, gnarled leafless orchards, twisted branches over mossy ground.
3. **Overgrown gardens and rural cemeteries** — vegetation reclaiming fences, gates, headstones, sheds; roses climbing a rotting roof.
4. **Poisonous and dangerous flora** — fungi and small mushrooms on mossy fallen logs, thorny brambles, carnivorous plants, hemlock-like umbellifers.
5. **Animal bones** — skulls, antlers, ribcages laid among ferns and moss with the tenderness of a bouquet.
6. **Witchcraft elements** — candle rings and petal circles on the forest floor, mirrors held over dark water, dried herbs, the solitary witch or hermit herself.
7. **Rustic vernacular architecture in decay** — cottages, huts, sheds barely visible under moss and climbers, swallowed by woods.
8. **Still, dark water** — ponds, bogs, puddles, rain-soaked ground; duckweed sheets, reflections of fog in slow streams, a bather among lily pads.
9. **Domestic macabre still lifes** — memento-mori shelves and cabinets: skulls on brass stands, glass cloches over taxidermy birds, oval frames, stacked antiquarian books under a single warm lamp. This interior mode is the style's gothic-domestic register (it touches Vulture Culture), and it balances the outdoor landscapes.
10. **Folkloric figures** — a child in a white nightgown facing something tall and black between bare trunks; a walker in a long dark coat seen from behind; a woman braided and half-submerged in a pond mirror. Figures are presences, never portraits.

### Materials and textures

Moss, lichen, damp bark, rotting log wood, leaf litter, thorn and bramble, fern and duckweed; bone, antler, tooth; tarnished brass, blown glass domes, candle wax, old paper and leather bindings; weathered shingles, rusted nails, iron gates; wool, linen, corduroy, lace, hand-stitched seams. Everything is matte, damp or dusty. Nothing is glossy, new, or machine-finished.

### Figures and fashion

When people appear, they appear alone. The wardrobe is Cottagecore cut through a gothic lens: long flowy black dresses and skirts, dark overalls, layered wool coats with large functional pockets, lace trims and puffy sleeves, simple handcrafted accessories — practical clothing for an isolated rural life, rendered in the muted palette. A plain white shift or nightgown is the accepted counter-note, used for folkloric contrast (the child, the bather) against black cloth and dark woods. Poses are turned away, obscured, half-submerged, or dwarfed by weather; faces are rarely the point.

### Composition and lighting

Fog functions as negative space: layered mist planes flatten depth and let a single tree, deer or figure read as silhouette. Figures and cottages sit small inside vast weather; conversely, intimate macro frames — a log of mushrooms, a bark map of lichen, a shelf of skulls — carry the same mood at hand scale. Framing through trunks, branches and doorways is common; vertical forest formats and low horizons swallowed by mist recur; still lifes and cabinet scenes allow centered, symmetrical, altar-like arrangements. Reflections — still water, old mirrors — double the subject quietly.

Light is diffuse and shadowless: overcast scatter, fog glow, twilight blue-grey, thunderstorm dimness. Indoors, a single warm incandescent bulb or a ring of candles provides the only amber point against dark wood. There is no direct sun, no hard shadow, no flash, no studio key.

### Medium feel

The style reads as analog film: visible grain, muted contrast with blacks lifted toward grey or green, a faint sepia or moss cast, softness from moisture and mist, the unhurried framing of a personal snapshot rather than a polished editorial scan. Digital crispness, HDR clarity and saturated grading break the spell.

## Mood & Values

Cottagegoth values solitude, decay and rebirth, and an appreciation for the darker aspects of nature. Its feeling is melancholic reverence rather than fear: dread is atmospheric and slow, never a jump scare. Decay is framed as part of a cycle — bones feed moss, roses eat a rotting roof — so the macabre reads tender, even cozy in its dark way. The aesthetic mixes pastoralism with the macabre in every frame: romance and melancholy held together, nature honoured as a formidable entity, imperfection treated as the only honest beauty.

## Era & Origins

An early-2020s internet microgenre, born as the shadow sibling of Cottagecore: it keeps the predecessor's rural traditionalism while reacting against its sunny, tidy idyll. Its deeper lineage is older — folk-horror and rural-horror cinema from the 1980s onward, dark fairy tales and gothic folklore, and the dark-folk / neoclassical-darkwave music tradition of archaic instrumentation. It overlaps online communities such as Witchcore, Vulture Culture, Goblincore and Fairy Grunge, borrowing the solitary witch archetype from one and the bone-and-curiosity domesticity from another.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Decide the weather before the subject: fog, rain, or twilight gloom is the canvas.
2. Pair one pastoral element with one macabre element in every image — rose on rot, bone in moss, lace on black wool, candle ring in leaf litter.
3. Clamp saturation; let value contrast (bone-white against near-black conifer) carry the image.
4. Keep figures solitary, turned away, obscured, or small against towering trees and mist.
5. Age everything: moss, lichen, rust, tarnish, rot, faded dye. Nothing new enters the frame.
6. Light diffusely — overcast scatter or a single warm point source in darkness.
7. Finish with analog grain, lifted blacks, a green or sepia cast, and moisture softness.
8. Let nature tower: low angles into canopies, fog erasing the horizon, human scale humbled.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: misted landscape or treeline; macro of fungi, lichen, thorns, antler; still life of skulls, cloches, books, candles; figure in dark layers or white shift; decaying cottage, shed, graveyard gate; animal encounter (wolf, deer silhouette, crow at the edge); pond, bog, bather.
- *Composition*: vast horizontal weather or vertical forest; intimate close-up or altar-like centered still life; framing through trunks and doorways; reflection doubles.
- *Lighting*: flat overcast, fog glow, blue-grey twilight, storm dimness, candle-or-bulb interior warmth.
- *Colour handling*: near-monochrome grey, moss-dominant green, leaf-litter brown dominance, bone-white accent placement; amber only as a point source.
- *Season and mood*: late autumn through winter to thawing early spring; tender melancholy at one end, quiet dread at the other.
- *Medium mimicry*: 35mm grain, muted medium-format tone, scanned snapshot candour.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette, weather, decay, solitude, macabre-pastoral pairing — not any reference image. A thunderstorm over a fenced pasture, a pressed-poison-plant herbarium page, or a rain-streaked cottage window with bones on the sill are all valid Cottagegoth even though no reference shows them; the text documents storms and poisonous flora directly, while interior memento-mori arrangements are firmly evidenced as the style's domestic register.

**Avoid**

- Sunlit golden hour, blue sky, spring blossom cheer, saturated hues of any kind.
- Manicured gardens, fresh paint, tidy farms, glossy modern objects, plastic, screens, cars, signage.
- Gore, blood splatter, zombies, monsters, jump-scare horror staging.
- Crowds, cult circles, daylight festival rites — community terror belongs to Folk Horror.
- Campus halls, libraries, tweed, blackboards, classical statues — Dark Academia territory.
- Castles, armour, dragons, visible spell effects, high-contrast epic lighting — Dark Fantasy territory.
- Scavenger clutter dominated by corvids, roadkill and rusted trash as the whole subject — that centre of gravity is Crowcore.
- Hyper-sharp HDR digital clarity, teal-orange grades, neon, flash photography.
- Cozy baking, picnics, honey-warm linen cheer — unmodified Cottagecore.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | How it differs | Cottagegoth tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Folk Horror** | Narrative dread of community, pagan cults and rites; can be bright daylight with flowers and white clothes; terror aimed at an intruder | No cult crowds or festival brightness; dread is atmospheric and solitary; includes domestic still lifes, fashion and fog landscapes Folk Horror never centres |
| **Cottagecore** | Same rural grammar but sunny, warm, tidy: golden light, spring and summer, baking, picnics, honey and pastel tones | Remove the macabre element and if the image stays cozy, it is Cottagecore; Cottagegoth always keeps bone, poison, fog or black cloth in frame |
| **Crowcore** | Scavenger aesthetic centred on corvids, roadkill, collected bones as loot, rusted metal and cluttered sheds; grittier and drier | Bones lie in moss as reverence, not trophy; fog, vegetation and melancholic romance dominate; crows optional, never the thesis |
| **Dark Academia** | Institutional interiors: lecture halls, libraries, tweed, candlelit desks, classical antiquity, urban campus autumn | Setting is wild or rural — woods, bogs, graveyards, cottages; books appear only as props on a curiosity shelf, never as study culture |
| **Dark Fantasy** | Illustrative spectacle: castles, armour, heroes, overt magic with glowing effects, high-contrast cinematic light | Grounded, photographic, domestic scale; magic only implied — a candle ring, a mirror over water — never shown as effect |

One further border: Witchcore puts the craft itself first (altars, tools, sigils, often warmer interiors), whereas Cottagegoth subordinates witchcraft to landscape, weather and decay — the witch is a silhouette in fog, not a practitioner at her bench.
