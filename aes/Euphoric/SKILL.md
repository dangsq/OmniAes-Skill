# Euphoric

Euphoric is the visual language of HBO's *Euphoria* (2019–) as it escaped the screen and became a Gen Z culture: a collection of character wardrobes and makeup looks unified by one cinematographic mood — dark, moody, glamorous, emotionally charged. Its foundation is stylized night lighting in saturated blues, purples and magentas that blurs the border between external reality and a character's inner state; its most famous invention is makeup as diary, above all the glitter tear. The style spread through TikTok and Instagram, so its native artifacts include vertical crops, collage grids, grain and timestamp overlays. It is not one look but a family of looks (Y2K it-girl, skater grunge, anime punk, vamp, cutesy, preppy) held together by color, light, glitter and feeling.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule: the frame is lit, not merely tinted. At least two saturated key colors from the core set act as light sources, shadows stay colored rather than neutral gray, and white appears only as specular sparkle, neon tube or starburst flare — never as ambient daylight.

| Role | Range |
|---|---|
| Shadow base | near-black indigo `#070312` – `#140A26` |
| Violet / purple body | `#3B1E6E` – `#7A3CC9` |
| Magenta / fuchsia | `#B01E8C` – `#E437B4` |
| Hot pink highlight | `#FF4FA8` – `#FF8AD4` |
| Electric blue / cyan counter-light | `#1E3AE0` – `#37E6FF` |
| Pastel minority note (cutesy / anime-punk wardrobes) | baby blue `#BFE3F2`, blush `#FFC9DE`, mint `#A8E8D0` |
| Prism overlay | full-spectrum rainbow at low opacity, as leak or refraction only |

Acceptable handling spans a monochrome purple wash, a blue-versus-magenta split duotone, and full neon multicolor (pink, blue, green, violet) sparkling against black in fairy-light scenes. Pastels may appear as clothing or accent inside a saturated frame; they never set the overall key.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Glitter tears** — rhinestones, gems, pearls and chunky glitter placed as tear tracks or constellations around and below the eyes; the text names this the most iconic element of the style.
2. **Dramatic colored lighting** — gel washes, neon tubes, practical string lights, haze catching beams; moody chiaroscuro in deep blue, purple, magenta.
3. **Graphic neon makeup** — bold sharp eyeliner wings, blocks of vibrant neon shadow, glossy lids and lips, star and pearl appliqués on skin.
4. **Y2K / McBling cut-out fashion** — body-con dresses and matching sets with dramatic cut-outs, halter and strap details (the I.AM.GIA register).
5. **Skater grunge layering** — oversized t-shirts, vintage workwear, the burgundy zip-up hoodie; androgynous, lo-fi silhouette.
6. **Sheer anime-punk layering** — mesh tops, tulle, stacked translucent fabrics, pastel stacks with a 90s pop-punk / Sailor Moon playfulness.
7. **Vamp hardware** — corsets, leather, harness straps, chokers.
8. **Counter-wardrobes** — cutesy baby blue and soft pink cut-out swimwear; preppy twee tartan, Peter Pan collars, knitted cardigans with red lipstick. Text-documented, but rare in the surviving image record.
9. **Night set-dressing** — butterfly and star fairy-light strings, tinsel fringe and sequin backdrops, neon-lit stairwells and bedrooms, fogged party floors, angel wings as prop.
10. **Social-edit layer** — vertical crops, grid collages, caption and date-stamp overlays, film grain, chromatic fringing; image-evidenced and consistent with the style's TikTok/Instagram home.

### Materials and textures

Holographic and chunky glitter, rhinestones, pearl beads, sequin cloth, metallic tinsel fringe, sheer mesh and tulle, feather boas, leather and harness straps, glossy vinyl lips, dewy skin with hard specular highlights, plastic butterfly diffusers on light strings, chrome and iridescent film. Sparkle must behave like light: four-point starburst diffraction flares, bokeh orbs, soft bloom — not flat sticker shapes.

### Recurring subjects

Face fragments carrying emotion (eyes, lips, cheekbones, glittered fingers over a mouth); full portraits wearing narrative makeup; reclining or dancing figures on sequin surfaces; party crowds reduced to silhouettes in haze; bedroom and club interiors as shrines of light; object still lifes of fairy lights, tinsel and gems; winged figures; night exteriors crossed by prism leaks.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Extreme macro fragment crops at one end, hazy environmental wides at the other, with intimate medium portraits between; overhead reclining angles; silhouettes against colored backlight; grid collages; vertical social ratios; implied sweep and motion through blur, drag-shutter streaks and off-axis framing (the show's dynamic camera work can only be suggested in stills). Lighting is one or two saturated gel sources, often split blue against magenta, with neon tubes or string lights visible in frame, fog to volumize beams, and blacks kept deep and colored.

## Mood & Values

Emotional expression, self-discovery, rebellion, vulnerability, drama. The style is dark, glamorous and emotionally charged, dreamlike to the point of psychedelia: outer reality dissolves into inner state. Its signature tension is joy and grief in one frame — crying and sparkling simultaneously, ecstasy with an ache underneath, teen interiority staged at operatic scale. Critics accuse it of aestheticizing trauma; that charge points at the style's actual engine, the fusion of beauty and pain. In generation, imply the ache through light, makeup and expression rather than depicting harm literally.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s, crystallizing with *Euphoria* from 2019: its cinematography, Heidi Bivens's costume design and Doniella Davy's makeup department. It became a Gen Z touchstone via TikTok and Instagram, assembling Y2K and McBling revival, 90s pop-punk and anime references, skater grunge and goth-vamp hardware into one mood. It reacts against conformist, beautification-only makeup by turning cosmetics into emotional storytelling — maximalist, non-conformist, diaristic.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:

1. **Light is the substrate.** Choose the gel palette before the subject; saturate the shadows, keep blacks colored, let practicals (neon tubes, string lights) glow in frame.
2. **Glitter means something.** Place sparkle as emotion — tear tracks, cheekbone constellations, glitter on a laughing or crying face — never as neutral decoration.
3. **Hold the duality.** Every high carries an ache; every ache carries a sparkle.
4. **Gloss plus grain.** Dewy specular skin and glossy lips, finished with film grain, soft bloom and slight chromatic fringe so flares read as optics.
5. **Night logic.** Interiors and exteriors read as after-dark; haze, fog and bokeh are welcome weather.
6. **Fashion as character.** Pick one wardrobe register (Y2K cut-out, skater grunge, sheer pastel punk, vamp leather, cutesy pastel, preppy twee) and let it inflect the frame while the lighting stays in the key palette.
7. **Social-native finish is optional.** Vertical crop, collage grid, timestamp or caption overlay may appear, or not.

Variation axes:

- **Subject:** face fragment, full portrait, dancing or reclining figure, crowd silhouette, bedroom/club interior, object still life of lights and tinsel, winged or costumed figure, night landscape with prism leak. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
- **Composition:** macro to wide, overhead, silhouette, centered or off-axis, single frame or grid collage, motion blur optional.
- **Lighting:** split gels, single neon practical, string-light bokeh, UV/blacklight glow, prism leak; haze amount free.
- **Color handling:** monochrome purple wash, blue/magenta duotone, multicolor neon on black, pastel minority accent inside a saturated frame; rainbow only as leak or refraction.
- **Mood:** ecstatic, tender, melancholic, defiant, dreamlike; smile, tears, or both.
- **Medium feel:** cinematic still, lo-fi phone edit, or collage zine.

Avoid:

- Daylight, golden hour, neutral white balance, beige or earth-tone dominance.
- Matte minimalist "clean" makeup, nude lips, bare unglossed skin without glitter or gems.
- Harsh direct on-camera flash with desaturated murk (the indie-sleaze documentary look).
- Faded brown-green analog decay, flannel-and-concrete grunge palettes.
- McBling consumer gloss: pastel-pink studio product shots, rhinestone logos, bling on merchandise instead of on skin as feeling.
- Flat, even, catalog-style studio lighting.
- Pure black goth with no saturated color light.
- Sterile vector sparkles or emoji glitter; confetti scatter in bright light.
- Warm-white cozy domestic lighting or candlelit amber as the key.
- Literal depiction of drugs, violence or self-harm; the style implies ache through beauty.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | What separates Euphoric |
|---|---|
| Grunge | Grunge is desaturated earth tones, flannel, distress and apathy; Euphoric borrows the loose silhouette but floods it with neon gel light and glitter. Grunge never cries in rhinestones. |
| Indie Sleaze | Indie sleaze is harsh direct flash, murky neutrals, cigarette-smoke candor; Euphoric is color-graded, dreamlike, staged emotion with saturated gels, bloom and starburst flares. |
| McBling | McBling puts bling on objects (phones, logos, tracksuits) in daylight or studio gloss; Euphoric puts rhinestones on skin as feeling under moody purple-magenta light. |
| Sadgirl | Sadgirl is muted pastel, matte, quiet lo-fi melancholia; Euphoric's sadness is saturated, glossy and theatrical — grief performed in glitter under magenta light. |
| Skater | Skater is daylight concrete, fisheye documentary, functional streetwear; Euphoric's skater pieces appear only at night under colored light, paired with sparkle makeup. |

Quick recognition: glitter tear tracks or rhinestone constellations on glossy skin, purple-magenta gel light with deep colored shadows, fairy-light bokeh or tinsel sparkle, night haze — if these co-occur with an emotionally charged expression, the image is Euphoric. Note honestly: the surviving image record skews toward makeup and light studies and party atmospheres, so the vamp and preppy wardrobes rest on textual documentation, and the show's sweeping camera motion can only be implied in stills through blur and dynamic framing.
