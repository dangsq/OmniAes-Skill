# Buffalo Style

Buffalo is less a look than a styling methodology: an underground London collective around stylist Ray Petri (active 1984–1989) that treated clothing as raw material for character-building. Its images juxtapose high-fashion tailoring with utilitarian sportswear, military surplus and non-Western traditional dress — oversized Armani-type blazers pinned and clamped into new silhouettes over boxer shorts, white T-shirts and vintage denim; wool kilts and leather skirts on men; MA-1 flight jackets as uniform. The name comes from Caribbean slang for a rebel or "rude boy," echoed in Bob Marley's "Buffalo Soldier." Photography was structural to the movement: 35mm film, high-contrast black and white or sepia, simple matte studio backdrops, a reportage candor descended from Henri Cartier-Bresson that strips away geography and leaves only garment texture and wearer attitude. Spread through *The Face*, *i-D* and *Arena*, Buffalo invented the modern fashion stylist as auteur and put multicultural, street-cast faces — Nick Kamen, Naomi Campbell, Neneh Cherry, Barry Kamen, Felix Howard — into a British press previously dominated by Eurocentric gloss.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** The frame is monochrome-first: ink black and paper white carry most images, with sepia and warm beige as the dominant tonal wash when color or toned prints appear. Olive drab and indigo sit underneath as the utilitarian layer (flight jacket nylon, denim), and muted grey or beige serves as the anonymous backdrop. Saturated color is rare and small-area — a red scarf, a tartan flash, a passing car — never a field of color.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them.**
1. *Altered tailoring*: oversized blazers and suits pinned, clamped and clipped to reshape the silhouette; layered over cheap mass-produced basics (white tee, boxer shorts, vintage denim).
2. *MA-1 flight jacket* as collective uniform, frequently embroidered on the back.
3. *Skirts on men*: heavy wool kilts, leather skirts, skirts layered over trousers — informed by Petri's Scottish heritage and African travels.
4. *Hats everywhere*: porkpie, bowler, fedora, cowboy, captain's cap, beret — often personalized with a word pinned or printed on the brim (the March 1985 *Face* cover's "Killer" brim codified this), plus badges, medals, rosettes and feathers on crown or lapel.
5. *American West iconography*: cowboy hats, bolo ties, fringed jackets, dropped against urban British sportswear.
6. *Sportswear intrusion*: tracksuits, sneakers, boxer shorts mixed into couture-grade layers.
7. *Grounded footwear*: Dr. Martens boots and heavy work shoes, often with rolled white socks; cropped trousers showing ankle.
8. *Street-cast bodies*: diverse ethnicities, youth wearing adult masculine codes, androgyny — bare male chests under open coats, earrings, a star drawn on a cheek; women in stripped black separates with heavy ethnic silver or in borrowed menswear.
9. *Matte studio backdrop*: seamless paper or painted canvas, sometimes with raw taped edges visible; occasionally an anonymous street instead.

**Materials and textures.** Wool suiting and tartan, satin lapels and waistcoats, nylon flight-jacket shell, leather skirts and boot leather, cotton jersey, rigid denim, suede fringe, lace veils and antique jewelry as occasional counterpoint. Texture is the subject: the light is arranged so fabric weight reads.

**Composition and lighting.** Vertical portrait format dominates. Full-body head-to-shoe framing (footwear is meaning), three-quarter figures, or tight head-and-hat close-ups. Subjects planted center-weighted with negative space around them, gaze deadpan into lens or turned in profile; hands in pockets or holding a removed hat; stances wide and grounded, or caught mid-stride in reportage frames. Duos and trios cluster tightly, bodies overlapping. Lighting is flat and even on seamless backdrops with a soft vignette, or hard direct flash outdoors; monochrome contrast runs from crushed blacks to soft sepia mid-greys. Visible 35mm grain throughout; no digital crispness.

## Mood & Values

Cool, defiant stillness. The "hard" look is a symbol of self-reliance and coolness — the urban outsider posed with unbothered authority. Values encoded in the frame: multiculturalism (a pan-cultural cast against Eurocentric beauty standards), androgyny (garments crossing gender and cultural codes), independence, and "style over fashion" — character constructed by manipulation of clothing rather than commercial display of designer goods. There is tenderness inside the hardness: youth in adult clothes, bare skin under heavy wool, feathers and roses pinned to severe black.

## Era & Origins

London, 1984–1989, formed around Ray Petri (Scottish-born, via Australia) with photographers Jamie Morgan, Cameron McVey, Roger Charity and Marc Lebon. It reacted against the glossy, designer-centric fashion photography of the era and against agency modeling, distributing instead through independent style magazines (*The Face*, *i-D*, *Arena*). Related media: Neneh Cherry's "Buffalo Stance" video and the 1985 Levi's "Launderette" commercial. The collective dissolved after Petri's death in 1989, but its layering logic fed Gaultier and Yamamoto runways into the 1990s and formalized the freelance stylist profession.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Collide at least two incompatible wardrobe languages in one silhouette: Savile Row-style tailoring + military surplus + sportswear + traditional skirt/kilt + Western gear.
2. Alter, don't display: pin, clamp, roll, crop, layer. Oversized shoulders cinched at the waist, trousers cropped above the ankle, skirt over trousers, socks rolled — the outfit must look authored, not bought.
3. Crown the head: nearly every look carries a hat, personalized with a pinned word, badge, medal or feather.
4. Ground the foot in heavy leather boots or work shoes; show the ankle.
5. Cast for attitude, not beauty: street-cast faces, any ethnicity, youth in adult codes, androgyny; deadpan stare, no smile, no glamour pout.
6. Shoot like 1985: 35mm grain, high-contrast black and white, sepia, or muted color; matte seamless backdrop or anonymous street; reportage candor even in studio.
7. Style over fashion: no visible logos, no head-to-toe single-designer coherence.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo male, female or androgynous figure, duo or trio group; child-youth through adult; shirtless or fully layered; any ethnicity. Composition: full-body vertical, three-quarter, tight head-and-hat crop; centered or offset; planted or mid-stride; clustered group. Lighting: flat studio with soft vignette, hard flash reportage, soft window light; contrast from crushed mono to gentle sepia. Color handling: pure black-and-white, sepia toning, or muted color confined to black/white/olive/indigo/beige with at most one small saturated accent. Mood: defiant deadpan, cool swagger, tender androgyny, playful defiance in groups. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — collage logic, altered silhouette, headwear, deadpan attitude, filmic mono/muted frame — not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Neon, candy or saturated club color; glitter, sequins, PVC. Rips, safety pins as jewelry, mohawks, anarchic distress (Buffalo pins to tailor, not to destroy). Frills, satin pirate shirts, blushed dandy decadence. Glossy commercial polish: retouched skin, digital crispness, logo placement, catalog smiles, sexualized couture poses. Coherent single-code costume (a proper rude-boy suit, a proper cowboy outfit) — the mix is mandatory. Narrative locations: interiors, landmarks or scenic backgrounds that explain who and where; the backdrop must stay anonymous. Delicate footwear such as stiletto heels. Modern tech or post-1990s sportswear silhouettes.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Club Kids:** neon saturation, glitter, cartoon costume, dance-floor frenzy. Buffalo is muted monochrome, utilitarian fabric, planted stillness, editorial studio calm.
- **New Romantic:** Blitz-club peacockry — satin, frills, historicist pirate-dandy costume, heavy eyeliner, color and sheen. Buffalo swaps theater for street-cast realism: matte wool, nylon, denim; attitude over decadence.
- **Punk:** destruction as statement — rips, safety pins, bondage straps, mohawks, snarling confrontation. Buffalo is construction as statement: intact garments re-engineered by pinning and clipping, composed deadpan face, curated collage; its pins reshape silhouettes rather than sabotage them.
- **Rude Boy:** a coherent Jamaican subcultural uniform — sharp thin-lapel suit, thin tie, porkpie hat, polished shoes, worn as identity. Buffalo borrows the hat and the stance but breaks the code with kilts, flight jackets, boxer shorts, layered skirts and multicultural editorial casting; rude boy is lived dress, Buffalo is styled authorship.
- **Americana (adjacent):** cowboy hats, bolo ties and fringe appear only as one ingredient inside the collage, never as a full Western costume; the surrounding tailoring and sportswear keep it Buffalo.
