# Goreweb

Goreweb, also called Chaoscore, AGC Style or LiveLeakcore, is a 2020s internet aesthetic and a visual subgenre of Opium. Its entire premise is recontextualization: the low-resolution gory visual language of mid-2000s shock sites (LiveLeak, Rotten.com), creepypasta and cursed-image culture is lifted out of its horror context and worn as a luxury streetwear brand filter. It was pioneered underground by Yabujin, the Reptilian Club Boyz and nana825763 (the Xpiritualism / HexD lineage), then catapulted mainstream in 2023 through Ken Carson's album *A Great Chaos* and the curated cursed-image feed of his private Instagram, shaped by creative director Nick Spiders, whose stated brief was images "louder, crazier, bloodier" than standard hip-hop imagery. Where Traumacore uses the same shock imagery to express vulnerability, Goreweb uses it to project aggression, desensitization and a chaotic rockstar lifestyle; where Yabujin used it for surrealism and nostalgia, Goreweb commodifies it into an edgy brand voice. The feed doubles as shitposting: music promotion and meme garbage are deliberately indistinguishable.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The chord is blood red on bitcrushed black, with white as either blowout or field. Red is the only saturated warm hue permitted; everything else is desaturated, crushed, or cold.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Arterial / dried blood red | `#6E0000` – `#E60000` | Watermark text, splatter, outline glows, banner blocks, katakana captions |
| Bitcrushed black | `#050505` – `#161616` | Default field; shadows collapsed to one value with visible banding |
| Bone / paper white | `#EDEDED` – `#FFFFFF` | Flash blowout, mask faces, or flat shitpost background |
| Thermal / night-vision green | `#1F4D2E` – `#39FF6A` | Optional flat cast; canonical but used sparingly |
| Cold cyan / electric blue | `#00B4D8` – `#2A3CFF` | Glowing monitors, portals, night-vision counterpoint |
| Concrete grey | `#3F3F3F` – `#8C8C8C` | Desaturated industrial backdrops: stairwells, boiler rooms, rooftops |

A correct image may sit anywhere in this range: near-monochrome black-and-white carrying only a red watermark, red-on-white splatter merch graphics, or a green-cast night-vision frame all read as Goreweb.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Shock-site watermarks and UI fossils.** LiveLeak and "Unregistered Hypercam 2" stamps, handle watermarks (`@agreatchaos`-style) in italic serif red, tiled in rows across the frame or banded top and bottom; Windows-9x dialog boxes, title bars, pixel buttons, timestamp counters.
2. **Fake gore.** Digitally added splatter vectors, smeared blood on hands and walls, organ and brain cutouts as collage clippings — always obviously composited, never photographic-real.
3. **Degradation as ornament.** Heavy JPEG macroblocking, oversharpening halos, dithering, bitcrush banding; the image must look like a forbidden 2005 video file.
4. **Slam Metal / grindcore logotypes.** Illegible, spiky, bilaterally symmetrical crests resembling piles of branches or veins, set above a figure or on a chest.
5. **Medical and dental horror.** X-ray film, dentures and teeth close-ups, vampire grills, and the gimp-mask circles of Rick Owens puffer jackets juxtaposed with the gore.
6. **Cult-film and creepypasta quotations.** Oldboy (2003) violence and captivity stagings (rooftop dangles, corridors), hollow-eyed Jeff the Killer / Mereana Mordegard Glesgorv faces, nods to nana825763's *username666*.
7. **Cursed selfies and distorted portraits.** Flash-burned, warped, masked faces; streetwear figures whose heads are replaced by white gimp-mask or balaclava ovals.
8. **Merch as canvas.** Black tees, all-over-print shirts, backpacks presented as degraded product mockups with red outer-glow outlines.
9. **Early-3D and flash-game renders.** Dithered low-poly corridors, Source-engine gloom, prone ragdoll bodies, one glowing cyan doorway.
10. **Grotesque cartoon silhouettes.** Flat black blob-figures with dot eyes on plain white, paired with condensed stencil sans logos — the deadpan shitpost register of the style.

Honest note on evidence: the infobox names thermal green as a key colour, but surviving frames skew red/black with cyan accents; anime-styled girls with hollow black eyes and red-blown palettes recur heavily in the image corpus although the text only implies them through the creepypasta lineage. Both are legitimate registers.

### Materials, textures, subjects, composition, lighting

Textures are digital rot: JPEG crunch, scanline-ish noise, dither grids, cut-and-paste lasso edges with white or red fringe, splatter stamped over brick and concrete, red glow halos around product shots. Subjects range across apparel mockups, masked streetwear crews, anime horror faces, medical clippings, Oldboy tableaus, dithered game renders, collage moodboard grids of six or more cursed tiles, and black-on-white cartoon shitposts. Composition tends to one of four structures: centered merch mockup; full-bleed degraded scene with watermark bands top and bottom; crowded collage grid; or asymmetric corner-logo on empty white. Lighting is never soft: flash-burn overexposure, flat night-vision or thermal cast, concrete gloom lit by a single emissive source (cyan monitor, red exit glow), or ironic product-shot rim glow. There is no three-point studio light and no golden hour.

## Mood & Values

Shock value, aggression, desensitization, chaos, digital decay. The persona is a nihilist rockstar scrolling past horror with a shrug; irony is armor, and clout is the point. Goreweb values loudness over taste, rot over polish, and brand cohesion over sincerity — it wants the viewer unsure whether this is music promo, fashion campaign or shitpost. It explicitly rejects vulnerability and earnestness.

## Era & Origins

2020s, mainstreamed 2023–present via Ken Carson's *A Great Chaos* cycle on Instagram and TikTok; underground blueprint from the Yabujin / RCB / nana825763 era of Web 1.0 horror, flash-game nostalgia and Jumpstyle Revival (Xpiritualism, HexD, the "Azeroy" mix of medical gore with ethereal digital nostalgia). Its source stratum is mid-2000s shock-site media. It reacts against clean hip-hop editorial imagery and, per its critics, against the underground scenes it borrowed from — accused of biting Xpiritualism and of trivializing real violence for clout. That controversy is part of its identity: a style aware it is a stolen, compressed, resold ghost.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- **Degrade last, degrade hard.** Build the image, then crush it: macroblocks, sharpening halos, dither, banding. Pristine output is not Goreweb.
- **Watermark as jewelry.** Overlay handle text, shock-site stamps or tiled red serif rows; treat typography as texture.
- **Keep the red-on-black chord.** One saturated warm hue (blood red); all else crushed, grey, white, or cold green/cyan.
- **Gore must read fake.** Splatter vectors, composited clippings, cartoon blood — digital artifice, never documentary realism.
- **Brand-frame everything.** Even horror should look like merch, promo or a curated feed post: metal logo crest, product glow, shirt print.
- **Mix registers freely.** Fashion photo + shock-site JPEG + shitpost cartoon can coexist in one frame.

Variation axes: subject (apparel, selfie, anime face, game render, medical plate, film tableau, cartoon silhouette, collage grid); composition (minimal white-field shitpost ↔ maximal collage grid); lighting (flash blowout ↔ night-vision flat ↔ single-glow dark); color handling (red/black/white core, optional green or cyan accent, optional near-monochrome); degradation intensity (light JPEG crunch ↔ total bitcrush, including clean vector silhouette mode when paired with brand type); mood (confrontational aggression ↔ deadpan irony). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: pristine 4K clarity, soft bokeh or gradient studio light; pastel Traumacore softness (plushies, pink, handwritten tenderness); vaporwave neon grids and sunsets; analog film grain, light leaks or dust (artifacts must be digital); legible elegant fashion-house serif branding; golden-hour or natural outdoor warmth; photographic-real gore; modern rounded flat UI (iOS-style chrome); wholesome candid warmth; earthy folk-horror props and organic decay; cohesive professional editorial photography with no degradation or watermark intervention.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Haunted Mound** is analog and rural: sepia and mud-toned VHS folk horror, graveyard mounds, physical sculptural props, southern-gothic dread. Goreweb is digital-native, urban-industrial, red-on-black, watermarked and merch-bound.
- **Opium** is Goreweb's sleek parent: polished black leather futurism, rave strobes, clean high-contrast fashion photography. Opium is gloss; Goreweb is rot — compression, splatter, metal logos.
- **Xpiritualism** (Yabujin) keeps the ethereal: iridescent pastel CGI, dreamy Web 1.0 nostalgia, surreal serenity. Goreweb strips the dreaminess and keeps only aggression and shock signifiers.
- **Deep Fried Meme** fries for comedy: emoji stamps, lens flares, caption text, bright yellow-red saturation, mainstream meme formats. Goreweb fries for horror-fashion, uses watermarks and slam-metal type instead of joke captions, and holds a black field.
- **Digital Horror** is the broad dread genre: analog horror, liminal CGI, uncanny renders, desaturated monotone, pacing-based fear. It lacks Goreweb's hype-branding layer — no handles, no merch, no metal crests, no streetwear irony.
