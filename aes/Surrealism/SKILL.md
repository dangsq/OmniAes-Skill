# Surrealism

Surrealism is the Paris-born movement consecrated by André Breton's 1924 *Manifesto of Surrealism* (the term itself coined earlier by Guillaume Apollinaire), grown out of Dada's anti-art and fed by Freudian psychoanalysis, whose goal was a "super-reality" fusing dream and waking life. Its pictures stage the impossible with a straight face: a soft watch drapes over a dry branch, a canvas on an easel masks exactly the slice of landscape behind it, a steam boiler grows a corrugated trunk and passes for an elephant, megalithic cylinders stand in a ploughed field as if they had always been there. Two working methods run through the whole roster: the **verist** wing (Dalí, Magritte, Tanguy, Max Ernst, Paul Nash, Delvaux, Remedios Varo, Dorothea Tanning) paints dreamscapes with academic illusionism, while the **automatist** wing (Miró, Masson, Arp) lets unpremeditated line and biomorphic signs scatter across flat matte fields. The grammar stays alive past the movement's core decades — in film (David Lynch) and in contemporary painting (Ivan Seal) — so the style is a logic, not a period costume.

## Visual Identity

### Color: muted grounds, saturation as event
The style's colors vary widely by artist, so the binding rule is distributional, not hue-based: large matte, desaturated fields carry the image, and high chroma appears only as small punctures — a glove, a flag, a pencil, a cross painted on a cylinder's end. Four recurring registers:

- **Verist plain and interior:** sun-bleached ochre and sand (#C9A876–#D8C69E), olive-khaki (#6E6B4A–#8A8560), slate and ash grey (#5E6467–#7C8285), umber and bitumen (#2E2620–#4A3A2C), bone-cream (#E4D9C0–#EFE7D2), horizon bands of dusk rose or pale teal (#D89B84, #9FB8B0). Accents: vermilion #BF3B2B, cobalt #2C4B9A, cadmium orange #DE7A2E.
- **Automatist field:** dusty rose, mauve and sand grounds (#C79A8C, #B48E96, #D6BFA4); calligraphic black line (#141414); primary punctuation in red #CE3A2C, blue #2E5FA3, yellow #E9C43B, green #3F7A4C.
- **Conceptual void:** flat cream or pale beige (#EDE3CB) holding a single sober object in pipe-brown and grey (#8A5A3B, #6E6E6E).
- **Late continuation:** matte moss-olive ground (#6C7050) with one painted object carrying turquoise strokes (#2FA9B4).

Any hue may enter if it obeys the muted-ground rule; neon never does.

### Light
Even, sourceless illumination, or a single hard sun low on the horizon throwing long crisp shadows across a plain; banded twilight skies where a dark slab of night sits above a lit ground; window light in interiors; flat studio light on an isolated object against a void. No chiaroscuro theater, no storm atmospherics. Crucially, shadows obey physics even when objects do not — consistent cast shadow and contact shadow are the anchors of believability.

### Surface, texture, materials
Verist work: enamel-smooth oil, invisible brushwork, hard edges, tactile material truth — brass, leather, plaster, stone, glass, wood grain on impossible things. From Ernst's grattage/frottage lineage comes a scraped, mottled grain in skies and machine-skins. Automatist work: flat matte tempera-like fields, thin calligraphic line, no modeling. Objects read solid, weighty, slightly worn; nothing glossy, nothing airbrushed.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)
1. **Dream and unconscious imagery** — hyper-real dreamscapes: sleeping or doubled figures, a kneeling bather beside his own reflection, coastal plains where time goes soft.
2. **Unexpected juxtaposition and conceptual paradox** — unrelated objects sharing one stage; picture-within-picture devices (easel canvas continuing or contradicting the view, window that is also a painting); handwritten captions denying the object above them.
3. **Everyday objects in strange contexts** — pipes, clocks, curtains, gloves, easels, domestic items monumentalized, melted, multiplied, or stranded in deserts; scale displacement (colossal object, tiny distant figure).
4. **Automatism** — unpremeditated wandering line, all-over scatters of signs: stars, eyes, ladders, hooks, commas, without narrative hierarchy.
5. **Biomorphic forms** — amoeboid blobs, soft monsters, machine-animal hybrids: boiler bodies with trunks, horned masks, frilled glove-funnels; rods and spheres behaving like organisms on a plain.
6. **Mythological symbols** — Narcissus, harlequins, bull heads, plaster nude fragments, chessboards as fate.

Recurring stage props: empty horizon plains; tiny far figures; standing staves and rods; spheres, cylinders, megaliths; folding screens or transparent panels; curtains framing a view; birds frozen in flight.

### Composition and space
Frontal, static, stage-like. Four recurring architectures: (a) **central monolith** — one colossal object occupying an empty ground; (b) **deep horizon** — high skyline, vast plain, forms shrinking to specks; (c) **framed aperture** — interior with window, easel, or panel splitting seen from depicted; (d) **all-over field** — automatist scatter with no focal point. Perspective stays rational and single-point even when content is not; emptiness is compositional material, not leftover space.

## Mood & Values
Uncanny calm; the deadpan acceptance of the impossible. The values are the marvelous (*le merveilleux*), freedom from reason, psychological truth over optical truth, personal and social revolution, and paradox delivered as a joke that refuses to laugh. Vastness carries latent dread (Tanguy's plains); dream-eroticism appears as fragment and suggestion — a plaster torso, a glove — never as display. The artwork is an artifact of a psychological experiment, not a decoration.

## Era & Origins
Literary experiments of the late 1910s; Breton's 1924 Paris manifesto consecrates the movement; Apollinaire coins the name. It inherits Dada's anti-art but replaces nihilist provocation with a program for unlocking the unconscious, using Freud as method (automatism, dream depiction). It flourishes 1920s–1950s across an international roster — Ernst, Dalí, Magritte, Miró, Man Ray, Meret Oppenheim, Tanguy, Carrington, Varo, Nash, Delvaux, Masson, Pierre Roy, Tanning — and survives in later film and painting practice.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. One impossible premise per image; everything else obeys physics, perspective, and shadow.
2. Paint with a straight face: academic finish, hard edges, consistent light; the style never winks.
3. Displace, don't decorate: strangeness comes from context, scale, softness, multiplication — not ornamental elaboration.
4. Suspend time: no motion blur, no wind, no gesture; birds hang mid-air.
5. Reserve saturation: muted grounds, chroma as a small event.
6. Let emptiness work: horizons, plains, and cream voids are active material.
7. In the automatist register, surrender hierarchy: wandering line, scattered signs, flat matte ground.

**Variation axes**
- *Subject:* any object, creature, interior, coast, desert, machine, or body fragment — pipe, boiler, megalith, sculpted head, sewing machine, tram ticket, server rack.
- *Composition:* any of the four architectures or a hybrid; vertical monolith or horizontal plain; near object or distant speck.
- *Lighting:* sourceless even light; low hard sun with long shadows; banded twilight; interior window light; flat studio light on an isolated object.
- *Color:* any register above or a blend — olive plain with cobalt accent, cream void with brown object, dusty rose field with primary dots, moss ground with turquoise.
- *Mood:* deadpan paradox, ominous vastness, comic tenderness, dream-erotic charge, quiet melancholy.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — deadpan impossibility, muted ground with punctual chroma, suspended time — not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Neon or electric saturation, blacklight glow, swirling ornamental pattern, optical vibration.
- Glitch, datamosh, compression artifacts, low-res webcam grain, overlaid meme or digital-font text.
- Collage scraps, torn-paper seams, readymade provocation as the entire point.
- Compulsive all-over patterning and naive untrained mark-making as the whole surface.
- Candid flash-lit domestic clutter and accidental snapshot eeriness.
- Expressionist gestural impasto and bravura visible brushwork.
- Heroic fantasy narrative, busy illustrative storytelling, cute anthropomorphism.
- Gore, shock imagery; lens flare, bokeh showcase, CGI sheen.
- Multiple competing impossible premises that turn paradox into clutter.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Neighbor's signature | Surrealism's difference |
|---|---|---|
| Psychedelia | Saturated neon, swirling ornament, optical vibration, euphoric 1960s energy | Muted matte grounds, still surfaces, academic finish; the trip is in the logic, not the color |
| Dadaism | Collage/photomontage seams, readymades, chance, satirical anti-craft | Crafted illusion or deliberate automatism in service of unconscious truth; paradox staged, not scrapped |
| Weirdcore | Low-res digital photos, compression decay, liminal rooms, blunt overlaid text | Authored composition, painterly or fine-print finish; uncanny by design, never by file decay |
| Art Singulier | Naive raw mark-making, obsessive personal patterning, untrained-hand density | Poised biomorphs and rational perspective; automatism is a method, not compulsive density |
| Blogspot Scene | Casual blog-era snapshots, flash-lit mundane clutter, unauthored eeriness | Every strangeness is staged; ordinary objects are lifted out of domestic flow and put on a stage |

Quick test: ask where the strangeness lives — in the logic (Surrealism), in the color (psychedelia), in the material scrap (Dada), in the file decay (weirdcore), in the raw hand (Art Singulier), or in the accident (blogspot scene). Only the first is this style.
