# Yabujincore

Yabujincore, also called Jumpstylecore, is a satirical, absurdist internet aesthetic that emerged on TikTok in late 2021 as a "NicheTok" edit format. It is visually derivative of the net.art movement Xpiritualism and takes its name from the Lithuanian musician Yabujin, but it operates as a shitpost or "poser" trend rather than a coherent art project: a formulaic recipe of jumpstyle dance clips, nonsensical foreign-language text, green Matrix code filters and cursed 2000s meme faces, assembled for virality and algorithmic "brainrot." Its content is intentionally chaotic, confusing, loud and "cursed," re-contextualizing harmless source material — innocent dance videos, K-pop idol clips, snowy portraits, drab playgrounds — into something that reads like sinister found footage.

## Visual Identity

### Color: the two-register rule
The palette is defined by a clash, never a harmony. The **base register** is degraded footage: blown-out whites and paper greys (`#EDEDED`–`#FFFFFF`, `#5A5A5A`–`#8C8C8C`), murky near-black (`#0B0B0B`), winter blue-grey (`#7A8CA0`–`#9FC5E8`), faded old-video beige (`#B9AA97`), optionally monochrome-tinted with Matrix green (`#003B00`–`#00FF41`) or bruised violet-magenta (`#3A0B3A`–`#B400FF`). The **overlay register** is crisp, flat, fully saturated graphic color: alarm red for Arabic type (`#C40000`–`#FF2A2A`), Matrix green (`#00C410`–`#39FF14`), emoji and sticker yellow (`#F5D000`–`#FFE14D`), hot magenta halos (`#FF00D4`), and pure black/white for crude paint-program linework. Rule: the base is always mushy, desaturated or single-tinted; overlays are always vector-sharp and saturated. The two never share a lighting model or a palette family.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the tradition documents them)
1. **Jumpstyle dance motion** — the primary element: rhythmic kicks and jumps rendered as jerky, strobed, surreal motion, performed by blocky Roblox avatars or low-quality street dancers.
2. **Nonsensical multilingual text** — scrolling or flashing overlays in Arabic (most frequent), Russian/Cyrillic and Japanese, used purely for aesthetic chaos, overlapping faces and frame edges.
3. **Cursed 2000s meme faces** — the "Epic Face" (Awesome Face) and Trollface, grinning emoji edited demonic or glitched, mutant grinning ovoids in green or yellow, and crude hand-drawn blob faces pasted over real people's heads.
4. **Distorted anime imagery** — creepy-cute heroines (Lain- or Saya-type characters) stretched to absurdist proportions, faces blown out to glowing white.
5. **Religious juxtaposition** — Christian crosses, ornate crucifixes and halo-like sigils placed against anime figures and dance footage.
6. **Green "Matrix" code filters** raining over night-street footage.
7. **Post-Soviet scenery** — Khrushchyovka concrete apartment blocks, bleak winter streets, drab playgrounds, snowy forests.
8. **The "Snow Girl" / "Winter Girl"** — a degraded portrait of a young woman in snow, blank or faintly smiling, framed as ominous found footage.
9. **Absurd mundane objects in motion** — dancing toothpaste tubes and similar props given jerky life.
10. **Annotation and sticker layer** (image-observed) — red arrows, circles and crosshair reticles pointing at nothing in particular, biohazard symbols, smiley stickers, advisory-style badges, national heraldic emblems, spiky black cybersigil shapes, vertical CJK text panels.

### Materials and textures
Bit-crushed JPEG mush, pixel sorting, solarization and posterization, chromatic-aberration rainbow fringes, scanline and sensor noise, VHS-style blur, blown highlights, paint-program line art with wobbly strokes, clip-art PNG stickers with hard edges, low-resolution video-screenshot grain. Everything should look saved, re-saved, filtered and re-uploaded too many times.

### Composition and lighting
Flat stacked collage with no depth hierarchy: a central figure besieged by competing overlays; text bands hugging the top and bottom edges; diptych splits pairing a photo panel with a drawn or text panel; corner sticker clusters; annotation arrows circling trivial details; a cross or sigil as a faux-symmetric anchor; vertical video crops or squarish low-res frames. Lighting is incoherent across layers by design — base footage is either flatly overexposed, murky night-lit, or grey winter daylight, while cursed elements carry their own neon magenta or green halos. Phone-flash harshness yes; studio lighting, soft gradients and consistent light direction no.

## Mood & Values

Absurdism, brainrot, virality, irony, chaotic confusion and "cursed" re-contextualization. The aesthetic manufactures haunted nostalgia (sped-up, bit-crushed Russian pop over snowscapes), creepy-cute discomfort (stretched kawaii faces beside crucifixes), and humor through overload and misplacement. It values the algorithmic over the authorial: images should look assembled in minutes and reposted a thousand times, deliberately annoying yet affectionate in their nonsense.

## Era & Origins

Coined by the TikTok community via hashtags; earliest known instance December 3, 2021, a night-street jumpstyle clip filtered with green Matrix code and flashing Arabic and Japanese text. It parasitizes Xpiritualism (early-2010s net.art) and misreads the original 2000s Belgian/Dutch jumpstyle rave culture, forcing a horror atmosphere onto a genre built on athleticism and kitschy fun. Viral waves: the Roblox jumpstyle clip of November 2022 and the "That One Guy's For You Page" parody of April 2024. It reacts to algorithmic feed culture itself, parodying the experience of scrolling a cursed feed.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** (1) Degrade the base, keep overlays crisp — compression damage is the canvas, flat saturated graphics are the paint. (2) Stack at least three unrelated registers: footage, meme face, foreign text, religious symbol, clip-art sticker. (3) Treat text as noise, not message — glyphs overlap, scroll, flash, and need not be legible or coherent. (4) Imply jerky motion: mid-kick poses, strobed limbs, blocky avatar rigging. (5) Re-contextualize the innocent as cursed: bit-crush a normal portrait, add ominous type. (6) Frame as found footage: vertical or squarish crop, re-compression artifacts everywhere. (7) Clash, don't compose — competing focal points are correct.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be any mundane or pop footage fodder: dancers, Roblox avatars, idols, toothpaste tubes, playgrounds, panelka courtyards, snowy forests, bedrooms, night streets, stray animals, toys. Composition may range from full-bleed glitch fields to centered stacks, diptychs, or sticker-corner layouts. Lighting may be blown daylight, murky night, flat winter grey, or halo-lit curse. Color handling may tint the base Matrix green, bleached white, winter blue or bruised magenta, so long as overlays stay saturated. Mood may slide from playful chaos to ominous curse to saccharine parody. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Clean high-resolution collage with coherent lore or narrative intent (that is Xpiritualism); sensible single-language captions or professional typography; sincere, undegraded post-Soviet winter photography (Slavic Winter); old-web UI chrome such as OS windows, dialog boxes or GeoCities tiling as the frame (Webcore); slow liminal dread with poetic captions (Weirdcore); harmonious palettes, consistent lighting, studio renders, 3D smoothness or polished vector design; sincere 2000s rave kitsch presented without irony; a lone meme face on a plain background with no layered stack; actual sacred scripture (e.g., Quranic text or pages) used as shock decoration, which the community itself condemns — keep the religious register to crosses and sigil shapes; any implication of 4K crispness or clean gradients.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Arabfunny** centers legible Arabic-script humor and MENA vernacular meme culture; the joke reads. In Yabujincore the Arabic is decorative nonsense inside a multi-register stack with dance motion, cursed faces and compression damage.
- **Keller Synth** is earnest DIY synth and basement-music nostalgia with analog warmth and a coherent muted mood; it lacks meme faces, multilingual text spam and ironic compression violence.
- **Slavic Winter** photographs post-Soviet snow and panelki with melancholic sincerity and photographic integrity; Yabujincore borrows those locations but bites them with meme faces, red type and jerky motion.
- **Webcore** frames nostalgia through old-web interfaces — browser chrome, OS windows, clip-art as UI; Yabujincore's clip-art lives inside degraded video screenshots, never inside UI framing.
- **Xpiritualism**, the parent movement, is serious net.art: high-resolution complex collage, constructed languages carrying narrative weight, ethereal uncanny tone. Yabujincore is low-res, formulaic, repetitive and loud; the presence of Epic Face, Roblox avatars and scrolling Arabic over bit-crushed footage is the tell.
- **Weirdcore** seeks quiet uncanny dread in empty amateur photos; Yabujincore is maximalist, meme-saturated and motion-implied — dread played at maximum volume as a joke.
