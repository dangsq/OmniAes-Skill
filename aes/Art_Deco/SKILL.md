# Art Deco

Art Deco is the international design language of the interwar machine age: born in Paris just before the First World War, named for the 1925 Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs et Industriels Modernes, and flourished through the 1920s and 1930s across architecture, furniture, glass, graphic art, fashion and sculpture. It was a deliberate rejection of Art Nouveau's whiplash vegetation in favor of hard geometry, symmetry and industrial finish, while keeping the craftsman's love of exotic material. Its emotional contract is optimism: luxury, speed, vertical ambition and faith in technology, expressed through ornament rather than through austerity. The style spans two temperaments — the ornate, veneered, gilded Deco of the 1920s and the simplified, aerodynamic Streamline Moderne of the 1930s — and both belong inside the style's borders.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Deco color operates in regimes; an image typically commits to one rather than mixing all:

| Regime | Range (hex) | Typical carrier |
|---|---|---|
| High-contrast base | piano black `#0A0A0C`, ink charcoal `#232326`, ivory/champagne cream `#F4EBD9–#E6D3A8`, warm parchment `#C9B68E` | façades, interiors, fashion plates, ebony-and-ivory furniture |
| Metallics | gold leaf/brass `#C9A227–#E3C567`, bronze `#7A5C33`, chrome/silver `#B9BEC4–#EDEFF1` | grilles, lettering, clock cases, trim, streamlined bodies |
| Jewel saturation | lacquer ruby `#8E1B12–#A31621`, emerald `#0F6B4F`, night sapphire `#14356B`, deep teal `#0E4F5C`, vermilion/coral `#D96C2C`, ochre `#D9A441` | posters, lacquer panels, Neo-Egyptian accents, mosaic floors |
| Powder pastels | powder rose `#E7C6C2`, peach cream `#F0D3B8`, pale celadon-grey | stage designs, boudoirs, bathrooms, textile walls |
| Monochrome machine | silver-grey scale `#8E949A–#EDEFF1`, sepia `#6B5B45` | streamline objects, photography, architectural renderings |

Rule: anchor every composition in the black–ivory–metal triad, then allow *either* jewel-tone saturation *or* powder pastel as the secondary voice. Chrome and gold may coexist only as small specular accents, never as field colors.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how firmly the style documents them)

1. **Bold geometric pattern**: sunbursts, chevrons, zig-zags, radiating fans — applied to crowns, grilles, parquet, inlay, mosaic entry floors, dial faces.
2. **Stepped forms**: ziggurat setbacks, tiered skyscraper masses, stepped stage prosceniums and clock cases.
3. **Symmetry**: bilateral, axial, hieratic; mirrored panels and enfilades double it.
4. **Stylized natural forms**: plants, birds, gazelles, female figures reduced to streamlined silhouettes and repeating friezes; frosted-glass florals; relief animal panels.
5. **Lavish ornament and gold detail**: gilded capitals, bronze door grilles with fountain-and-ray motifs, gilt lettering on stone.
6. **Verticality**: emphasized piers, tall window bays, crowns and spires designed for dramatic night lighting.
7. **Poster grammar**: flat shapes, simplified icons, limited inks, one gradient sky, modular grid alignment, monolithic vehicle masses.
8. **Neo-Egyptian motifs** (post-1922 Egyptomania): lotus, papyrus, scarabs, hieratic friezes, intense lapis–turquoise–gold jewelry combinations.
9. **Streamline curves** (late phase): teardrop contours, rounded corners, horizontal speed ribbing on radios, fridges, cars and corner balconies.

### Materials and textures

Macassar ebony and mahogany veneer with mirror gloss; ivory and mother-of-pearl inlay against dark wood; shagreen; lacquer (including geometric patchwork lacquer panels); marble and onyx clock cases with bronze figurines; frosted, layered and molded glass; chrome, stainless steel and aluminum; Bakelite for radios, clocks, combs, door handles; terrazzo and mosaic flooring; cream limestone and stucco façades; bronze and wrought-iron grilles. Surfaces read *finished*: polished, molded, plated — never raw or hand-wobbled.

### Recurring subjects

Skyscrapers and theater façades; department-store and apartment-block corner fronts; salon, boudoir and bathroom interiors with lacquer panels and pedestal fittings; veneered cabinets and consoles on tapered legs; mantel clock sets (square, octagonal or triangular dials, Deco numerals); lithographic posters of liners, trains, products and destinations; pochoir fashion plates of elongated figures in bias-cut gowns; chryselephantine sculpture on marble pedestals; streamlined automobiles and appliance-like buildings; entry floors, grille doors and mirror niches as ornament carriers.

### Composition and lighting

Symmetry first: axial elevations, centered vanishing points down mirrored enfilades, balanced poster layouts on modular grids. Vertical emphasis in architecture; low, monolithic angles for vehicles and towers. Graphic work uses flat unmodulated planes with a single gradient (sky, glow) and crisp alignment; fashion plates isolate a slender figure in negative space, often on a black ground. Lighting modes: floodlit night crowns and glowing cove interiors; warm lamplight on lacquer and glass; crisp daylight on cream stone; specular highlights tracing chrome; flat even light in lithography.

## Mood & Values

Luxury, glamour, modernity, progress, faith in technology, the "machine aesthetic," speed. The mood is celebratory and poised — jazz-age nightlife, ocean-liner dining salons, gala staircases — carried by an underlying optimism and energy, with a retrospective glance (Egyptian, classical) folded into geometric dress. Even its austerity (Streamline Moderne) sells sleekness rather than humility.

## Era & Origins

Paris, 1910s–1930s. Early work absorbed Neoclassicism, Neo-Rococo and Neo-Louis XVI restraint; the mature style synthesized Cubist geometry, Egyptian revival after Tutankhamen's tomb (1922), Russian Constructivism, Futurism, Orphism and Fauvism into a decorative system. It overtook Beaux-Arts and neoclassical habit, peaked at the 1925 Exposition, simplified into Streamline Moderne through the 1930s (Miami Beach, the 1939 New York World's Fair), and faded around the Second World War as Modernism and Bauhaus minimalism removed ornament. Reliance on private patrons and incompatible-with-mass-production craft kept high Deco short-lived, while Bakelite household goods spread its look widely.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Make symmetry the grammar; break it only for poster diagonals that still balance.
- Treat geometry as ornament: run chevrons, sunbursts and zig-zags along edges, crowns, grilles, floors and inlay lines.
- Build stepped silhouettes; let towers tier upward and interiors rise in setbacks.
- Contrast materials loudly: dark glossy veneer against pale inlay, frosted glass against chrome, black lacquer against gilt.
- Stylize nature and figures into repeats and streamlined silhouettes; never render botany or anatomy naturalistically.
- In graphic work: limited inks, flat planes, one gradient, modular alignment, bold geometric capitals with wide tracking.
- Finish everything to machine smoothness; reserve visible texture for veneer grain, mosaic tesserae and frosted glass.
- Light as spectacle: floodlit crowns, glowing glass, specular metal streaks.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any building, interior, furniture piece, clock, radio, poster, fashion plate, sculpture, vehicle, floor or grille within the period vocabulary. Composition: axial elevation, low monolithic angle, centered enfilade, flat poster plane, isolated object on neutral ground. Lighting: night floodlight, warm interior glow, flat litho light, daylight on stone, monochrome studio. Color: any of the five regimes above. Mood: opulent gala, elegant graphic wit, or streamlined austerity. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Whiplash tendrils, asymmetric botanical curves, dragonfly-and-orchid handcraft (Art Nouveau).
- Fractured multi-viewpoint planes and matte ochre collage (Cubism as subject treatment).
- Ornament-free functionalism: exposed tubular steel, primary-color asceticism, anonymous white cubes (Bauhaus core).
- Rustic distress, grunge, weathering, wabi-sabi irregularity, visible brushy handwork.
- Neon signage, LEDs, cyberpunk or digital anachronisms.
- Contemporary gold-mirror kitsch, saturated teal-and-gold party renders (Deco-Luxe revival gloss).
- Organic free-form plywood curves, Scandinavian light-oak minimalism, mid-century blob furniture.
- Candid snapshot realism: unstylized faces, cluttered casual framing, snapshot lighting.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Flapper**: a subset, not a sibling. If the image's subject *is* the bobbed-hair, drop-waist, jazz-club figure and nightlife behavior, it reads Flapper; Deco proper keeps geometry, materials and objects as the protagonist, with figures stylized into the pattern system.
- **Art Nouveau**: curve quality decides. Nouveau = asymmetric whiplash line, sage/olive/mauve/amber glass, handcraft wobble; Deco = hard-edged arcs and angles, bilateral symmetry, black/chrome/jewel contrast, machine finish.
- **Bauhaus**: ornament and patronage decide. Bauhaus strips decoration for function and industrial anonymity; Deco's geometry is decorative, symmetrical and clad in veneer, lacquer, gilt or frosted glass. Note the border zone: pared-back angular Deco furniture and plain slab buildings with ribbon windows sit at the style's modernist edge and may look proto-Bauhaus; they remain Deco when period materials or ornamental accents survive.
- **Cubism**: Deco borrows Cubism's geometry but keeps the object intact, symmetrical and luxuriously finished; Cubism dissolves the object into faceted planes with a matte ochre-grey-brown palette and no metallics.
- **Deco-Luxe**: the modern revival exaggerates — mirror-polished gold fields, hyper-saturated interiors, glossy 3D-render sheen. Historic Deco shows period matter: veneer grain, Bakelite, shagreen, frosted glass, cream stone, period display typography, and restraint between ornaments.
- **Streamline Moderne (internal late phase)**: rounded corners, teardrop masses, horizontal ribbing, white enamel and chrome; earlier Deco is angular, vertical and ornamented. Both are valid outputs of this skill, but mixing heavy zig-zag ornament with full teardrop streamlining in one object blurs the period logic.

**Evidence note:** the style's painting output is less documented than its architecture, graphics and design objects; photographic records include monochrome modernist-leaning buildings that mark the outer edge of the variation space rather than its core.
