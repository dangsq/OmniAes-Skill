# Symbolism

Symbolism is the art of the second sight: instead of recording what the eye catches, it stages what the mind hides. Emerging in the late 19th century around figures such as Gustave Moreau, Odilon Redon, Jean Moréas and Stéphane Mallarmé, it rejected Impressionism's devotion to surface appearance and sought instead to give tangible form to intangible experience — visions, dreams, mystical states, the subconscious. A Symbolist picture is a sealed letter: every object is a sign, every color a mood, every silence part of the meaning. The movement functions as a hinge between Romanticism and modernism, and it bleeds into Aestheticism, Art Nouveau and Decadence without collapsing into any of them.

Note on evidence: the movement's doctrinal core here rests on its stated key motifs (dreams, visions, myths, the occult, the erotic, death, the subconscious) and key values (subjectivity, emotion, spirituality, the power of suggestion, inner experience); surviving pictures supply the palette, surface and lighting rules below. Where the written record is thin, the visual rules are stated as ranges, not laws.

## Visual Identity

### Color language
The governing rule: **a muted or tonally unified ground, punctuated by one evocative luminous or saturated accent that marks the symbolic focus.** Color never describes local reality; it induces a state. Acceptable families, any of which may carry a whole picture:

| Family | Range | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Noir / charcoal monochrome | `#141414`–`#3d3d3d`, ivory `#e9e4d6` | dream-charcoals, smoke, silhouettes, night visions |
| Muted sage / celadon / dusty rose | `#7a8b7f`, `#9aa899`, `#b98f8f` | veiled figures, interior reverie, hieratic portraits |
| Pale fresco cream and azure | `#e6dcc6`, `#a8c0c4`, `#7d9aa8` | mural-like mythologies, shores, matte daylight without sun |
| Jewel enamel | ultramarine `#27408b`, gold `#c9a227`, carnelian `#8e2323`, deep emerald `#1e4d3b` | thrones, idols, halos, ornate visionary interiors |
| Earthy umber and rust over luminous horizon | `#6b3a2a`, `#8a4a2f` against `#e8d9a0`→`#b8cfe0` | masked rites, grotesques, elemental figures |
| Graphic poster | black `#141210`, vermilion `#c0392b`, cream `#f0e6d2` | theatre bills, lithographic emblems |

Pale nude flesh functions as a color in its own right — often the brightest value in the picture, reading as soul-light rather than skin.

### Light
Light is mood, not description. Recurring schemes: twilight or nocturne with no visible source; interior gloom broken by one supernatural glow (halo, apparition, glowing niche); backlight that flattens figures into silhouette against a graded horizon; soft diffuse fresco light with no cast shadows. A thin rising thread of smoke or incense often stands in for movement in otherwise motionless air.

### Motifs, ranked by textual prominence
1. **Dreams and visions** — sleepers, apparitions, picture-within-picture glimpses of another world.
2. **Myth and the occult** — idols on thrones, sunburst halos, winged demons and seraphs, knights with halos, temple interiors, summoning gestures.
3. **Death** — skeletons in robes, cypresses, isolated isles, hooded figures approaching the living in broad fields.
4. **The erotic, veiled** — nudes seen from behind, behind veils, or behind drapery; androgynous bodies; desire staged as mystery, never as display.
5. **The subconscious** — masks, hollow-eyed effigies, doubled selves, creature-costumes that swallow the wearer.
6. **Supporting objects** — veils, censers, mirrors, fountains, crowns, jewels, checkered floors, sealed doors, shores and towers as thresholds.

### Materials and surface
Oil paint in two opposed finishes: an enamel-hard, jewel-like finish with miniaturist detail (visionary interiors), and a matte, scumbled, semi-opaque finish that keeps edges soft (reverie and fresco modes). Charcoal and pastel for black-toned dream noirs. Watercolor and gouache for luminous apparitions. Flat lithographic ink for the poster branch. Visible canvas or paper tooth is acceptable; glossy photographic smoothness is not.

### Composition
Vertical, altar-like formats are characteristic but not mandatory. Recurring structures: a single hieratic figure isolated against a void or luminous ground; frieze-like processions parallel to the picture plane; horror vacui ornament crowding margins around a calm center; silhouette against a graded sky strip above a bare floor or sea line; framed apertures inside the picture (niches, mirrors, painted frames) that hold the true subject. Depth is ambiguous — thresholds, shores, stairways and sealed rooms with no exit. Figures stand or sit statue-still; eyes are closed, averted, or fixed on something outside the frame.

## Mood & Values

Reverie, dread, hieratic stillness, melancholy eroticism, occult secrecy. The core value is **suggestion**: meaning must remain one remove from literal statement, so the viewer completes the picture inwardly. Subjectivity, emotion and spirituality outrank observation; the physical world is accepted only as a veil over a spiritual reality. Silence is a compositional material — even crowded Symbolist pictures feel soundless.

## Era & Origins

Late 19th century, crystallizing in France in the 1880s around Moréas's manifesto and Mallarmé's poetry, with painted antecedents in Moreau and Redon; rapidly pan-European (Norway, Vienna, the Netherlands, Germany, Belgium, Poland, Russia). It reacts against Impressionism's surface truth, against realism and materialism, and inherits Romanticism's appetite for the sublime while turning it inward. It feeds early modernism's abstraction of meaning into pure form.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Begin with an idea or emotion (grief, desire, fate, awakening) and choose an emblem for it — a creature, object, gesture — rather than illustrating a scene.
2. Veil the emblem: withhold one piece of narrative information so the meaning stays suggestive.
3. Set the ground muted or tonally unified; reserve luminosity or saturation for the symbolic focus (halo, flesh, apparition, jewel).
4. Light for mood: twilight, gloom-plus-glow, backlight silhouette, or sourceless fresco light.
5. Keep figures slow and statuary; let gazes miss the viewer and the obvious center.
6. Treat space as threshold: shore, tower stair, sealed room, checkered floor at the edge of a void.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any dream, vision, myth, occult rite, death emblem, veiled erotic subject, or subconscious double — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. Composition: vertical altarpiece, horizontal frieze, solitary figure against void, or ornate crowded margin with empty center. Lighting: nocturne, graded twilight, interior gloom with single glow, pale diffuse daylight. Color: full monochrome noir through muted sage/cream to jewel enamel or umber-over-luminous-horizon, or flat poster triads. Surface: enamel detail, matte scumble, charcoal tooth, lithographic flatness. Mood: from serene reverie to funereal dread.

**Avoid**
- Impressionist broken touch, plein-air sunlight, observed modern leisure.
- Academic history painting's legible narrative and rhetorical gestures.
- Pure decoration with no hidden meaning (that drifts to Art Nouveau or Aestheticism).
- Cynical, couture-level luxury and urban perversity as the subject itself (that is Decadence).
- Fauve or expressionist saturated arbitrary color, machine-age subjects, snapshot candidness, hard geometric abstraction, glossy digital airbrush finish.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Art Nouveau** shares flatness and ornament, but its whiplash line and organic pattern are the content; Symbolism subordinates line and pattern to a concealed idea, and keeps psychological narrative even in flat modes.
- **Decadence** overlaps in eroticism and luxury but flaunts artificiality, decay and perverse wit; Symbolism keeps a spiritual or oneiric seriousness behind the veil.
- **Modernisme** is the Catalan regional inflection (poster art, architecture, national identity); Symbolism is transnational and idea-first, with no regional program.
- **Romanticism** dramatizes external nature and historical event with vigorous brushwork; Symbolism interiorizes — stillness, muted or unreal color, dream logic instead of event.
- **Aestheticism** declares beauty itself the message (harmony, arrangement, refined domesticity); Symbolism uses beauty as a mask for a second, hidden meaning. If nothing lurks behind the picture, it is not Symbolism.
