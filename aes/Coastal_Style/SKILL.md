# Coastal Style

Coastal Style is an interior-design aesthetic whose single goal is to make a room feel like being beside the sea. It borrows its colors from water, sky, sand and sun-bleached timber, and its textures from the working materials of coastlines: rope, rattan, linen, driftwood, canvas. Born in the beach houses of the early-20th-century American leisure class and refined against older European seaboard vernaculars (the French *bord de mer*, the Mediterranean coast), it spans private residences, hotels and yachts, and absorbs a range of registers from kitsch-free Modern Coastal to plush Hamptons to terracotta-warm Mediterranean Coastal. What unites all registers is a high-key, light-filled envelope, natural fiber under hand and foot, and a casual, unhurried arrangement of things.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The governing rule: **white or blue establishes the foundation; every other hue enters only through textiles, tile and small objects.** Values stay high-key and saturation stays sun-bleached.

- **Whites and creams** (walls, ceilings, slipcovers, sheer curtains): crisp paper white to warm shell — `#FDFCF9` → `#EFE9DE`.
- **Blues** (upholstery, stripes, tile, art): pale sea-glass `#C7D8E2`, chambray `#8FA9BC`, denim `#46688C`, deep navy `#1E3A5C`; Mediterranean lagoon teals and mints `#2E8C8C`, `#7FC4C0`, `#BFE3D9`.
- **Neutrals** (rugs, baskets, wood, floors): jute and sisal `#CBB493`, sand `#E6DFD2`, driftwood gray `#A6A095`, light oak `#D6BE9C`.
- **Warm accents, used sparingly** (a cushion, a ceramic, a print): terracotta `#C0694B`, ochre `#D9A441`, crustacean red `#B33A2B`; soft grays `#D7D9DA` bridge white and blue.

No single hue except white should occupy a large saturated field; even navy appears as upholstery, stripe or print rather than wall-to-wall color, except in Mediterranean tilework where lagoon teal may cover a floor or backsplash.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Woven natural fibers and light wood** — rattan, wicker, seagrass, jute, sisal in seating, baskets, rugs and pendant shades; light oak or pine, whitewashed, gray-stained or driftwood-weathered, in furniture, floors and wall paneling. This is the style's backbone.
2. **The blue-white-neutral envelope with striped or maritime-patterned textiles** — ticking and awning stripes, embroidered fish, wave and shell prints on pillows, upholstery and bedding.
3. **Flooded natural light and airiness** — sheer white curtains, open-concept plans, indoor–outdoor continuity, mirrors and glass used to bounce daylight.
4. **Architectural texture** — white-painted or natural wood ceiling beams, vaulted shiplap ceilings, shiplap or polished-concrete walls, light wood or concrete floors topped with natural-fiber rugs.
5. **Maritime accessories (the classic register)** — seashells, starfish, driftwood, rope, fishing nets, anchors, lifebuoys, ship wheels, lighthouses; marine-life or driftwood wall art; glass jars of white stones; sail-shaped pennants and crustacean prints; lavender or rosemary sprigs for scent.
6. **Woven light fixtures and simple ceramic or metal lamps** — rattan ring pendants, wicker lanterns, brass or ceramic bases; metal always in small doses.
7. **Vintage meets contemporary** — shabby-chic weathered tables and woven baskets paired with clean-lined modern sofas and lamps.
8. **Themed summer tableware** — striped-rim plates, lobster and crab motifs, glassware set for warm-season dining.
9. **Variant-specific signals** — Mediterranean: arches, wrought iron, handcrafted ceramics, indoor palms, dried flowers, occasional cactus; Hamptons: plush slipcovered sofas, gray-stained oak or parquet, elegant lanterns; Modern Coastal: abstract horizon art and zero overt nautical kitsch. (Tropical Coastal is named in the literature but thinly described; treat palm-heavy looks as a border case.)

### Materials and textures

Linen and cotton canvas (often slipcovered, often wrinkled on purpose); rattan, wicker, seagrass and jute weave; light, salt-weathered wood; rope and canvas; polished concrete or pale wood floors; subway, encaustic or terracotta tile in Mediterranean rooms; brass, wrought iron and ceramic as sparse hardware; glass and mirror as light amplifiers; occasional cognac leather ottomans or benches as a warm counterpoint in Hamptons-adjacent rooms. Texture variety is the point: a correct frame layers at least two or three distinct fibrous surfaces — a sisal rug, a linen slipcover, a rattan chair, a seagrass basket.

### Recurring subjects

Whole-room interiors lead: living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, bathrooms, entryways, stair halls, porches and verandas. Equally native are the vignette and the object study — a styled console or shelf, a tablescape, a reading nook with bench and baskets, a close-up of fish-embroidered linen or a striped-rim plate, a gallery wall of sea photographs and sail pennants. Views of water through windows are welcome but optional; the style reproduces the seaside even landlocked.

### Composition and lighting

Compositions favor open, axial or corner views of airy rooms with the ceiling (vaulted, shiplap, beamed) often in frame, plus tightly styled detail shots grouped in odd numbers on shelves, trays and consoles. Foreground texture — a rug edge, a thrown throw, a tray of objects — anchors the frame. Lighting is always natural and diffuse: bright midday bounce, soft morning side-light through sheers, or late golden warmth in Mediterranean rooms. Shadows are soft; exposure runs high-key; nothing is lit for drama.

## Mood & Values

Relaxation, lightness, naturalness, maritime inspiration, serenity. The style values casual comfort over formal display, collected ease over clutter, and permanence of summer: rooms look lived-in, sun-washed and ready for bare feet. Elegance, when present (Hamptons), is laid-back rather than ceremonious.

## Era & Origins

Emerges in the early 20th century as affluent Americans built beach homes in places like the Hamptons, prioritizing comfort and connection to the natural surroundings; parallel seaboard traditions in western France and the Mediterranean supply the *bord de mer* and Mediterranean Coastal branches. Older maritime romance — ship wheels, anchors, lighthouses, rope and canvas — feeds its accessory language. Modern Coastal arises later as a deliberate reaction against overt beach kitsch, stripping signs, ropes and anchors back to abstract color and texture.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build the envelope first: white-dominant walls and ceiling, maximal diffuse daylight, sheer or uncovered windows.
2. Lay the foundation color as white or blue; introduce every other hue only at textile and object scale.
3. Layer at least two or three woven natural-fiber textures per frame.
4. Keep wood light and weathered — whitewashed, gray-stained, driftwood — and let beams or shiplap show.
5. Set the nautical dial deliberately and hold it: whisper (a stripe, an abstract seascape), medium (shells, driftwood, a sail print), or explicit classic (ship wheel, lifebuoy, pennants). The Modern Coastal register sets it to zero kitsch.
6. Arrange casually: slipcovers slightly rumpled, pillows layered, throws tossed, objects grouped in odd numbers.
7. Use metal sparingly (brass, wrought iron); use glass and mirror to multiply light.
8. Keep saturation sun-bleached and finishes matte, washed or softly worn — never glossy, never neon.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any room type, porch, vignette, tablescape, textile or tableware study, even a coastal-material fashion or still-life object, so long as the material language holds. Composition: wide axial room view, corner nook, top-down tablescape, texture macro; symmetric or off-center; sea view optional. Lighting: bright midday diffuse, soft morning side-light, warm Mediterranean afternoon — always natural and airy. Color handling: blue-on-white classic; neutral-dominant with blue whispers (Modern, Hamptons); lagoon teal plus terracotta (Mediterranean); tiny warm pops of coral, ochre or crustacean red. Mood: serene-minimal to collected vintage shabby-chic. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Dark, moody, low-key lighting; night scenes; black-dominant or navy-wall-to-wall palettes.
- High-gloss lacquer, chrome, mirrored glam, plastic shine, neon or fully saturated color fields.
- Theme-park nautical saturation: anchor prints everywhere, rope trim on every edge, bunting, signal-flag walls.
- Heavy formal drapery, valances, damask, brocade, ornate Victorian carving.
- Industrial vocabulary en masse: exposed steel, Edison bulbs, matte-black hardware grids, raw brick.
- Western or ranch signifiers: cowboy hats, boots, fringe, concho belts, cowhide.
- Tiki or tropical-kitch overload: flamingos, neon palms, thatch-bar staging.
- Dense dark maximalist pattern stacking; clutter that blocks light and air.
- Dark polished teak or mahogany as the dominant wood.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Coastal Cowgirl** mixes the beach palette with Western gear — hats, boots, fringe, conchos, cowhide, sunset pinks. Coastal Style carries zero Western signifiers; its rustic note is driftwood and rattan, not ranch leather.
- **Coastal Grandmother** is a cozy, fashion-adjacent lifestyle variant: cream-heavy, floral prints, cardigan-soft layers, matriarchal collected clutter. Coastal Style is the broader interior system — crisper blue-white, weave-led, and comfortable being minimal and modern.
- **Dark Nautical** inverts the value scale: navy-dominant walls, polished dark teak or mahogany, brass ship hardware, moody yacht-club gravity. Coastal is light-dominant; navy appears as accent, and wood stays pale and weathered.
- **Minimalism** shares the light and openness but strips texture variety and reference. A bare white room with one chair is Minimalism; Coastal requires layered natural fibers and at least a whisper of sea-derived cue (stripe, weave, horizon art).
- **Nautical** is theme-first and graphic: bold red-navy-white color blocking, anchors, rope trim, uniform stripes reading as costume or ship interior. Coastal is environment-first: sun-bleached, texture-led, nautical elements optional accents — a beach house, not a boat.

**Quick recognition test:** high-key daylight + white or blue foundation + two or more woven natural fibers + light weathered wood + casual arrangement = Coastal Style; adjust the nautical dial and warm-accent level to place the register within it.
