# Dungeon Synth

Dungeon Synth (also "Dark Dungeon Music" or "Medieval Ambient") began as ambient synthesizer side-projects by members of the Norwegian black metal scene in the late 1980s and early 1990s, circulated on home-dubbed cassettes. Its visual culture is exactly what that origin implies: black metal's photocopy-and-corpsepaint austerity redirected toward medieval fantasy, Tolkien, and tabletop roleplaying, then filtered through zine-grade reproduction. The result is an aesthetic of *archaic escapism* — ruins, forests, moonlight and solitary robed figures rendered as if scanned from a forgotten tape insert or a crumbling manuscript. A home-produced, solo-endeavor ethos keeps the craft deliberately amateur: naive painting, degraded duplication, typewriter liner notes. Since the term was coined in 2011 and the mid-2010s Bandcamp boom, the style has broadened (neoclassical, new age, chiptune branches; subgenres like Comfy Synth and Dungeon Rap), but the core visual grammar remains lo-fi, muted, and melancholy-fantastical.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is desaturation first: everything reads faded, toner-crushed, or candle-dim. Monochrome black-and-white dominates the classic strain — crushed blacks near `#0b0b0d`–`#1c1c1c` against photocopy paper whites `#d9d5c9`–`#efece4`, with coarse mid-grey dither between. The painterly strain uses earthbound muted tones: moss and conifer greens `#3f4a38`–`#5d6b4a`, bark and soil browns `#4e3d2a`–`#6e5a3e`, parchment and bone `#c8b98f`, faded gilt `#a98b3f`, and occasional manuscript reds `#7a2e2a` kept dull. Cold moonlight blues `#2f3e55`–`#4a5f7d` appear as night washes or duotone tints; rarely, a degraded violet-magenta `#6d4a6d`–`#8a5a86` stains a xerox collage. At most one tint accent over greyscale; never a saturated, glossy, or neon color anywhere.

### Signature motifs and subjects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Castles, dungeons, ruined structures** — towers on crags, mist-wrapped keeps, collapsed stone.
2. **Forests** — dense conifer woodland, often misted, often dwarfing any figure.
3. **Winter landscapes** — snowbound mountains, moonlit peaks, frozen silence.
4. **Ancient lore and high fantasy** — heraldic shields and banners, swords held point-down, crowned spectral sovereigns, hooded or cowled wanderers, magical glades; Tolkien and Dungeons & Dragons as explicit touchstones.
5. **Melancholy and adventure** — the solitary figure, the candlelit vigil, the implied quest.
6. **Black metal persona elements** — corpsepaint faces and medieval attire including chainmail coifs in promotional portraits; highly stylized, near-illegible black-metal-style artist logos.
7. **Medieval-style painting** — manuscript-flattened figures in dull reds, blues and golds; and the 19th-century Norwegian romantic-nationalist painting lineage (Theodor Kittelsen's misty trolls and mountains) as a named influence.
8. **Nocturnal symbols** — full moons, candelabra, single candle flames, starless black skies.

Some contemporary practitioners deliberately de-emphasize overt high fantasy; a sparse monochrome vignette with no castle at all still qualifies if it keeps the archaic, lo-fi, introspective register.

### Materials, textures, medium

Photocopy and cassette culture define the surface: xerox contrast-crush with toner speckle, halftone or dither patterns, scan grain and skew, paper fiber and creases, hand-written or typewritten tape labels and liner-note columns. Painterly works look like naive acrylic or oil on board, or like a reproduced plate from an old book — visible brush naivety, flattened perspective, no airbrush smoothness. Engraving and etching line work (mountain ranges, moon discs) is common. Tape hiss has a visual equivalent here: noise, warble and degradation are intentional authenticity markers.

### Composition and layout

Recurring structures, none mandatory: a frame-within-frame (small grainy image window centered in a large black field); margins or bands carrying blackletter titles above and below; vertical spine-like text columns along one edge; symmetrical heraldic arrangement (candles flanking a central emblem or face); a tiny figure swallowed by vast landscape; and the cassette J-card spread — small xerox image on one panel, dense typewriter text on the other. Logos sit in ornate blackletter or spiky black-metal letterforms; body text is typewriter monospace or gothic minuscule.

### Lighting

Candlelight, moonlight, and mist-diffused daylight are the only sources. Shadows crush to black; highlights are paper-white or flame-white, never specular gloss. Scanned-print flatness is acceptable and typical: many covers read as reproduced artifacts rather than windows onto a lit scene. No studio strobes, no rim-lit cinematic drama, no lens flare.

## Mood & Values

Escapism, isolation, introspection, mystery, atmosphere, nostalgia. The image should feel like a door into a private, pre-modern world reached alone — melancholy first, but the spectrum legitimately extends to whimsical, epic, contemplative, or quietly heroic. Values expressed: amateur sincerity over professional polish, secrecy over spectacle, the handmade tape over the commercial product, memory and myth over the present day.

## Era & Origins

Emergent late 1980s–early 1990s, Norway initially, from black metal's ambient interludes and cassette-trading circles (Mortiis, Burzum as early proponents); named only in 2011 by fan Andrew Werdna, then revived and internationalized through blogs, forums and Bandcamp in the mid-2010s. The style reacts against both mainstream polish and against black metal's own aggression — keeping its gloom and its xerox craft while swapping misanthropy for fantasy yearning.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Degrade before you decorate: decide the reproduction medium (xerox, scan, naive painting, engraving) first, then the subject. Keep saturation low and value range either crushed (monochrome) or dim (earth tones). Prefer one solitary figure or an empty landscape over crowds and action. Let typography be period-correct: blackletter, typewriter, hand-lettering. Treat noise, grain and duploss as composition elements.

**Variation axes.** Subject: castle, dungeon interior, forest glade, winter peak, moonlit water, robed wanderer, crowned spectre, corpsepaint portrait with candles, heraldic device, tape-insert spread — or a non-fantasy archaic vignette. Composition: centered emblem, framed window on black, full-bleed painting, J-card spread, vertical text band, sparse minimal field. Color: pure monochrome xerox; greyscale plus one cold or violet duotone; muted earth-and-parchment painting; sepia parchment. Light: candle, moon, mist, flat scanner luminance. Mood: melancholic to whimsical to heroic. Medium look: photocopy, book-plate reproduction, naive board painting, engraving; pixel-art edges are a permissible modern chiptune-branch outlier, though sample evidence for it is thin.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Saturated or neon color; glossy CGI, smooth gradients, airbrushed fantasy key art; crisp DSLR photography with studio lighting; clean modern sans-serif type, minimalist Swiss layouts, vector filigree; modern subjects (cars, cities, screens, contemporary dress); gore, desecration and occult shock iconography as the focus (that is black metal's lane); military insignia and monumental neoclassical architecture (martial industrial); warm, cozy, saturated hearth-and-tavern comfort as the dominant note (Comfy Synth); high-detail dragons and cinematic rim light (generic dark fantasy).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Black Metal:** shares xerox monochrome, corpsepaint and spiky logos, but black metal centers band ferocity, misanthropy and occult confrontation; dungeon synth centers landscape, castle and medieval painting, allows muted color and naive illustration, and feels yearning rather than hostile.
- **Martial Industrial:** neoclassical monuments, eagles, insignia, sepia-steel palettes and clean graphic design signal martial industrial; dungeon synth is pre-modern, organic, and hand-degraded.
- **Comfy Synth:** the cozy subgenre — hearthlight, taverns, food, warm storybook saturation and gentle humor; dungeon synth proper stays cold, ruinous, solitary and desaturated.
- **Dark Fantasy:** polished digital illustration with dramatic cinematic lighting and high finish; dungeon synth is deliberately lo-fi, amateurish, scan- and xerox-marked.
- **Dungeon Rap:** keeps fantasy motifs but pairs them with beat-tape and hip-hop signifiers and often brighter or pixelated graphics; evidence for its visual code is thin, so treat fantasy-plus-rap-tape graphics as the cue.
