# Blob World

Blob World — also called mushbuhcore or Cool Blob World — is the wave of graphic design that emerged in the mid-2010s, built entirely on 3D rendering. It makes blobby proportions: gelatinous, curvy, organic shapes that are translucent, glossy and strange, mixing playful textures with fluid, bizarre and sometimes unsettling body-horror compositions. It lives in motion design, music videos, TV bumpers, campaign art, game visuals (Fall Guys, Nour: Play With Your Food), web skins and — as physical extensions — chunky resin homeware, curvy "squiggle" mirrors and wavy pastel furniture. The style was coined by Jack Grimes; Jack Sachs is its iconic figure. Its declared key motif is simply: 3D shapes.

## Visual Identity

**Color.** Unity comes from finish and form, not hue — any hue is admissible if it is rendered either as powdered pastel or as saturated candy. Three registers recur: (1) dreamy pastels — lavender around #C9B8F2, baby pink #F7C6DA, mint #BFE6D8, butter #F7E39A, powder blue #BFE0F5; (2) saturated candy and gradients — magenta #FF4FA3, violet #7A3BFF, teal #00BFD1, cherry #E4263C, tangerine #FF8A3D, often melting into one another; (3) monochrome saturation, where an entire scene commits to one hue family (all cobalt, all crimson, all pink). Neon highlights — chartreuse #D8FF3E, hot pink #FF2E88 — appear as sparse accents. Backgrounds are flat single color, soft gradient, or empty void. These hexes are working ranges distilled from the style's documented registers, not a fixed canon.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the text documents them.** First, blobby 3D shapes themselves: gelatinous curvy masses as the core subject. Second, deformation as grammar — stretching, melting and inflating of structures into tubes, coils, sagging ropes and balloon-swollen volumes. Third, shader play: surfaces alternating between matte rubber, ultra-glossy jelly finishes and tactile, strange ridges, replicating clay, rubber, plastic or glass. Fourth, abstract landscapes incorporating floating rings, checkerboard floors and minimalist architecture — stairs, arches, plinths, tiny towers. Fifth, limbless, stylized or hyper-flexible figures in the Jack Sachs lineage: mascots with stubby limbs, googly eyes, or no limbs at all. Sixth, the mood-color trio of dreamy pastels, saturated gradients and neon highlights. Seventh, physical product echoes: resin homeware, squiggle mirrors, wavy furniture. Eighth, the uncanny undertow — fleshy coiled tubes, tangled soft anatomy — playful on the surface, disquieting in structure.

**Materials and textures.** Matte clay and rubber with a soft sheen; ultra-glossy jelly carrying wet specular highlights; translucent resin and glass with subsurface scattering and inner glow; inflated vinyl and soft plastic; fuzzy wool or flocked fur patches. All surfaces are subdivision-smooth and clean: no facets, no grain, no weathering.

**Recurring subjects.** Abstract soft sculptures; mascot characters; inflated typography lockups (balloon-puffed rounded glyphs, sometimes sparkling); surreal dioramas and playgrounds; product-as-hero still lifes; stylized portraits, and — in campaign and music-video contexts — photographed live-action people placed inside rendered blob environments; gradient UI panels studded with blob icons.

**Composition and lighting.** Composition modes: objects floating in a void against a flat or gradient field; a scatter of small mascots orbiting a central type lockup or hero object; horizon-based dioramas with miniature architecture; centered product idols; and chaotic assemblage collages pressing forms against the frame. Scale is toy-like: giant soft objects beside tiny props. Lighting is studio-soft — diffuse key, ambient fill, gentle contact shadows and ambient occlusion; specular highlights and subsurface glow provide the drama. Gradient backlights or colored environment glows appear; harsh directional sun and chiaroscuro never do.

## Mood & Values

Cheerful but surreal. The style promises squeeze-ability, toy abundance and absurd humor, yet keeps an uncanny strain — soft body-horror, tangled flesh-like coils — that prevents it reading as mere cuteness. It values play, digital craft, anti-minimalist excess and wit over corporate polish.

## Era & Origins

A mid-2010s wave of graphic design, named by Jack Grimes, flourishing wherever soft-body simulation and render engines met commercial art: bumpers, lyric videos, racing-video spoofs, game worlds, brand sites. It reacts against early-2010s flat minimalism by re-inflating design into tactile 3D, and it spills from screens into interiors via resin and squiggle-mirror products. Neighbors: Claymorphism, Neo-Y2K, Silicon Dreams, Wacky Pomo.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Soft-body logic first: every dominant form looks inflated, melted or stretched; round every edge.
2. Restrict the shader vocabulary to two or three of: matte clay/rubber, glossy jelly, translucent resin, fuzzy flock, soft plastic — kept render-clean.
3. Choose a color register deliberately (pastel, candy gradient, or monochrome saturation); spend neon only as accent.
4. Light it like a studio render: diffuse, even, soft-shadowed; let speculars and subsurface scattering carry depth.
5. Set the uncanny dial consciously, from pure play to fleshy disquiet — both ends are in-style.
6. If type appears, inflate it: balloon letters, chunky rounded glyphs.
7. Treat gravity as a suggestion: float, tilt, sag, squash.

Variation axes: subject (abstract sculpture, mascot, product, interior, landscape, portrait, type lockup, UI skin); composition (void scatter, diorama, centered idol, collage); lighting (soft studio, gradient glow, flat ambient); color handling (pastel / candy / monochrome); mood (playful ↔ uncanny). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — soft-body deformation, candy shader vocabulary, studio-render light, cheerful-surreal mood — not any reference image.

Avoid: hard-edge minimalism or machined geometry as the dominant language (small rigid props for contrast are fine); photorealistic skin, nature, grime, film grain or lens dirt; desaturated earthy heritage palettes; chrome, brushed metal or iridescent tech gloss as hero material; flat matte UI softness with no surrealism or gloss; literal gore — the body-horror here is bloodless and soft; low-poly facets, wireframes, pixelation or retro-CGI dither; flat Memphis-style geometric confetti without 3D softness; dramatic chiaroscuro, hard noon sun, cinematic anamorphic flare.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Claymorphism** is a soft-UI manner: matte clay-like rounded panels, muted pastels, cozy inner-shadow calm, applied to interfaces and icons. It stays matte, hygienic and quiet. Blob World is a whole render culture — glossy jelly, translucency, fur, deformation narratives, surreal scenes and an uncanny edge. If it could pass as a cozy app onboarding screen, it is Claymorphism; if it reads as a music-video still or a toy diagram from a dream, it is Blob World.
- **Neo-Y2K** worships technology: chrome, metallic shine, frosted glass, gadgets, futuristic branding, glossy black and iridescence. Blob World replaces chrome with rubber and jelly, gadgets with organic absurdity; its shine is candy glaze, not metal reflection, and its futurism is biological rather than tech-fetish.
- **Silicon Dreams** is dreamy, pristine CGI surrealism: smooth ethereal gradients, serene floating forms, a contemplative early-3D sublime. Blob World is tactile and comedic — squish, sag, googly eyes, checkerboard floors — and its humor plus body-horror undertow breaks Silicon Dreams' serenity.
