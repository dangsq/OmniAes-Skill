# Frutevil Aero

Also called Frutiger Evil or Frutiger Hell, Frutevil Aero is a joke-aesthetic that emerged on Reddit in 2024 as the dark twin of Frutiger Aero. It keeps the parent style's compositions and motif vocabulary — glossiness, bubbles, computer monitors, auroras, bokeh light, liquids — but swaps the eco-friendly, humanistic, technological optimism for destruction, pollution and chaos. It is deliberately an "opposite": the same visual sentence, spoken with every noun replaced by its corpse. Because it is a community meme format rather than a design movement, its images range from smooth 3D-render stock art to rough cut-and-paste collages, and both ends of that fidelity range are legitimate.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

Red owns the sky and the liquids; brown and sepia own the earth and the smoke; grey and ash own the rubble and the overcast; black owns the silhouettes (bats, crows, skylines, webs, charred shapes). Green survives only as a small ironic or sickly patch — a strip of grass, a contaminated field — and yellow appears almost exclusively as hazard signage or the white-hot core of a sunburst. Crucially, specular whites stay candy-clean: glossy highlights remain pristine even inside the ruin, and that contrast between clean shine and dirty world is the style's core tell.

- Blood / lava reds: `#5E0000` → `#A80000` → `#D90000`, hot edge `#FF2A00`
- Magma oranges: `#FF6A00` → `#FF9500`
- Sepia earth and smoke: `#6B4F35`, `#8A6A4B`, `#A98963`, `#C2A878`
- Ash greys: `#6E6E6E` → `#9C9C9C` → `#C9C4B8`
- Silhouette black: `#0A0A0A` → `#1C1C1C`
- Ironic / toxic greens: `#3E8E2E`, `#7CB342`, sickly lime `#A8C000`
- Hazard yellow: `#F2C200` → `#FFD400`
- Candy translucency: ruby `#C0003C` / `#E0003C` over milky `#F5F5F5`

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the text documents them

1. **Pollution and toxicity** — smoky, contaminated skies; trash heaps; acid and nuclear waste; cooling towers and discharge pipes.
2. **Destruction and destroyed cities** — abandoned or burning skylines, rubble fields, usually accompanied by nuclear plants; catastrophe events such as meteorite strikes, volcanoes, floods and nuclear strikes.
3. **Red liquid** — water readable as blood, lava and magma flows, melting puddles beneath dying objects.
4. **Skeletons and dead life** — human and fish skeletons, dead plants.
5. **Edited Frutiger motifs** — the retained gloss vocabulary: glossiness, bubbles, computer monitors, auroras, bright bokeh lights, liquids, translucent candy-colored computers, glossy globes and spheres, now corrupted (screens full of skulls, globes melting).
6. **Fire** — flame trails, burning objects, sunbursts glaring through smog.
7. **Dry cracked dirt** — barren cracked mud with no living plants.
8. **Unpleasant fauna** — bats, crows, snakes.
9. **Military hardware** — weapons, warships, jets.
10. **Hazard iconography and seasonal props** — radiation trefoils, warning boards, and in community practice pumpkins and haunted-house props as extensions of the "scary" motif; these are image-documented extensions of the evil-edit idea rather than text-listed core motifs.

### Materials and textures

Glossy plastic and tinted glass (the translucent candy shell of late-90s/early-2000s consumer tech), liquid chrome and melting surfaces, glass bubbles with internal reflections, mirror-bright reflective floors, halftone dot screens and grunge scratch overlays, JPEG-soft photomontage edges with visible cut-out borders, cracked mud plates, ash and rubble grain, smoky airbrushed gradients. Specular highlights frequently ignore the scene's actual light source — a deliberate-naive collage artifact.

### Recurring subjects

Translucent desktop computers and flat monitors; glossy Earth globes and perfect spheres (often melting, burning, or filled with red cities); fish and bird skeletons; bat flocks and crows; snakes; city skyline silhouettes; nuclear cooling towers and factory chimneys; hazmat-suited figures; warships and jets; thermometers and measuring props; hazard sign boards; pumpkins; patches of grass blades in the extreme foreground.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Horizontal banding:** a textured foreground strip (cracked mud, grass blades, rubble), a mid-distance silhouette band (skyline, chimneys, towers), and an event sky filling the upper half.
- **A central glossy anchor:** one shiny object standing or floating on a reflective plane, with its mirror image below.
- **Collage layering:** cut-out elements at mismatched scales stacked without consistent perspective or depth of field.
- **Split or diagonal moral contrast:** living green against dead cracked earth as a diagram of loss.
- **Lighting:** backlit smoky suns with god rays; starburst lens flares; fire and lava glowing from the horizon or from below; bokeh sparkles; red aurora glows; or flat, even clip-art light in rougher collages.
- Occasional grungy propaganda-style caption text intrudes, but text is optional, not defining.

## Mood & Values

Camp dread played completely straight. The humor comes from the "evil variant" meme logic — corporate-utopia gloss applied to ecological collapse without a wink — so the style values sincerity over irony-signaling. Underneath the joke runs genuine eco-anxiety: pollution, nuclear waste and dead nature satirized through the exact visual language that once sold techno-optimism. Community values are remix, parody and affectionate vandalism of a beloved parent aesthetic; a melancholy strand also exists, where sepia desolation reads as unironic grief.

## Era & Origins

Coined 15 March 2024 by Reddit user u/Sarasinapellido in a post on r/FrutigerAero ("I've created Frutevil Aero, a new sick and twisted aesthetic..."), which spread rapidly; the subreddit r/FrutevilAero followed the next day, created by u/transshapiro. It reacts directly to Frutiger Aero (the 2000s–early-2010s corporate eco-optimist style) and to the internet's "evil remake" meme format. The community names conspiracy-theory collage artist David Dees as an iconic figure whose digital photomontage manner closely resembles and possibly inspired the style — treat this as lineage, not documented causation. Criticism that Frutevil Aero is "merely an inversion" of Frutiger Aero is itself diagnostic: the inversion, executed with the parent's grammar, *is* the style.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:

1. **Build with Frutiger grammar, then poison the content.** Compose as if for a clean 2006 tech-utopia brochure, then substitute blood for water, smoke for sky, skeletons for wildlife.
2. **Keep one glossy anchor** with pristine specular highlights; the shine must not degrade even as the world does.
3. **Observe palette discipline:** red/brown/grey/black dominant, green only ironic or sickly, yellow only hazard or sun-core.
4. **Respect the naive-fidelity ceiling:** allow cut-out edges, scale slips, mismatched lighting, compression softness, halftone grain. No seamless photoreal integration.
5. **Let sky and ground each narrate:** the sky carries smoke, fire or flare; the ground carries cracks, rubble, blood-water, or one ironic grass strip.
6. **Play it straight.** No winking, no parody captions required; camp emerges from sincerity.

Variation axes:

- **Subject:** any glossy or mundane object capable of carrying doom — computers, globes, bubbles, pumpkins, signs, cooling towers, hazmat figures, military vehicles, melting planets, or invented objects entirely.
- **Composition:** central hero object on a reflective plane; wide banded horizon; dense full-frame collage; minimal sphere-in-smoky-sky; split green-vs-cracked diagram.
- **Lighting:** backlit smoky sun with rays; starburst flare; fire underglow; red aurora; flat clip-art evenness.
- **Color handling:** red-dominant; sepia/brown-dominant; ash-grey with red accents; rarely green-forward with red intrusion.
- **Mood and fidelity:** from smoother 3D stock-render to rough paint-program collage; camp horror, melancholy desolation, or ironic cheer.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — Frutiger gloss grammar, apocalyptic substitution, palette discipline, naive collage fidelity — not any reference image. A melting globe, a hazmat worker, a pumpkin, or a wholly new object such as a translucent candy toaster stranded on ash dunes all qualify equally if the rules hold.

**Avoid:** pristine Frutiger Aero content (clear azure skies, dewy healthy grass, clean droplets, hopeful auroras); coherent photoreal CGI with matched lighting and cinematic grades; matte desaturated gloomcore or doomcore lo-fi photography; grunge or horror imagery with no Frutiger gloss residue (no bubbles, flares, bokeh, candy tech); cyberpunk neon, vaporwave grids, synthwave suns; pastel softcore, kawaii, cottagecore; hand-painted traditional media (oil, watercolor, impasto); minimalist vector posters led by clean typography; anime cel shading; wholesome eco-messaging without inversion; monochrome black-and-white photography; modern frosted-glass UI minimalism (the gloss must be early-2000s plastic, not 2020s glassmorphism); pure Halloween kitsch carrying neither glossy tech nor a pollution signifier.

## Discrimination Cues

| Element | Frutiger Aero | Frutevil Aero |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | Azure gradients, clean clouds, serene auroras | Red/orange/sepia smoke, flares through smog, red auroras |
| Liquid | Clear water, droplets, bubbles | Blood-red water, lava, acid, melting puddles |
| Ground | Lush dewy grass | Cracked mud, ash, rubble, or one ironic grass strip |
| Life | Living fish, birds, leaves | Skeletons of those same fish and birds, dead plants, bats, crows, snakes |
| Tech | Glossy devices in harmony with nature | Same devices amid ruin, screens full of skulls |
| Mood | Optimistic, clean, corporate-calm | Camp apocalypse, straight-face dread |

The trap is the shared grammar: both styles use gloss, bubbles, bokeh and lens flares, so judge by the *state* of the content — if the glossy object sits in clean nature it is Frutiger Aero; if it sits in collapse it is Frutevil Aero. Against generic apocalyptic concept art, Frutevil Aero is distinguished by its surviving stock-art gloss vocabulary and naive photomontage fidelity; disaster concept art is coherent, matte and cinematic, while Frutevil is saturated, shiny and clumsily collaged. Against David Dees-style conspiracy collage, Frutevil adds the Frutiger gloss objects and the meme-aware red palette. Against Halloween collage, Frutevil retains pollution, tech or eco-disaster signifiers; spooky props alone drift out of the style. The one-line test: if the image looks like a 2006 clean-tech brochure in which everything the brochure promised is now on fire, it is Frutevil Aero.
