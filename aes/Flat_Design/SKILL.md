# Flat Design (Vector Minimalism)

Flat Design is the house style of the 2010s screen: the visual language that took over operating systems, apps and websites after the glossy skeuomorphic Frutiger Aero era collapsed around 2013, and that held them until gradients and glass crept back in the early 2020s. Its entire project is reduction of visual load — solid fills instead of rendered materials, pictograms instead of illustrations, grids instead of ornament, so that a interface stays legible on a small smartphone screen. Though born digital, it leaks into print: booklets, posters, sticker sheets and exhibition graphics that behave like interfaces. Its deeper ancestry is print modernism — the International Typographic Style's "function over style," Bauhaus geometry, even the flat matte color planes of early-20th-century Secession and Plakatstil posters — but the style itself is unmistakably a product of iOS 7, Windows Metro/MDL2 tiles and Material Design.

## Visual Identity

### Color

The palette rule: one neutral canvas (white or near-white grey), a cool family of light blues and light greens, and a short list of two to four saturated accents — never a full rainbow, never muddy. Earth tones (brown, ochre, olive, beige, terracotta) are excluded by definition; the aesthetic wants clean and fresh, not organic. Saturation of accents runs low to medium-high but stays pure and unmodulated: each fill is a single flat value with no gradient inside it. A dark-mode variant inverts the canvas to near-black or deep charcoal while keeping the same accent hues, which is the style's only legitimate "lighting" change.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Current technology surfaces.** Smartphone and tablet screens, OS shells: grids of square or rounded-square tiles, app-icon arrays, status bars, cards, toggle switches, pill buttons, floating action buttons, navigation bars. The device itself, shown front-on, is a canonical subject.
2. **Visual organization and cleanliness.** An implicit column grid governs everything; edges align, margins breathe, modules repeat (tile mosaics, icon sets, sticker sheets, specimen spreads). Whitespace is an active compositional element, not leftover.
3. **Easily decipherable icons and pictograms.** Silhouette-first glyphs — cloud, gear, envelope, heart, arrow, play triangle — drawn in solid fill or uniform stroke, legible at thumbnail size.
4. **Rounded geometry.** Consistent corner radii on rectangles; circles, semicircles and pills as buttons and as abstract poster elements; triangles and quarter-circles arranged in balanced geometric compositions.
5. **Flat vector illustration.** The Corporate Memphis subgenre: simplified humans with solid unnatural skin tones (blue, yellow, pink), oversized limbs, dot-and-smile faces. Also layered silhouette landscapes and cityscapes: banded hills, triangular pines, blob clouds, a winding road or river, all in matte planes.
6. **Sans-serif typography as structure.** A single neo-grotesque or geometric sans, highly legible, often set in caps for titles; text blocks snap to the grid; type carries hierarchy alone, with no drop shadows or effects.
7. **Clean staging.** Devices or printed collateral presented on seamless white or pale grey surfaces under even studio light, sometimes with one token prop (a potted plant, a hand holding a booklet) — the mockup culture of product presentation.

### Materials and textures

There are none. Every surface is a matte, uniform fill with crisp vector edges; the print analogue is uncoated matte stock or screen-print flatness. No grain, no gloss, no glass, no brushed metal, no wood grain, no fabric weave, no paper texture. The only permitted depth cues are overlap of flat planes and, optionally, a single soft diffuse elevation shadow under a card or floating button (the Material Design inheritance). Hard cast shadows with a directional light source do not exist here.

### Composition and lighting

The picture plane is orthographic and front-facing: everything sits on one surface, layered like paper cutouts. Composition follows Swiss-derived grids — centered symmetry for icon-and-logo subjects, asymmetric column grids for editorial and poster layouts, mosaic tiling for interface subjects. Lighting is uniform and ambient: shadowless, highlight-free, with no volumetrics and no falloff. Dark mode is a palette choice, not a lighting effect.

## Mood & Values

Clarity, accessibility, efficiency, optimism. Flat Design expresses a democratic promise: technology that anyone can read at a glance, designed for ease of access and streamlined process. Its tone is confident neutrality — friendly-corporate, rational, calm, slightly cheerful in its accent colors. The same reduction that reads as honesty eventually read as sterility (the "oversimplified logos" mockery), so the style carries a faint aftertaste of corporate blandness; lean into poised orderliness rather than angst or warmth.

## Era & Origins

Roots in the 1950s International Typographic Style and its "function over style" philosophy, plus Bauhaus (1920s), Memphis Design (1980s–90s) and 2000s Superflat Pop / Vectordelia; the flat matte color planes of early-20th-century Secession and Plakatstil posters are visible deep ancestry. First uses: Pepsi's 2008 minimalist logos, Microsoft's Metro experiments (2009 Zune HD, 2010 Windows Phone 7, 2011 Xbox dashboard). Mainstream establishment 2013–2014 with iOS 7, Windows 8/10, macOS Yosemite, Material Design and console dashboards; Corporate Memphis emerges 2017. Decline from 2023 as Glassmorphism, then "Liquid Glass," reintroduce gradients, depth and glass; Google's playful Material You remains a flat outlier.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Think plane-first: the canvas is a surface (screen, poster, page), not a space.
2. Budget the palette: one neutral canvas, one cool family, two to four pure accents.
3. Solid fills only; every edge crisp and vector-clean.
4. Icons silhouette-first: if it fails at 16 pixels, simplify it.
5. One consistent corner radius across all rounded rectangles.
6. Align to an implicit grid; let whitespace carry the composition.
7. Depth by overlap only, plus at most one soft elevation shadow per floating element.
8. One sans-serif family, no type effects.
9. If people appear, draw them as simplified vectors or omit facial detail entirely.
10. Light everything uniformly; choose light mode or dark mode, never a light source.

Variation axes: subject may be any interface, device, poster, infographic, booklet spread, sticker sheet, event key art, flat landscape or cityscape illustration, character illustration, or clean product mockup; composition may range from centered symmetry to Swiss asymmetry to tile mosaic to layered silhouette bands; color may run cool white-and-blue corporate, pastel-playful, saturated primary set, or dark-mode near-black with bright accents, and illustration may borrow warm sunset hues as long as fills stay matte and the count stays limited; mood may slide from corporate calm to Material-You playfulness. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: gradients, gloss, glass, reflections, bevels, embossing; skeuomorphic textures (wood, leather, metal, paper grain); realistic 3D rendering, depth of field, directional cast shadows, volumetric light; earth tones; film grain, noise, distress, grunge; hand-drawn wobble, serif ornament, decorative flourishes; heavy black outlines with hard offset drop shadows; clashing patterns, squiggles, terrazzo; neon glow, HUD thin-lines, cyberpunk darkness; photorealistic human faces as the primary subject.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cyberminimalism** is dark, moody and futuristic: near-black fields, neon single accents, thin monospace or HUD linework. Flat Design is light, rounded, multi-accent and friendly, with pictograms instead of readouts.
- **Neubrutalism** shouts: thick black strokes around everything, hard offset shadows, raw default type, deliberately clashing color. Flat Design has no stylistic outlines, soft or absent shadows, and polished harmonious geometry.
- **International Typographic Style** is print-first: photography plus Helvetica on white, often black with a single red accent, strict asymmetric grids, square corners, no illustration vocabulary. Flat Design is screen-native, rounded, icon- and vector-illustration driven, with a cool multi-accent palette.
- **Memphis Design** is chaotic kitsch: squiggle lines, terrazzo and laminate patterns, black-and-white motifs clashing with pastels. Flat Design borrows Memphis geometry but keeps order, limited palettes and zero pattern noise.
- **Minimalism** at large is medium-agnostic and material-honest: monochrome, physical objects, real texture and light. Flat Design is digital-native, color-accented, defined by interface vocabulary, and forbids texture outright.

Recognition test: if the image looks like a screen or poster where every shape could be a UI element — matte fills, rounded corners, grid alignment, shadowless light — it is Flat Design.
