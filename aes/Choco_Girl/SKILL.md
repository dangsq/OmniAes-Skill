# Choco Girl

Choco Girl — also called Neapolitan Girl or Chocolatecore — is not a standalone subculture but a dessert-themed filter applied over established Japanese street fashions, above all Sweet Lolita and Himekaji, with softer appearances in Otome Kei. Its entire identity rests on two constraints: a strict Neapolitan color scheme (chocolate brown, strawberry pink, vanilla cream) and a vocabulary of edible motifs — chocolate bars, biscuits, dripping glaze, strawberries — rendered as prints, hyperrealistic accessories, and decoden craft. It grew out of 2000s Harajuku kawaii culture, matured through 2010s decoden and Lolita print series, and received its name only around 2023 from Tumblr and Pinterest users searching for a tag, later amplified by Shoujo Girl TikTok's revival of 2010s Himekaji. The result is an aesthetic that feels simultaneously handmade, nostalgic, and deliberately toy-like: chocolate as a craft material and a costume, never as gourmet luxury.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: the Neapolitan lock
The palette is a closed system of three families plus one tiny accent. Browns run from dark cocoa (`#3B2417`) through milk chocolate (`#6B4226`, `#7B4B2A`) to biscuit tan (`#B98A5A`); pinks run from powdered pastel (`#F4C9D3`) to strawberry (`#E78A9E`); creams and whites run from ivory (`#FBF3E4`) to warm vanilla (`#F1E3CC`) and plain white. A single berry red (`#B3242E`-ish) is permitted only as fruit flesh — a strawberry, a cherry on a glaze dome. The rule is relational, not proportional: brown must be structurally present (as garment, glaze, hair, or object body), pink and cream may trade dominance, and no hue outside this trio may enter. An image may be brown-heavy (chocolate-dominant), pink-heavy (strawberry-dominant), or cream-heavy (vanilla-dominant) and still be correct, so long as the trio reads as Neapolitan ice cream.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the style documents them
1. **Chocolate bars** — segmented squares, wrapper graphics, bar-shaped purses, necklaces and phone cases.
2. **Biscuits and cookies** — scalloped shortbread edges, iced swirls, checkerboard squares, heart-centered rings; cookie-shaped berets, purses and jewelry.
3. **The Neapolitan scheme itself** — brown/pink/cream blocks, stripes and trios quoting ice cream tubs.
4. **Dripping chocolate** — the signature pattern: glaze with a scalloped drip line along hems, edges and domes, often trimmed with piped cream or sugar pearls.
5. **Chocolate-covered strawberries** — whole berries dipped in glaze, the source of the pink-brown pairing.
6. **Hyperrealistic dessert accessories** — clay and plastic sweets (the Q-pot lineage): biscuit earmuffs, cupcake headpieces, cookie buttons, chocolate-bar charms in retail packaging.
7. **Decoden** — phones and small objects crusted with piped resin cream, miniature plastic donuts, cookies, berries, pearls and bows.
8. **Dessert-printed Lolita and Himekaji garments** — Sweet Lolita ("Choco Lolita") dresses in the Angelic Pretty "Melty Ribbon Chocolate" / "Royal Chocolate" lineage; casual blouses with biscuit-print skirts and cardigans in the trio colorway.
9. **Sanrio characters in choco attire** — Hello Kitty or My Melody dressed in brown-pink-cream Lolita, never in their default palettes.
10. **Heisei Retro "things shaped like other things"** — food-shaped erasers, headphone-earmuffs, plus the snapshot culture around them: handwritten captions ("yummy"), scalloped frames, sticker-like labels.

### Materials and textures
Glossy chocolate glaze with a wet specular highlight; piped whipped-cream ridges and rosettes; matte crumbly shortbread; satin and grosgrain ribbon bows; eyelet lace and frilled collars; soft knit cardigans; printed cotton sateen carrying dessert patterns; the resin-plastic sheen of hyperreal charms and decoden cabochons; pearl beads and sugar-pearl trim. Contrast between glossy glaze and matte crumb or cream is a core texture event.

### Recurring subjects
Girls wearing or holding dessert objects, frequently with the face playfully concealed behind a bar, cookie or headpiece; dolls and ball-jointed figures posed with heart-shaped chocolates; illustrated Lolita figures on diamond-argyle grounds with cake stands and candles; close-ups of accessories worn on head, ear or hand; confectionery still lifes (cookie plates, boxed chocolates in checkerboard arrays); packaged charm goods; mirror-selfie outfit checks in brown-pink coords.

### Composition and lighting
Composition favors intimacy over spectacle: tight crops on a headpiece or purse, overhead flat lays on lace or plain cloth, product shots on seamless cream grounds with cute small typography, mirror selfies, and collage-like snapshot frames with handwriting and scalloped borders. Lighting is soft and diffused, often hazy and slightly overexposed, or warmed toward sepia; contrast stays low, detail slightly soft, in the amateur 2010s phone-or-webcam register. Studio product light, when present, is even and shadowless. (The wiki text fixes palette and motifs but not photography; these lighting tendencies are distilled from the image corpus and are characteristic, not mandatory.)

## Mood & Values

Kawaii, sweetness, nostalgia. The style values handmade craft (decoden, DIY charms) over purchase-luxury; whimsical disguise (a cookie that is a hat, a phone that is a chocolate bar) over realism; and a Victorian-descended linking of desserts with femininity, played entirely in a childlike, tender register. Its nostalgia is double: for 2000s Heisei kawaii goods and for 2010s Harajuku street fashion, re-staged by 2020s internet communities. Playfulness beats elegance: even glossy glaze reads as toy food, not patisserie.

## Era & Origins

Roots: 2000s Harajuku kawaii / Heisei Retro, with its food-shaped novelties and sweet motifs. Development: 2010s — decoden craft, Himekaji splitting away from Gyaru, and Sweet Lolita print series such as Angelic Pretty's chocolate themes. Naming: circa 2023, coined on Tumblr and Pinterest as a search tag and spread by Shoujo Girl TikTok, a coquette-adjacent community reviving 2010s Japanese fashion independently of its original styling context. The aesthetic is therefore a retro theme recognized late: its objects are 2000s–2010s, its label is 2020s microtrend culture.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Lock the palette to the Neapolitan trio; keep brown structural; allow berry red only as fruit.
2. Include at least one edible signifier (bar segments, biscuit, drip glaze, piped cream, strawberry) or one dessert-shaped object.
3. Use the drip line and piped-cream ridge as the style's pattern grammar on hems, edges and domes.
4. Finish everything kawaii-craft, not gourmet: plastic-clay charm realism, printed cotton, resin cream — never gold-foil truffle luxury.
5. Light softly: diffused, hazy, low-contrast, optionally warm-sepia; avoid harsh flash and dramatic shadow.
6. If clothing appears, descend from Lolita or Himekaji silhouettes — collars, bows, lace, skirt volume, or the casual blouse-plus-print-skirt and colorway cardigan formula.
7. If text appears, keep it small, handwritten or cute-label style ("yummy", product tags), never bold modern display type.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a fashion coord, an accessory close-up, a confectionery still life, a decoden object, a doll, a digital illustration, packaged merchandise, or a Sanrio-style character in choco dress. Composition may be flat lay, worn close-up, mirror selfie, product shot, or illustrated poster. Lighting may range from overexposed snapshot haze to even studio cream-ground light. Color weighting may shift brown-, pink- or cream-dominant. Mood may run playful, wistful-nostalgic, or crafty-documentary. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Rainbow or neon candy colors; mint, lavender, baby blue or any cool pastel; black-dominant gothic palettes; gold, metallic or minimalist luxury styling; savory food, rustic bakery wood, or dark moody gourmet food photography; harsh direct flash or chiaroscuro; industrial or modern-minimal settings; floral-only or heart-only prints with no dessert motif; bold contemporary typography; mature or sexualized styling; plain monochrome cream imagery with no brown anchor.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Overlap | Choco Girl tell |
|---|---|---|
| Candycore | Sweets as subject | Candycore spans the whole confectionery rainbow — gummy reds, mints, lemons, neon sprinkles; Choco Girl never leaves brown/pink/cream and always keeps cocoa brown structural. |
| Pianocore | Cream/ivory daintiness | Pianocore is monochrome keyboard black-white-ivory with sheet music and recital cues; no food motifs, no pink, no glaze brown. |
| 2010s Soft Kawaii | Hazy pastel snapshot mood | Soft Kawaii admits cool pastels, plushies and pastel hair; Choco Girl excludes cool hues and demands literal chocolate signifiers. |
| Dollette | Dusty rose/cream vintage-doll femininity, bows | Dollette is coquette-doll-like with ballet flats and porcelain imagery but no dessert motifs; Choco Girl always carries bars, cookies, drips or strawberries. |
| Himekaji | Parent casual style | Himekaji accepts any colorway and floral prints; only coords applying the Neapolitan trio plus dessert prints are Choco Girl — and Choco Girl also lives on Sweet Lolita silhouettes Himekaji never uses. |
| Sweet Lolita / Sanriocore | Silhouette and characters | Plain Sweet Lolita may be sax or pink with no sweets; Sanriocore is character-first in default palettes. Choco Girl requires the dessert theme to override both. |

When in doubt, apply the two-part test: is the hue set exactly Neapolitan, and is something in frame edible or shaped like edible? Both yes — Choco Girl.
