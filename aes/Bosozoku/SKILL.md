# Bosozoku

Bosozoku (暴走族, "run-out-of-control tribe"), also called kaminarizoku or "Thunder Tribe," is the Japanese subculture of biker gangs organized around deliberately excessive customized motorcycles and the embroidered gang coats worn on outings. It grew out of 1950s yanki delinquency, hardened into its own custom-bike culture through the 1960s–80s, and survives as a strongly coded visual language: loud machines, louder coats, and the group formation as portrait. The aesthetic is fundamentally *collective and declarative* — every surface (coat back, fuel tank, flag cloth) carries the gang's name and slogans in kanji.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule: two coordinated fields.** The garment field is a dark ground carrying metallic light: lacquer black (#0B0B0D–#1A1A1A) satin or leather, overlaid with metallic gold thread (#B8860B–#E6C35C), vermilion and crimson (#B01E24–#E4002B), and off-white stitch (#F2F0E6); an indigo/blue happi-coat variant (#1F3A6E–#2E5E9E) with white kanji stitching is a documented alternate garment ground. The machine field is candy and chrome: magenta and hot pink (#C2186B–#FF7EB0), violet purple (#5E2A84–#9B4DCA), pearl white (#EDE6F2), chrome silver (#B9C0C7–#E9EDF0), gold leaf (#C9A227), with accent red and rising-sun ray graphics in red/white or gold. The governing rule is contrast of grounds: coats are dark so gold embroidery reads; bikes are saturated candy or pearl so chrome and paint graphics read. Outfits and machine paint are frequently color-matched as one designed system — the source notes coordination "down to the acrylic nails."

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the text documents them:**

1. **Tokkofuku (特攻服) coats** — long black coats, worn only for outings and graduations, whose back panels are fully embroidered: vertical kanji columns (gang names and slogans, stereotypically nonsense phrases), a central crest (chrysanthemum, rising sun), phoenix/dragon/eagle birds, floral sprays, all in raised metallic thread. These are costly heirlooms handed down the gang hierarchy.
2. **The over-customized motorcycle** — eye-catching paint jobs (candy, pearl, gold leaf, airbrushed rays, flames, kanji); the **sandan seat**, a tall three-tier seat with a towering backrest, often in white or pearl vinyl; exaggerated fairings, fins and antenna-like extensions that push the silhouette vertically.
3. **Large flags and banners** bearing gang kanji, mounted on bikes or carried in formation.
4. **Hachimaki headbands** with slogans.
5. **Air horns** in trumpet clusters, and straight-pipe or removed mufflers — the historical thunder-noise signature.
6. **Crew styling**: pompadour/regent hair, white boots or tabi-style footwear, masked or sunglass-hidden faces in the photographic record, women's hair, makeup and nails matched to the bike's paint.
7. **Group grammar**: lineups, rows of machines, backs turned to the camera so embroidery reads.

**Materials and textures:** matte satin and cracked leather under dense raised embroidery; chrome plating and weld seams; candy paint with pearl or flake depth; piped vinyl seats; cloth flags; hand-painted touch-ups. The prevailing medium is the amateur film snapshot — grain, slight overexposure, saturated snapshot color.

**Recurring subjects and settings:** convenience-store forecourts, roadside and highway parking areas, night streets; posed crews with raised fists or folded arms; a lone rider astride a towering machine; parked bike rows treated as group portraits; detail views of coat backs and tank paint.

**Composition and lighting tendencies:** center-weighted full-body standing poses; rear views to display embroidery; diagonal rows of bikes receding through frame; low angles that exaggerate seat height, fins and flags. Lighting ranges from harsh midday sun with punchy color to night scenes lit by on-camera flash or streetlight. Framing is candid and documentary, not studio-staged.

## Mood & Values

Defiant spectacle: loudness (exhaust thunder, air horns, towering seats) as identity. Loyalty and hierarchy expressed through heirloom coats; pride of craft in expensive embroidery and custom paint; teenage bravado and territorial pride; communal belonging in matched outfits and formations; a nostalgic, slightly grainy memory of Showa and Heisei street life.

## Era & Origins

Emerged in the 1950s from the yanki biker gang "Thunder Tribe" (kaminarizoku), whose members removed mufflers for speed and produced thunder-like noise; gangs multiplied through the 1960s, and after a 1972 mass incident in Toyama City the media fixed the term bosozoku ("boso" = losing control, effectively road rage). Peak visibility 1970s–80s, with female crews and revivals carrying the look later. Media lineage includes the documentary *God Speed You! Black Emperor* (1976), film *Crazy Thunder Road* (1980), and manga/anime such as *Great Teacher Onizuka*, *Shakotan Boogie* and *Tokyo Revengers*. The source names a custom-car branch, zokusha, but leaves it undocumented — treat car-based imagery as thin-evidence extension, not core.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles:**
1. Treat coat and machine as one designed system: match or deliberately rhyme embroidery palette with bike paint.
2. Density over restraint: embroidery fills the back panel; graphics fill the tank; chrome fills the frame edges.
3. Push verticality: tall sandan backrests, flags, fins, antennas.
4. Use kanji as graphic texture — vertical columns and central crests; nonsense slogans are idiomatic, not errors.
5. Keep snapshot realism: mundane real-world backdrops (storefronts, parking lots, night streets), amateur framing, film color.
6. Prefer group grammar: lineups, rows, rear views.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo rider, male crew, female crew, mixed crew, machines without people, coat-back or tank detail crops, moving night runs. Color handling: orthodox black/gold; candy pink-purple pearl; indigo happi variant; chrome-dominant. Composition: posed lineup, candid ride-by, rear view, diagonal bike row, tight detail. Lighting: midday sun, overcast day, dusk streetlight, night flash. Mood: celebratory, menacing, playful (peace signs), nostalgic. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid:** Western outlaw-biker codes (leather cut-off vests with Latin-alphabet patches, cruiser/Harley silhouettes, ape hangers, bandana identity); clean studio or showroom product photography; matte wraps and minimalist design; unmodified modern sportbikes; cyberpunk neon-grid futurism (candy paint is pearl and chrome over metal, not emissive glow); military-uniform reenactment (tokkofuku allude to kamikaze dress but are embroidered gang coats, not uniform accuracy); zen minimalism, muted earth tones, empty negative space; pristine condition with no wear, welds or hand-finished imperfection.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Sukeban:** female delinquency expressed through modified schoolwear — long skirts, sailor collars — without machines or embroidered coats; bosozoku women keep the tokkofuku and the custom bike and coordinate both.
- **Yanki:** the parent street-deliquent fashion (pompadours, tapered trousers, souvenir jackets) with no mandatory machine; bosozoku is its biker-gang branch where the customized motorcycle and the coat carry the identity.
- **Biker (Western/outlaw):** black leather, chrome austerity, Latin-script club patches, low-slung cruisers; bosozoku reads through kanji embroidery coats, candy pearl paint, vertical sandan seats, flags, and vintage Japanese standards stretched upward rather than down.
- **Gyaruo:** male gyaru fashion — tanned skin, bleached hair, flashy branded casualwear — with no gang coats or bikes; overlap exists only where gyaru hair, makeup and nails ride on bosozoku machines, and the coat, embroidery and custom bike remain the deciding markers.
