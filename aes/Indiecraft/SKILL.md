# Indiecraft

Indiecraft is the aesthetic of the handmade miniature world: dioramas, puppets, paper costumes and collage sets built on tabletops and photographed or filmed as if they were real places. It emerged in the mid-2000s inside the same indie-music milieu as Hipness Purgatory, and it expresses itself through craft techniques — collage, maquettes, stop motion, papercraft-like 3D illustration — rather than through drawn or vector graphics. Its cultural home is the indie music video, the craft-styled album cover, the papercraft video game (LittleBigPlanet, Tearaway, Lumino City, Yoshi's Crafted World), and later the Instagram craft-photo feed. The governing idea is childlike imagination executed with adult materials: a world where a cloud can sit in a waffle cone, a mug can hug another mug, and a person can wear a paper cone as a body.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The text anchors the palette in **brown and muted colors**, with **warm colors** carrying the cozy atmosphere. The image corpus confirms that base and shows how far accents may travel: saturation is allowed up to the density of dyed construction paper, never up to screen neon. Two registered modes both count as correct:

- **Muted craft mode:** cardboard browns, oatmeal felt, cream paper, dusty sage and faded teal, often under a vintage cross-processed filter with lifted blacks and a warm cast.
- **Construction-paper mode:** flat matte sheets of mustard, tomato, teal, raspberry and lavender laid side by side like a paper collage, still reading as pigmented fiber rather than glow.

Rule: any hue is admissible if it reads as a material dye — paper, felt, yarn, painted card — and if the overall key stays warm or paper-neutral. Glossy, emissive or gradient color breaks the style.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Maquettes and dioramas** — miniature built worlds: cardboard villages, paper castles, tabletop stages with tiny inhabitants, viewed head-on like a proscenium.
2. **Collage layouts** — cut-paper shapes, bunting flags, letter blocks and pasted geometry arranged on flat grounds; the graphic-design arm of the style (posters, album and book covers).
3. **Puppets and stop-motion characters** — felt monsters, yarn people, cloth creatures with stitched seams and button or bead eyes.
4. **Papercraft objects and papercraft-like 3D illustration** — folded cones, creased planes, torn edges; the look of craft materials rendered or built as game worlds.
5. **Felt and construction paper as declared materials** — the text names these explicitly; their fuzz and fiber should be visible.
6. **Pareidolia objects** — everyday things given simple childlike faces: a smiling mug, a happy coffee cup, a shy power outlet. Directly serves the text's "childlike imagination."
7. **Cozy domestic craft props** — mugs, cupcakes, knitted and crocheted forms, stitched textiles, handmade signs.
8. **Honest stagecraft** — visible backdrop stands, rigging, tape, thread, bare feet under paper costumes; the machinery of the handmade set is allowed to show.

### Materials and textures

Cardboard corrugation, construction-paper matte, felt fuzz, yarn and knit loops, cloth stitching, clay fingerprints, glue ridges, scissor-wonky cut edges, paper creases and tape tabs. Texture is the style's fingerprint: every surface should announce what it is made of and that a hand made it.

### Recurring subjects

Miniature towns and rooms; puppet and felt creatures; paper-costumed humans partially hidden by their own costumes; toys photographed as protagonists; objects with faces; crafted food still lifes; cut-paper patterns and confetti grids; tiny figures dwarfed by handmade landscapes or props.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Composition favors the **tabletop stage**: frontal, slightly elevated or eye-level-with-a-miniature framing, as if looking into a built set; the **flat collage ground**: subjects arranged on a single colored backdrop like pasted elements; and the **shallow-depth close-up**: miniature figures or craft objects shot with macro lens falloff so the handmade scale reads instantly. Scale jokes are structural — a held object replacing something huge, humans dwarfed by paper geometry, tiny paper people in a paper field. Lighting is soft and diffuse: overcast daylight, warm tungsten glow from practical lamps inside dioramas, or a faded vintage film cast with teal shadows and warm highlights. Hard flash, crisp HDRI studio light and perfect ambient occlusion are absent.

## Mood & Values

Cozy, kooky, charming. Indiecraft values the made over the bought: visible effort, imperfect symmetry, and the sincerity of childlike play executed with care. There is room for a quiet uncanny undercurrent — stitched blank faces, dim rooms, slightly wrong proportions — inherited from its stop-motion and puppet-TV lineage; the kookiness can tip toward gentle melancholy without leaving the style. The emotional contract is warmth with a wink: never cynical, never slick.

## Era & Origins

Coined for the mid-2000s (term attributed to Jack Grimes, CARI Institute), emerging alongside and overlapping Hipness Purgatory in the indie music scene: Franz Ferdinand's "Take Me Out" (2004) and the 2004–2009 wave of craft-styled music videos mark its arrival; the 2008–2015 papercraft game wave (LittleBigPlanet, Kirby's Epic Yarn, Tearaway, Lumino City) is its native digital territory; Instagram became its primary sharing platform. Its precursors are claymation and puppet stop motion of the 1980s–90s (Pingu, The Neverhood, Gregory Horror Show). It reacts against seamless digital polish by insisting on the physical artifact; its cousin Skeuomorphism shares that insistence in interface form.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Material first.** Decide what the world is built from — cardboard, construction paper, felt, yarn, clay, cloth — before choosing a subject; the material logic drives every other decision.
2. **Leave the hand in.** Seams, stitches, tape, glue, wobbly cut lines and slight asymmetry are mandatory evidence of making.
3. **Stage, don't document.** Treat the frame as a tabletop theatre or a collage board; even a photograph of a real place should feel like a built set.
4. **Play with scale and substitution.** One surreal equation executed in craft logic (a held object standing in for a landscape element, humans scaled against oversized props) is idiomatic; keep the substitution tactile, not digital.
5. **Light it like handmade film.** Soft diffuse key, warm practicals inside dioramas, or a faded vintage cast; shadows stay gentle.
6. **Color as dye, not emission.** Use the palette rule above; matte paper and fiber saturation only.
7. **Faces are a gift.** Simple dot eyes and stitched smiles on objects welcome; puppet faces stay spare and slightly odd.
8. **Typography as craft.** Any lettering should be cut paper, painted card, bunting or letter blocks — never clean digital type floating free of material.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- **Subject:** dioramas, puppets, paper-costume photo staging, pareidolia object portraits, craft still lifes, cut-paper pattern fields, papercraft game-world frames, collage posters and covers — anything that can plausibly be built by hand.
- **Composition:** frontal proscenium stage, flat collage ground, macro shallow-focus close-up, forced-perspective scale joke, grid or scatter pattern; horizon may be a tabletop edge.
- **Lighting:** overcast daylight, warm tungsten interior glow, dim cozy practicals, faded cross-processed film cast; intensity from bright-and-airy to dusk-dim.
- **Color handling:** full muted cardboard monochrome at one end, full construction-paper saturation at the other, vintage-filtered or clean matte in between.
- **Mood:** sunny charm → quiet melancholy → kooky uncanny; all in range so long as warmth and handmade sincerity survive.

**Avoid**

- Seamless glossy CGI, plastic sheen, chrome, glass, neon gradients or emissive digital color.
- Mass-produced plastic toy clutter and rainbow-primary maximalism (that is Kidcore's bought nostalgia, not made craft).
- Flat vector minimalism with no texture or seams (Hipness Purgatory's screen-native surface).
- Candid flash documentary photography of unstyled real life (Indie sleaze territory).
- Drawn caricature with ink outlines and exaggerated anatomy (Caricaturism builds with line, Indiecraft builds with material).
- Machine-perfect symmetry, crisp corporate render lighting, hyperreal surfaces.
- Gothic darkness, grunge decay, vaporwave grids — any mood that mocks or corrodes the cozy.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Hipness Purgatory:** same mid-2000s indie air and muted warmth, but HP is flat, vector-clean, deadpan and screen-native; Indiecraft always has fiber, seam and stage. If nothing in frame looks physically built, it is not Indiecraft.
- **Whimsicraft:** shares felt and yarn, but Whimsicraft stays domestic and sweetly earnest — embroidered hoops, knitted toys, cottage décor. Indiecraft is kookier and set-driven: dioramas, puppets, collage posters, music-video stagecraft, with license for the absurd and lightly uncanny.
- **Caricaturism:** exaggeration by drawn line and grotesque proportion; Indiecraft's exaggeration comes from material clumsiness — wonky cuts, oversized paper forms — in photographed or craft-rendered worlds.
- **Indie:** lo-fi authenticity of real rooms, real parties, analog grain and thrift clothes; Indiecraft fabricates miniature worlds. The test: is the subject lived-in or built-in?
- **Kidcore:** glossy 90s plastic nostalgia at full primary saturation; Indiecraft is matte paper, felt and yarn over a warm muted base, celebrating making rather than owning.

Where the textual record is thin — lighting specifics, for instance — the patterns above are read from the corpus of exemplars: soft warm keys, vintage casts and practical glow inside sets. Treat those as observed norms, not doctrine; the doctrine is the hand, the material and the stage.
