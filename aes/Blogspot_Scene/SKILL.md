# Blogspot Scene

## Overview

The Blogspot Scene was the online-driven music community that coalesced in the early 2000s inside the American post-noise underground, releasing hypnagogic drone and ambient on handmade CDRs and cassettes through netlabels such as New Age Tapes, Pacific City Sound Visions, Housecraft Recordings and Ekhein, distributed and exhibited via Blogspot-hosted blogs. Its visual side is inseparable from its physical format: surrealist, psychedelic printed collage sized to cassette J-cards and CDR inserts, photocopied until grainy and deliberately made to look old and degraded. The style travels under many aliases — Dr/Am Cassette Era, Blogbient, Hypnagogic Tape Ambient-Drone, Post-Noise Psychedelia — and its artifacts read less like designed album covers than like scanned relics of an occult archive: paper, toner, scissors, glue, and a fold crease down the middle. James Ferraro is its named originator, with a circle of practitioners including The Skaters, Spencer Clark, Oneohtrix Point Never, Pocahaunted, Emeralds, Yellow Swans, Sun Araw and Lamborghini Crystal.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The documented core is monochrome: the photocopier's spectrum from toner black (around #0b0d0c) through xerox mid-greys (#6f736f to #8b8f8a) to paper white (#eceae4 to #f4f2ec). Much of the output shifts that spectrum into a single duotone tint — sage or teal-grey (#5d6b64 to #7d8b84), or sepia-ochre (#7c6a45 to #a8926b). A minority of artifacts are degraded color prints: murky moss green (#4f5c3d to #6a7a52), amber-brown water (#6b5a3a), night indigo (#10141c) speckled with white star dots, and at most one sliver of comparatively saturated color — a wedge of sky blue (#4e86a5 to #7fb2c9), a faded rose shirt (#c9a3a0). The rule: monochrome first; color never arrives clean, only as drift, murk, or a single isolated sliver. A fully saturated palette is never correct.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the text documents them

1. **Cut-and-paste photocopy collage** — the infobox's sole key motif; every other element lives inside this logic of scissors and toner.
2. **Esoteric figures** — aliens (big-eyed grey masks), cryptids; the text names these first among subjects.
3. **World landmarks and antique science** — Egyptian pyramids per the text; artifacts extend the family to obelisks, armillary spheres, celestial globes, classical columns and ornate engraved machines.
4. **Deliberate degradation** — grain, halftone, blur, creases; images that "sought to appear old and degraded."
5. **Package grammar** — cassette J-card panels with a center fold, square CDR insert fields, and tiny typewritten or label-maker caption strips carrying artist and title.
6. **Surrealist stage props** (observed across artifacts, secondary to the text): checkerboard floors, mirrored or kaleidoscopic repetition of a single cutout, tropical palms and dense foliage, starfields over rivers, murky water reflections, nude or sculptural figure cutouts, vintage engraving clip-art of vessels and instruments.

### Materials and textures

Photocopier toner speckle and halftone rosettes; paper fiber and the flat glare of scan glass; cut-paper hard edges with an occasional faint lift shadow; newsprint and steel-engraving linework; embossed label-maker tape; typewriter strikes; a center fold crease; degraded color print with color cast and soft blur. Everything should feel like it has passed through a reproduction chain — original, copy, copy, scan.

### Composition and lighting

The camera is a flatbed scanner: a flat frontal plane with no perspective of its own. Each fragment keeps its own internal lighting, scale and grain, and coherence between fragments is refused — a noon engraving can sit inside a night photograph. Composition ranges from dense full-bleed montage (a halftone cityscape as ground, pierced by a diamond or rectangular window into another image) to sparse cutouts floating on paper white; from mirrored bilateral symmetry to a single off-center window; from square album fields to portrait J-card panels with a fold to landscape scanned prints. Caption strips anchor to a bottom edge or corner. Scale jumps are welcome: a head as large as a building, a sphere hovering over a palm grove.

## Mood & Values

Hypnagogic — half-dreamt, half-remembered. Occult wonder played deadpan. Degradation is treated as memory rather than damage: the third-generation copy is more truthful than the original. The style carries a DIY anti-professional ethic (cheap duplication, netlabel community, pseudonymous artists, the blog as gallery) and a Dada-descended absurd humor in its juxtapositions. It values mystery over legibility, process over polish, and the cheap object over the glossy one.

## Era & Origins

Early 2000s, United States, born in the post-noise underground and reacting against both clean digital production and the professional design economy: the answer to Pro Tools polish was a photocopier and a blank CDR. It feeds the later hypnagogic drift toward vaporwave's consumer nostalgia, but stays paper-bound and monochrome where vaporwave goes neon and chrome. Artifacts dated as late as 2025 show the grammar still in use by current netlabels, so treat it as a living revival whose canonical body sits roughly 2007–2010.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Assemble, never depict: build the image from discrete cut-out fragments with visible hard edges; there is no unified painted scene underneath.
2. Degrade in generations: aim for a third-generation photocopy — toner speckle, halftone dots, blur, crushed blacks, blown highlights, paper fiber, a fold crease.
3. Monochrome by default; a single duotone tint is optional; muted degraded color with one saturated sliver is the outer limit.
4. Mix source eras freely: Victorian engravings, mid-century press photos, 1970s–90s UFO and cryptozoology snapshots, cheap clip-art, faded landscape prints.
5. Juxtapose without explaining: no narrative glue, no caption that resolves the mystery.
6. Honor the package: square or J-card proportions, fold lines, tiny mechanical caption strips.
7. Light is a reproduction artifact: flat scanner illumination overall, conflicting internal lighting per fragment; no consistent cast shadows across layers.
8. Keep type minimal and mechanical — typewriter, label-maker, photocopied sans — and small.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: anything esoteric or archival — aliens, cryptids, pyramids, obelisks, armillary spheres, foliage, starfields, water, machines, figures — including ordinary pastoral material (walkers by a river, garden leaves, swimmers) made esoteric by degradation and juxtaposition.
- *Composition*: dense full-bleed montage ↔ sparse cutouts on paper white; mirrored symmetry ↔ single geometric window; square ↔ portrait J-card ↔ landscape print.
- *Color handling*: pure black-and-white ↔ single-tint duotone (sage, sepia, teal) ↔ murky degraded color with one sliver.
- *Lighting/contrast*: blown-out xerox ↔ murky mid-tone print ↔ night fragment with star specks.
- *Mood*: occult dread ↔ pastoral hypnagogia ↔ deadpan absurdity.
- *Text*: none ↔ tiny caption strip ↔ stamped title.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — cut-paper logic, reproduction decay, esoteric archival mood, package grammar — not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Clean vector shapes, crisp digital gradients, glossy 3D renders, seamless photographic realism, bokeh, lens flare.
- A unified single-point-perspective scene with consistent lighting across all elements.
- Neon vaporwave palette: magenta-cyan grids, chrome, pastel gradients, iridescent glow.
- Digital corruption as the decay language: pixel sorting, datamoshing, JPEG crush, screen-UI overlays.
- Ransom-note shouting typography, slogans, political flyer energy.
- Rain-slicked empty urban night and seamless photographic desolation without collage.
- Crystals, angels, soft-focus affirmation glow.
- Pristine white studio backgrounds without paper texture; stock-photo sharpness; smartphones or modern devices; large high-fidelity branding type.

## Discrimination Cues

- **DIY Punk** shares xerox collage and DIY ethics, but punk collage is a flyer: ransom-note type, slogans, confrontational high-contrast black-and-white, agitational urgency. Blogspot keeps type tiny and mechanical, mood hypnagogic, palette open to tint and murk, subjects archival-esoteric rather than political.
- **Weirdcore** is digital amateur dread: low-res camera-flash photos of liminal rooms, uncanny captions in plain system fonts, JPEG artifacts. Blogspot's decay is analog reproduction — toner, halftone, paper, fold — and its space is assembled from cutouts, not photographed; Weirdcore has no scissors.
- **Xpiritualism** offers New Age glow: crystals, angels, iridescent pastels, reassuring soft light. Blogspot shares esoteric subject matter but renders it through grainy photocopy and deadpan archival distance — no glow, no pastel, no comfort.
- **Dadaism** is historical photomontage as satirical manifesto: sharp political intent, clean print reproduction, modernist typography. Blogspot inherits the cut-and-paste grammar but adds generation-loss photocopy, cassette-package format and dream-logic; its collages haunt rather than argue.
- **Draincore** is smooth digital gloom: desaturated rain-soaked urban night, empty streets, seamless photographic sadness. Blogspot's murk sits on paper with cut edges and esoteric clip-art; if there is no collage layering and no grain of reproduction, it is not Blogspot.
- **Vaporwave**, its descendant, is the easiest false friend: neon grids, chrome busts, pastel gradients. Blogspot is the grainy paper ancestor — if it glistens, it is elsewhere.
