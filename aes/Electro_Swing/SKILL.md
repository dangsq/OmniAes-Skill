# Electro Swing (Swing House, Boom Swing)

Electro Swing is a dance-music genre and its companion visual aesthetic: vintage swing and jazz welded to house and hip-hop, and a picture language that does the same welding visually. Its images are a 2010s internet-era interpretation of the 1920s through 1950s — deliberately impure, deliberately playful, described by its own community as an "anachronism stew." Speakeasies and flapper girls share the frame with robots and neon; rubber-hose cartoon limbs bounce over grainy film overlays; halftone photograph fragments get collaged against flat vector shapes and letterpress type. Nothing in the style is meant to look like a faithful period document. Everything is meant to look like a record sleeve, a gig poster, or an animation cel from a decade that never happened, scored to a four-on-the-floor beat with a horn section on top.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
The ground is always aged and desaturated; the accent is always loud. Base layers run through sepia and parchment cream (`#E8DCC3`, `#D9C9A3`, `#C9B68A`), warm ink browns (`#8A6F4D`, `#6B5638`), and grayscale or black-and-white (`#1A1A1A` to `#F2EDE2`). On top of that ground sit one of two accent systems, sometimes both: period print inks — oxblood and crimson reds (`#8E2434`, `#6E1420`), mustard and brass yellows (`#D9A425`, `#E3B32C`), muted verdigris teals (`#4E7E7B`, `#6FA3A0`) — or electric neon highlights, chiefly electric blue (`#00E5FF`, `#2BD9FF`) and magenta (`#FF2E9A`, `#E93CAC`). Neon is documented as a defining accent but behaves as spice rather than base: signage tubes, glow rims, a single saturated hue against grayscale, never a neon-saturated environment. Muted multi-color sunbursts (rust, teal, cream alternating) are a common middle path.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them
1. **Rubber-hose animation revival** — the style's stated major pillar: noodle limbs without joints, heavy ink outlines, pie-cut eyes, bouncy off-balance poses synced to syncopated rhythm, grainy film overlay (the lineage of *Bendy and the Ink Machine* and *Cuphead*).
2. **Speakeasies, stages, dance floors, stylized old buildings** — the default settings: hidden bars, bandstands, parquet floors, deco facades.
3. **Flapper girls and vintage-dressed gentlemen** — bobbed hair and headbands, drop-waist dresses, suspenders, waistcoats, boaters, top hats, bow ties.
4. **Robots** — riveted tin-plate retro robots, the kind of toy machine that belongs to no real decade, seated at gramophones or standing in for band members.
5. **Anthropomorphic characters in vintage attire** — pandas, horses, dogs in three-piece suits playing keyboards or horns; a core graphic-design pillar.
6. **Neon signage and neon highlights** — the modern intrusion that flags the anachronism.
7. **Music apparatus as emblem** — gramophone morning-glory horns, stacked shellac records and vinyl, trumpets, trombones, clarinets, saxophones, and DJ decks or cassettes placed where they historically cannot belong. The phonograph horn in particular functions as the scene's heraldic device.

### Materials and textures
Aged paper fiber, foxing, creased and torn deckle edges; halftone dot screens and photogravure tonality on any photographic element; letterpress ink squash and slight misregistration; linocut or woodcut line work for seals and emblems; grainy film overlay, scratches and dust unifying every layer; scalloped medallions, sunburst rays, star bursts and double-rule borders as framing hardware. A clean, pristine surface is never correct — wear is the proof of concept.

### Composition and lighting
The grammar is poster and record-sleeve grammar: a centered emblem or medallion with radial sunburst behind it; curved banner type arcing over and under a subject; symmetrical stacks of display typography; ornamental corner flourishes and thin double-line frames. Collage logic governs layering — cutout photography, flat vector shapes, engraved linework and type pasted together with visible seams. Dance scenes instead use bouncy asymmetry: knees bent, arms flung, bodies off vertical. Lighting is graphic rather than cinematic: flat vector light, soft vignettes darkening the paper edges, local neon glow as the only "real" light source, halftone tonality on photo fragments. No depth-of-field showmanship, no HDR sheen.

## Mood & Values

Positivity and joy are the style's declared values, and they are non-negotiable: every frame should read as a party invitation. The nostalgia is active reinterpretation, not museum replication — the archive is sampled like a vinyl hook, chopped and recontextualized with a wink. The community also holds that swing is jazz rooted in Black culture, so the celebratory energy carries respect for a living musical lineage, not costume-play emptiness. Whimsy, absurdity (animal-headed orchestras, robots at the turntable) and dance-floor inclusivity complete the emotional register: buoyant, syncopated, generous.

## Era & Origins

Musically the genre originates in early-1990s European and American experiments (Doop's 1994 single, Mr Scruff's 1999 "Get a Move On!") under the nu-jazz banner, resurges in the mid-2000s via labels such as Freshly Squeezed and Wagram, breaks through with Caravan Palace in 2008, Yolanda Be Cool's 2010 chart hit, and Parov Stelar's 2013 mainstream exposure. The visual code, however, crystallizes in the 2010s internet: indie animation, fandom, and album-art culture remixing the 1920s–1950s. The style reacts against two things at once — faithful period revival, which it treats as raw sample material, and sterile contemporary EDM minimalism, which it answers with grain, collage and humor.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Put two eras in one frame: an archival base (paper, halftone, period dress) plus at least one impossible intrusion (robot, neon tube, headphones, cassette, DJ deck).
2. Desaturated ground, loud accent: sepia/grayscale/cream first, print inks or a single neon hue second.
3. Texture is mandatory: grain, halftone, paper wear, ink squash. Clean digital rendering breaks the style.
4. Typography is a subject, not a caption: stacked Art Deco display serifs, circus slabs, script signatures, curved banners, letters ornamented with stars, records or bow ties.
5. Make syncopation visible: poses mid-bounce, weight off-axis, limbs in rubber-hose curves.
6. Keep the party: even odd or macabre-adjacent props stay celebratory in tone.
7. Collage over realism: cut, layer, misregister; let seams show.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
- *Subject*: object-only emblems (a gramophone horn in a medallion), portrait cutouts in top hats, dancing couples, full band lineups, animal-headed orchestras, robot vignettes, stylized street or dance-hall scenes, vintage transport as collage fragments.
- *Composition*: centered medallion with sunburst, symmetrical poster stack, scattered collage field, full-bleed illustrated scene with a type band, linocut seal or emblem.
- *Color handling*: monochrome sepia or grayscale; sepia plus oxblood/mustard/teal inks; muted multi-color sunburst; grayscale photo with one electric neon accent.
- *Lighting*: flat graphic light, soft vignette, local neon glow, halftone photo tonality, grainy cel shading.
- *Medium*: flat vector, paper collage, woodcut/linocut seal, halftone photo mix, grainy animation cel.
- *Mood*: exuberant, wry, romantic-nostalgic, absurd-playful.

**Avoid**
- Faithful period replication with no anachronism (that is Flapper-era revival, not Electro Swing).
- Neon-dominant nightscapes, chrome grids, sunset gradients, chrome sans-serif type (Synthwave territory).
- War machinery, oil grime, olive-and-steel industrialism, propaganda severity (Dieselpunk).
- Pure monochrome surreal rubber-hose worlds with uncanny or dreamlike mood and no party context (Fleischer).
- Glossy 3D renders, smooth gradients, pristine vector with no grain or paper wear.
- Minimalist modern sans-serif typography, corporate flat design, clean white space.
- Casual contemporary clothing or technology played straight; modern tech only appears as a deliberate anachronistic gag.
- Horror, dread, desolation, or any desaturation that reads as grim rather than aged.
- Candy-bright saturated palettes with no aged base layer.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Overlap | The Electro Swing tell |
|---|---|---|
| Flapper | 1920s dress, speakeasies, Deco type | Flapper stays period-faithful and elegant; Electro Swing always inserts one impossible element — robot, neon, headphones, cassette — and treats the archive as remix material with poster flatness and party energy. |
| Fleischer Style | Rubber-hose limbs, heavy ink, grain | Fleischer is a self-contained 1930s surreal monochrome animation world; Electro Swing borrows the hose-limbed body as one ingredient inside a collage-poster system, adds color or neon accents and dance-music iconography, and stays celebratory rather than dreamlike-uncanny. |
| Dieselpunk | Retro machines, robots, mid-century settings | Dieselpunk is industrial-military: chrome, oil grime, olive and steel, war hardware, severe propaganda tone. Electro Swing's machines are toys and party props — tin robots, gramophones — on sepia paper with neon accents and joy. |
| Synthwave | Neon highlights | Synthwave is 1980s night: neon as the dominant field, dark grids, chrome sunsets. Electro Swing's neon is a sparse accent over an aged sepia or grayscale ground, with Art Deco and letterpress type and 1920s–50s props. |

Where the record is thin, say so in practice: neon environments, photographic club documentation and three-dimensional set designs are barely evidenced in the scene's surviving artwork, which leans heavily on printed poster and sleeve design — when in doubt, build the image as paper, ink and grain first, and let the neon be the wink.
