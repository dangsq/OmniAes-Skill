# Straight Edge (sXe)

Straight edge is a subculture of deliberate sobriety that split off from American hardcore punk in the early 1980s, naming itself after Minor Threat's 1981 song "Straight Edge." Its aesthetic is a *rebellion within a rebellion*: it keeps hardcore's speed, volume, DIY print culture and club intensity, but strips out punk's self-destructive props and replaces them with a uniform of discipline — clean-cut grooming, athletic casual wear, and one unmistakable glyph, the X, reclaimed from the mark bouncers put on underage hands to deny them alcohol. Everything visual in the style serves that statement: clarity, self-control, unity, and pride in refusing intoxication.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The base is monochrome: marker and print ink black (`#0D0D0D`–`#1C1C1C`), bone and photocopy white (`#EDEAE2`–`#F5F2EA`), and halftone greys (`#6E6E6E`–`#A9A9A9`). One accent is permitted and earned: protest red (`#B3271E`–`#D8402C`) on buttons, prohibition slashes and occasionally the X itself. Garment neutrals sit muted and utilitarian — khaki and olive (`#A89A72`, `#5F6146`), denim indigo (`#35425C`), heather grey cotton. When color appears photographically, it reads as faded analog snapshot: flash-warmed skin (`#D8A184`), cream-blown highlights, dull venue walls — never saturated, never digital-vivid. Rule of thumb: black-and-white dominates; red is the only loud voice; everything else is cotton-neutral.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how firmly the culture documents them)

1. **The X.** The primary identifier. Worn as black marker on the backs of both hands, as tattoo, and printed on clothing, patches and pins; the triple-X (`XXX`) variant appears on buttons and prints. Any faithful image should carry at least one legible X.
2. **Clean-cut athletic uniform.** Youth Crew era dress: varsity jackets, hooded sweatshirts bearing band logos, athletic sneakers (Vans above all), plain jeans or khakis; military-style crew cuts or shaved heads. Leather, studs and mohawks are explicitly abandoned.
3. **Band logos and merch print.** Hardcore band names and "STRAIGHT EDGE HARDCORE"-style lockups on tees and hoodies, in condensed caps or hand-drawn logo lettering.
4. **Slogan ephemera.** Pins, patches and flyers carrying the creed: "DRUG FREE", "USE YOUR HEAD", "IT'S OK NOT TO DRINK", prohibition circle-slash over cigarettes and bottles, skull-and-marked-hands emblems in flat black-on-white.
5. **The hardcore show.** Small club or basement stage, shouted vocalist leaning into or over the crowd, guitar and bass behind, amp stacks, packed underage audience in caps and hoodies.
6. **Unity gestures.** Crossed arms presenting both marked hands, raised back-of-hand to camera, circles of stacked fists, crew grouped shoulder-to-shoulder — the body language of collective commitment.
7. **Tattooed commitment.** X tattoos and script ink on otherwise athletic, groomed bodies; sleeves are acceptable on modern carriers, but the haircut and the message stay clean.

### Materials and textures

Cotton jersey and fleece (tees, hoodies), wool-and-snap varsity cloth, canvas sneakers, canvas work pants and denim; paper stock of the scene: xerox flyers with tonal collapse and halftone dots, cut-and-paste collage edges, typewriter and ransom-note type, screenprinted patches, metal pin-back buttons; skin textures of sweat, flash glare and marker ink; film grain and motion blur as the default surface of truth.

### Subjects and composition

Subjects range from performers mid-shout to audience members, promo portraits, groups of fists, hands close-up, tattoo detail, and still lifes of buttons and patches. Composition tends documentary and direct: crowd-level or slightly low angles, tilted frames, subject squared to camera with eye contact or a pointing finger; frontal promo portraits on plain or institutional backdrops with arms crossed; flat lays and grids for ephemera; flyer layouts as binary black-white collages. Intimacy over spectacle — the camera is inside the scene, not above it.

### Lighting

Three legitimate regimes: (a) on-camera flash in a dark venue — hot highlights on skin and white tee, rapid falloff to black; (b) flat, even light for portraits and ephemera, studio seamless or fluorescent hallway; (c) xerox binary contrast for print matter, where midtones simply vanish. Daylight appears only as overcast, unglamorous snapshot light (parking lots, backyards, corridors).

## Mood & Values

Earnest, militant, sober. The style projects self-control as strength and clarity as defiance: pointed fingers, direct stares, stated slogans, no irony and no intoxication. Beneath the confrontation is brotherhood — stacked fists, crew portraits, unity lyrics made visible. The emotional register is pride without excess: a "drug free" skull is still a skull, but it wears its message like a badge of honor.

## Era & Origins

Washington D.C., 1980–81: the Teen Idles' hands marked by San Francisco bouncers supply the X; Minor Threat's "Straight Edge" supplies the name. The mid-to-late-1980s Youth Crew wave (Youth of Today, Gorilla Biscuits) hardens the fashion into its athletic uniform and adds a heavier, metal-tinted sound. The culture persists in later decades — modern tattooed carriers and contemporary snapshots keep the grammar alive — but the visual signature remains analog: film grain, flash, photocopy. (The written record is rich on fashion and iconography but thin on photographic technique; the lighting and grain rules above are distilled from the scene's own documentation practices, consistent with its hardcore origins.)

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Place at least one legible X: marker on hands, tattoo, print, patch or pin.
- Keep grooming clean-cut: crew cut, shaved head, or short tidy hair; athletic or plain-bodied, never glam.
- Dress Youth Crew: band hoodie or varsity jacket or plain tee, jeans/khakis, Vans-type sneakers.
- Set the scene in sXe space: club/basement show, hallway, parking lot, backyard, or a print-matter flat lay.
- Light like a document: flash snapshot, flat promo light, or xerox binary — never glamorous rim light.
- Let type speak: condensed caps slogans, cut-and-paste flyer text, prohibition symbols.
- Favor unity: crossed arms, raised marked hand, stacked fists, crew framing.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be any of: performer, audience, promo portrait, tattoo or hand close-up, group unity shot, ephemera still life, flyer/graphic design, or an everyday sober-life moment (street, corridor, gym-adjacent casual). Composition may run from tilted crowd-level snapshot to dead-center frontal portrait to orthographic flat lay to collage flyer. Lighting may be night flash, flat studio, overcast daylight, or binary print. Color handling may be full monochrome, faded warm analog, or black/white/red graphic. Mood may slide from militant defiance to warm fellowship to plain earnestness. Era surface may be 1980s grain or a modern document of the ongoing scene. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Alcohol, cigarettes, smoke, drug paraphernalia, bars or party hedonism as positive content.
- Mohawks, liberty spikes, studs, spikes, safety pins, leather biker gear — 1970s punk excess.
- Neon, psychedelic or candy palettes; glossy luxury fashion; designer monograms.
- Skateboards, decks, grip tape, skate parks, fisheye sun-bleach as the picture's center.
- School-letter letterman jackets, team uniforms, stadium or field jock settings.
- Airbrushed digital polish, creamy bokeh, pastel softness, beauty lighting.
- Anarchy signs or nihilist chaos imagery standing in for the X.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Overlap | The sXe tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Punk** | Hardcore tempo, DIY flyers, club shows, tattoos | X instead of anarchy sign; crew cut/shaved head instead of mohawk; plain tee and hoodie instead of studs and leather; sobriety slogans instead of vice and chaos imagery; earnestness instead of nihilist irony |
| **Jock** | Varsity jackets, hoodies, sneakers, athletic bodies | Band logos instead of school letters; marker X on hands; club shows, hallways and photocopy print instead of fields, stadiums and team pride; monochrome-plus-red instead of saturated school colors; the athletic wear is subverted, not earned |
| **Skater** | Vans sneakers, casual pants, youth snapshot culture | No deck, grip tape or park in frame; flash-lit indoor night instead of sun-bleached fisheye daylight; band merch and marked-hand gestures instead of skate-brand graphics and trick action |

Fast test: a black X on the back of a hand, raised at a hardcore show, on a clean-cut kid in a band hoodie — with not a bottle, cigarette or stud in sight — is straight edge and nothing else.
