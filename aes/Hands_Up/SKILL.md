# Hands Up

Hands Up is both a 140–150 BPM eurodance genre (Commercial Trance, Dancecore) and the composite graphic style that grew around it on YouTube, Facebook and TechnoBase.fm between roughly 2005 and 2013. Born in Germany from Eurodance and Hard Trance pushed toward the commercial dancefloor, its visuals were assembled by fans who needed artwork for tracks with no music video: a stock photo of a dancer or DJ, run through Photoshop light-trail brushes, particle clouds and pre-made "dance wallpaper" templates. That production method is the style's DNA — it looks like a layered digital kitbash of glow effects, chrome type and one borrowed human figure, and it carries the values of the scene: energy, intensity, motion, digital euphoria, and the communal slogan "We Are One."

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
The canvas is almost always a deep black or dark gray void (#000000–#1A1A1A), sometimes vignetted, existing solely to maximize artificial light. Onto it go one to three maximum-saturation neons plus chrome:

| Role | Hex range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Backdrop | #000000–#1A1A1A | void, vignette, negative space |
| Electric blue | #0044FF–#00BFFF | trails, auras, lightning arcs |
| Neon pink / magenta | #FF00CC–#FF2D95 | trails, smoke, club-wear accents |
| Vibrant green | #39FF14–#00FF7F | silhouettes' backlight, fractal flares |
| Electric orange | #FF6A00–#FFA500 | bursts, swirl cores |
| Chrome / silver | #FFFFFF–#6E6E6E gradient | beveled type, spheres, rims, specular hits |
| Rainbow spectrum | full hue wheel | reserved for equalizer bars and type fills |

Accepted deviations: pastel fractal-smoke fields (pink #F5D7E8 into cyan #BFE9F2) on mix covers, and bright saturated compilation gloss (sky blue with white lightning). In every case saturation stays pinned high and light sources stay artificial.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the culture documents them
1. **Neon light trails and swirls** — the essential element: curved glowing lines, luminous rings and wispy streaks that trace movement and wrap around the central figure to suggest speed and flow.
2. **Figures in motion** — dancers or DJs caught mid-action (breaking freezes, hip-hop poses, arched backs, hair whips), rendered as pure silhouettes or as heavily desaturated, near-monochrome photographs; styled in streetwear (hoodies, trainers, baggy jeans) or, on commercial releases, minimal club-wear, swimwear or lingerie-like attire.
3. **Glowing particles, energy bursts and light flares** — particle clouds, fractal smoke, star flares; plus the dramatic **shattering-glass fragments** radiating from the figure as a stand-in for explosive impact.
4. **High-gloss 3D typography** — blocky futuristic letterforms with chrome, metallic or glossy plastic finish, heavy drop shadows or neon outlines; artist-name conventions bleed into the type: "S" swapped for "Z" (Topmodelz, Partystylerz), "Bass" monikers, provocative names, the "We Are One" slogan.
5. **Digital music iconography** — colorful equalizer bars (often rainbow, often pixel-dripping), turntables and CDJ decks, glowing speakers, waveforms, and oversized headphones worn by nearly every human subject.
6. **Import tuner cars** — a documented secondary motif: JDM sports cars (Nissan Skyline, Subaru Impreza, Acura Integra type) with exaggerated body kits, large spoilers, chrome rims and high-gloss paint, neon-lit on black.
7. **Paratext and flourishes** — tracklist or artist-list strips along the bottom edge, mix numbers and year stamps, station or Facebook URL watermarks, and thin vector filigree / floral swirls interwoven with the light trails.

### Materials and textures
Everything reads as digital composite: polished chrome bevels and disco-ball facets, smooth vector gradients, soft fractal smoke, lens flares, glass shards, pixel-stepped bar graphs. Surfaces are glossy, self-illuminated or metallic. There is no film grain, no paper, no fabric close-up texture, no organic matter — even skin is desaturated toward plastic.

### Recurring subjects
A dancing figure (silhouette or desaturated stock photo); a DJ or model posing with oversized headphones; a hoodied streetwear teen; a glamour model for compilation covers; a modified tuner car; or no figure at all — pure equalizer bars, treble clefs and swirl fields carrying the image alone.

### Composition and lighting
Composition matches high tempo: a central or offset figure orbited by wrapping trails, with logotype as a hero block (center or upper third) and music iconography layered as background furniture. Formats are the era's containers — widescreen 16:9 visualizer/wallpaper, square compilation cover, flyer with text strips. Lighting is exclusively artificial: rim glow, backlight silhouetting, self-luminous graphics, flares and vignettes. Contrast is extreme; the black does as much work as the neon.

## Mood & Values

Euphoria as engineering. The style promises collective release — hands in the air, "We Are One" — and celebrates motion, speed and bass pressure (the culture's car-audio obsession is literalized in tuner cars and "Bass" names). It is unapologetically commercial and hedonistic: the music of rural German Großraumdiscos, Poland's Wixa party culture around the Ekwador club, Austrian Krocha and Swedish Partille-Johnny youth scenes. Its maximalism is sincere, not ironic: more glow, more chrome, more saturation equals more joy. Viewed today it also carries a nostalgic, early-internet communal warmth — fan-made, template-shared, border-crossing.

## Era & Origins

Emerged mid-2000s in Germany and on the internet (YouTube's 2005 launch is the enabling event), peaking 2005–2013 and spreading through Poland and Central Europe. It reacts to nothing so much as it commercializes: Eurodance and Hard Trance simplified into verse-chorus pop structures, pitched-up "chipmunk" vocals, bootleg remix culture. Visually it sits in the same glossy Y2K cluster as Frutiger Aero, Vectordelia and Y2K Futurism, but relocates that gloss from daylight software interfaces to the nightclub void. Distribution channels — YouTube visualizer channels, TechnoBase.fm wallpaper contests, Clubland-style compilation covers — dictated its reusable, brush-based construction.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Start from a black void; light is the subject, not illumination of a scene.
2. Let light draw motion: trails, rings and swirls must orbit or wrap whatever figure exists.
3. Treat the human as a symbol of energy: silhouette or desaturated photo, mid-action, headphones or streetwear/club-wear — never a psychologically nuanced portrait.
4. Make typography an object: blocky futuristic letters, chrome or glossy bevel, drop shadow or neon outline; optional era-correct spellings ("Z" for "S", "Bass", "We Are One").
5. Furnish the background with music iconography: equalizer bars, decks, speakers, waveforms.
6. Embrace composite logic: layered stock-photo-plus-brush effects, slight kitbash seams are period-correct, not errors.
7. Max saturation, max contrast, gloss everywhere; matte is forbidden.

**Variation axes.** Subject: dancer, DJ, hoodied teen, glamour model, tuner car, or pure abstract graphics. Composition: centered figure with orbiting trails; figure pushed to one side against a logotype; type-only hero; full-bleed abstract; widescreen, square, or flyer with bottom text strips. Lighting: pure black void, gray vignette, colored fractal-smoke field, or (rarely, compilation-gloss) bright saturated backdrop with lightning arcs. Color: single-neon-on-black monochrome accent, two-neon duotone, or full rainbow via equalizer elements; chrome may carry the image almost alone. Mood: euphoric crowd-energy, lone moody raver, aggressive burst impact, or pastel dreamy mix-cover calm. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Natural daylight, windows, outdoor landscapes, weather, organic materials (wood, paper, plants, film grain).
- Muted, earthy or matte palettes; beige, brown, olive dominance; quiet negative space.
- Minimalist flat design, thin geometric sans-serifs, hand-lettering, serifs, understated branding.
- Candid flash-photography party snapshots with no composite light graphics.
- Grime, rust, splatter, cracked concrete, monstrous or mechanical menace.
- Bubbles, water droplets, grass, skies, daylight glass gloss.
- Anime bishoujo centerpiece with pastel soft-focus romance.
- Vaporwave grids, glitch/databend corruption, CRT scanline irony.
- Analog band instrumentation (guitars, drum kits, vinyl-crated bohemianism).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Brostep** shares the dark canvas and neon but swaps gloss for grime: rusted metal, splatter, fractured machinery, distorted grunge lettering, sickly acid-green on brown murk, menacing mood. Hands Up keeps chrome polished, surfaces clean, green candy-bright, mood euphoric.
- **Frutiger Aero** shares gloss and saturation but lives in daylight: sky blue, white, grass green, bubbles, droplets, leafy nature-tech harmony, clean humanist type. Hands Up is nocturnal — black void, club figures, beveled chrome type with drop shadows; no water, foliage or sky.
- **Krocha** is the Austrian subculture that soundtracks itself with Hands Up but photographs itself: flash-lit snapshots of neon fashion, artificial tans, mullets, hardstyle grit. Outfit photo without light-trail compositing or chrome logotype means Krocha, not Hands Up.
- **Nightcore (aesthetic)** is an editing practice whose cover art centers anime illustration with pastel glow, soft bokeh and dreamy romance. Hands Up centers photographic or silhouetted humans and chrome type; its pastels appear only as fractal smoke fields, never as anime skin tones.
- **Partille-Johnny** is Swedish snapshot culture: waxed hair, rubber bands, duck-face poses, compact-camera flash in bedrooms. Photography-first, design-second; Hands Up is design-first, the human merely one stock layer inside a system of light.

One-glance check: black void + neon trails wrapping a silhouetted or desaturated figure + chrome-beveled logotype + equalizer bars or oversized headphones = Hands Up.
