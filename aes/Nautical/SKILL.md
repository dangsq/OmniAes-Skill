# Nautical

Also called Sailorcore, Navy or Marine, this style translates working sea culture — the sailor's uniform, the ship's fittings, the fishing port — into leisure, domestic and fashion elegance. It mimics the timeless, crisp atmosphere of coastal life and sea-faring ships: a white and navy palette, weathered woods, crisp stripes and authentic maritime artifacts. Where broader coastal styles settle for generic beach relaxation, Nautical commits to one of two postures: yacht-club polish or rugged sailor charm. Its values are serenity, simplicity, environmentalism and an appreciation of nature; its lifestyle is calming — boating, fishing, surfing, reading by the beach.

## Visual Identity

### Palette
The rule is a structural pair plus regulated accents: crisp white and deep navy carry the image; signal red appears only in small loud doses (a stripe, a life ring, a trim); brown weathered wood or silvered driftwood grounds it; water contributes a secondary family from turquoise to slate grey-blue. Cream, light pink and black enter through fashion; brass is the only metal.

- Navy `#0A1F3D–#1F3A6E`; cobalt/royal blue `#0047AB–#2E5EB8`
- Crisp white `#FFFFFF–#F6F4EE`; cream/ivory `#F2E9D8`
- Signal red `#B22234–#D22630` (accent only)
- Wood and driftwood `#6B4F35–#9C8B72`, silvered grey `#A8A297`
- Brass `#C9A227`
- Sea: turquoise `#177E89–#7FD1D6`; overcast sea `#5C6B7A–#8FA3AD`

Proportion rule: white ≥ navy > wood/teal > red. If red becomes a field color, or warm sunset oranges replace the cool blues, the image has left the style.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)
1. **The sea and coast** — open water, breaking surf, rocky coastline; the style favors rocky coasts and lighthouse shores over warm sandy beaches.
2. **Lighthouses** — the emblem of shore life, on grassy headlands or storm-worn rocks.
3. **Boats and ships** — sailboats, yachts, hulls tied to docks, piers and boardwalks; wrecked hulls appear in folk-painted narratives.
4. **Sea life** — seagulls first, then turtles, dolphins, schooling fish; starfish and shells in décor still lifes.
5. **Beach houses** — shingled gables, fish-scale shingle siding, shiplap walls, white porches.
6. **Crisp stripes** — Breton horizontals, awning stripes, striped rugs and upholstery; scale/shingle patterns act as the stripe's cousin.
7. **Authentic maritime artifacts** — ship's wheel, anchor, life ring, coiled rope, fishing nets, knots, brass fittings, displayed as décor with respect, not as party props.
8. **Sailor dress codes** — navy jacket with brass buttons, crisp white sailor collar with square back flap, sailor hat, sou'wester, yellow slicker, cable-knit fisherman's sweater; sailor-collared dresses running from Victorian seaside walking suits to mid-century resort wear to sailor Lolita.

### Materials and textures
Weathered and painted wood (deck planks, shiplap, driftwood), rope and canvas, cable-knit wool, polished brass and bronze, wicker and rattan, striped cotton canvas. Fashion additionally admits transparent, translucent and iridescent fabrics and scale patterns — text-documented but lightly evidenced visually, so treat them as an optional shimmer layer, not core. Surfaces are scrubbed, sun-bleached and clean; weathering reads as salt-silvered, never rotting.

### Recurring subjects
White-walled interiors with navy cushions and a ship's wheel over the mantel; fashion editorials and vintage fashion plates; seascapes from shore, sea or air; lighthouse headlands; underwater views; décor still lifes of nets, starfish and shells; scrapbook collages and mood boards; wave-swoosh corporate graphics; painted game vignettes and folk-naive marine paintings. Human types: sailors, captains, fishermen, resort-goers, surfers, children in sailor suits.

### Composition and lighting
Composition tends toward order: symmetrical interiors, strong horizontal horizons, clean color-blocked diagonals in editorials, tidy flat-lay collages, centered emblem-like artifacts. Light is predominantly bright and crisp — midday sun, small hard shadows on white walls, window-flooded airy rooms. A second legitimate mode is soft overcast silver light over grey-green surf for the rugged fisherman register; underwater scenes use diffuse turquoise light. Shadows stay cool blue-grey, never warm brown; high-key whites dominate.

### Medium
The style survives translation across media because its code is color plus motif: photography (interior, editorial, seascape), hand-colored Victorian fashion plates and woodcut engravings, mid-century fashion illustration, folk-naive painting, digital painterly game art, collage, and flat logo graphics all read as Nautical when the palette and motifs hold.

## Mood & Values
Serenity, simplicity, environmentalism, appreciation of nature. A calming lifestyle of local shopping, reading by the beach, fishing and boating. Two registers of prestige coexist: yacht-club elegance (crisp linen, polished brass, white paint) and rugged sailor charm (salt-worn wood, cable knit, overcast sea). Both are wholesome and orderly; anxiety, decay and excess are foreign to the style.

## Era & Origins
Created by sailors and navies: functional workwear and shipboard fitting come first. Nineteenth-century seaside resort culture translated naval dress into fashion — anchor-trimmed walking dresses, parasols, straw hats — and the early-to-mid twentieth century fixed the sailor suit, Breton stripe and resort wear; mid-century fashion illustration and later interior-design codification and maritime corporate branding kept the code alive. Today it persists in resort collections and a sailor-based Lolita subgenre. The style is therefore a leisure-and-domestic adoption of working maritime culture: it borrows the navy's authority and the fishing fleet's honesty.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Build on the white/navy structural pair; add red only as accent; ground with wood brown or sea teal.
2. Include at least one explicit maritime code: stripe, anchor, ship's wheel, life ring, rope or net, sailor collar, lighthouse, hull, fish-scale shingle.
3. Keep surfaces scrubbed and clean; weathering is salt-silvered, never rot or rust streaks.
4. Light crisp daylight or soft overcast; cool shadows; high-key whites welcome.
5. Order over clutter: symmetrical, horizon-anchored, or color-blocked compositions.
6. Mood is serene leisure or dignified labor — never menace, murk or mockery.
7. Medium is free: photo, plate, folk painting, collage and logo graphics all qualify.

Variation axes:
- **Subject**: interiors, fashion for any age or gender, seascapes, lighthouses, sea life, décor still lifes, branding graphics, illustrated narratives, shipwrecks in folk idiom, aerial surf scenes, underwater views.
- **Composition**: wide interior, editorial diagonal color-block, horizon seascape, top-down aerial, flat collage, centered emblem.
- **Lighting**: high-key sun, diffuse overcast, underwater caustics, soft window light.
- **Color handling**: strict navy/white; teal-forward sea; cream-and-red vintage plate; monochrome blue graphic.
- **Mood**: yacht-club polish ↔ fisherman ruggedness ↔ quiet nature reverence.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette proportion, maritime codes, crisp order — not any reference image.

Avoid:
- Tropical warmth: palm fronds, hibiscus, sunset orange-pink skies, warm sand dominance.
- Grunge and pollution: trash, plastic murk, rust, rotting wood, desaturated muddy green (Ocean Grunge).
- Gothic storm terror: blackened seas, ominous fog, drowned dread (Dark Nautical).
- Pirate codes: skulls, cutlasses, tattered leather, treasure maps, galleon broadsides.
- Schoolroom framing: classrooms, school badges, pleated-skirt uniform context (Nanchatte Seifuku) — the sailor collar must read maritime or resort, never scholastic.
- Seapunk neon: vaporwave glitch, chrome, neon cyan-magenta.
- Boho drift: terracotta, macramé excess, rattan clutter without a navy-and-white spine.
- Red as a field color; warm brown chiaroscuro; matte earth palettes.

## Discrimination Cues
- **Coastal Style**: broader beach-relaxation neutrals — sand, seagrass, driftwood beige, airy white without commitment. Nautical insists on the navy spine, red accent, crisp stripe and ship artifacts, plus a yacht-club or sailor posture. If the palette could belong to any beach house, it is Coastal.
- **Dark Nautical**: same motifs, inverted values — blackened palette, storm dread, drowned gothic. Nautical proper stays bright or silver-overcast and wholesome.
- **Nanchatte Seifuku**: the sailor collar in school-uniform grammar (classrooms, badges, pleated skirts, indoor light). Nautical places the collar among anchors, stripes, brass, rope, sea or resort settings.
- **Ocean Grunge**: polluted, murky, angsty; decay and waste as subject. Nautical weathering is clean salt-silver, water stays legible blue or grey-green, mood calm.
- **Pirate**: rebellious disorder — leather, skulls, loot, ragged edges. Nautical is institutional order: navy uniform, polished brass, painted wood, the lawful sea.
- **Tropical**: warm sandy beaches and lush plants; Nautical's own texts separate itself by rocky coasts, lighthouses and a cool palette.

Evidence note: iridescence, translucency and scale patterns are text-listed fashion traits with only light visual support (fish-scale shingles, faint resort-wear sheen); the media list (films, games, albums) confirms the painted-vignette and illustrated modes but adds few visual rules beyond subject matter.
