# Spainwave

Spainwave is a primarily visual subgenre of Vaporwave that coalesced around 2020 on the subreddit r/spainwave. Its premise is simple and specific: take the editing grammar of Vaporwave and Synthwave — pastel and neon color grading, VHS distortion, glitch artifacts, surreal collage, retro-futuristic grids — and point it at Spain instead of at 1980s–90s American and Japanese consumer culture. The source pool is the whole span of Spanish life: Golden Age painting and literature, regional vernacular architecture, roadside icons, department-store catalogs, resort advertisements, television idents, press-conference footage, pop singers, and prime ministers. The result is a nostalgic, ironic, surreal portrait of Spain made by people who know exactly what each borrowed image means at home.

The style is visual-first; its musical wing (slowed, reverb-drowned samples of Spanish pop, TV themes and political speeches, under names like "Rajoywave") is a later and less established development. Treat Spainwave above all as a collage and color-grading practice applied to Spanish archival and vernacular imagery.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Two registers share one hue family — magenta, violet, turquoise, sunset orange — and a given image may sit anywhere between them:

| Register | Core hues | Approximate hex range |
|---|---|---|
| Neon night (Synthwave inheritance) | fuchsia/magenta grids, indigo-violet skies, electric turquoise, chrome silver type | `#FF00FF`–`#E0218A`, `#1B1035`–`#6A0DAD`, `#00E5D0`–`#4DD0E1`, `#C0C0D0` |
| Pastel day (Vaporwave inheritance) | faded pink skies, lavender haze, mint-turquoise water and pool tile, cream paper | `#F6C9D8`, `#CDB4F6`, `#7FE3D4`, `#F5F0E6` |
| Shared accent | sunset orange-yellow for suns, globes, headline type | `#FF9E00`–`#FFD23F` |

The rule: magenta or pink dominates the grade, violet-indigo carries shadows and skies, turquoise/cyan appears as water, pool surfaces, signage or grid lines, and warm orange-yellow punctuates as sun, globe or display type. Saturation is free to swing from candy-neon to washed-out VHS fade; the hue family is not free to swing — green-and-brown naturalism or blue-and-white corporate coolness reads as outside the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the tradition documents them

1. **Spanish Golden Age and classical lineage** (the style's declared key motif): bronze and marble statues cut out and glowing — the Giraldillo of Seville, busts of Cervantes in ruff collars — Don Quixote and windmiles restaged as collages or as pixel-art video-game battles, old-master portraits invaded by digital artifacts.
2. **Regional landmarks and vernacular architecture**: hórreo granaries on stilts, lighthouses, stone bridges over turquoise rivers, the pink geometric stairways of La Muralla Roja, roadside silhouettes against sunset skies, and city names (Sevilla, Logroño) set as centered titles over local views. Regional identity — Galicia, Asturias, Andalusia, the Rioja — is a value, not a backdrop.
3. **Nostalgic Spanish consumer and television culture**: scanned department-store toy catalogs, surreal resort advertisements of the Marina d'Or type, SEAT car ads, CRT television sets glowing with Spanish idents, Telefónica/InfoVía-era computing, and 8-bit hardware relabeled with Spanish bureaucracy (tax-software NES cartridges, pixel-font program titles).
4. **Political and public-life imagery**: broadcast stills of politicians, press-conference frames with flags and lower-third banners, statesmen collaged into VR headsets or chrome "HACKERMAN"-style type. Politics enters as deadpan social commentary, not propaganda.
5. **2000s pop culture**: reality-show singers and pop portraits framed in floral collage and window chrome.
6. **Surrealist lineage**: Dalí-esque figures and impossible juxtapositions, dream logic applied to mundane objects (a yield sign monumentalized over a pastel cityscape).
7. **Retro-digital garnish inherited from Vaporwave**: Windows 9x / classic Mac windows, color-palette swatch strips, pixel fonts, chrome italic display type, perspective neon grids, scanline bands, and occasional katakana side-text as a borrowed accent — garnish only, never the leading language.

### Materials and textures

Mediation is mandatory: the image must look copied, transmitted, or scanned. Recurring textures are VHS scanlines and tracking noise, horizontal glitch displacement bands, chromatic-aberration fringing on cutout edges, 8-/16-bit pixelation for sprites and game UI, CRT phosphor glow and curved-glass television photographs, paper scan texture with catalog or deckled edges, chrome-gradient metallic lettering, neon tube glow on grid lines, and collage cutouts keyed with a soft outer glow or thin white outline. Flat, even, advertisement lighting survives under the grade, so glossy print color and digital decay often coexist in one frame.

### Recurring subjects

Statues and busts; writers and painters of the national canon; politicians and news presenters; pop singers; vernacular buildings (granaries, lighthouses, bridges, postmodern housing blocks); resort pools and tourist utopias; household cars; catalogs, cartridges, consoles and television sets; signage and bureaucratic forms; sunsets over silhouetted land.

### Composition and lighting

Composition follows poster and collage logic rather than photographic naturalism: a centered cutout icon floating above a receding perspective grid; a silhouette held against a full-bleed gradient dusk; a centered, letter-spaced place-name over a landscape postcard; dense collages stacked with OS windows, swatches and floral borders; catalog-page grids with pixel-type headers; game-screenshot frames with health bars and menus; busts planted on checkerboard or grid floors. Horizons sit low so grids and skies dominate. Lighting is always mediated: backlit sunset silhouettes, neon emission at night, flat studio ad light, hazy pastel daylight, or — for the melancholic end — stormy grey-teal overcast. Whichever lighting is chosen, it passes through a grade, a glow or a decay layer.

## Mood & Values

Nostalgia, irony, national and regional identity, social commentary, surrealism. Spainwave feels like remembering Spain through a broken tuner: affectionate, slightly absurd, faintly melancholic. Its core gesture is the blend of the mundane and the fantastic — a tax program as a holy relic on a cartridge, a press conference staged as cosmic event, a granary drifting in pastel fog. The irony is warm, never contemptuous; the national identity is playful and self-knowing, never chauvinist. Dreamlike stillness and deadpan humor coexist: everything is at once monumental and ridiculous.

## Era & Origins

Emerged in the early 2020s on Reddit (r/spainwave, ~40,000 members), as a deliberate localization of Vaporwave beyond its American and Japanese reference points. It reacts against classic Vaporwave's US/Japan consumer source pool by insisting that Spanish history — Golden Age to present day — is equally valid dream material. Technique-wise it inherits Vaporwave's glitches, pastel grading and surreal collage plus Synthwave's neon lights and retro-futuristic grids. The musical offshoot ("Rajoywave") came later and remains secondary.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Spanish source first.** Every image must read as a Spanish or Iberian cultural artifact — statue, landmark, ad, catalog, broadcast frame, pop portrait — processed through retro-digital filters. At least one unmistakable Spanish signifier (Spanish-language text, a known landmark type, a regional object) anchors the frame.
2. **Pick a register, then grade.** Neon-night (magenta grid, indigo sky, chrome type) or pastel-day (pink haze, mint water, cream paper), or a blend; keep the magenta–violet–turquoise–orange hue family.
3. **Degrade the signal.** Apply at least one mediation layer: scanlines, glitch bands, chromatic aberration, pixelation, CRT glow, paper-scan edges.
4. **Typography is a motif.** Spanish headlines in pixel fonts, chrome italic display type, or letter-spaced sans-serif place-names; bureaucratic labels and catalog headers count as content.
5. **Collage logic.** Cut out figures and statues from their original context; float them over incompatible skies, grids or checkerboards; stack OS windows, swatches and borders without apologizing.
6. **Mundane-fantastic blend.** Give banal objects (forms, signs, cartridges, resort brochures) dreamlike or monumental staging; give monumental objects (statues, masters) banal digital company.
7. **Irony with affection.** Humor deadpan and warm; melancholy permitted and encouraged at the pastel end.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any Spanish/Iberian icon from the Golden Age to the 2000s, from any region — canon art, folk architecture, consumer ephemera, politics, pop, sport, food, signage. Composition: poster, collage, catalog scan, game screenshot, landscape postcard, CRT photograph, portrait bust study. Lighting: neon night, pastel dusk, flat ad light, hazy daylight, stormy overcast. Color handling: saturated neon through washed VHS fade within the core hue family. Mood: celebratory kitsch through quiet melancholy. Medium: filtered photograph, digital collage, pixel art, illustrated poster. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a Spainwave frame of a subject no reference ever showed is still Spainwave if the source logic, palette family, mediation and Spanish anchor hold.

**Avoid**

- Leading with American or Japanese Vaporwave props: Miami palms, mall interiors, Fuji-style logos, Greek or Roman busts with no Spanish framing, Japanese-led typography (katakana only as minor garnish).
- Cyrillic text, Soviet iconography, cosmonauts, brutalist concrete nostalgia (that is Sovietwave).
- Clean, untreated contemporary photography; modern flat UI, smartphones, social-media frames.
- Polished hyperreal 3D rendering with no degradation layer.
- Earnest nationalist pomp or propaganda tone; the style's identity play is ironic and affectionate.
- Generic cyberpunk dystopia, horror, gore, aggression.
- Non-Iberian landmarks standing in for Spanish ones.
- Pure speed-machine Synthwave subject matter (anonymous supercars, highways, sci-fi pilots) with no Spanish anchor; Spanish cars and machines qualify precisely because they are Spanish.
- Textless abstract grid landscapes with no cultural signifier at all — the anchor is what makes it Spainwave.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared surface | What separates Spainwave |
|---|---|---|
| **Vaporwave** | Pastel grading, glitch, collage, classical busts, OS windows | Source pool and language: Spainwave's statues are the Giraldillo and Cervantes, its text Spanish, its landmarks Iberian granaries and resort pools; classic Vaporwave's malls, Greco-Roman busts and English/Japanese consumer text are absent as subjects. Spainwave also runs warmer — magenta-violet with turquoise — and admits political broadcast imagery Vaporwave never touches. |
| **Synthwave** | Neon grids, chrome type, sunset suns, retro-futurism | Synthwave is earnest retro-futurist speed culture — supercars, drivers, sci-fi horizons, clean vector glow. Spainwave borrows the grid and chrome but collages real Spanish archival material over it, adds VHS decay, bureaucratic mundanity and deadpan irony, and rarely depicts motion or machines for their own sake. |
| **Sovietwave** | Post-nostalgic melancholy, faded memory of a recent past | Sovietwave is Cyrillic, brutalist, cosmic-program nostalgia in desaturated film tones with earnest state longing. Spainwave is brighter (magenta/turquoise), Spanish-language, sourced from consumer ads, regional folk life and TV kitsch, and its nostalgia is threaded with irony and social commentary rather than state reverence. |

When in doubt, ask one question: is this image dreaming about Spain, in Spanish, through a broken retro screen? If yes — Spainwave.
