# Theriancore

Theriancore is the visual arm of the modern therian and alterhuman communities: people who identify psychologically or spiritually as non-human animals. Where therianthropy itself is an internal identity (documented online since the early 1990s), Theriancore is specifically the *look* — a DIY art style that simulates a feral, non-human perspective through three registers: the digital "spirit edit" (crude white scribbles over real wildlife photography), the craft movement (handmade animal masks, faux-fur tails, claw jewelry), and "quadrobic" cinematography (shaky, ground-level footage mimicking an animal's point of view). It lives on Tumblr, TikTok, Instagram and Pinterest as profile pictures, moodboards and video performances. It is deliberately anti-cartoonish: realistic photography and naturalistic masks are chosen *against* the furry fandom's anthropomorphic fursuits, and the slightly unpolished handmade quality is a value, not a flaw.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is a two-layer system: a muted, earth-toned photographic base, plus a stark-white ink layer that exists only as line art. Environmental white (snow, fog, overcast sky) may flood a frame, but *stylistic* white belongs to the doodles.

| Layer | Roles | Approximate hex range |
|---|---|---|
| Base earth tones | moss green foliage, charcoal fur and hoodies, rust accents (bandanas, autumn leaf litter), bark brown, cream/bone fur and felt | `#5A6B4A`–`#7E8B6B` moss; `#262622`–`#3B3B36` charcoal; `#8C4A2F`–`#A8603C` rust; `#4A382A`–`#6E563E` brown; `#E4DAC6`–`#F1EADB` cream |
| Overlay ink | spirit-edit scribbles, ΘΔ sigil, silhouette outlines | `#F4F4F4`–`#FFFFFF`, often with a faint glow |
| Grade | desaturated, melancholic autumn cast over everything; winter scenes drift gray-cream but never pastel | — |

These ranges are a distillation of the style's habit, not fixed swatches: any desaturated natural tone that reads as forest, fur, soil or rust qualifies, and the only saturated "pop" permitted is the white ink.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the text documents them)

1. **The spirit edit.** The most prominent motif. High-resolution wildlife or animal photography overlaid with crude, glowing white raster line art: stars, hearts, swirls, exclamation marks, and loose outlines tracing the animal's silhouette. Sampled images extend the vocabulary with paw prints, lightning bolts, feathers, sparkles and tiny handwritten exclamations or emotive scribbled text — tolerated extensions of the same hand-drawn grammar. The contrast between photoreal fur and MS-Paint-grade doodles produces the style's signature scrapbook look.
2. **The Theta-Delta (ΘΔ) symbol.** The subculture's sigil, most characteristically placed directly on the animal's (or mask's) forehead, and repeated smaller among the surrounding scribbles. The single strongest recognition cue.
3. **DIY animal masks.** Paper-mache or felt bases, painted markings, mesh eye coverings, glued faux-fur tufts, added whiskers — visibly hand-made, slightly asymmetrical, "craftcore" rather than costume-shop.
4. **Wearable fur gear and camouflage layering.** Faux-fur tails, paw gloves/mitts, claw-inspired jewelry; wardrobes of hoodies, tank tops, plaid shirts and bandanas in earth tones, layered to mimic natural camouflage. Ordinary clothes, not uniforms.
5. **Quadrobic / feral-POV framing.** Shaky-cam, low-angle, ground-level viewpoints; crouching four-limbed poses; liminal nature settings — foggy woods, empty fields, autumn leaf litter, snow.
6. **Masked group snapshots.** Rows or circles of masked friends in parks, plazas or bedrooms; faces concealed (mesh eyes), preserving digital anonymity.
7. **Moodboard assembly.** Profile-picture crops, collaged edits and scrapbook-like arrangements rather than fine-art composition.

### Materials and textures

Faux-fur pile with visible glue lines; felt and paper-mache ridges under paint; mesh over eye holes; acrylic brush strokes on mask muzzles; real fur, feather and snow detail in the photographic base; pixel-jittered raster doodle lines with uneven pressure and opacity; smartphone-photo grain and slight motion blur. Everything should feel touched by hand and uploaded, not rendered.

### Recurring subjects

Any real, biologically accurate animal — canids (wolves, huskies, dogs), felids (house cats, bobcats, servals, snow leopards), bears, raccoons, ungulates (horses, bulls), birds, and even invertebrates like snails, since a theriotype is personal. Masked humans of any gender and age, solo or in groups, outdoors or in casual indoor rooms. Feral posture and anatomy are essential: animals behave like animals; masked humans crouch, perch and paw at the ground.

### Composition and lighting

Spirit edits favor a single centered animal, portrait or full-body, with doodles orbiting the subject and landing on the forehead. The craft register favors eye-level or low-angle snapshots: crouching quad poses, overhead circles of masked faces looking down into the lens (the animal's-eye view reversed), group rows in front of trees or buildings, close mask portraits. Lighting is natural and unglamorous — overcast daylight, dappled forest shade, flat snow glare, soft indoor window light — graded toward muted autumn. No studio strobes, no golden-hour glamour, no dramatic rim lighting.

## Mood & Values

Visual escapism and nature appreciation filtered through play: joyful, childlike scribbles laid over solemn feral portraits produce a tender, sincere dissonance. DIY crafting is a core virtue — a handmade mask outranks a bought one. Digital anonymity (masked faces, mesh eyes) protects the identity being expressed. Underneath the play runs melancholic liminality: fog, empty fields, autumn decay, the sense of an animal moving through spaces humans have left. The aesthetic is community-guarded; like Traumacore, participation by outsiders is frowned upon, so images carry the intimacy of an insider's journal page rather than a marketing mood.

## Era & Origins

Therianthropy as identity dates to early-1990s internet forums, notably the 1992 Usenet group alt.horror.werewolves. Theriancore as a *visual style* emerged in the 2010s on Tumblr and consolidated in the 2020s on TikTok, Instagram and Pinterest, when a text-based community translated its lore into moodboards, edits and video performance. It reacts against two things: the furry fandom's cartoonish anthropomorphism (rejected in favor of feral realism) and polished internet aesthetics (answered with crude raster doodles and visible glue). Its edit grammar shares lineage with Traumacore's deliberately naive digital scribbling.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build in two layers: a realistic photographic base (animal or masked human) plus a crude white raster doodle layer. In the mask/craft register the doodles may be absent, but the handmade mask and fur gear must then carry the style.
2. Keep the doodle hand dumb and honest: wobbly single-stroke lines, uneven opacity, jittery curves, MS-Paint brush feel. Never vector-smooth, never airbrushed.
3. Place the ΘΔ sigil deliberately — a forehead stamp is the strongest single cue; scatter smaller ones among stars, hearts, swirls and exclamation marks.
4. Mute the base to earth tones with an autumn-melancholy grade; reserve pure white for ink (environmental snow and fog excepted).
5. Show the hand that made it: asymmetrical masks, visible brush strokes and glue seams, fur tufts that sit slightly wrong.
6. Shoot from animal height: ground-level, low-angle, crouching, or a centered wildlife portrait; allow slight shake and snapshot cropping.
7. Dress masked humans in ordinary clothes — hoodies, tank tops, plaid, bandanas, paw gloves — layered like camouflage, never as fantasy costume.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any species (mammal, bird, reptile, invertebrate), solo or group; masked humans solo, in pairs, in crowds; indoor rooms as valid as forests. Composition: centered portrait, full-body environmental, crouching quad pose, overhead circle, group row, extreme mask close-up. Lighting: overcast flat, dappled shade, window light, snow glare, dusk blue. Color handling: autumn rust-brown grade, gray-green winter, moss-dominant summer — all acceptable as long as saturation stays muted and the ink stays white. Mood: playful, tender, melancholic, or feral-snarl intense. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a spirit-edited portrait of a species never seen in the references, or a masked group in an urban plaza with no doodles at all, still reads Theriancore if the rules hold.

**Avoid**

- Cartoonish fursuits, anthropomorphic animal characters in clothing, upright talking-animal poses (furry fandom).
- Clean vector overlays, professional illustration, neon glow effects, pastel kawaii doodle styles.
- Glossy studio lighting, fashion-editorial polish, CGI or rendered animals.
- Techwear straps, buckles, tactical gear, dystopian survival props (Forestpunk territory).
- Skulls, bones, taxidermy, carrion still lifes (Vulture Culture).
- Cluttered trinket hoards, mushroom-and-moss maximalist still lifes (Goblincore).
- Pristine, unedited wholesome nature photography with no edit layer and no craft (Naturecore).
- Saturated neon or candy palettes; horror gore; occult ritual props; face-revealing glamour portraits (anonymity is a value).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | How Theriancore differs |
|---|---|
| **Forestpunk** | Forestpunk is utilitarian techwear-survival fashion in woodland settings: straps, buckles, functional gear, visible human faces, dystopian tone. Theriancore has no gear fetish; it hides faces behind handmade masks and marks photos with white scribbles and ΘΔ. |
| **Goblincore** | Goblincore is muddy maximalist still life — hoarded buttons, shells, bones, mushrooms, snails as treasured objects. Theriancore's snails or birds are *portraits of a self*, centered, often sigil-marked, and paired with feral POV or mask craft, not object hoards. |
| **Naturecore** | Naturecore is clean, wholesome, unedited celebration of flora and fauna in bright natural color. Theriancore always adds a subcultural layer: doodle edits, sigils, masks, anonymity, melancholy grade. |
| **Vulture Culture** | Vulture Culture centers death objects — skulls, bones, taxidermy — with gothic darkness. Theriancore celebrates living feral identity and play; its darkness is autumn melancholy, not mortuary collection. |
| **Furry fandom** (bonus cue) | Furry imagery is anthropomorphic character art; Theriancore insists on feral, biologically accurate animals and naturalistic masks, and its doodles are naive raster scribbles, not polished character illustration. |

Quick recognition test: realistic animal photo + crude white scribbles + forehead ΘΔ = spirit edit; paper-mache mask + mesh eyes + faux-fur tufts + ordinary hoodie + low angle = craft register; either over a muted moss/charcoal/rust base = Theriancore.
