# Starrflesh

Starrflesh is a surreal internet aesthetic built from eerie, heavily edited photographs of bodies — animal and human — made wrong: too many teeth, too many eyes, scales growing out of skin, flesh wrapped in sheer nylon, jaws rendered in translucent wax. It circulates under a cold, bleached palette of white, icy blue, mint and black, with red reserved for mouths, wounds and glowing irises, and an oil-slick iridescence riding on every wet or scaled surface. The mood is a paradox the style insists on: visually calming, even serene, while depicting horror, emptiness and rage. It emerged on Tumblr in the early-to-mid 2010s under the generic labels "icecore" or "coldcore", was revived on TikTok, and was mistakenly named after the username of its main popularizer, @starrflesh — an origin the original creator has publicly distanced themselves from, which fits the style's folk-anonymous, hard-to-archive character (much canonical material is distressing, explicit, and lost to broken links).

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The field is almost always pale; darkness arrives as void, not as shadow-rich chiaroscuro. Treat color as a rule set, not a fixed scheme:

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Bone / milk / pearl white | `#F5F3EF` – `#FBFAF7`, blown to `#FFFFFF` | Dominant field: fur, skin, jelly, overexposed flash highlights |
| Icy blue / glacier | `#8FB4C6` – `#C4DCE6` | Overall grade or overlay; water, ice, tinted shadows |
| Mint & pale green | `#9CCDB8` – `#BFE0D2` | Secondary cool accent: slime, hair, scale sheen, glowing eyes |
| Deep green / black-green | `#1E332A` – `#244034` | Cold darks in backgrounds, foliage-free murk |
| Black void | `#0B0B0D` – `#141416` | Flash-photography backgrounds, specimen framing |
| Blood / eye red | `#7E1220` – `#B31F2E`, eye-glow up to `#C4502E` | Punctum only: mouths, tongues, wounds, irises, fake blood |
| Iridescent sheen | hue-shifting `#BFE6E0` → `#E8C6DE` → `#C9C2E8` | Not a flat pigment: holographic flare on wet, scaled, glassy or glittered surfaces |

Rule of thumb: if the image reads warm anywhere outside a red wound or an amber eye, it is not Starrflesh. Iridescence behaves like light on a soap film or holo glitter flake, never like pastel paint.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how the documentation weights them)

1. **Animals, deformed or enraged** — the style's core subject. White cats and dog/wolf-type animals snarling with mouths fully open, hissing, or mutated: duplicated eyes in mismatched colors (orange, mint, blue), glowing irises, fur matted by flash. Ordinary domestic animals made uncanny is the single most characteristic move.
2. **Teeth, fangs, claws** — mouths as architecture: rows of too-many needle teeth, oversized canines, jaws detached from any believable skull (including translucent 3D-rendered jaw specimens). Hands with long, sharp, pale nails; clawed or webbed paws.
3. **Eyes** — extreme close-ups of eyes with star-shaped or slit-star pupils, bleached lashes, pale gray-blue irises; multiplied eyes on one face; eyes presented in grids or arrays as if catalogued.
4. **Ice, snow, water** — cracked ice and shattered-glass surfaces, pool caustics, submerged objects, wet everything. Water is a lighting condition as much as a subject.
5. **Flesh and body horror** — distorted limbs, soft flesh bulbs and tendrils, veins, corpses at the intense end of the spectrum; flesh wrapped, compressed or sheathed in sheer fabric; skin hosting scales or fin patches that do not belong to it.
6. **Scales, fish, mermaids/sirens** — pearl-iridescent fish bodies, schools of translucent fish invading mundane rooms, scale-textured legs and arms, siren/mermaid figures as the human-facing mythic form.
7. **Glass and translucency** — shattered panes, glassy slime and jelly masses (heart-shaped or noodle-like) suspended in water with holographic flakes, wax-translucent early-3D renders of skulls, serpents and jaws.
8. **Stars and divine beings** — star glyphs and sparkles (holo star glitter, star jewelry, handwritten star-scrawled lettering), star-shaped pupils, and at the mythic end, biblically accurate angels: many-eyed, winged, overwhelming. (Stars and angels are text-documented but appear less often in surviving image archives than animals and eyes; treat them as accent and ceiling, not baseline.)

### Materials and textures

Wet gloss first: slime, jelly, mucus, water film. Pearl and holographic scale sheen on fish and skin. Sheer nylon or stocking stretched over flesh. Shattered glass and cracked ice with radial fracture lines. Matted faux fur and flash-flattened animal coats. Beaded and rhinestone craftwork (pearl-bead face masks, star charms, chains). Grainy, compressed digital photograph surfaces; wax-like subsurface scattering in rendered objects. Everything looks cold to the touch.

### Recurring subjects

White animals (cats, dogs, wolf-types); isolated body parts (eyes, mouths, hands, legs); scale-grafted human skin; siren/mermaid and angel figures; fashion bodies in white layered grunge-fantasy dress — ripped knits and distressed cloth, lace-trimmed robes and cloaks, fishnets, faux fur, chains, spikes and studs, animal-ear headbands, tails, cosplay wings, crystal/rhinestone jewelry bearing stars, eyes, bones or weapons, long sharp nails, traces of fake blood, snow boots or worn sneakers; and mundane domestic or suburban spaces invaded by the uncanny (a kitchen full of floating fish, a yard with a snarling white beast).

### Composition and lighting

- **Snapshot vernacular:** vertical phone-photo crops, awkward centering, direct on-camera flash that blows whites to pure `#FFFFFF` and drops the background to black void; visible grain, compression artifacts, slight chromatic fringing.
- **Specimen framing:** a single object or creature centered on black, lit diffuse and shadowless, like an evidence photo of an impossible thing.
- **Macro intimacy:** extreme close-ups of an eye, a mouth, a hand, a jelly mass — crop until the body part becomes landscape.
- **Grids and collages:** arrays of eye close-ups; surreal insertions composited into ordinary rooms with deliberately visible, dreamlike seams.
- **Cold diffuse light:** overcast, underwater or caustic light patterns for water/jelly/render images; no golden hour, no warm key light, no candlelight.
- Lighting may range from harsh flash to soft shadowless glow, but the color temperature stays cold in every case.

## Mood & Values

Starrflesh holds two temperatures at once: calm and rage. Its stated values — emptiness, coldness, surreality, horror, uncanniness, calmness, rage — appear as a serene, bleached surface under which something snarls. The style values the uncanny over the grotesque-for-its-own-sake: wrongness is most potent when the frame is quiet, pale and still. It expresses an internet-native intimacy with body horror and transformation (siren flesh, extra eyes, grafted scales), a folk-anonymous authorship (misnamed, creator-disowned, archived in broken links and scattered Pinterest boards), and a soundtrack-adjacent sensibility of cold witch-house and dreamy ambient noise (Crystal Castles, Pastel Ghost, Øneheart, femtanyl). Film touchstones signal its range: *The Thing* (1982) for flesh that betrays, *The Thirteenth Year* (1999) for siren scales breaking out of ordinary skin.

## Era & Origins

Early 2010s, Tumblr-first, under the working names "icecore" and "coldcore"; revived and renamed on TikTok in the early 2020s after @starrflesh, the username of its primary popularizer rather than its inventor. The original creator's public distancing from the label is part of the style's lore: Starrflesh belongs to no one, which matches its anonymous, distressed, hard-to-archive image culture. It sits in a family of cold and uncanny internet aesthetics — Icepunk, Mochette, Bastardcore, Fairy Grunge, Dark Fantasy, Winter Aesthetic, Weirdcore, Dreamcore, Meatcore — while keeping its own center of gravity: the mutated animal body photographed cold.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Bleach the field, freeze the grade.** Build the image on white/pearl masses graded with icy blue or mint; keep darks as near-black void or deep cold green.
2. **Make one body wrong.** Exactly one or two uncanny violations carry the image: an extra eye, a star pupil, too many teeth, scales on human skin, a clawed hand, flesh sheathed in nylon. Restraint reads stronger than abundance.
3. **Red is a wound, not a palette.** Blood red and amber-red appear only in mouths, tongues, irises, fake-blood traces — small, sharp, alarming.
4. **Iridescence is light, not paint.** Holographic sheen lives on wet, scaled, glassy or glittered surfaces and shifts hue across them.
5. **Choose a cold medium.** Lo-fi flash photograph with grain and compression, shadowless specimen photo, underwater/caustic light scene, or wax-translucent early-3D render. Never clean vector, never painterly brushwork.
6. **Hold calm and rage together.** Either a serene, empty, almost soothing frame with one wrong detail, or an aggressive snarl frozen in a bleached, quiet palette — both are canonical.
7. **Stars as glyph, not theme.** Small star sparkles, star-shaped pupils, handwritten star-scrawled lettering are welcome accents; keep them sparse.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may span any animal, any body part, water/ice/glass objects, slime or jelly still-lifes, siren or angel figures, fashion/outfit shots, or domestic rooms invaded by fish and water. Composition may span macro close-up, vertical snapshot, centered specimen on black, eye-grid, or collage insertion. Lighting may span harsh direct flash, diffuse shadowless cold light, or underwater caustics. Color handling may lean mint-green, glacier-blue, or near-monochrome white-on-black, with iridescence optional but frequent. Mood/intensity may span quiet uncanny (one star pupil) through body-horror explicit (veins, corpses, blood) — the text documents the whole spectrum even if surviving archives skew toward animals and eyes. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A bowl of jelly, a pair of hands, a suburban yard or a rendered serpent can all be perfect Starrflesh with none of the reference subjects present.

**Avoid**

- Warm light or warm palette: golden hour, sepia, browns, oranges as field color, candlelight.
- Candy pastel or kawaii softness; saturated warm pinks as the dominant field.
- Raw-meat marbling, butcher-block gore, viscera as the main surface (that field belongs to Meatcore).
- Cartoon characters, meme corruption, crude paint-tool absurdity (Bastardcore's register).
- Painterly medievalism: castles, armor, ornate chiaroscuro, epic dragons rendered heroically (Dark Fantasy).
- Survivalist cold: parkas, goggles-as-gear, machines, industrial ice infrastructure, worldbuilding tech (Icepunk).
- Nostalgic liminal rooms, warm haze, comforting emptiness (Dreamcore).
- Clean, grain-free studio product photography; flat vector illustration; polished typography (sparse handwritten scrawl excepted).
- Healthy, unedited animal portraits or conventional pet photography with no uncanny violation.
- Lush greenery, sunny skies, summer warmth.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Icepunk:** both are cold, but Icepunk's cold is societal and technological — survival gear, scavenged machines, a frozen world to endure. Starrflesh's cold is bodily and intimate: fur, teeth, eyes, scales, flash-lit snapshots with no survival narrative. If the image asks "how do people live here?", Icepunk; if it asks "what is growing out of this body?", Starrflesh.
- **Vs Meatcore:** Meatcore floods the frame with warm raw flesh — marbled reds, fat, butcher textures. Starrflesh keeps flesh pale, wet, translucent or sheathed, sets it in white/icy fields, and spends red sparingly as punctum. Warm meat field = Meatcore; bleached flesh with one red mouth = Starrflesh.
- **Vs Bastardcore:** Bastardcore corrupts familiar cartoon and childhood media with crude, jokey, deliberately ugly edits. Starrflesh edits photographs atmospherically — graded, grainy, eerie — and never leans on licensed cartoon icons or comedy. Absurd humor and sprite corruption point away from Starrflesh.
- **Vs Dark Fantasy:** Dark Fantasy is painterly or cinematic, ornate and epic: armor, ruins, dramatic shadow, saturated or sepia darkness. Starrflesh is lo-fi and modern-domestic: phone-flash grain, suburban yards, kitchens, specimen-on-black frames, cold white palette. Even its dragons and serpents arrive as translucent x-ray-like specimens, not heraldic beasts.
- **Vs Dreamcore:** Dreamcore soothes with nostalgic liminal space, soft haze and human absence; its unease is vague melancholy. Starrflesh unsettles with bodily presence and aggression — teeth, claws, multiplied eyes, snarls — inside a cold, flash-hard surface. Familiar emptiness = Dreamcore; wrong flesh = Starrflesh.

**Honest note on evidence:** surviving image archives over-represent snarling white cats and dogs, eye close-ups and jelly/water textures, because the most explicit body-horror and corpse material is frequently deplatformed or lost. The textual record additionally documents veins, corpses and biblically accurate angels as canonical motifs; treat these as legitimate but less-sampled members of the motif family rather than as absences.
