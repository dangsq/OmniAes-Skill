# Larme Kei

Larme Kei (ラルム系) is the name the Western internet gave to the "Girly fashion" (ガーリー系) core of the Japanese magazine *LARME*, founded in 2012 by Haruna Nakagoori as a "sweet and cute girl's fashion picture book" for cultured women aged 18–25. It is a dainty, sophisticated femininity built on deliberate tension: every look balances *kawaii* against *sexy*, girlish against mature, so that neither pole wins. The style lives not only in outfits but in an editing language — cluttered collage spreads, cutout models, transparent bows and pearls, handwritten English quotes — and in a melancholy performance: models rarely smile, holding neutral, sensual, or pouting expressions inside rose gardens, tea parties, cluttered bedrooms and vintage-parlor sets. Though codified by Western Blogspot, Tumblr and Polyvore users in the mid-2010s from the magazine's 2014–2017 issues, its heart is Tokyo boutique culture (Shibuya-109 labels like EATME, Swankiss, Katie the Store, F.i.n.t) and iconic model Risa Nakamura.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette.** The rule is a soft pastel field anchored by a dark or saturated counterweight. Distilled range: baby and shell pinks (#FADADD–#F4A7B9), cream and ivory (#FFF8F0–#FDF5E6), lavender-mauve and dusty rose (#E3D0E0–#C9A9C0), pure white; anchors of ink black (#141414–#2B2B2B) and deep cherry or bordeaux red (#B3202E–#D64550). Pink-plus-black is the most themed combination, with red-white gingham and plaid as the sanctioned patterns. The magazine itself deviates into other pastels (mint, sax blue, butter), so an all-pastel lavender or cream coordinate is still in-range — but a coordinate with *no* dark or red anchor and *no* pastel base drifts out of style.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the text documents them.** (1) Makeup as motif: cry-eye look — heavy pink eyeshadow placed to look tear-swollen, false lashes, circle lenses, shimmer — over a light clean base with experimental blush placement; (2) bangs, blunt or wispy, always covering the forehead, plus intricate braids woven with ribbons and hairclips; (3) ribbons, bows and pearls, both worn and floating as transparent page decorations; (4) chokers, deliberately contrasting the top (pink heart-charm choker over a sexy black top; black collar-style choker over a pink outfit); (5) pencil and form-fitting skirts, including the viral garter-peplum skirt with dangling garters, and the mini-skirt-under-sheer-maxi construction; (6) sheer layering — lace cut-outs, sheer blouses over tanks, strap and slip dresses in satin or pleats worn over tops; (7) fur and feathers — marabou-trimmed shoes and mules, leopard-print fur coats in the sexier "pheromone fetish" register; (8) gingham and plaid; (9) hearts as charms, props and graphic stamps; (10) persona details: Peter Pan and sailor collars, maid-style white collar on black blouse, schoolgirl and doll cues, suspenders with shoulder ruffles, corset-look high waistbands; (11) cherries and gingham red from the "American/French Lolita" (nymphet-film) spreads; (12) grunge borrowings — bomber jackets, platform boots, metal hardware — always re-feminized.

**Materials and textures.** Texture mixing is the structural law: lace, leather, sheer tulle and organza, satin, feathers, fur, pleats, appearing at least two-to-three per coordinate. A fluffy tulle skirt answers a sleek cold-shoulder top; a leather pencil skirt answers a frilled blouse.

**Recurring subjects.** Young women in full-body coordinates; beauty and makeup close-ups and tutorials; outfit flatlays and shoe walls (Mary Janes, pumps, T-straps arranged in grids); magazine spreads and lookbook pages; shop-girl snaps against boutique or street backgrounds; illustrated renderings of the same coordinate logic. Settings carry girly connotation: cluttered bedrooms, rose gardens, tea parties, vintage parlors with gilded frames, wainscoting and checkered floors, or seamless pastel studio cycloramas.

**Composition and layout.** Two families. Photographic: centered full-body or three-quarter figure on a plain pastel ground or inside a dressed vintage interior, model facing camera with a blank, pouty or sensual gaze, limbs kept dainty and closed. Graphic: collage magazine pages — multiple cutout figures scattered asymmetrically, props and product shots pinned around them, lace-trimmed or scalloped frames, transparent bow/pearl/heart overlays, handwritten or vintage serif English text with dreamy quotes, hearts and stars stamped in corners. Flatlays follow the same graphic instinct: items arranged in loose grids on white or pink grounds with lace borders and script labels.

**Lighting and medium.** Soft, diffused, slightly overexposed light with a hazy bloom; pastel washes rather than contrasty modeling. Interior sets use gentle ambient warmth, never hard flash. The style is equally at home in photograph, scanned magazine page, moodboard collage and fan illustration — the graphic treatment (soft haze, pink cast, collage ornaments) is what binds the media together.

## Mood & Values

Sophistication, femininity, youth — held in a dreamy, melancholy register. The emotional core is *wistful prettiness*: eyes made to look just-cried, lips pouting, a bedroom full of roses and pearls that feels like a remembered afternoon rather than a party. The style values cultivation (its reader was imagined as a museum-going young woman) and deliberate self-styling: contrast is intellect, texture is luxury, daintiness is strength. Sexy elements are admitted but always softened or countered by something girlish; girlish elements are always sharpened by something mature.

## Era & Origins

Tokyo, 2012, with *LARME* magazine; the codified "Larme Kei" look crystallizes 2014–2017 through Shibuya boutique snaps and Western Blogspot/Tumblr/Polyvore codification, borrowing the rule-writing habits of Lolita and Fairy Kei communities. It reacts against both plain casual fashion and against Lolita orthodoxy (early *LARME*'s loose use of "Lolita" annoyed Japanese Lolitas); after the magazine's 2020 relaunch it drifted toward mainstream trends, freezing Larme Kei as a distinctly mid-2010s artifact.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Build every look on the contrast rule: one girlish element (puff sleeve, tulle, ribbon, pink) against one mature/sexy element (black pencil skirt, leather, cold shoulder, choker).
2. Mix at least two opposing textures: lace + leather, sheer + fur, satin + pleats.
3. Keep the palette in the pastel-field-plus-anchor range above; gingham or plaid may carry the pattern load.
4. Give the face its canon: forehead bangs, pink cry-eye makeup, large shimmering eyes, light clean base, unsmiling pout or neutral gaze.
5. Set the figure in a girly-connotation scene (cluttered boudoir, rose garden, tea party, vintage parlor, pastel studio) or render the image as a magazine collage/flatlay with bows, pearls, hearts and script English quotes.
6. Finish footwear as classic feminine heels — Mary Janes, pumps, T-straps, platforms — with sheer or lace socks/tights; fur or feather trim is a period-correct flourish.
7. Light softly: diffused high-key with haze, or gentle vintage-interior ambience; add a faint pink cast.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a full coordinate, a makeup close-up, a flatlay or shoe grid, a collage spread, an interior still life, or an illustration. Composition may be centered studio figure, dressed-interior portrait, asymmetric collage, or ordered flatlay grid. Lighting may range from bright pastel high-key to softly moody parlor light. Color handling may lean pink-dominant, black-dominant with pink accents, red-gingham, or all-pastel lavender/cream. Mood may slide from melancholy-dreamy to sensual-pout to doll-blank, and substyle lean may touch grunge-lite (bomber jacket over tulle), pheromone-fetish (slip dress, leopard fur, tighter silhouette), or film-nymphet (gingham, cherries, sailor collar). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Big smiling or laughing expressions; sneakers, sportswear, streetwear logos, plain jeans-and-tee casualness; neon or electric saturated color; heavy contour, tanned base, or matte nude "Instagram" makeup; minimalist modern interiors, tech props, quiet-luxury beige greyscale; full classic Lolita silhouette (pannier bell skirts, bonnets, ott platforms) presented as orthodoxy; infantile props (pacifiers, plush-toy overload) as the core subject; unrelieved grunge or goth hardware with no girlish counterweight; hard direct flash or harsh midday sun; clean corporate studio catalog styling without haze, collage or vintage dressing.

## Discrimination Cues

| Balletcore | Balletcore is dancewear-derived — wrap tops, leg warmers, pointe shoes, buns, studio bars — in monochrome blush without black leather anchors, chokers, garter skirts, cry-eye makeup or collage editing. Larme Kei borrows tulle and ribbons but always adds the pink×black fashion contrast and magazine graphic language. |
