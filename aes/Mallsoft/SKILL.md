# Mallsoft

Mallsoft is the visual and musical subgenre of vaporwave that fixates on the enclosed shopping mall as a memory palace. Born in early-2010s internet communities around sampled muzak, smooth jazz, and easy-listening loops drenched in reverb, it translates that sound into images of vast mercantile interiors: atriums under skylight grids, concourses of glossy quarry tile, fountains jetting into conditioned air, palms standing in fiberglass planters. The aesthetic holds two opposite faces in tension — the pristine "golden era" mall of abundant artificial light and active storefronts, and the "dead mall" of shuttered steel shutters, burnt-out fixtures, and peeled signage — and binds both together with the texture of distance: grain, haze, VHS smear, overexposure, the look of a found photograph or a half-remembered errand. Whether sincere or ironic, every mallsoft image is about commerce after the shoppers have gone home.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette rule: **pastels own the surfaces; saturated color arrives as light.** Walls, stucco, tile, and carpet carry soft pink, mint, teal, lavender, and off-white; the intense hues — magenta neon, teal cove glow, purple dusk — appear as illumination spilling onto those surfaces and mirroring in polished floors, rarely as flat object paint. Three registers are canonical:

- **Golden-era photographic:** warm fluorescent white (#F2EFE6), brass and honey-gold trim (#C9A227–#D9B44A), rose-beige quarry tile, with magenta neon spill (#FF4FA3–#E86AA6) and teal accents (#35C4B5) tinting columns and floor reflections.
- **Dream-pastel render:** overexposed mint (#A8E6CF), blush (#F2A6C9), lavender (#C7B8E8), chalk white (#F4F1F6), pale gold (#E4C05A); values pushed toward white, shadows tinted pink or lilac rather than gray.
- **Dead-mall fade:** desaturated beige (#C9C1B4), institutional gray (#A9A49C), faded rose (#D8B7B7), cold dirty-skylight cyan (#DCE8E8), half-dead fluorescent greenish cast.

Supporting constants across registers: purple-to-magenta dusk sky for exteriors (#8E6BBF–#B678D4), teal skylight glow (#4FD8CB), and chrome/brass metallics. Flat-illustration and album-cover variants may break the light-only rule and color-block pastels and mid-tones as graphic planes; this is the one register where saturated shapes sit flat.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how firmly the style documents them)

1. **Enclosed mall architecture:** skylit atriums, multi-level corridors, balcony railings overlooking a central void; escalators and curved stair flights as the connective tissue between levels.
2. **Indoor water features:** tiered jetting fountains as centered centrepieces, circular fountain beds ringed by built-in benches, small reflecting pools.
3. **Potted plants, particularly palms and ferns:** in raised planters, planter-benches, and atrium beds; palm silhouettes also frame exterior shots.
4. **Neon signage:** storefront scripts, fascia bands, cove neon; magenta and pink glow bleeding onto tile and stucco.
5. **Fluorescent and coffered ceiling lighting:** recessed coffer grids, constellation downlights, steel skylight trusses and glazed barrel vaults.
6. **Glossy reflective floors:** polished quarry tile, terrazzo, and marble-look tile mirroring neon, skylights, and planters.
7. **Food courts and mercantile fixtures:** tables, counters, kiosks, directory signs, department-store aisles and grocery shelving. (The text lists food courts and grocery stores as core subjects; surviving imagery skews toward concourses and atriums, so treat food-court and supermarket scenes as fully licensed but less frequently pictured.)
8. **Consumerist decay:** shuttered storefronts, burnt-out fixtures, water-stained tile, empty planters, faded signage — the dead-mall branch.
9. **Memphis-derived forms at architectural scale:** curved balustrades, circular and arched openings, pastel color-blocking on walls and soffits, geometric tile patterns.
10. **Furniture of waiting:** slat benches, planter-integrated seating, brass railings, lone trash receptacles — objects that imply absent bodies.

### Materials and textures

Ceramic and quarry tile, terrazzo, polished marble-look tile, painted stucco and drywall, acoustic ceiling tile, glazed skylight panels on steel trusses, tinted and mirrored glass, brass and chrome railings, fiberglass planters, plastic or real palm fronds, neon tube and fluorescent diffusers, laminate counters; in dead-mall registers add carpet squares, peeling vinyl lettering, and dust-filmed glazing. Surfaces read smooth, hard, and slightly reflective — the texture of conditioned air.

### Subjects

Any enclosed public mercantile environment: mall concourse, atrium void, food court, supermarket or grocery aisle, department-store floor, kiosk row, bank-of-escalators hall. Secondary subjects: the mall exterior at dusk with neon fascia and palm silhouettes, and the album-cover staging of these interiors with overlaid typography (Latin serif or sans, often paired with Japanese kana). Human figures, when present at all, are tiny, distant, or back-turned — the space is the protagonist.

### Composition and lighting

One-point perspective down a concourse; symmetrical centering on a fountain, atrium void, or rotunda; eye-level walking-snapshot framing; tall verticals to catch atrium height and skylight trusses; wide angles that let emptiness accumulate. Lighting is even and ambient: fluorescent wash from coffered ceilings, daylight shafts through skylight glazing, neon spill coloring columns and floor, or dusk purple outside glazed walls. The dream-render register overexposes everything toward pastel haze. Degradation is characteristic but not mandatory: VHS scanlines and chroma bleed, film grain, soft focus, faded contrast, snapshot flash falloff — anything that says *memory*, not *documentation*. A clean image still qualifies if its light stays soft, fluorescent, and period-correct.

## Mood & Values

Mallsoft expresses nostalgia, hyper-consumerism, liminality, comfort, and isolation simultaneously. The mall is treated as a liminal space — built for transit, never habitation — offering "joyful apathy," the pleasure of existing with no expectation. It is reconstructed nostalgia for localized commerce: information and goods once housed in physical hubs, now gone. The mood swings between sincere romanticization of communal consumer space and haunting commentary on degree-zero capitalism and the retail apocalypse; both swings share the same empty tile floor.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in the early 2010s on 4chan, Bandcamp, and YouTube as a subgenre of vaporwave; Hantasi's *Vacant Places* (2012) is foundational, the "mallsoft" tag crystallized by 2013, and Cat System Corp's *Palm Mall* (2014) is the definitive release, alongside Disconscious, 식료품groceries, and PowerPCME's *Kmart 1989-1992*. The style looks backward at 1980s–90s North American and European enclosed malls — the anchor-store world of Kmart, Sears, JCPenney, The Gap — and forward at their abandonment, binding its imagery to the retail apocalypse.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. The space is the subject; architecture carries the narrative, people never do.
2. Keep it mercantile and enclosed; if shooting exterior, frame a commercial facade with palms, neon fascia, or dusk sky.
3. Obey the palette rule: pastel surfaces, saturated color as light, one consistent register (golden, dream-pastel, or dead-mall fade).
4. Light tells the era: fluorescent coffers, skylight trusses, neon cove — never LED video walls or smart fixtures.
5. Populate with residue of crowds: benches, planters, directories, signage, a lone fountain running for no one.
6. Add distance: haze, grain, VHS smear, overexposure, or at minimum a soft faded wash.
7. Keep details period-plausible: script and serif storefront logos, brass, tile, stucco, kana or serif cover typography.

**Variation axes.** Subject: golden-era atrium, dead concourse, food court, grocery aisle, department-store floor, escalator hall, dusk facade, cover art. Composition: one-point corridor, symmetrical rotunda, corner snapshot, tall atrium vertical, low-angle exterior. Lighting: bright fluorescent, skylight daylight shafts, neon night glow, overexposed dream haze, dim half-lit decay. Color handling: photographic fade, pastel render, flat graphic color-block. Medium: found photograph, VHS still, soft-shaded CGI render, flat illustration, album cover. Mood: comforting nostalgia through eerie haunting. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Crowds, shoppers in motion, faces or portraits as focal points.
- Modern retail: LED video walls, glass curtain-wall storefronts, self-checkout kiosks, smartphones, post-2000 branding, open-air lifestyle centers.
- Non-mercantile interiors: homes, offices, schools, hospitals, pools.
- Vaporwave props: classical busts, retro computers, Windows UI, glitch squares, chrome type, grid horizons, sunburst suns, sports cars.
- Noir values: black-dominated frames, rain-slick streets, candlelight, hard chiaroscuro.
- Memphis as object study: confetti-terrazzo furniture close-ups, squiggle graphics dominating the frame.
- Fitness content: leotards, aerobics studios, gym equipment.
- Sterile contemporary HDR clarity with saturated realism and no atmospheric softness.
- Post-apocalyptic overgrowth or horror gore; dead-mall decay stays commercial — stains, shutters, burnt fixtures.

## Discrimination Cues

- **After Hours:** night-time noir of streets, bars, and offices after close; dark value range, wet asphalt, sodium-warm against neon-cool. Mallsoft stays pastel-valued, interior-first, lit by fluorescents and skylights, nostalgic rather than cinematic-moody.
- **Memphis Design:** object-scale graphic design — laminate furniture, squiggle shelving, clashing primaries and confetti patterns in studio product shots. Mallsoft borrows Memphis curves and pastels only as architecture-scale backdrop inside empty commercial space with atmospheric depth.
- **80s Workout:** bodies in neon spandex, aerobics studios, sweatbands, motion and high-saturation neon. Mallsoft has no fitness subject, near-zero people, and architectural scale in pastels.
- **Liminal Space:** any empty transitional space — hallways, waiting rooms, playgrounds — often raw and unstylized, uncanny without nostalgia. Mallsoft is the mercantile, pastel-and-neon, memory-degraded subset; an empty home hallway with no commercial signage is Liminal Space, not Mallsoft.
- **Vaporwave:** the parent style, carrying Greek busts, retro tech, glitch, chrome typography, sunset grids, and advertising collage. Mallsoft drops those props entirely and commits to mall architecture and ambient space; busts or computers in frame push an image back to vaporwave proper, even if kana typography (shared with mallsoft cover art) remains.
