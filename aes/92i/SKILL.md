# 92i

92i is a French maximalist makeup and fashion trend that emerged in early 2025 in the Hauts-de-Seine (département 92) and spread through French-speaking TikTok and Instagram. It takes its name from the postal code and borrows cultural weight from the 92i hip-hop collective founded in 1999 by Booba and other Boulogne-Billancourt rappers, but the beauty aesthetic itself arose independently as a viral social-media phenomenon. Its core is a deliberately constructed, high-contrast glam face — matte, sculpted, symmetrical — worn with accessible streetwear ("Zara drip") and an attitude of confident, unapologetic presence in urban space. It positions itself against both the "clean girl" minimalism of the preceding years and the "quiet luxury" of exclusivity-coded fashion; its glamour is technical skill, not price tag.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is a single warm, low-to-medium chroma family: bronze and warm browns on the face, matte tan skin tones, nude-brown lips, greige-and-cream surroundings, black apparel, and small warm-gold metal accents. Everything in frame should be nameable as bronze, brown, nude, beige, black, white or warm gold; cool hues are excluded except one muted apparel accent.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual authority)

1. **High-coverage matte foundation** — a uniform, impeccably smooth velvet base; concealer several shades lighter carved under the eye and above the cheekbone for sharp facial contrast.
2. **Carved block-like eyebrows** — thick, symmetrical, hard-edged, built with heavy pencil and gel; one of the look's most character-defining elements.
3. **XXL false lashes** — extremely long, voluminous strips or spikes; the text names them the most visually striking component of the full look.
4. **Winged eyeliner with warm brown shadow** — a sharp outer-corner flick over brown-toned lids.
5. **Constructed contour and bronzer** — cream then powder sculpting nose, temples and cheekbones into warm, deliberately artificial dimension; blush minimal or absent.
6. **Overlined matte lip** — darker liner halo filled with nude, brown or mauve matte lipstick.
7. **Accessible streetwear** — fleece hoodies, tracksuit pants, technical sportswear shells, ribbed cotton tanks; fast fashion over designer pieces.

Companions documented only in the visual record (the text is silent on them; treat as frequent, optional): sleek, very long dark hair worn straight and glossy or pulled into a high ponytail; nude or french-tip manicure; thin gold necklaces and rings, stacked bead or pearl bracelets; a chunky gold chain as a bag strap; a peace-sign hand raised near the cheek; the phone itself visible in mirror shots; tiny handle or caption text overlaid on collage grids.

### Materials and textures

The style's core texture contrast is powder-matte skin against gloss-sleek hair. Around it: cotton fleece, nylon technical shell, ribbed knit cotton; gold-tone chain metal; beaded and pearl bracelet stacks; painted plaster walls, mirror glass, phone-case plastic. Images carry faint smartphone sensor grain and mild compression — the medium is part of the texture.

### Recurring subjects and settings

Self-shot portraits of young women — the "meuf 92i" — in bedrooms, bathrooms, hallways and fitting corners with beige walls; car interiors at night; occasional vanity or getting-ready moments. The face is the subject; the room is incidental backdrop.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical 9:16 frames; arm's-length close crops or mirror half- and full-body shots; frequent diagonal tilt of the camera axis; the face may be partly occluded by the phone in mirror shots; two-to-four panel collage grids with small caption text are a native format. Lighting is ambient and unstyled: flat ceiling light, warm dim bulbs, direct on-camera flash at night, or soft window daylight falling on a greige wall. Shadows stay soft and short; there is no studio catchlight geometry.

## Mood & Values

Full glam, audacious beauty, technical precision. Makeup is craft and self-assertion: symmetry and contour as architecture, lashes as declaration. The attitude is confident, unapologetic presence in urban space — banlieue pride, accessibility over exclusivity, maximalism as identity rather than consumption. It is glamour that does not ask permission and does not justify itself with luxury brands.

## Era & Origins

Hauts-de-Seine, France, 2025; viral on TikTok and Instagram among French-speaking users. The name descends from the 92i hip-hop collective (1999, Booba, Boulogne-Billancourt), whose legacy gave the region a cultural identity, though the beauty trend emerged independently. The look reacts against clean-girl minimalism and quiet luxury, and draws on a French early-1990s sensibility of high-contrast, high-maintenance glamour — reinterpreted with cleaner blending, more precise base work and stronger symmetry than the source era used. It is sometimes read as the French counterpart to "Latina makeup," while keeping distinct regional coding. Mass brands (Maybelline, Zara) signal its accessibility ethos.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Face first, always: the makeup architecture is the subject; even full-body frames keep the face legible.
- Matte is law: skin finishes velvet-powder; shine lives only in hair gloss and metal hardware.
- One warm family: bronze, brown, nude, beige, black, warm gold; keep chroma low-to-medium.
- Anonymous clothing, loud face: streetwear without logos; the glamour budget is spent on cosmetics, not brands.
- Phone-native realism: vertical crop, selfie distance, mirror or front camera, slight grain, casual backdrop; perfection in the makeup, not in the production.
- Pose as attitude, not performance: unbothered chin tilt, neutral or half-smiling mouth, optional peace sign.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: beauty macro (lash, brow or lip crop), head-and-shoulders selfie, half-body mirror shot, full-length fit check, getting-ready vanity, night-car candid, or a 2–4 panel collage; hair straight-sleek, center-parted or in a high ponytail; skin depth anywhere in the warm tan-to-bronze band.
- *Composition*: straight or tilted axis; phone visible or hidden; single frame or grid; crop from macro to full-length; subject centered or off-axis against a plain wall.
- *Lighting*: window daylight on a greige wall, warm dim interior bulb, direct flash at night, car dome light — always ambient, never styled.
- *Color handling*: bronze pushed deeper or base kept lighter; backdrop cream, greige or dark interior; apparel may add one muted cool accent (powder-blue shell, white tank) while the face stays warm.
- *Mood*: cool unbothered, playful (peace sign, slight pout), or soft-lipped confidence.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A macro of lashes and a carved brow on a dark backdrop is as 92i as a beige-wall mirror selfie, provided palette, finish and attitude hold.

**Avoid**

- Dewy, glass or satin skin; highlighter beams; cream-blush flush; "your-skin-but-better" bare texture.
- Natural feathered or soap-brow brows; mascara-only lashes; eyes without a wing.
- Pink, coral or classic red lips; glossy lip finishes; blush as a color event.
- Luxury signage: monogram canvas, logo belts and buckles, fur coats, silk scarves, champagne flutes, hotel-lobby props.
- Editorial studio production: seamless paper backdrops, softbox catchlights, rim hair-lights, medium-format crispness.
- Cool or pastel grades: teal shadows, mint walls, neon accents, blue-hour casts.
- Vintage cosplay: cassette tapes, CRT monitors, 90s windbreakers as costume, film-border or VHS pastiche — the 1990s lives in the makeup grammar, not in props.
- Pastoral or boho scenery: flower fields, rattan furniture, linen curtains, golden-hour meadows.
- A bare-faced tracksuit posture as the whole image (that is Racaille territory).
- Tracing any reference frame: no copied watermarks, handles or exact pose geometry.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Boujee** shares glam intensity and gold, but Boujee's subject is wealth display — designer logos, fur, champagne, jewelry as price tag. In 92i the gold is a chain strap and a ring, and the flex is technique. If logos and luxury props outshout the face, it is Boujee.
- **Clean Girl** is 92i's declared antagonist: dewy minimal skin, slicked-back bun, small hoops, beige wellness athleisure, soft morning light. 92i inverts every variable — matte over dewy, carved over feathered, XXL over bare, constructed over natural. If the skin glows and the lashes look real, it is Clean Girl.
- **Racaille** shares the banlieue streetwear coding (tracksuits, technical shells, urban posture) but centers the clothed body and street attitude with little or no makeup, and runs a colder black-grey-sportstripe palette. 92i centers the made-up face, warms everything toward bronze, and lets streetwear play support. If the face is bare and the tracksuit is the subject, it is Racaille; if the glam face leads, it is 92i.

**Quick tell:** matte sculpted skin + block brow + XXL lashes + nude-brown matte lip + logo-free streetwear + warm beige-or-night phone-shot frame = 92i.
