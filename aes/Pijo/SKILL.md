## Overview

Pijo is the visual culture of Spain's affluent, trend-conscious youth and young adults — the Spanish cousin of preppy, and the masculine/mixed-group half of a pair whose feminine counterpart is the Cayetana. It is a subculture of appearance: high income, or at minimum the convincing performance of it, expressed through impeccable grooming, current fashion, and leisure coded as upper-class (sailing, tennis, paddle, golf, skiing, riding, upscale parties). The look is conspicuously consumptive but not couture: mass-marketed luxury, advertising-familiar brands, and a cheerful, slightly smug self-presentation. Images in this style read as commercial fashion campaigns, glossy magazine editorials, clean group snapshots, or even flat vector illustration — always bright, always groomed, always socially legible as "money, or the appearance of money."

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette operates in three registers, and a given image usually combines a neutral base with one of the other two:

| Register | Role | Representative range |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral base | trousers, shirts, backdrop, knitwear | beige/khaki `#D8C9A3–#EFE6D4`, optic white `#F7F5F0`, navy `#1B2A4A–#274060` |
| Pastel layer | polos, blouses, knits, illustrated flats | powder pink `#F2C9CE`, baby blue `#BFD9E8`, mint `#CDE4D4`, cream `#F6E9C9`, lemon `#F0E68C` |
| Saturated accent | polos, knotted sweaters, color-block backdrops, flags | flag red `#C60B1E`, flag yellow/gualda `#FFC400`, magenta `#D02A8C`, orange `#EF7F1A`, bottle green `#1E7A46`, cobalt `#1F5FA8` |

Rule of thumb: tailoring stays neutral or pastel; the saturation lives in knitwear, polo shirts, and backdrops. National colors (red and yellow together, sometimes as an actual Spanish flag worn, carried, or draped) are a permitted and characteristic accent, never the whole palette.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the source culture documents them)

1. **The wool sweater draped or knotted over the shoulders** — the single strongest garment code; sleeves tied at the chest over a polo or shirt.
2. **Impeccable, trend-current grooming**: hair gel on men, straightened hair on women; pressed, tailored silhouettes; nothing wrinkled or worn.
3. **Men's kit**: tailored trousers or pressed jeans, made-to-measure striped shirts, piqué polo shirts with small emblem, diamond/argyle-patterned socks, loafers.
4. **Women's kit**: flowing blouses or borrowed men's shirts, skirts at whatever length is current, fitted jeans, suits, silk scarves, gold jewelry.
5. **Status accessories for all genders**: sunglasses, watches, and luxury cars recognized through advertising.
6. **Equestrian motif** (the style's named key motif): riding clubs, horses as backdrop, tack-adjacent details — present as leisure context rather than as the subject.
7. **Upper-class sport and leisure settings**: tennis and paddle courts, golf, ski slopes, marinas and yacht harbors, gym-kept bodies, upscale parties and nightclubs.
8. **Spanish traditionalist cues**: the flag, bullring architecture, an understoned nationalist traditionalism expressed through props and settings rather than costume.
9. **Brand legibility**: Ralph Lauren, Lacoste, Tommy Hilfiger, El Ganso, Calvin Klein, Dior; historically the Don Algodón sweatshirt of the 1980s.
10. **The group of friends**: arms over shoulders, playful grappling, conspiratorial grins — camaraderie as a status display.

### Materials and textures

Piqué cotton polos, fine-gauge merino or wool knits, crisp poplin and striped shirting cotton, silk scarves, smooth leather loafers and belts, pressed denim, gold metal, polished watch crystals. Surfaces look new, clean, and lightly glossy — never distressed, faded, or artisanal.

### Recurring subjects and composition

Young adults and teenagers, solo or in groups of three to six; couples at leisure; crowds at events. Compositions favor eye-level, centered arrangements: a row of friends linked by arms, a solo portrait with crossed arms showing the knotted sweater, full-body or three-quarter crops with clean negative space. Backdrops are either seamless studio white, a single saturated color-block field, or recognizable leisure architecture — brick arcades and plazas, harbor masts and moored yachts, club terraces, manicured sport venues. Flat, simplified vector illustration of the same wardrobe is a legitimate medium for this style.

### Lighting

Bright and even: shadowless studio flash or softbox for campaign-like images; open, slightly overcast or Mediterranean daylight for outdoor snapshots; for 1980s-set iterations, direct on-camera flash with saturated color casts and mild grain. Highlights stay clean, skin tones warm, colors saturated but never murky.

## Mood & Values

Cheerful smugness. The style expresses wealth, social status, exclusivity, traditionalism, conformity, refined appearance, and conspicuous consumption — and it does so joyfully, not austerely. There is playfulness (grappling friends, grins, a flag worn as a cape) atop strict conformity to trend. The emotional register is "carefree because provided for": leisure prioritized, appearance meticulously managed, belonging to a closed circle made visible.

## Era & Origins

Spain, term and subculture crystallized in the 1970s (Puerto Banús, Pacha-era display of wealth), with roots reaching back through 19th-century elite fashion consciousness and mid-20th-century Francoist high society. The 1980s mass-marketed it (Don Algodón sweatshirts, the band Hombres G); the 1990s added glossy luxury display and a trickle of grunge influence; the 21st century produced the "Cayetano" archetype and fast-fashion diffusion. It reacts against nothing explicitly — it is defined *for* consumption and status, and against countercultural scruffiness by simple opposition.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Grooming before everything: pressed fabric, set hair, clean shoes. Any wrinkle or stray strand breaks the code.
2. Make status legible in one glance: a watch, sunglasses, an emblem polo, a car hood, yacht masts, a club terrace.
3. Use the sweater-over-shoulders knot as the default strong signal; it may be omitted only if other codes carry the image.
4. Build color per the three-register rule: neutral tailoring plus pastel ensemble or saturated knit/backdrop; flag red-yellow only as accent or prop.
5. Prefer social poses: linked arms, hands on shoulders, light grappling, confident smirks.
6. Set the scene in the leisure repertoire: marina, paddle club, ski slope, brick plaza, party, studio color-block.
7. Finish glossy and bright: advertising-grade clarity or clean snapshot realism; pick one decade register (80s flash, 90s gloss, contemporary digital) and keep hair, grade, and props consistent with it.

Variation axes — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image:
- **Subject**: solo man, solo woman, mixed group, teenagers or young adults, illustrated figures; portrait or full-body.
- **Activity**: sailing, tennis, paddle, golf, skiing, riding, gym, party, street stroll, event crowd with flag.
- **Composition**: studio color-block, environmental group row, candid outdoor snapshot, flat vector illustration.
- **Lighting**: high-key studio, even daylight, vintage on-camera flash.
- **Color handling**: neutral-dominant with one pop; full pastel; saturated color-block; flag accent.
- **Mood**: from polished smugness to rowdy playfulness.

Avoid (style-breakers):
- Distressed, faded, wrinkled, or thrifted clothing; ripped denim; grunge flannel; punk or metal insignia; dirty sneakers and athleisure as the focal outfit.
- Boho earth tones, terracotta, macramé, folk embroidery, flamenco folklora dress.
- Streetwear logomania, oversized graphic hoodies, hype-brand drops.
- Dark chiaroscuro, neon cyberpunk nightlife, desaturated matte minimalism.
- Bullfighting costume (traje de luces, montera, cape) worn as clothing; the bullring may appear as architecture only.
- A horse-and-tack scene with no social display (that is the Equestrian aesthetic); horses stay background motif.
- Dominant American Ivy codes (varsity crests, campus iconography) or French bourgeois formality (tweed-and-pearl conservatism) — both drift the image out of Spain.
- Logo-free, color-free quiet luxury with ungelled hair — that is Old Money, not pijo.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cayetana**: the feminine counterpart. If the frame centers women's refinement alone — flowing blouses, silk scarves, gold jewelry, fair and equestrian social scenes — without polo-shirted men, group camaraderie, sport-club settings, or nationalist props, it reads Cayetana. Pijo keeps the masculine/mixed group and the trend-driven brand legibility in view.
- **Old Money**: inherited restraint — muted heritage tones, no visible logos, mature subjects, estate interiors, matte finishes. Pijo is younger, louder, brand-legible, saturated, and glossy; it advertises rather than inherits.
- **Torero Aesthetic**: bullfighting iconography as costume — embroidered jackets, montera, capes, red-black-gold ornament. Pijo may stand in a bullring plaza or signal traditionalist sympathy, but its clothes are contemporary leisurewear.
- **Bon Chic, Bon Genre**: French bourgeois conservatism — darker tailoring, scarf-at-the-throat formality, family-coded Parisian propriety. Pijo is sportier (polo, paddle, sailing), brighter, and charged with youth-culture and mass-media trend energy.
- **Equestrian**: the horse, tack, and riding action are the subject. In pijo, riding is one leisure option among many; the subject is always the social display of groomed people, with horses at most as setting.
- **Preppy** (nearest foreign cousin): swap the American campus for Spanish marinas, brick plazas, and bullring arcades, add flag red-yellow and Mediterranean light, and the image crosses from preppy into pijo.

Honest note: the feminine wardrobe and the equestrian motif are well documented in text but thin in surviving imagery, which skews male-group and studio-portrait; treat those text-documented elements as fully canonical despite their smaller visual footprint.
