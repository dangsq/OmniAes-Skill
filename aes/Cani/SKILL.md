# Cani

Cani is the aesthetic of a Spanish youth subculture born in the working-class neighborhoods of Andalusia in the 1990s and stereotyped into national fame in the 2000s. It is a sportswear-and-gold uniform worn as class identity: tracksuits, bootleg football jerseys, muscle shirts, conspicuous chains, sideways caps, tribal tattoos, scooters. The term began as a classist slur and was later partly reclaimed; the culture's own self-image is one of crew loyalty, brand pride, performative toughness and party hedonism. Visually, Cani survives almost entirely as *found photography*: compact digital camera and disposable-film snapshots, direct flash, saturated color, casual framing — the look of a night out documented by the crew itself, not by a photographer.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The color rule is a neutral base plus metallic gold plus one loud team color. Black and white carry the clothing (black tracksuit sets, white muscle shirts and tank tops); gold carries the jewelry and must catch the light; a single vibrant football-team hue — red, green or blue — interrupts the monochrome as a jersey, cap, jacket or scooter panel. Environments contribute warm beige and tan (plastered interior walls, brick, tiled plazas) and, at night, a near-black falloff around flash-lit skin.

- Blacks: `#0A0A0C`–`#232323` (tracksuit nylon, night backgrounds)
- Flash whites / off-whites: `#EDEAE2`–`#FFFFFF` (tank tops, overexposed skin)
- Metallic gold: `#B8860B`–`#E6C15C`, always with a specular hotspot from the flash
- Team red: `#B01E24`–`#E03A3E`; team green: `#007A33`–`#3FA650`; team blue: `#1F4FA3`–`#3E7BD6`
- Mediterranean beige / brick: `#CBB794`–`#E4D6BC`, terracotta `#A65A3A`
- Denim and bomber grey-blues: `#5A7BA6`, `#8C8F94`

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the culture documents them)

1. **Tracksuits and bootleg sportswear** — zip jackets with contrast piping, matching pants; counterfeit or misprinted brand logos are characteristic, not accidental.
2. **Gold jewelry** — thick curb chains, cross or medallion pendants, rings; worn over the shirt, never tucked away.
3. **Baseball caps sideways or backward**, swoosh-style sports brands.
4. **Bootleg football jerseys and muscle shirts / ribbed tank tops**, sleeves cut or absent to show arms.
5. **Tribal tattoos** — black armbands and shoulder pieces in sharp pointed linework.
6. **Scooters and customized mopeds** (and small quads or bikes in the snapshot record), the culture's prized machines.
7. **Piercings and earrings** — eyebrow or lip studs, gold or silver hoops in both ears.
8. **Hair and face code** — gel-stiff spiky tops with frosted blonde tips, or shaved sides with a spiked crest; goatee stubble; small dark sunglasses (rectangular semi-rimless or oval).
9. **Brand vernacular** — Karl Kani, Rottweiler, El Niño, Dj's Band logos; flat cartoon brand-logo parody is a genuine artifact of the culture's humor.
10. **Social props** — beer bottles held up to the lens, public benches, tiled plazas, brick walls, disco entrances, colored club light.
11. **Late-period tail (overlap with MDLR)** — puffer jackets, balaclavas, fanny packs worn across the chest; use only when deliberately depicting the post-2010 evolution.

### Materials and textures

Polyester tracksuit sheen, nylon bomber cloth, denim, ribbed cotton tank tops, metallic gold with flash glare, gel-hardened hair, vinyl scooter seats, painted plaster and glazed tile, brick. The image surface itself is a texture: low-resolution JPEG softness, sensor noise, flash red-eye, slight motion blur, occasional date stamp, 4:3 framing.

### Recurring subjects and composition

Working-class young men, solo or in crews; group huddles with arms over shoulders; hands gesturing at the lens (pointing, reaching, throwing signs); leaning on benches, walls, scooters; candid party and club moments; held-camera or mirror selfies in the later period. Framing is centered, casual, sometimes tilted; subjects look into the lens grinning or glaring. The composition always reads as *social* — someone from the crew took this photo.

### Lighting

One hard light source: on-camera flash at night producing hot skin, gold glare and black falloff; flat, slightly overexposed Mediterranean daylight outdoors; indoor flash against beige walls; club strobes and colored gels for disco scenes. No softboxes, no bounce, no golden-hour romance.

## Mood & Values

Group identity above individuality; brand consciousness as class pride rather than consumption; perceived aggression and hypermasculinity performed for the camera; hedonism of the disco, the bench and the scooter ride; defiance toward respectability and toward the police stop-and-search reality of being working-class in a "pijo" neighborhood. Underneath the bravado sits loyalty, humor and, after years of media caricature, an ironic self-awareness that later hardened into reclaimed pride.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in 1990s Andalusia; crystallized as a national stereotype in the 2000s with rap quinqui (Haze), flamenco-pop and early reggaeton on the stereo and films like *El Bola* and *7 Vírgenes* on screen; cemented as caricature by viral internet comedy. The name is thought to derive from the hip-hop label Karl Kani. The 2008 financial crisis gutted the lifestyle — construction jobs vanished, scooter customization and discos died — and the 2011 15M movement pulled much of the demographic toward politics; the residue rebranded itself as MDLR. Cani defines itself against its enemies: pijos, jebis, emos, frikis.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Snapshot first: the image must read as a found photo from a compact camera or disposable film, never as a campaign.
2. Sportswear plus gold is the irreducible core: at least one tracksuit/jersey/tank element and at least one visible gold chain or ring.
3. One vibrant team color against black, white and beige; gold always catches the flash.
4. Flash logic: a single hard light, hot highlights, dark falloff, or flat overexposed daylight.
5. Bootleg energy: logos slightly wrong, overprinted, mismatched sets welcome.
6. Social context: street, plaza, bench, scooter, disco, home interior — never a void or a studio.
7. Dignity: depict the style as self-identification and class pride, not as slur-caricature or ethnic mockery.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo portrait, crew huddle, couple, scooter or quad ride, club night, plaza hangout, family party, beach promenade, mirror selfie. Composition: centered, tilted, gesturing, leaning, huddled. Lighting: night flash, daylight overexposure, indoor flash, club color. Color handling: any team hue as the accent; gold constant. Medium: compact digital, film snapshot, camcorder still, early camera-phone. Mood: defiant, joyful, tender, ironic. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Professional studio lighting, HDR sharpness, modern computational-phone clarity (unless deliberately post-2010 MDLR).
- Muted, pastel, earth-tone or Scandinavian palettes; quiet luxury, tailored minimalism.
- Elegant authentic haute-couture logos; the bootleg look is the point.
- Punk, emo, grunge, skater or hipster coding; band tees, beanies, groomed 2020s fades.
- Pijo preppiness: polos, boat shoes, pastel chinos.
- Rave neon, glowsticks, smiley acid-house graphics (that is Bakala territory).
- Fine-line or realistic tattoo sleeves instead of black tribal work.
- Clean vector illustration as the default register (flat cartoon logo parody excepted).
- Caricatured ethnicity or poverty-porn framing.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Choni** is the female-coded twin of the same class milieu: heavy liner and glossy lips, straightened highlighted hair, large hoops, manicured nails, tracksuit bottoms with feminine tops. Cani frames center male crews, caps, muscle shirts and visible gold.
- **MDLR** is Cani's own successor: smartphone and trap-video register, puffer jackets, balaclavas, chest-rig fanny packs, darker drill styling. Cani keeps the 2000s compact-flash surface, sideways cap, football colors and uncovered face.
- **Pokero** overlaps heavily in gelled hair and synthetic sportswear; the documented distinction is thin, but Pokero leans toward early-2000s electro-latino dance dressing and club-centered scenes, while Cani anchors on football jerseys, gold chains and street/scooter context.
- **Racaille** is the French banlieue counterpart: Lacoste or Sergio Tacchini polos, Nike TNs, tower-block and French-signage settings. Cani swaps those for Andalusian plazas, tiled benches, beige interiors, cross pendants and a flamenco-pop/reggaeton atmosphere.
- **Bakala** is the Valencian rave route: neon colorways, glowsticks, warehouse strobes, long-exposure crowd shots. Cani's party happens in discos and streets under a single flash, with gold and team colors instead of rave neon.

**Quick recognition check:** amateur flash snapshot + tracksuit or jersey + visible gold chain + cap or gelled frosted tips + Spanish street/plaza/disco setting = Cani.
