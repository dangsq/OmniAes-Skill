# Decora

Decora (デコラ, "decoration style") is the purest maximalist expression of Harajuku street fashion: a coord is not an outfit but an accumulation, where dozens of cheap, bright, often handmade accessories are layered until the base clothing is nearly invisible. It began in late-1990s Tokyo from J-pop idol Tomoe Shinohara's plastic-jewelry idol look, was exaggerated by her teen fans ("Shinora"), and got its name from the streetsnap magazine *FRUiTS*. Its ethics are DIY and anti-fashion: the wearer is the maker, taste is deliberately bad-loud, and childhood is worn publicly as joy and defiance. Everything below treats the person as the canonical canvas, but the style also lives on objects (decoden: phone cases and mirrors buried under resin cabochons and whipped-cream glue) and, more rarely, on maximalist interiors and installations covered edge-to-edge in toys and flowers.

## Visual Identity

### Color language

The rule is **rainbow simultaneity**: several saturated hues at once, harmonized by intensity rather than by hue family. Clashing is correct; a single dominant color is the exception, not the norm.

| Palette mode | Hex range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Neon rainbow (default) | hot pink #FF4FA0, cherry #FF3B4E, tangerine #FF8A00, lemon #FFE135, lime #4CE04C, electric blue #00A8E8, grape #A06CD5 | 4–7 hues present simultaneously; saturation high; white/black only as small anchors |
| Pastel rainbow (Fairy Kei base) | #FFD1DC, #FFF3B0, #B9FBC0, #A0E7E5, #CDB4F6, #FFC8DD | sugar-toned but still multi-hue at once |
| Monochrome / Pink / Oshi Decora | one hue family, e.g. pinks #FF6FB5–#FFD1DC | density unchanged; theme or franchise dictates the hue |
| Dark Decora | base #0D0D0D–#1A1A1A with neon stabs (#FF2E93, #7CFF00) | black plus horror kitsch (eyeballs, skeletons, bats) and punk hardware |

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how firmly the style documents them)

1. **Hair clips in dense rows and clusters** — snap clips, star and heart clips, piled across the fringe or over a bandana/headdress; dozens, never one accent clip.
2. **Face stickers and band-aids (plasters)** on cheeks, nose bridge and forehead, plus colorful makeup matched to the coord; surgical masks also belong to the motif set (strongly documented in text, less visible in street imagery).
3. **Stacked plastic bead bracelets and necklaces** on both wrists, chunky and DIY-strung.
4. **Plastic toys and plastic jewelry** as pendants, rings, bag charms — the Shinohara inheritance.
5. **Child-like character motifs**: Sanrio (Hello Kitty, My Melody), Pokémon, Sailor Moon and magical girls, plus Western toycase icons — SpongeBob, Care Bears, Rainbow Brite, Strawberry Shortcake, Sesame Street, Peanuts — as prints, plushies and charms.
6. **Layered legwear**: socks over socks, patterned tights, striped knee-socks, fuzzy leg warmers over sneakers or platform Mary Janes.
7. **Tulle tutus and layered skirts**, often with clips and charms pinned into the tiers.
8. **Plushies carried and worn**: crossbody plush bags, plushies tucked into hats and hair.
9. **Unnatural wigs and hair**: pink, rainbow braids, twin-tails, crimped extensions.
10. **Busy prints** on the base garments: hearts, stars, polka dots, cartoon all-overs, mixed pattern-on-pattern.
11. **Handmade/DIY accessories** with visible craft hand: glue-gun decoden, beaded straps, felt charms.
12. **Subgenre markers**: tie-dye and psychedelic swirls (Hadeko, the androgynous sister style); franchise-single-color coords (Oshi Decora); eyeball/skeleton/bat kitsch with punk studs (Dark Decora).

### Materials and textures

Glossy plastic (clips, beads, charms, toy parts), resin cabochons and piped "whipped cream" resin on decoden surfaces, plush minky and fleece, stiff tulle and netting, fuzzy acrylic knit leg warmers, cotton jersey tees, canvas and nylon bags, rubber platform soles, laminated paper stickers, elastic-cord beadwork. Texture should read cheap, tactile and handmade — craft irregularity (uneven bead strings, lopsided charms) is authenticity, not flaw. Absent by value: silk sheen, fine metals, leather, tailoring.

### Recurring subjects, composition and lighting

The canonical subject is **one wearer photographed head-to-toe**, frontal and centered, feet and platform shoes in frame because legwear and footwear are part of the coord; poses are playful or deliberately deadpan — arms spread to show bracelet stacks, peace signs, hip cocked, crouching with a plush. Groups, paired friends, close-up selfies cropped to the clip-covered headpiece and stickered face, object still-lifes of decoden cases, and wide shots of toy-saturated rooms all sit inside the style. Backgrounds tend to be **mundane urban furniture** — concrete sidewalks, shuttered storefronts, yellow vending machines, crosswalks — so the maximal figure reads against ordinary city grain; alternatives are night streets with neon signage bokeh, or flat pastel studio seamless. Lighting is snapshot-honest: diffuse overcast daylight, direct on-camera flash at night, bright even studio light; color is pushed saturated and high-key. No chiaroscuro, no cinematic desaturated grading.

## Mood & Values

Child-like innocence worn as armor; playfulness and loud energy as public goods; maximalism as ideology ("more is more" against minimalist good taste); anti-fashion — a rejection of high fashion, luxury signals and adult restraint; non-conformity and DIY authorship, where the maker's hand is the point. The mood can be grinning exuberance or flat deadpan contrast against the chaos of the coord; both are correct. Nostalgia for childhood media is sincere, never ironic.

## Era & Origins

Late 1990s, Harajuku district, Tokyo. Tomoe Shinohara's idol style (childish hats, plastic jewelry) → teen fans emulating and exaggerating it → *FRUiTS* streetsnaps coin the term. Modern icon Haruka Kurebayashi (KERA! model, designer of the Takeshita Street arches) carries it into the 2010s–2020s; Tumblr and Instagram become its platforms alongside the street itself. Vendors such as 6%DOKIDOKI, ACDC RAG, CANDY☆TRAP and Decopanic supply it; music ties (Rainbow Panic, cybr.grl) exist but are thin evidence. The style reacts against mainstream minimalism, luxury fashion logic and adult conformity.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Density before design.** Count accessories in dozens; the base garment should be barely legible under clips, charms, beads and plush.
2. **Clash by saturation.** Place 4–7 high-saturation hues side by side; unify by brightness, not by hue family.
3. **Clips in clusters.** Hair clips arrive in rows and piles across fringe or headdress — a single clip is a different style.
4. **The face is part of the coord.** Stickers and band-aids on cheeks and forehead, makeup echoing the clothing's colors.
5. **Extremities never bare.** Socks layered over tights, leg warmers over socks, bracelets stacked on both wrists, platform shoes.
6. **Toy-box materiality.** Plastic, plush, tulle, beads, resin cabochons; visible handmade irregularity welcome.
7. **Character cargo.** Sanrio-style characters, cartoon prints and plush charms ride along on bags, hats and tees.
8. **Snapshot framing.** Head-to-toe or face-close, mundane street or pastel studio or neon night; saturated, high-key, candid energy.
9. **Joyful or deadpan, never sultry.** Expression sells play, not glamour.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any wearer of any gender or age presentation, solo, paired or grouped; or object-only decoden still-life; or a maximalist interior/installation. Palette: neon rainbow, pastel rainbow, single-hue monochrome/Oshi, or black-based Dark Decora with neon and horror-kitch accents. Base silhouette: Fairy Kei tulle skirt, casual graphic tee with capri pants, kimono-sleeve top, cardigan over dress, punk base for Dark Decora — any clothing works as canvas. Composition: full-body streetsnap, three-quarter, selfie close-up on headpiece and face stickers, flat-lay of accessories, wide interior. Lighting: overcast flat daylight, night flash with neon bokeh, even studio light on pastel seamless. Mood: exuberant, deadpan, or tenderly childish. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid (style-breakers)**

- Minimalism: negative space, capsule-wardrobe logic, one or two accent accessories, bare hair without clip rows.
- Desaturated, earthy or "sophisticated" palettes (beige, olive, terracotta, greige) outside Dark Decora's intentional black.
- Luxury or elegant materials and signals: silk satin sheen, fine gold/silver jewelry, leather, sharp tailoring, couture silhouettes, designer logos.
- Mature/sexy styling: bodycon cuts, sheer glamour, stiletto heels.
- Perfectly matched single-hue sets (unless a named monochrome subgenre, which still keeps full accessory density).
- Sleek futuristic techwear — PVC uniforms, cyberlocks, metallic panels — without toy-like handmade layering (that is Cyberpop).
- Austere goth solemnity; Dark Decora keeps kitsch horror and play, not mourning.
- Polished editorial fashion photography: moody chiaroscuro, teal-orange grading, muted film looks.
- A rave/club frame (dark warehouse, UV blacklight, DJ booth) as the organizing context — it drags the image toward Kandi Raver or Cyberpop.
- Candy-as-sole-motif monopoly (sweets only, no toys/clips/characters) — that is Candycore.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Kidcore:** shares child-like maximalism and primary color, but Kidcore is a Western-leaning nostalgia aesthetic of rooms, objects and graphic design with chunky color-blocking and smiley/90s-toy motifs; it lacks Decora's worn-fashion rituals — clip rows on the fringe, face stickers, layered socks over leg warmers, tulle tutus — and its Harajuku streetsnap body language.
- **Candycore:** organized around confectionery (candy, desserts, sugar pastels); Decora's organizing principle is accessory accumulation and character/toy motifs across the full rainbow, neons included. Candy prints may appear in Decora but never run the show.
- **Cyberpop:** futuristic techwear, neon-on-black, PVC, goggles, cyberlocks, club context; Decora borrows from it only in Cyber Decora, and even then keeps handmade toy layering, plushies and tutus. No clip rows or face stickers means it is not Decora.
- **Fairy Kei:** pastel 1980s-childrenswear silhouettes with light, dreamy coordination and restrained accessory use; Decora frequently uses Fairy Kei as a base but adds obscuring accessory density, neon clash and stickered faces. Fairy Kei is silhouette and pastel restraint; Decora is additive chaos.
- **Kandi Raver:** EDM festival culture — kandi bead trade, PLUR, UV neons, crop tops, dark club or field context; its beads are the whole bead story. Decora's beads are one layer among clips, tutus, leg warmers and plush, worn in daylight street contexts with character goods and face stickers.

**Quick recognition checklist:** clip clusters piled on the hair; stickers/band-aids on the face; bracelets stacked on both wrists; tulle or layered skirt with pins and charms; socks/leg warmers over platform shoes; 4+ saturated hues at once; toy or character motifs; snapshot street or pastel-studio framing. Hit five or more and it is Decora.
