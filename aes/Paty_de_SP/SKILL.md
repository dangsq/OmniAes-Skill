# Paty de SP (Patycore, Paty Girl, Patricinha)

Paty de SP is the aesthetic of the Brazilian "patricinha" — the affluent, supposedly frivolous pretty girl — rebuilt by São Paulo teenagers for TikTok. It keeps the old slang's aspiration to elegance and luxury but sources it from fast fashion (SHEIN, Shopee), sneaker culture and a phone camera: the look is always staged, always selfie-ready, always posted. Its wardrobe fuses night-out shine (black leather, sequins, glitter, satin) with daytime candy accents (pink, blue, cream), and its native format is the vertical mirror selfie taken in a bedroom, a closet hallway or a mall bathroom. Underneath the gloss runs the soundtrack of Brazilian trap and funk, which gives the polish its street confidence. Substyles radiate from the core — Paty Cristã, Afro Paty, Paty do Nordeste, Paty do Sul, Paty de RJ, and the male Enzo/Mauricinho variant — so the core style is best understood as the neutral-and-shine São Paulo original against which those variants define themselves.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
The palette is an **anchor-plus-accent system**: a dominant neutral base (black, optic white, metallic silver, light-wash denim blue, cream) carrying at most one loud candy or neon pop. Silver is a constant micro-accent through jewelry regardless of outfit color. Warm earth tones, olive, rust and multi-color florals are outside the core palette.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the source documents them
1. **The smartphone mirror selfie** — the defining act: phone held at face or chest height in a mirror, phone case visible, face often half-hidden by the device; multi-panel grids and collages of repeated selfies are a native format.
2. **Black leather and shine** — leather mini skirts and pants, sequin and glitter fabric, glossy satin; "shiny clothes" are a key motif, so some reflective surface should exist in nearly every look.
3. **Silver accessories** — layered silver necklaces, trinket bracelets, hoop or drop earrings, wristwatches; the metal is silver, not gold.
4. **The silhouette** — crop top (off-shoulder, camisole, bandeau, knit) over mini skirt, high-slit skirt, bodycon dress, or high-waisted wide-leg jeans / denim shorts; midriff visible.
5. **Sneaker and platform culture** — Nike Air Force, Air Jordan, V10, Vans; platform sandals, leather platform boots, high heels for night.
6. **Grooming code** — long decorated nails with polish matched to the outfit (usually black); basic glam makeup: gloss, blush, highlighter, mascara, eyeliner; long straightened or waved hair; dreads and afro puffs in the Afro Paty variant.
7. **Settings** — parties, clubs and shopping centers; domestic interiors (bedroom, closet, white-walled hallway); bright lights and flash at night.
8. **Bags** — crossbody, handbag or clutch; straw/raffia pieces with pearl chains sit comfortably inside the look.
9. **Emoji grammar** — ⚡ 🖤 🥂 👙 💋 in captions and edits.
10. **Food props** — sushi and champagne are documented tastes; treat as occasional props, not required elements (they barely appear in the visual record).

### Materials and textures
Matte and patent black leather; sequin mesh and glitter knit; satin and silk-look sheen; ribbed and crinkled knit cotton; clean light-wash denim; straw and raffia with pearl detailing; polished silver-tone metal. Texture contrast is the point: a soft cream knit against hard denim, a sequin top against plain white walls.

### Composition and lighting
Vertical 9:16 phone framing is the default canvas. Three-quarter or full-body mirror selfies dominate; secondarily, a friend's casual full-body snapshot outdoors in daylight, and collage grids or mirror-multiple tricks that repeat the same outfit. Interiors are ordinary and Brazilian-domestic: white walls, wooden doors, wardrobes, tiled corridors, fitting rooms, condo entrances. Light is soft window daylight or warm ceiling light by day; direct flash, neon and pink club glow by night. The grade is phone-native: slightly lifted saturation, smoothed skin, mild glow — a CapCut/VSCO finish, never a studio strobe look.

## Mood & Values

The stated key value is **elegance**, but it is elegance as aspiration rather than inheritance: fast-fashion pieces styled to read luxe, champagne taste on a SHEIN budget. The mood is confident, cheeky and performative — life organized for the feed, friendship displayed in group mirror shots, flirtation carried by the emoji set (🥂💋). Beneath the polish sits funk and trap: this is a girl who dances to MC Ryan SP and Matuê, not a preppy doll. The aesthetic also carries an inclusive uplift current, most explicitly in Afro Paty, which frames Black girls' rise in fashion and high society as part of the style's meaning.

## Era & Origins

The slang root "patricinha" (diminutive of Patrícia) dates to 1990s–2000s Brazil, describing affluent middle- and upper-class girls of luxury venues, with cinematic ancestors in Cher Horowitz (*Clueless*) and Regina George (*Mean Girls*); the male counterpart is the mauricinho, today also "Enzo" or "playboy" (the Enzocore variant). The visual aesthetic itself coalesced in the early 2020s among female influencers and TikTok users in São Paulo, spread to other Brazilian states, drew heavy inspiration from Bubblegum Bitch, and was reinvented again in the mid-2020s by absorbing Mandrake, Baddie and Bimbocore elements. Its home platforms are TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest, CapCut and VSCO.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Build the outfit as neutral anchor + one accent color, and guarantee at least one shine element (leather, sequin, satin, patent) or, failing that, a visible silver jewelry stack.
- Frame it as phone vernacular: vertical format, mirror selfie with the device in hand, casual friend snapshot, or a grid/collage of selfies. Never a tripod-perfect editorial.
- Keep the grooming code legible: long decorated nails, glossy lips, highlighted or styled long hair.
- Set it in ordinary Brazilian interiors or commerce spaces — white-walled rooms, wooden doors, closets, mall corridors, condo entrances — or under club flash and neon at night.
- Grade it like a phone app: mild saturation lift, smooth skin, soft glow; allow slight overexposure.

**Variation axes (what may change)**
- *Subject*: teenagers and young women of any ethnicity (Afro Paty explicitly centers Black girls, dreads and afro puffs); solo, pairs or friend groups; the male Enzo/Mauricinho variant is licensed.
- *Composition*: mirror selfie, direct pose, candid walking shot, multi-panel grid, mirror-multiple effect.
- *Lighting*: daylight interior, warm indoor lamplight, sunlit street, or night flash with neon/pink glow.
- *Color handling*: core neutrals with pink or blue accent at the center; neon lime, teal or fuchsia at the outer edge; cream-and-denim daytime looks and all-black sequin night looks are both valid poles.
- *Mood*: polished elegance ↔ cheeky party girl ↔ soft devotion (Paty Cristã with cross necklace and Bible, as a flagged substyle).

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — anchor-plus-accent palette, shine, silver, selfie vernacular — not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Dominant floral prints and gladiator sandals (Paty do Nordeste markers) in a core look.
- Long evangelical dresses with a Bible as the default outfit (Paty Cristã substyle only).
- Heavy winter coats and blazers as the focal layer (Paty do Sul marker); light jackets are fine.
- All-white flashy styling with frayed denim and CASIO watches as the core (Paty de RJ marker); core denim is clean-wash.
- Matte earth tones, boho linen, cottagecore clutter, quiet-luxury minimalism without any shine.
- Heavy contoured matte "baddie" makeup; Paty makeup stays basic and glossy.
- Professional studio strobes, widescreen cinematic crops, heavy analog film grain.
- Heritage-monogram luxury handbags or haute-couture gowns; the luxe here is fast fashion plus sneakers.
- Grunge distressing, punk hardware, thrift-store layering.
- Plush pastel coquette interiors with bows and stuffed toys (Bimbocore territory).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Baddie**: shares crop tops and confidence, but Baddie is US-derived with contoured matte makeup, beige athleisure bodysuits and posed Instagram glamour; Paty de SP keeps glossy basic makeup, silver trinket jewelry, sneaker-brand specificity (Air Force/Jordan/V10) and the unpolished mirror-selfie vernacular of Brazilian homes and malls.
- **Mandrake**: same trap/funk soundtrack and some borrowed streetwear, but Mandrake is the masculine-coded street style — jerseys, caps, chunky chains, outdoor urban settings; Paty de SP is feminine, interior, skirt-and-crop-top based, selfie-first.
- **Bimbocore**: overlaps in pink and hyper-femininity, but Bimbocore is pastel-only, plush, bow-and-plushie coquette irony with no black leather, no sequin night layer and no funk context; Paty de SP always keeps a neutral anchor and street confidence.
- **Bubblegum Bitch**: the direct ancestor — bratty pink 2010s Tumblr with heart motifs and grainy DIY edits; Paty de SP is its 2020s Brazilian reinvention: cleaner phone-camera grade, silver jewelry, sneaker culture, mirror-selfie format, and black/white/silver balancing the pink.
- **Milipili**: the Latin American preppy-teen cousin — schoolgirl pastel sets and mall-cute styling; it lacks Paty de SP's leather-and-sequin night layer, its trap/funk edge and its selfie-as-artifact framing. (Evidence here is thinner: the two overlap heavily in teen-girl mall subject matter, so palette anchor and shine are the safest tells.)

**Honest gaps**: the emoji set and the sushi/champagne props are documented in text but essentially absent from the visual sample; the male Enzo variant and club-night lighting are under-represented in images, so treat them as text-licensed rather than visually verified.
