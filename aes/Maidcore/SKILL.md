# Maidcore

Maidcore (Мейдкор) is an internet music scene and visual aesthetic that formed around 2013–2014 on the Russian platform VK, when anonymous producers began releasing instrumental breakcore/post-rock/post-metal under borrowed personas: the Nijiura Maids, a cast of original characters invented by anonymous users on Futaba Channel's Nijiura board. The visual side is cover-art culture first and foremost — every artist commissions or draws an anime-style portrait of their maid ("Yakui the Maid," "Ozoi the Maid," "Kairi the Maid"…) and treats it with a raw, anti-polish finish the scene itself calls **Oekaki Punk**, after the early-internet doodle boards. Everything downstream follows from that founding gesture: a performed anonymous identity, drawn crudely, standing in fog or in a dark void, while glitch noise eats the edges.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is a two-layer system, and the rule is the relationship between the layers rather than any fixed hue:

- **Base layer — suppressed and moody.** Near-blacks and charcoals (`#0b0b0e`–`#26262a`), fog greys and grey-greens (`#8f9a94`, `#b9c2bb`), dusty violets (`#6f5b76`), desaturated teals (`#4f7f7a`), oxblood/maroon (`#4b2026`), chalky off-whites for aprons. Monochrome and near-monochrome greyscale treatments are fully canonical.
- **Accent layer — digital and neon, used sparingly.** Electric magenta (`#ff2bd6`), cyan (`#24e0ff`), acid green (`#9dff3f`) and violet (`#8e46c8`) appear only as glitch fringes, chromatic-aberration splits, pixel-font text, scanline glows or a single luminous object in a dark frame. Never as overall saturation.
- **Permitted alternate base — chalky pastel doodle.** The oekaki-room variant uses flat, low-saturation pastel fills (pale pink-lilac walls, straw-yellow floors, cardboard brown) with the same deadpan mood; these reads as faded marker, not candy pop.

Any image whose base is bright, saturated, or warmly lit has left the style, no matter how many maids it contains.

### Line, texture and medium ("Oekaki Punk")

The defining material fact is a **disregard for digital polish**: non-anti-aliased jagged line work, wobbly hand-drawn strokes, flat MS-Paint-style fills with visible rough edges, dithering, low-resolution upscale artifacts, pixel sorting, datamosh blocks, scanline and compression noise, and sketchy hatching that never gets cleaned up. Figures may be collaged over desaturated photographs (foggy grass verges, panel-block outskirts) with a deliberate cut-out mismatch. Smooth gradients, clean vector curves and rendered lighting are absent by principle, not by budget.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the scene documents them

1. **The Nijiura Maid persona** — an anime-style maid figure: frilled apron over a dark dress, white headpiece or cap, often striped stockings, sometimes a symbolic attribute (cross pendant, cigarette, weapon, sickly pallor). Original characters in the same naming/design logic count.
2. **The "X the Maid" pseudonym as textual motif** — the persona name set in bitmap/monospace pixel type or blocky condensed caps, placed like cover typography; this naming convention is the lore glue of the whole scene.
3. **Glitch artifacts and digital noise** — RGB channel splits, corrupted pixel bands, mosaic distortion, VHS-ish interference over the figure or type.
4. **Lo-fi oekaki line quality** — the jagged, sketchy, early-2000s-board roughness described above.
5. **Gloomy urban / post-Soviet environment** — fog over grass and concrete, khrushchyovka outskirts, bus windows, basements, cluttered apartments with cardboard boxes, taped papers and a fridge; routine and alienation as set dressing.
6. **Drug paraphernalia** — cigarettes held in deadpan mouths, pills, syringes, references tied to the character Yakui; handled flatly, matter-of-factly, never glamorously.
7. **Early-internet frames** — imageboard thread screenshots, oekaki doodle boards, visual-novel/game UI with a tiny sprite and a "PLAY" prompt, 8-bit/16-bit sprite rendering of the maid.
8. **Character-attribute props** — juice boxes, small blob creatures, guitars, cans: each maid's personality translated into held objects.

### Recurring subjects

A single maid figure is the default subject: standing full-body in fog or void, seated in a cramped room, close portrait with hollow or deadpan eyes, or reduced to a small sprite centered in a black field. Secondary subjects: the empty post-Soviet landscape itself, the cluttered basement room, forum pages, and paired maids interacting flatly. The figure's expression is tired, blank, wary or mildly unhinged — never idol-cheerful.

### Composition and lighting

Cover-art logic dominates: square or letterboxed frames; figure centered or shoved to one edge; vast negative black space swallowing a small sprite; anime cut-out pasted into a photographic landscape; amateurish crops and misaligned type accepted as part of the voice. Lighting is murk: fog-scattered flat daylight, dusk smudge, flat fluorescent scanlight in interiors, or low-key darkness where one neon accent or white apron glows. There is no studio key light, no rim-lit glamour, no golden hour.

## Mood & Values

Maidcore expresses **anonymity as creative method**: the artist disappears behind a drawn persona, and the artwork is that persona's portrait. The emotional register is "depressive-aggressive" — melancholy, social anxiety, existential dread and routine alienation (the scene's own phrase: sad music for riding a bus in post-Soviet Russia) delivered with abrasive noise and a tongue-in-cheek grin. The core pleasure is **dissonance**: a cheerful frilled costume and cute anime tropes placed against bleak environments, drug references and corrupted pixels. Values are experimentation, lore-building across a community of pseudonyms, and loyalty to the rough handmade web of oekaki boards over commercial polish.

## Era & Origins

Founded circa 2013 by Yakui the Maid on VK, with the term coined in 2014 for the album *Regressive Maidcore*; persona practice descends from the Nijiura board on 2chan and the defunct Russian imageboard Omichan, and the visual language reaches further back to early-2000s oekaki doodle boards. A 2020s resurgence via Bandcamp/Spotify and Maidcore Records, plus the "Oekaki Punk" visual novel *Project Basement Dating*, extended the aesthetic internationally without smoothing it out.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Build the image as a *persona artifact*: an album-cover-like frame belonging to an anonymous "X the Maid," even when no text is present.
2. Lo-fi before everything: jagged aliased lines, rough flat fills, noise, dithering, pixel decay. Polish is the enemy.
3. Keep the base muted or monochrome; spend neon only on glitch fringes, type, or one glowing element.
4. Stage the dissonance: cute maid syntax (apron, frills, headpiece) against bleak setting, deadpan face, or corrupting artifacts.
5. Let text behave like lore: bitmap/monospace pseudonym type, corner-placed, slightly misregistered — optional but highly characteristic.
6. Add one layer of degradation last: compression noise, scanlines, pixel sort, fog haze.

**Variation axes (what may change freely).**
- *Subject*: any maid persona or OC, any symbolic attribute (sickly, martial, paranormal), small companion creatures, or no figure at all — just the foggy outskirts, the basement room, a forum thread, a game menu.
- *Composition*: full-body standing figure, bust portrait, tiny sprite in a void, photo-collage, thread screenshot, two-figure deadpan scene, extreme letterbox.
- *Lighting*: fog-flat daylight, dusk murk, flat interior scanlight, near-total darkness with one accent glow.
- *Color handling*: greyscale, teal or green duotone, dusty violet/maroon, chalky pastel doodle fills, or heavy neon glitch over monochrome.
- *Mood*: from quiet melancholy to aggressive corrupted noise to dry deadpan humor.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A foggy field with no figure, a forum-page doodle, or a pixel sprite on black are all valid Maidcore if the palette, roughness and persona logic hold.

**Avoid.**
- Polished modern anime key-visual finish: clean anti-aliased lines, smooth cel shading, rendered light, soft gradients.
- Bright saturated idol-pop or kawaii palettes; warm cozy cottagecore interiors.
- Glamorous, fetish or café-service framing of the maid (seductive pose, glossy latex, frilly boudoir); Maidcore's maids are tired characters, not pin-ups.
- Witch-house occult apparatus: sigils, triangles/crosses glyphs, chopped-and-screwed gothic type.
- Pure abstract datamosh with no persona, maid or cover-art logic (that drifts to Glitchbreak).
- Photoreal cosplay photography of maid outfits.
- Clean corporate or elegant serif typography; ornate framing.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Maidcore's difference |
|---|---|---|
| Glitchbreak | Glitch is the subject: abstract datamosh, breakcore brutality, no character lore | Glitch is a *treatment* over a drawn maid persona and cover layout; "X the Maid" naming and oekaki roughness persist under the noise |
| Lolicore | Childlike figures, fetishized cuteness, often cleaner or sweeter rendering | Maids are deadpan/weary persona characters; roughness, post-Soviet gloom and depressive-aggressive mood replace cuteness-as-subject |
| Maid | The uniform as fashion: clean illustration, cosplay photo, café elegance | The uniform as anonymous mask in lo-fi cover art; ugliness, fog, drugs and pixel decay are intentional |
| Witch House | Occult sigils, purple-black VHS haze, glyph typography (▲▽✝) | Same dark electronic mood, but iconography is anime maids, imageboard doodles and post-Soviet mundane realism; type is bitmap pixel, not occult glyph |

When in doubt, ask three questions: is there a maid persona treated as a pseudonym? is the finish deliberately crude, aliased and noisy? is the mood bleak-under-cute rather than cute, occult, or purely abrasive? Three yeses is Maidcore.
