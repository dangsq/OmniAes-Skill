# Manguebeat

Manguebeat (also Manguebit) is the visual arm of the countercultural movement born in Recife, Pernambuco, in 1991, where maracatu, coco and ciranda were wired into rock, hip-hop, funk and electronic music. Its imagery works the same way as its sound: a regional, earthbound body — mud, mangrove roots, crabs, rope-tensioned drums — fitted with a global antenna. The style is simultaneously a street style, a poster style and a mural style: vibrant maracatu-derived color, thrifted mixed-up clothing, crustacean symbolism, and the manifesto metaphor of a parabolic dish stuck in the mud. Everything is fusion with a critical edge: celebration that never forgets poverty, inequality and the threatened mangrove ecosystem.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The wiki fixes the key colours as vibrant white, blue, yellow, red and green, echoing maracatu performance dress. Treat white as a structural colour (ground, straw hat, drum foam, cloud), not as empty space. Saturation stays high and surfaces stay flat, like screen-print or painted signwork; hot gradients appear only as occasional accents on mascots and vector crabs.

Three legitimate colour regimes: (a) the full five-colour maracatu flag on white; (b) rainbow stripe stacking — many thin saturated bands in shirts, patterns and borders; (c) monochrome ink (black or grey on white) carrying a single hot accent such as orange rays or a stamp logo. Mud brown is the gravity of the palette: whenever earth appears, it is dark, wet and red-brown, contrasted against a loud blue sky.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **The crab (caranguejo)** — the movement's central symbol, from the "Caranguejos com Cérebro" manifesto to public monuments. Appears as animal, metal sculpture, vector mascot, and as human posture: hands held up claw-like.
2. **Parabolic dish in the mud** — the manifesto's metaphor for tradition wired to global modernity, and a critique of technology arriving amid poverty. Dishes, antennas, cables and samplers grafted onto organic or folk objects.
3. **Mangrove ecosystem** — mud flats, tangled prop roots, tidal water, the "lama" of *Da Lama ao Caos*.
4. **Maracatu percussion and pattern** — the alfaia drum with its rope-laced tensioning, drum foam/cloud bursts, and the psychedelic geometric patterns of maracatu dress.
5. **Mangueboy / Manguegirl fashion** — straw hat, dark sunglasses, printed or rainbow-striped shirt, animal prints, cord necklaces, rings; thrifted, inexpensive, anti-elitist mixing of traditional Pernambucan and modern pop items.
6. **Urban graffiti and murals** — the movement's symbols painted on walls and public space.
7. **Fusion montage and social critique** — old beside new, folk beside electronic; imagery of poverty, stilt-house riverbanks and ecological defence woven into otherwise festive frames.

A secondary register, visible in manifesto-era print ephemera though not named in the wiki prose: cordel-style woodcut black-and-white line, including hybrid creatures (crab-human figures) labelled with pseudo-scientific captions. Use it sparingly, as a print broadside voice.

### Materials and textures

Rope and rawhide drum lacing; woven straw; cheap cotton and thrifted synthetics; wet mud and mangrove root tangle; spray paint on concrete; welded and painted metal of street sculpture; ink on paper, newsprint and screen-print grain; hand-painted sign lettering. Surfaces read handmade and resourceful, never luxurious.

### Subjects

Crabs in any register (zoological, sculptural, cartoon, human gesture); drums and percussion; satellite dishes, cables, samplers, CRT-era tech; mangrove landscapes and riverfront promenades; portraits of mangueboys and manguegirls; muralists and wall paintings; stage scenes with projected imagery; folk-painterly skies with cumulus over red mud; poster emblems combining drum, cloud and lettering.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends toward the emblem: a single central object (drum, crab, dish) isolated on white with radial sunburst rays behind it, lettering embedded in foam or cloud shapes, a small stamp-like logo in a corner. Equally valid are the collage/montage logic (tech bolted onto organic), the leaning studio portrait with claw hands and a tilted hat against a plain bright ground, the full-bleed mural wall, and the folk-painterly scene with a high horizon of mud under a saturated sky. Lighting is even and tropical: flat studio or print light, hard midday sun with crisp shadows, or no modelled light at all in graphic work. Low-key, candlelit or noir lighting does not exist here.

### Typography

Bubble and cloud letters, hand-painted signage, woodcut capitals, manifesto broadside type; Portuguese words and band/album names ("Maracatu Atômico", "Manguetown") are welcome as design elements.

## Mood & Values

Defiant joy. The mood is groove-carried protest: carnival energy with a political spine, optimism that climbs "from the mud to the chaos" without denying the mud. Values encoded visually: self-expression, cultural resistance, pride in Northeastern Brazilian culture, ecological defence of the mangrove, artistic renewal, and an anti-elitist resourcefulness — dressed and designed with what is at hand, and proud of it.

## Era & Origins

Recife, Pernambuco, 1991, after Brazil's democratic opening; conceptualized by Chico Science, Fred Zero Quatro, Renato L, Mabuse and Héder Aragão. The 1992 manifesto "Caranguejos com Cérebro" supplied the core metaphors; Chico Science & Nação Zumbi's *Da Lama ao Caos* (1994) and *Afrociberdelia* fixed the sound-image; Chico Science's death in a car accident in February 1997 closed the first phase. The style reacts against regional cultural stagnation, poverty, and the perceived conservatism of earlier preservationist movements, answering them with an antenna in the mud.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- Fuse at least one traditional Northeastern element (alfaia drum, straw hat, maracatu stripe, woodcut line, crab) with one modern element (satellite dish, cable, sampler, spray can, projected image).
- Keep earth gravity: mud, roots, tide flat, or at least a folk object that implies them.
- Keep colour flat, saturated and print-like; let white carry the composition.
- Prefer emblem logic or montage logic over naturalistic scene-building.
- Give figures resourceful, thrifted styling and claw-like, playful body language.
- Light everything evenly or with hard sun; never with mood lighting.

Variation axes: subject may range from zoological crab to vector mascot to human portrait to mural to tide-flat landscape; composition from centered poster emblem to full-bleed mural to studio portrait to painterly scene; colour handling from the full five-colour flag to rainbow stripes to monochrome ink plus one hot accent to a hot gradient mascot; lighting from flat print light to midday sun; mood from festive defiance to satirical critique to tribute solemnity. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid:
- Rave nightgear: UV neon, lasers, glowsticks, acid-house smileys, warehouse darkness.
- Carnival glitter: sequins, feathers, floats, metallic body paint, night-parade floodlight.
- Hip-hop still-life as sole iconography: chrome wildstyle lettering, boomboxes, turntables without any mangue symbol.
- Funk gloss: gold chains, branded glossy sportswear, neon lycra, flash-party sheen.
- Free-floating psychedelia: melting forms, mandalas, kaleidoscopic symmetry unanchored by crab, drum, dish or mud.
- Muted minimalist palettes, beige interiors, Scandinavian calm.
- Chiaroscuro, fog, candlelight, vignettes.
- Generic tropical postcard: palms, cocktails, turquoise sea with no mud, no critique, no fusion.
- Contemporary smartphones and social-media UI; keep technology analog-1990s.
- Copying any known poster, monument or photograph composition outright.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Psychedelia:** Manguebeat pattern can be psychedelic, but its colour is maracatu flag-bright and flat, and every composition anchors in a regional symbol (crab, dish, drum, mud). Psychedelia dissolves form and lacks the earth anchor and the thrifted streetwear.
- **Vs Brazilian Carnival:** Carnival is sequin, feather, glitter and night floodlight; Manguebeat is daytime, mud, rope, straw and cheap cotton, and its joy carries explicit social critique rather than pure spectacle.
- **Vs Funk:** Baile funk's visual world is glossy, branded, gold-chained, neon-synthetic and post-2000; Manguebeat is 1990s, organic, print-textured, with acoustic percussion visibly present.
- **Vs Hip-Hop:** Hip-hop contributes attitude and beat but not iconography; swap boomboxes and chrome lettering for alfaia drums, bubble-cloud type and mangrove fauna, and you have Manguebeat.
- **Vs Rave:** Rave is nocturnal, neon, laser-lit and warehouse-bound; Manguebeat's electronic side appears as a parabolic dish or sampler grafted onto mud in broad daylight, never as glowstick night.

**Quick recognition checklist:** flat maracatu primaries on white or mud; crab or claw gesture; dish/antenna in earth; rope-laced drum; straw hat plus dark sunglasses; sunburst rays and bubble letters; handmade print or mural texture; defiant, sunlit joy with a critical undertow.
