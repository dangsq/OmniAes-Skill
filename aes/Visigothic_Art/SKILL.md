# Visigothic Art

Visigothic Art is the artistic production of the Visigothic Kingdom, the East Germanic state planted in modern Spain, southern France and southern Portugal between 418 and 711 AD, with its creative center at Toledo after 554. It sits inside Pre-Romanesque art and is a synthesis rather than a pure tradition: Germanic animal-energy and cloisonné metalcraft fused with Late Roman, Paleo-Christian, Byzantine, Coptic, Syriac and Sassanid currents, all redirected toward the Church after the conversion to Catholicism in 589. Surviving output concentrates in four media — goldsmithing, masonry architecture, architectural bas-relief, and (rarely) mural painting — so the style reads today as a culture of buried treasure, weathered stone and fragmentary plaster: lavish votive objects preserved pristine in hoards, beside buildings and paintings that survive as ruins and ghosts. Religion is the style's governing value; nearly everything commissioned that survived was made for or by the Church.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
The palette is a **triad of gold, red and blue laid over neutral mineral carriers**. Gold runs from deep honey bronze to pale leaf-gold (#B8860B → #E6C35C). Red is the translucent ember of garnet and carnelian in cloisonné cells (#7E241B → #C05A33). Blue is sapphire and lapis, deep and cool (#1E3A8A → #3F5FA5). These jewel tones sit on carriers: pearl and bone white (#EDE6D6), limestone and ashlar grey (#A9A396 → #C6C0B0), oxidized bronze and iron brown (#5E4630), and — in painting — a pale verdigris/celadon plaster ground (#9FB6A4) drawn over with ochre-red line (#9A5B33). Saturation is bimodal and both poles are correct: fully saturated jewel color on metalwork, and desaturated, chalky mineral pastels on fresco and weathered stone. Never neon, never synthetic.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how strongly the tradition documents them)
1. **Cloisonné treasure-work.** Votive crowns and crosses (the Treasure of Guarrazar) hung from chains with dangling pendilia of pearls and letters; gold openwork bands set with sapphires framed by pearls; eagle-shaped fibulae in mirrored pairs; bronze and silver belt buckles with openwork geometric plates. The cloisonné grammar: a metal honeycomb of small compartments packed with garnet, glass and shell, producing an all-over mosaic of red cells edged in bright gold wire.
2. **The compact basilica.** High-quality ashlar masonry, short and wide elevation, basilican plan with three naves, stone vaulting, a portico, small punched openings, and the **horseshoe arch** as the defining silhouette; decorative friezes running over entrance and triumphal arches and apses.
3. **Bevel-carved relief (técnica de bisel).** Shallow two-plane bas-relief on capitals, friezes and chancel screens: chi-rho monograms in circles, crosses, rosettes, lozenges, vine scrolls, zoomorphic and plant motifs; historiated capitals with biblical scenes (Daniel in the lions' den, the sacrifice of Isaac). No freestanding sculpture in the round.
4. **Faded expressive fresco.** Register compositions of haloed figures drawn in ochre-red outline over pale green-grey plaster, separated by painted bands and lozenge borders; Late Roman inheritance handled with a rougher, more expressive linear hand; surfaces abraded with losses to the plaster.
5. **Minor media, thin evidence.** Illuminated manuscript miniatures (the Ashburnham Pentateuch, attribution contested), simple ceramics with geometric decoration, and textiles known mostly from inference. Treat these as peripheral; do not build a style definition on them.

### Materials and textures
Gold sheet and filigree; garnet, carnelian, sapphire, pearl and colored glass inlays; cast bronze and iron with dark patina; fine-cut limestone and granite ashlar; carved marble and limestone plaque with crisp bevel edges; powdery matte plaster and flaking pigment. Texture contrast is essential: glassy translucency of inlay against chalky stone and dusty paint.

### Composition and lighting tendencies
Heraldic frontality and pairing: fibulae and eagles appear as mirrored symmetrical pairs; crowns as centered radial objects with hanging chains; churches as low horizontal masses against open sky; interiors as colonnade rhythms where warm light rakes across carved capitals and vaults; reliefs as centered monograms within fragmentary plaques; frescoes as horizontal registers bounded by ornamental bands. Three lighting regimes belong to the style: even diffuse cabinet light that makes cloisonné cells glow from within; dim, warm, directional light in crypts and naves grazing stone carving; flat overcast or soft Mediterranean daylight on plain masonry walls. Painting is always matte, never specular.

## Mood & Values
Votive solemnity: treasure made to be suspended before an altar, not worn. The style expresses devotion through costly material — gold, garnet, sapphire — offered to a Church that had just become the kingdom's own. There is a dual temper: the triumphant glitter of buried crowns, and the melancholy of ruin — abraded plaster, fragmented plaques, churches standing bare in fields. Beneath both runs a synthesis anxiety resolved into order: Germanic zoomorphic vigor disciplined into Mediterranean symmetry, geometry and register.

## Era & Origins
Fifth to eighth centuries, from the Toulouse kingdom (418) through the Toledo kingdom (554–711), ending with the Umayyad invasion. It reacts to and absorbs the provincial Late Antique world: Roman masonry and fresco technique, Paleo-Christian iconography, Byzantine and Eastern (Coptic, Syriac, Sassanid) ornament, filtered through Germanic metalcraft. It is the Iberian branch of Pre-Romanesque art and the ancestor of Mozarabic and Asturian traditions.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Choose the medium first: cloisonné metalwork, masonry church, bevel relief, or faded fresco. Each carries its own ornament grammar (cell-mosaic, plain wall plus horseshoe arch, shallow two-plane carving, linear outline with wash).
- Build color from the gold–red–blue triad on a neutral mineral carrier; pick one saturation pole (jewel-saturated or mineral-faded) and hold it.
- Favor symmetry, pairing and heraldic frontality over dynamic diagonals.
- Load religious signifiers: crosses, chi-rho monograms, votive suspension chains, haloed figures, biblical scenes confined to capitals and friezes.
- Apply age logic consistently: metal may be pristine (buried hoard) or patinated; stone is weathered; plaster is abraded with losses.
- Light subdued and warm, or diffuse and even; let inlay glow translucently and stone grain catch raking light.

**Variation axes.** Subject may range from a macro of a single cloisonné cell to a full church elevation, a crypt interior, a relief fragment, a fresco register, or a simple geometric ceramic. Composition may be mirrored pair, centered radial treasure, fragment crop, frontal elevation, or interior vista. Lighting may be cabinet-diffuse, candle-warm raking, or overcast flat. Color handling may span saturated garnet-and-sapphire to near-monochrome limestone and verdigris. Mood may run from triumphant votive gold to elegiac ruin. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image** — no reference composition is canonical.

**Avoid**
- Pointed arches, rib vaults, flying buttresses, rose windows, spires, stained-glass walls (Gothic).
- Deeply undercut sculpted tympanum portals and full narrative portal programs (mature Romanesque).
- Gold-ground mosaic tesserae, domed central plans, enamel icon panels (Byzantine).
- Classical naturalism: contrapposto, idealized anatomy, freestanding marble statuary (Hellenic); Visigothic figure work is linear, flat and expressive, and its sculpture is relief only.
- Heraldry, knights, half-timber, tracery (generic later Medieval).
- Pristine, saturated, complete mural cycles or glossy finishes; Visigothic painting survives fragmentary and matte.
- Glazed tilework, arabesque and calligraphy (later Islamic/Mudéjar Iberia); the horseshoe arch may appear, but never with alfiz frames or tile panels.
- Modern Goth or whimsigoth subculture trimmings (black lace, bats, occult props) — unrelated despite the name.
- Anachronistic neon or synthetic color, plastic or polished modern materials.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Visigothic |
|---|---|
| Byzantine | Byzantine gold is a *background* (mosaic ground, icon field); Visigothic gold is an *object* (crown, fibula, buckle). Domes and pendentives vs. compact vaulted basilicas; hieratic enamel polish vs. rustic cloisonné cells and bevel-cut stone. |
| Hellenic | Naturalistic anatomy and marble idealism vs. abstracted zoomorphic interlace, garnet inlay and flat linear figures. |
| Medieval (generic) | No heraldry, no chivalric content, no Gothic structure; Visigothic is Pre-Romanesque, earlier and liturgical-metallic. |
| Romanesque | Closest neighbor: shares historiated capitals and round arches. Separate by the horseshoe arch, the shallow bisel bevel cut vs. deep rounded undercutting, short wide elevations vs. taller massing, and faded sparse fresco vs. saturated complete cycles. |
| Southern French Gothic | Verticality, pointed arch and luminous glass vs. horizontal masonry mass, tiny openings, and light held in metal and stone rather than windows. |

Extra caution: the horseshoe arch recurs in later Mozarabic and Islamic Iberian building; date it Visigothic only in company with plain ashlar, basilican plans, bevel-relief friezes and cloisonné treasure culture — never with tilework or calligraphy. Evidence for painting, textiles and ceramics is genuinely thin; when generating those media, keep them simple, geometric and fragmentary rather than inventing rich cycles.
