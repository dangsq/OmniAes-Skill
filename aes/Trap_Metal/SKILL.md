# Trap Metal

Trap Metal is what happens when SoundCloud rap decides to scream. Born in the mid-to-late 2010s American underground — Florida especially — out of artists like Bones, XXXTentacion, Scarlxrd, Ghostemane and Lil Darkie, it welds trap's 808 skeleton to nu-metal and industrial noise, and its imagery behaves the same way: hip-hop's streetwear body dressed in punk hardware, goth occultism and horrorcore gore, then run through a photocopier until it clips. The aesthetic is deliberately anti-polished. Blown-out flash, scratched surfaces, jagged linework and obscured faces are not accidents; they are the visual equivalent of a distorted 808, a statement of anti-mainstream sentiment built on aggression, nihilism, trauma processing and shock value. Everything below describes rules, not recipes: the style lives in a range, and a correct image can sit anywhere inside it without echoing any particular existing artwork.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (the rule, not a swatch)
Every frame is built on a three-value spine: **near-black** (`#000000`–`#1a1a1a`) as the dominant field; **bone/paper white** (`#e8e4dc`–`#f5f5f5`) as the stark contrast value; and **red** spanning from dried, muted brick (`#8a2b1f`, `#a63c2e`) to arterial bright (`#d40000`–`#ff1a1a`). Between these sit muted, desaturated intermediates — grey-beige domestic walls, washed-out skin, faded denim — which carry the snapshot side of the style. **Neon accents** (magenta `#ff00c8`, acid yellow `#ffe600`, cyan `#00e5ff`, violet `#b400ff`) are permitted only as incidents: a gel wash, a motion-blur streak, one garment, one poster. Never as a field color, never more than two at once; most images use one or none. Red-black duotone and high-contrast black-white are both canonical treatments of photographic source material.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the tradition documents them
1. **The obscured face.** Masks and heavy makeup that hide identity: painted hockey masks, ski masks and balaclavas (sometimes cut with ears or horns), clown masks, corpse-paint-style makeup; or obscuration by motion blur, shadow or collage. This is the style's first reflex.
2. **Occult and anti-signs.** Inverted crosses, scratched pentagram-like marks, anarchy A's, Soviet ushankas borrowed as superficial rebellion signifiers, horns collaged onto a shaved head. Satanic and anarchist symbolism used as spectacle, not belief.
3. **Horror gore.** Dripping blood, exaggerated death grins, screaming mouths ringed with fangs, severed-head props, realistic gore applied to cartoonish or clay bodies.
4. **The high-contrast striped long-sleeve.** Black/red or black/white horizontal stripes, a grunge-and-punk homage worn as a scene staple.
5. **Distressed typography as texture.** Illegible blackletter and metal-band-style logos on hoodies and covers, scratchy hand-scrawled tags, dripping crude lettering, repetitive scratched symbols over flat color fields.
6. **Body hardware.** Heavy chains, spiked collars, piercings, face tattoos, liberty spikes, dyed locs, bleached buzzcuts — the "anti-icon" grooming kit.
7. **Techwear over streetwear.** Utility vests, cargo straps, functional gear, distressed or torn black cotton, combat boots and creepers.
8. **Anime/manga shorthand.** Recognizable internet-culture references, typically degraded, scribbled over or collaged rather than presented clean.
9. **DIY clay figures.** Crudely molded sculpted characters combining realistic gore with cartoonish expressions — a documented 2019–2022 profile-picture micro-trend that adds a tactile, handmade layer.
10. **Tobacco and consumption props.** Cigarette packs and brand signage used as rebellious or ironic props, part of the scene's contradictory posturing.

### Materials and textures
Xerox grain and clipped highlights; on-camera flash blowout with black falloff; motion blur and scanline-style digital damage; scratched, engraved-looking marks bitten into flat color; torn and distressed fabric; matte clay fingerprints; heavy chain metal; poster-plastered walls; photocopied zine contrast. Nothing should look retouched, rendered or airbrushed.

### Recurring subjects
Masked or tattooed solo performers; shirtless flexing figures in balaclavas and chains; duos and collectives slouched in domestic rooms or on streets; screaming creature or animal mouths as cover icons; deformed illustrated figures with disjointed limbs and death grins; clay sculpture portraits; still arrangements of scene gear (masks, chains, striped shirts, boots).

### Composition and lighting
Two grammars coexist. **Snapshot:** harsh direct flash in dark bedrooms and beige interiors, off-center or fisheye framing, cluttered backgrounds, cropped limbs, full-body fashion shots against bare walls. **Cover icon:** centered head or screaming mouth treated as a bootleg poster — flat red or black field, duotone contrast, collage layers of horns, tags and scratches breaking the frame. Illustration follows the same chaos: jagged scratchy linework, rushed and deformed figures, compositions packed with repetitive blood drips and graffiti. Lighting ranges from flat frontal flash to flat white studio light for stark black-white, red gel washes, and neon club light smeared by motion blur.

## Mood & Values
Rage as therapy. The persona — the masked alter-ego — is presented as the honest face of internal turmoil: aggression, nihilism, rebellion, emotional turmoil, shock value, anti-mainstream contempt. Yet the style keeps a manic, almost clownish undercut: gore paired with cartoon grins, a masked figure offering flowers, absurd clay creatures. Menace and joke are the same gesture. DIY ugliness is integrity; polish is selling out.

## Era & Origins
Seeded mid-2010s in the US SoundCloud scene, broken out in early 2017, consolidated late-2010s through collectives and platforms (SoundCloud, YouTube, Discord), with a clay-figure micro-trend running 2019–2022 and mainstream decline by roughly 2022 as Rage music absorbed its audience. It reacts against the clean conventions of 2010s mainstream rap and against hip-hop's luxury imagery, borrowing instead from goth, industrial, metalhead, punk and horrorcore subcultures.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Black first, red second, neon last — and neon only as an incident.
2. Obscure or damage the face: mask, makeup, blur, collage, crop.
3. Degrade the signal: flash blowout, grain, clipping, motion blur, scratched marks. Polish breaks the style.
4. Let typography bleed: illegible blackletter, scrawled tags, dripping letters as surface texture.
5. Dress the body in punk × streetwear × techwear: stripes, chains, straps, torn black cotton, combat boots.
6. Compose chaotically (tilt, fisheye, clutter, crop) or as a centered bootleg icon — never as balanced editorial design.
7. Pair gore with a wink: shock is spectacle and persona, often undercut by absurd tenderness or cartoonishness.
8. Keep it tactile and DIY: clay, xerox, collage, snapshot — never vector-clean.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be any of: masked performer portrait, tattooed figure, collective snapshot, screaming creature cover, deformed illustration, clay sculpture, gear still life, anime-inflected collage, or an interior/street scene carrying the wardrobe and palette. Composition may run from centered icon to candid clutter to full-body fashion frame. Lighting may be direct flash, flat studio white, red gel, duotone, or neon smear. Color handling may be strict black/white/red, muted snapshot with a single red object, or neon-accented distortion. Medium may be photograph, digital collage, scratchy illustration, xerox scan or clay photography. Mood may be pure rage, nihilistic stillness, grief, or manic humor. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Clean commercial studio gloss, softbox gradients, beauty retouching; pastel palettes, cozy bedroom warmth, vaporwave grids, sunset gradients; drift cars, cowbells and Memphis-cassette night-street imagery; icy diamond bling and sincere luxury display (chains yes, sparkle-no); pristine un-degraded anime key visuals; symmetrical minimal layouts and corporate sans-serif type; golden-hour naturalism and shallow bokeh; unironic wholesome joy and clean-cut grooming; more than two neon accents or full-spectrum color; glossy high-fidelity tactical military rendering (that clean saturation belongs to Necrotrap, a divergent offshoot).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Drift Phonk** lives outdoors at night: JDM cars mid-drift, cowbell-Memphis cassette lore, purple-teal neon, chrome type, cleaner graphic design. Trap Metal keeps the camera on the masked body indoors, in red-black, with gore and flash grain instead of taillights.
- **E-Boy** is soft TikTok bedroom style: curtain haircuts, layered thrift wear, candy or pastel saturation, playful poses, chains as cute jewelry. No masks, no gore, no clipping; its light is ring-light soft, never flash-blown.
- **Emo Rap** shares face tattoos and SoundCloud DNA but mourns instead of rages: lilac-blue-pink skies, clouds, roses, guitars, tear-gloss. Swap the lilac for arterial red and the sigh for a scream and you have crossed into Trap Metal.
- **Mainstream Hip-Hop** celebrates legible wealth: clean lighting, unmasked confident faces, readable brand logos. Trap Metal inverts every one — illegible logos, hidden faces, distressed fabric, anti-polish as virtue.
- **Horrorcore** is the older cousin: boom-bap-era cartoon Halloween gore, clown face paint as character costume, comic-book illustration. Trap Metal separates via metal/industrial typography, streetwear-techwear wardrobe, digital clipping and flash-snapshot grammar, anime shorthand, and the DIY clay-figure layer; its gore is persona and texture, not narrative costume.
- Near-miss note: the occult symbols can resemble **Witch House**, but Witch House is cold, slow and geometric — purple-grey glitch and anonymous type — where Trap Metal is hot, red, loud and bodily.

**Quick check:** a flash-lit masked figure in a black hoodie with an unreadable metal logo; a red-black cover of a fanged screaming mouth; a scratchy deformed drawing dripping blood; a crude clay figure with cartoon eyes and realistic gore — any of these, carrying black-red-muted palette and deliberate degradation, is Trap Metal.
