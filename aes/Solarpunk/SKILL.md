# Solarpunk

Solarpunk is a speculative-design and fiction aesthetic that imagines a post-scarcity, post-carbon future in which humanity behaves as one organism inside nature rather than its owner. It is grassroots in origin and temperament — manifestos, zines, community gardens, indie games — and its "punk" is a rejection of extractive capitalism and climate doomerism rather than an affection for leather and neon. Visually it fuses three inheritances: the vegetated, organic architecture of visionaries like Luc Schuiten; the sweeping, ethically-tinged landscapes of Miyazaki-era animation, with distant cities embedded in living terrain; and the craft ornament of Art Nouveau. The result is an imagery of abundance under maintenance: gardens on every ledge, energy hardware worn openly on the building skin, and people gathered in the commons they tend.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
Four families carry the style: **green, blue, yellow, white**. Green holds the largest surface area in almost any correct image, running from deep canopy teal-green (#14555A, #1C3F2A) through mid leaf (#2F5D3A, #4C8C4A) to sunlit chartreuse and sage (#8FBC6D, #A3C9A8). Blue appears as sky and as water: pale cerulean to turquoise (#7EC8E3, #40B5AD, #2E8B8B). Yellow is the sun itself and its echoes — warm gold light, ochre plaster, harvest tones (#F4D06F, #D9A441, #C98F2D). White and warm off-white carry the built fabric: plaster, membrane shells, lattice, paper (#F7F3E8, #EDE8DA, #E3DCC8). Earthy timber and terracotta (#8B5E3C, #A9744F, #C46A4A) ground the frame, with small floral accents permitted. Saturation is daylight-bright but never neon; shadows are warm or teal-tinted, never black; value range stays high-key even at dusk.

### Signature motifs, ranked by canonical weight
1. **Vegetation fused to architecture** — vertical gardens, green roofs, vine-draped balconies and balustrades, trees growing from terraces, facades half-disappeared into foliage. This is the style's first signature.
2. **Visible renewable energy** — solar panels (canonically ornamental: stained-glass photovoltaics), small wind turbines, rainwater catchment cisterns and gutters, micro-grid hardware. Technology is displayed on the building skin, never hidden.
3. **Art Nouveau lineage** — whiplash curves, organic structural logic, timber or iron lattice that behaves like stems and ribs, flowing silhouettes in architecture and clothing alike.
4. **Community life at human scale** — shared terraces, rooftop and courtyard gardens, edible landscapes, market stalls, repair cafes, maker spaces; figures gardening, cooking, cycling, conversing in groups of mixed age and appearance.
5. **Water as living infrastructure** — canals, cascades, catchment ponds, coastal edges, visible irrigation runnels.
6. **Greenhouse and dome volumes** — geodesic domes, skylit atriums, winter gardens, clerestory glazing flooding interiors with diffuse light.
7. **Cluttered, hand-touched detail** — potted plants on every sill, patchwork and visible mending, upcycled furniture, drying laundry, tools, chalk signs; "cluttered visuals" is a canonical motif, and it is what separates lived-in solarpunk from a showroom.
8. **African and Asian patterns** — textually canonical in textiles, tilework and screens, though under-represented in surviving imagery; treat as a high-value accent, not a requirement.
9. **Soft mobility (image-observed, text-silent)** — lighter-than-air craft, sailboats, trams and bicycles recur in illustrative work and fit the low-energy ethos, but the written canon does not name them; optional.

### Materials and textures
Timber, bamboo, rattan, rammed earth and adobe, hand-troweled plaster, woven and mended textiles, recycled metal, ceramic tile, greenhouse glazing, and living substrate: soil, moss, bark, leaf matte. Concrete appears only as an old substrate being reclaimed by vines. Expect wood grain, crumbly soil, condensation on glass, water sheen, fiber weave, and — in painted work — a paper or gouache tooth.

### Recurring subjects
Green-wrapped towers and low courtyards; atrium interiors with waterfalls and canopy trees; coastal and river settlements; farm terraces and orchards threaded between buildings; street markets and workshops; fashion portraits in loose layers, cloaks, overalls and aprons with patchwork and mending; sprawling landscapes with a distant city on the horizon.

### Composition and light
Favored framings: an elevated vista over a mosaic of canopy and roofs; eye-level street or terrace with cluttered foreground; interior looking up into a flooded skylight; aerial mosaic of gardens and water; the vertical green tower against open sky. Figures are small-to-medium and embedded in activity, never isolated heroes. Depth comes from layered foliage planes. Light is the protagonist: direct sun, golden-hour rake, dappled shade under canopy, greenhouse diffusion, warm haze. Night is rare; when present it is lantern-warm or luminous blue-hour with lit windows, never noir.

### Medium range
The style tolerates many hands: hand-painted gouache or watercolor concept illustration, clean digital painting, architectural visualization renders, physical scale-model photography, diagrammatic presentation boards, and straight photography of real vegetated buildings. Medium is a free axis; the rules above are not.

## Mood & Values

Radical hope with dirt under its fingernails. Solarpunk expresses inclusion, mutual aid, environmental regeneration, local craft over corporate production, and renewable energy as civic pride. Its mood is generous, busy, optimistic and *worked* — a utopia under maintenance, full of use-wear and care, refusing both doom and gloss. Conflict, where shown, is the constructive kind: organizing, building, repairing, planting.

## Era & Origins

Coined in 2008 in the blog essay "From Steampunk to Solarpunk," rooted in a Brazilian speculative-fiction community of the late 2000s; gained traction around 2014 through concept art, and coherence through the 2019 solarpunk manifesto and adjacent design-writing. Its visual tone was prefigured by Miyazaki's films and by vegetated-architecture visionaries; a 2021 commercial film later mainstreamed the look. The style reacts against cyberpunk nihilism, steampunk nostalgia, greenwashed corporate branding, and climate despair.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Answer two questions visibly in every frame: where does the energy come from, and where does the food grow?
- Lived-in over showroom: add mending, pots, tools, laundry, signage, wear.
- Keep human scale: even towers show reachable terraces, stairs, hand-height beds; include people in community.
- Ornament is ethics: curves, patterns and stained-glass photovoltaics read as craft, never as branding.
- Let daylight drive the palette; keep shadows colored and soft.
- Technology appears as gentle furniture — panels, turbines, cisterns — integrated, not dominant.

**Variation axes.** Subject: city vista, atrium interior, market, farm terrace, coastal settlement, fashion portrait, repair-workshop close-up, transit scene. Composition: vista, eye-level, interior upward, aerial mosaic, portrait. Light: noon, golden hour, greenhouse diffusion, luminous dusk. Color handling: painterly pastel, saturated illustration, photoreal render, near-monochrome model study with green accents. Mood: quiet domestic, festive communal, laboring, contemplative. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Neon signage and rain-slick night noir; chrome-and-glass corporate towers with a token rooftop garden (the greenwash tell); glossy stock gradients with leaf icons, Earth globes and bubbles; ruins, smog, dust and scavenged survival gear; laboratory vats, spliced flesh and wet biotech; vector-clean retro-future leisure with no plants and no politics; lone armored heroes; mass-produced minimalism; logos and branding; desaturated palettes and black shadows; brass-gear steampunk; weaponry.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Solarpunk's counter-tell |
|---|---|---|
| Frutiger Eco | Glossy 2D stock gradients, symbolic leaves/globes/bubbles, corporate cleanliness, empty or token people | Lived-in clutter, soil and use-wear, real gathered communities, hand-touched medium |
| Afrofuturism | Black diasporic cosmic futurism, metallics, purple-gold-neon, ancestral-cosmic iconography | Ecology-first green/white/yellow/blue in daylight; horticulture rather than cosmos; diasporic patterns as accent, not center |
| Avant Apocalypse | Post-collapse ruin, dust, scavenged gear, desaturated survivalism | Thriving maintenance and abundance in saturated daylight; ruins appear only as substrate reclaimed by vines and community |
| Biopunk | Wet labs, vats, engineered flesh, corporate-dark biotechnology | Biology lives in soil, canopy and garden beds, not tanks; tone hopeful, not visceral |
| Cyberprep | 1980s corporate optimism, chrome and vector pastels, consumer leisure, apolitical sleekness | Anti-consumerist craft and repair culture, vegetation as structure, explicit politics of the commons |

**Quick recognition checklist:** dominant greens plus plaster white; sun as light source and as hardware; plants growing *from* the building; people in groups, working or gathering; clutter of care; curves and craft ornament; daylight everywhere.
