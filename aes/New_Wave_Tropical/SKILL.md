# New Wave Tropical

New Wave Tropical is the look of a vacation that never existed, sold everywhere between the mid-1980s and mid-1990s: rainforest and beach imagery boiled down to irregular, chunky cut-out silhouettes, then splashed across resort canopies, soda bottles, aprons, mugs, album covers and textile posters at maximum saturation. It blends high-energy tropical flora and fauna with motifs borrowed from African, Caribbean and Latin visual cultures, filtered through a Henri Matisse cut-out revival and a freeform, retro-punk willingness to let shapes collide. The result balances two registers at once — an upscale, relaxed resort mood and a loud, playful, nostalgic kitsch — and holds them together with bold, clashing color rather than with restraint. Though the style peaked in the 80s–90s commercial world (fast-food and beverage branding, resort identity programs, new-wave record packaging), its origin reaches back to the late 1970s, and the term itself is a retrospective coinage by Evan Collins.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is electric, high-contrast saturation with deliberate complementary clashes; the infobox core is green, orange, red, yellow and blue, extended in practice by the style's signature turquoise, hot pink, sun-washed yellow and lime green.

- **Core electrics:** hot pink / magenta `#FF2E88`–`#F64C8F`, turquoise / teal `#00A8A8`–`#17BEBB`, lime green `#7ED957`–`#A4D65E`, sun yellow `#FFD23F`–`#F9E547`, tangerine orange `#FF7A29`, signal red `#D62828`, cobalt / royal blue `#1F5FA8`–`#2653A3`.
- **Secondary accents:** violet / orchid `#7B4FA8`, coral, leaf green.
- **Grounds:** paper white `#F6F1E7` or flat black `#0E0E0E`, both used to make cutouts pop; or no ground at all in allover maximalist patterns.
- **Sun-washed variant:** pastel counterparts — mint `#BFE8DC`, pale pink `#F4A9C0`, lavender `#C9A6E0`, washed yellow `#F7E8A4` — appear as a bleached, tie-dye-softened handling of the same hues, never as an earthy or beige desaturation.

Palette handling rule: pick two or three clashing electrics as dominants (pink against lime, turquoise against sun yellow), support them with the remaining core hues, and keep every fill flat and uniform. Saturation may be dialed down only toward the sun-washed pastel end, never toward mud.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Tropical flora as cutouts** — the style's key motif is tropical imagery itself, above all rainforest foliage: palm fronds, monstera-type leaves, and irregular, chunky, abstract botanical silhouettes in construction-paper style. These are the style's backbone; almost every artifact carries them.
2. **Resort and beach leisure apparatus** — domed canopies, striped canvas awnings, patio umbrellas, painted metal palm-tree sculptures, signage letters arched over an entrance. The "kitsch & leisure mix" puts vacation infrastructure on par with the foliage.
3. **African, Caribbean and Latin cultural motifs** — folk-geometric zigzag and diamond borders, patchwork panel grids, stylized patterned animals (elephants, giraffes), naive line-drawn faces, script and hand-lettered logotypes treating place names and brand names as decorative objects.
4. **Rainforest fauna** — toucans, parrots, songbirds, butterflies, rendered as flat layered silhouettes with patterned bodies rather than realistic plumage.
5. **Retro-punk freeform marks** — scribbled hand-drawn frames, dashed and leaf-shaped border repeats, sun and ship-wheel doodles, childlike linework overlapping polished shapes.

### Materials, textures and medium

The style lives across applied arts: screen-printed cotton apparel and aprons with allover prints; glazed ceramic mugs carrying cutout birds on black; painted metal cutout sculpture (palm trees, canopy edges, sign letters); striped awning canvas; paper and laminate packaging for bottles and boxes; textile posters and patchwork wall hangings; promo posters collaging photography inside drawn frames. The unifying texture is *flatness*: gouache-or-construction-paper opacity, hard scissored edges, no internal gradient or modeled shading. Where pattern density is high, the surface reads as silkscreen layering — slight misregistration and overprint energy are welcome, glossy 3D rendering is not.

### Recurring subjects

Brand and place identity programs (resort names, city names, beverage labels), album and promo artwork for new-wave-era acts, restaurant exteriors and interiors, apparel and home textiles, tableware, and standalone cutout illustrations on plain grounds. Human figures, when present, appear either as stylized naive line drawings or as photographed models framed by drawn cutout borders — the people are guests in the pattern, not its subject.

### Composition and lighting

Composition follows cutout logic: shapes overlap in flat planes with depth implied only by layering, never by perspective or shadow. Four recurring layouts coexist and all are correct: (a) the **isolated vignette** — a small cluster of cutout animals and plants centered on a large white or black field; (b) the **allover maximalist repeat** — foliage, blooms and fauna packed edge to edge with no negative space and no single focal point; (c) the **framed border** — a central image or photo enclosed by a band of repeating leaves, dashes or zigzags; (d) the **patchwork grid** — quilt-like panels, each carrying a different pattern or motif block. Graphic work uses uniform, shadowless light; photographic work uses bright, slightly overexposed midday sun for exteriors (sun-bleached daylight flattening color toward pastel at the edges), warm incandescent glow for saturated interiors, and hard direct flash for promo-style shots of models. The written record is thin on photography and typography; those cues are carried by the surviving artifact corpus rather than by the defining text.

## Mood & Values

Playful abundance, leisure as identity, and kitsch embraced without apology. New Wave Tropical sells the feeling of being on holiday in a world assembled from paper scissors: optimistic, cosmopolitan in a collage-naive way, generous to the point of chaos. It values energy over elegance, collision over harmony, and nostalgia over minimalism — a resort lobby's calm holding a carnival of color inside its frame.

## Era & Origins

Emerging in the late 1970s and prevalent from the mid-1980s to mid-1990s, the style grows from three roots named in the defining text: the Matisse cut-out revival, freeform Pacific punk experimentation, and a retro art-revival impulse. It flourished in commercial channels — beverage and fast-food branding, resort identity, record packaging — alongside new-wave and world-pop acts whose sensibility it mirrors (The B-52s, Culture Club, Oingo Boingo, Bow Wow Wow, General Public). Its retrospective naming by Evan Collins marks it as a recognized period style rather than a self-declared movement.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Cutout first.** Every organic form should read as scissors-cut construction paper: flat uniform fill, hard irregular contour, no gradient, no soft internal shading.
2. **Clash on purpose.** Pair complementary electrics at full saturation (hot pink vs lime, turquoise vs sun yellow); ground them on white, on black, or on nothing at all in an allover print.
3. **Keep the kitsch–leisure balance.** One register of upscale resort calm (canopy, awning, umbrella, elegant script logotype) plus one register of loud playful noise (scribble borders, clashing pattern panels) in the same frame.
4. **Motif ranking holds.** Flora cutouts lead; resort apparatus and folk-geometric borders support; fauna and naive doodles accent.
5. **Flat light for graphics, sun-bleached or warm light for photos.** Never model the cutouts with rendered shadow.
6. **Type is a motif.** Script logotypes, spaced serif capitals, and hand-drawn lettering sit inside the composition as shapes, often arched or framed by leaves.
7. **Maximalism is correct.** Edge-to-edge pattern with zero negative space is as valid as a lone vignette on white.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range across apparel, packaging, signage, murals, restaurant interiors and exteriors, ceramics, textile posters, album covers, or pure illustration. Composition may range across the four layouts above (vignette, allover, framed border, patchwork grid) plus photo-inside-drawn-frame collage. Color handling may range from electric-on-black to electric-on-white to sun-washed pastel to multi-hue allover. Lighting may range from shadowless graphic flatness to bleaching midday sun to warm interior glow to hard flash. Mood may range from serene resort to chaotic punk-playful. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Gradients, glossy 3D rendering, soft drop shadows inside cutout shapes.
- Photorealistic botanical or wildlife depiction; realistic plumage or leaf venation.
- Earthy 1970s desaturation (avocado, harvest gold, brown, wicker-toned beige) — that belongs to Global Village Coffeehouse.
- Non-representational geometric systems: terrazzo, confetti squiggles, laminate grids without tropical content — that is Memphis Design.
- Art Deco symmetry, metallic leaf, lacquered elegance, muted jade-and-cream palettes — that is Tropideco.
- Xerox grit, photocopied collage, abstract neon squiggle fields with no flora or fauna — that is Pacific Punk Wave in its raw mode.
- Modern minimalist vector flat design: thin outlines, generous negative space, two-color palettes, line-art monstera on beige.
- Watercolor softness, blended edges, airbrushed fades.
- Documentary-style jungle photography or contemporary tropical-boho interiors.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Pacific Punk Wave:** both share freeform punk energy and neon brightness, but Pacific Punk Wave stays grittier and more abstract — xerographic texture, surf-skate zine chaos, neon squiggles on black — while New Wave Tropical keeps representational botanical and zoological cutouts, folk-cultural motifs and a brand-grade resort polish.
- **Vs Global Village Coffeehouse:** the coffeehouse look is 1970s handcraft — wicker, macramé, earthy browns and harvest tones, quiet folk authenticity. New Wave Tropical replaces earth with electricity: synthetic saturation, commercial finish, loud pattern.
- **Vs Memphis Design:** same era and same fearlessness with color, but Memphis is non-representational geometry — terrazzo, squiggles, asymmetric furniture, laminate surfaces. New Wave Tropical always depicts something tropical: leaves, birds, blooms, suns, animals in folk pattern.
- **Vs Tropideco:** Tropideco is interwar elegance — streamlined symmetry, gold and lacquer, restrained jade and coral. New Wave Tropical is its unruly descendant: irregular chunky contours, clashing electrics, kitsch playfulness instead of deco poise.
