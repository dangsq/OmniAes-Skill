# Cagole

Cagole names the stereotype — and, reclaimed, the badge — of the "excessive" young woman of Marseille and the wider Provence: loud, extroverted, hyperfeminine, unapologetically sexual, dressed in a festival of color, chrome and animal print. The look is a southern French inflection of 2000s bimbo/McBling fashion, but it carries a regional soul: it is tied to Provençal and Occitan pride, to pageant-parody culture (Miss Cagole), and to a working-class, often immigrant-descended Marseille history that northern French "good taste" has always mocked. Visually, cagole is excess used as grammar: nothing is tonal, nothing is understated, and the snapshot — flash-blasted, laughing, squeezed together with friends — is its native medium.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The palette runs on three anchors plus clashing support tones. At least one anchor should dominate a frame; anchors are meant to collide with each other and with animal print, never to harmonize quietly.

| Role | Range | Representative hexes |
|---|---|---|
| Anchor: light purplish pink / mallow | lilac-rose to orchid | #E8B7D4, #C8A2C8, #B76E9B |
| Anchor: deep red | cherry to burgundy | #A31621, #8E1B2E, #6E0F1A |
| Anchor: hot magenta accent | electric pink | #E0218A, #FF2D95 |
| Support: animal-print neutrals | leopard tan, zebra cream, jet black | #C89F6E, #E8DCC8, #1A1A1A |
| Support: nude & gold | nude lip, beige top, chunky gold | #C99B8A, #F5EFE6, #D4AF37 |
| Support: skin & pops | tanned skin, blue/white eyeshadow, occasional violet-blue gradient | #C68863, #7EC8E3, #F2F2F2 |

Pink against leopard, deep red against zebra, mallow top under gold hoops: the clash is the point. A desaturated or monochrome handling of these hues is no longer cagole.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the tradition documents them

1. **The makeup mask**: thick, cakey foundation a shade off the wearer's tanned carnation; blue or white eyeshadow; heavy kohl liner; lips over-lined with nude lip pencil. This mismatch is diagnostic, not a flaw to correct.
2. **Jewelry overload**: garish necklaces (including rhinestone word-necklaces spelling "love" or names), large ear hoops, philtrum piercing, stacked rings and bracelets — "chrome on the ears, neck, arms."
3. **Revealing silhouette**: low cuts, uncovered cleavage, sometimes exposed underwear edges; tight dresses, pencil skirts, skinny jeans, yoga pants, tank tops.
4. **Animal print**: leopard first, zebra second, as cloth and as fur boots; print worn head-to-toe or clashed, never as a discreet accent.
5. **Hair**: long, flat-ironed straight, natural dark or dyed blonde (the "fake blonde" is part of the lore); teased volume, satin headscarves or headbands, sunglasses parked on the crown.
6. **Footwear**: stiletto heels, platform sandals, fur boots.
7. **Props**: logo-aspirational handbags, cigarettes, drink glasses, phones; pageant sashes and crowns in contest contexts.
8. **Settings**: Marseille skyline and sea light, tiled pedestrian streets, fountain plazas at night, club interiors, press walls and pageant stages.

### Materials and textures

Stretch lycra and satin sheen; sequins and rhinestone sparkle; faux fur; ripped denim with embellished patches; vinyl and lamé; chunky gold-tone metal and plastic hoops. Skin reads matte-cakey against glossy nude lips; hair reads glassy-straight. Texture contrast — hard flash on soft tan, glitter on cheap satin — is central.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

The native frame is the candid snapshot: center-weighted, full-body or three-quarter, subjects squared to the camera with loud expressions — open-mouth laughter, chin-up pouts, hands planted on hips, groups tangled in arm-around-shoulder hugs. Lighting modes: direct on-camera flash at night (hard shadow thrown behind, hot highlights on tan skin and sequins); harsh midday Mediterranean sun bleaching pavement; golden-hour terrace light over sea and city; flat event lighting against logo press walls; warm club strobe. Detail crops (cleavage plus print trim, jewelry close-ups) and annotated stereotype-cartoon diagrams are accepted secondary modes.

## Mood & Values

Assertiveness and sexual confidence worn as armor and as joy; extroverted loudness; carefree strength. The style laughs at itself and dares you to join — Miss Cagole parodies Miss France — while carrying genuine regional pride: a reclaimed slur, a southern identity defined against Parisian restraint, and warmth between friends that reads in every squeezed group hug. Excess here is sincerity, not irony alone.

## Era & Origins

The word reaches back to late-19th-century Provençal (possibly *cagoulo*, the apron of date-packing factory workers, poor and often Italian-immigrant women of Marseille), and hardened into derogatory slang in the 1980s. The visual code as recognized today is 2000s bimbo fashion. Popularity declined in the mid-2010s, then revived through the resurrected Miss Cagole contest (2018), the queer-and-inclusive Miss Cagole Nomade (since 2021), reality TV such as *Les Marseillais*, and high-fashion borrowings like Balenciaga's "Le Cagole" bag (2021). The style reacts against northern French respectability and minimal "good taste," and against the class contempt embedded in its own name.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Excess is the grammar: if one piece of jewelry is present, add two more; if one print, clash it with a color anchor.
2. Tan plus mask: tanned skin, foundation visibly too pale or too orange and matte-cakey; loud eyes (blue/white shadow, kohl), overlined nude lips.
3. Print clash: animal print must touch pink, deep red, mallow or gold within the same frame.
4. Loud body: open-mouth laugh, pout, hip-cocked stance, group squeeze; reserved posture breaks the style.
5. Light tells place: flash-night street or club, bleaching Mediterranean sun, golden-hour sea terrace, flat press-wall glow.
6. Bling typography: rhinestone words, logo hardware, sequin spelling.
7. Regional wink optional but strengthening: Marseille rooftops and sea light, pageant sash, pastis glass, scooter, Provençal tile.

**Variation axes (what may change freely)**

- *Subject*: solo portrait, friend group of two to six, pageant contestant, queer or drag performer in the Nomade spirit, women from late teens to mature adults; on streets, club floors, beach promenades, markets, balconies, fountain plazas, press walls, stages.
- *Composition*: full-body snap, waist-up portrait, tangled group frame, detail crop, annotated illustration.
- *Lighting*: any of the modes above, from noon glare to strobe.
- *Color handling*: pink-dominant, red-dominant, mallow-dominant, or print-dominant with a pink accent.
- *Mood*: defiant pride, parody camp, tender friendship, sultry pageant glamour.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Minimalism, quiet luxury, beige normcore, muted Scandinavian tones, generous negative space.
- Parisian-chic coding: trench coats, tasteful silk scarves, ballet flats, no-makeup makeup, neat bobs.
- Clean-girl glass skin and understated modern nude makeup.
- Goth, punk, grunge darkness; flannel; combat boots as the dominant note.
- Sportswear as uniform (tracksuit plus baseball cap as the whole outfit); yoga pants only as fashion paired with heels and jewelry.
- Pastel fairycore, soft grunge, cottagecore.
- Leopard styled "tastefully" with black tailoring; prints must clash, not coordinate.
- Cool desaturated grades, film-muted tones, teal-orange cinema grading.
- Closed-mouth polite smiles and contained posture as the dominant expression.

## Discrimination Cues

The wiki lists Choni, Chav, Chonga, Cocalar and Fjortis as relatives; the following separations are working heuristics (neighbor evidence beyond names is thin, so treat borderline cases with care).

- **Choni (Spain)**: shares hoops, heavy makeup and excess, but leans on tracksuits, sporty brand logos and high ponytails in Spanish street and fairground contexts; cagole keeps mallow and deep-red anchors, leopard-with-stilettos, pageant-parody culture and Mediterranean Provençal settings.
- **Chav (UK)**: unisex sportswear code — Burberry check, Adidas, caps, trainers — on council-estate backdrops; cagole is firmly hyperfeminine: dresses, heels, tan and cakey mask, jewelry overload.
- **Chonga (US)**: big hair and hoops overlap, but chonga sits in US mall, boardwalk and Latina/Italian-American contexts with tracksuits or denim shorts; cagole adds French-southern markers, purple-pink anchors and sash-and-crown pageant culture.
- **Cocalar (Turkey)**: counterfeit-logo street style with a more masculine, arabesque-inflected coding; cagole's hyperfemininity and Provençal context separate it (evidence thin on both sides).
- **Fjortis (Scandinavia)**: adolescent bedroom-and-selfie coding, dark liner with brand hoodies, Nordic winter light; cagole is adult, outdoors or club-bound, sun- or flash-lit, deeper in reds and animal print.

Against nearer cousins like McBling and Gyaru, cagole keeps a working-class French-southern snapshot rawness: flash glare, cheap satin, loud laughter, and the sea light of Marseille behind it all.
