# Witchcore

Witchcore is the visual language of the witch as lived identity rather than costume: an aesthetic of modern witchcraft, occultism and women's empowerment that coalesced on Tumblr, Instagram and TikTok. It imagines (or documents) a working practice — herbs drying from a rack, a grimoire left open, candles burned down in their own wax — so its images feel inhabited even when empty of people. It is explicitly an aesthetic, not a religion: anyone may borrow its visual grammar, though it draws respectfully on Paganism, Wicca and esotericism, and on pop-culture witches from *The Craft* and *Charmed* to Stevie Nicks's seventies bohemian stage wear. Thrifting, foraging and handmade decor are core values, so everything in frame should look found, aged or crafted, never bought new.

## Visual Identity

**Palette.** Two branches share one rule: a dark or earthy anchor, one warm light-source color, and one metallic or violet accent, all kept low-to-medium saturation with a faded, matte, slightly grainy finish.
- *Dark altar branch:* soot black and charcoal (#0D0B0E–#1A1721), deep forest green (#24301F–#3C4A2E), plum and aubergine (#3A2438–#55355C), lit by candle amber (#D9A441), copper and brass (#B87333), burnt orange (#C0662A), occasional oxblood red (#6E2B2B).
- *Pale apothecary branch:* parchment cream (#E9E1D2), bone white, pale wood (#C8B08C), sage grey-green and dusty lavender (#B9A7C9) — but only legible as witchcore when dried botanicals, glass bottles or occult marks carry the frame.
- *Accents anywhere:* antique gold (#C9A227), tarnished silver (#B9BAC2), amethyst violet (#6C4A78), moss olive (#6B7A4F). Bright flame-amber is usually the most saturated note in the image.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.**
1. Candles, incense and drifting smoke; wax drips and ash.
2. Crystals, stones and crystal balls; pentagrams and tarot cards; moon, sun, star and planetary imagery on tapestries, tiles and embroidery.
3. Books — aged, leather-bound grimoires and craft notebooks, stacked in tall leaning piles.
4. Plants in every state: living herbs and succulents, bouquets, mushrooms, moss, and above all bundles of dried flowers and herbs hanging or pressed.
5. Glass vessels: potion bottles, mason jars, hanging terrarium orbs, violet and amber apothecary glass.
6. Familiar animals: black cats first, then owls, frogs, bats; human and animal skulls, taxidermy.
7. Fashion: wide-brimmed hats, cloaks, shawls, bell-sleeved and maxi dresses, black lace camisoles, velvet, flower crowns, laced boots or bare feet.
8. Setting pieces: altar cloths and tapestries, lanterns, wands and brooms, old and Gothic architecture, forests and flower fields, tea and homemade bread, meditation corners with canopies and floor pillows.
Seashells, butterflies and bread are text-documented but rarer in practice; treat them as occasional guests (the sea-witch and kitchen-witch inflections).

**Materials and textures.** Velvet, lace, embroidered gold thread; foxed paper and cracked leather; wax, ash and smoke; clear, frosted and tinted glass; tarnished brass, copper and silver; raw and carved wood; twine and jute; brittle dried plant matter; faceted crystal; woven tapestry; fur and hide. Surfaces should show use: chipped wax, faded cloth, fogged glass, water-stained pages.

**Composition and lighting.** Objects gather in intentional clusters — an altar arrangement on cloth or wood, a shelf shrine tucked into an ordinary room corner, an overhead flat-lay of kit laid out with care. Vertical logic recurs: stacks (books, boxes) and hangings (dried bundles, terrariums, strung cards and charms). Light is motivated and singular: candle flame carving warm pools out of darkness, soft north-window daylight on pale still lifes, dappled forest shade, or string-light glow at night. Haze, smoke and mild vignetting soften edges; deep shadow is intimate, never threatening. Casual snapshot framing (a single incense stick on a domestic counter) is as valid as the styled altar — authenticity beats polish.

## Mood & Values

The occult and the unknown approached with familiarity rather than fear; energy, self-growth, connection to nature, and female empowerment. Witchcore is cozy secrecy: a private practice, half-hidden, half-inviting. Its DIY ethos — foraged, thrifted, handmade — is a value statement against mass-produced decor, and its playfulness (self-assigned witch "types," homemade crowns with pipe cleaners and googly eyes) keeps the esoteric warm and personal.

## Era & Origins

Named and spread by youth influencers across Tumblr (Witchblr), Instagram and TikTok (Witch Tok) from the mid-2010s into the 2020s, under aliases like Hexcore, Magicore and Wiccacore. Its visual lineage runs through nineties pop witchcraft (*The Craft*, *Charmed*, *Practical Magic*, *Sabrina*), seventies Stevie Nicks bohemian goth, and older Wiccan and esoteric practice; it reacts to consumer interior culture by privileging the found and the made, and reclaims the witch as a figure of empowerment.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- Build from practice, not costume: objects should appear mid-use — burning incense, an open book with hands near it, herbs mid-drying.
- Age everything; nothing in frame should look newly manufactured.
- Layer at least three motif families (flame + plant + occult symbol is the classic triad) in stacks, clusters or hangings.
- Light with one motivated source and let flame-amber or daylight be the brightest value.
- Keep saturation restrained; prefer faded film matte over glossy clarity.
- Human presence is optional: a dressed figure, only hands, or an implied practitioner through absence all work.

Variation axes: subject (altar still life, apothecary bottles, shrine corner or celestial bedroom, forest rite, fashion portrait, drying-rack or market abundance, familiar-animal portrait, muted digital illustration, single-object domestic snapshot); palette branch (dark candlelit, pale apothecary, green woodland, violet celestial, sea-glass for sea-witch); lighting (chiaroscuro candle, diffuse day, dappled shade, string-lit night, overcast outdoors); composition (flat-lay, cluster, stack-and-hang, corner shrine, centered figure, close snapshot); mood (cozy, eerie-occult, collectively empowering, whimsical). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: neon or hyper-saturated color; glossy plastic, chrome, LEDs and corporate product-shot cleanliness; commercial Halloween props (plastic pumpkins, candy corn, party bunting, cartoon ghosts); horror gore or frightening darkness — witchcore's shadow is intimate, not menacing; minimalist modern interiors (white Scandinavian, concrete, glass); sci-fi technology, modern logos and sportswear; high-key clinical studio light or heavy HDR; mass-produced new-age retail packaging; pristine untouched objects.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cottagecore** shares dried flowers, tea, bread and pastoral softness, but excludes occult apparatus and the black anchor; if removing tarot, pentacles, black candles and skulls leaves a sunlit cream pastoral scene, it is cottagecore. Witchcore keeps esoteric symbols and a dark or violet undertow even in its pale branch.
- **Cryptidcore** also uses night woods and flash-lit snapshots, but its subject is cryptozoological evidence — fog, pine forest, redacted documents, creature sightings — with no altars, craft tools or fashion. Witchcore outdoors is a ritual space of herbs and offerings, not an investigation site.
- **Dragoncore** offers hoards of gems, scales, heraldry and medieval weaponry in saturated jewel tones; witchcore's "treasure" is small, personal and practical — one amethyst, one worn deck, one bottle — set in domestic or woodland intimacy.
- **Goblincore** shares foraged trinkets, bones and moss but celebrates chaotic, mud-streaked, ugly-cute hoarding with no ritual order and no elegance; witchcore arranges its found objects with intent and retains velvet, lace, gold thread and celestial motif.
- **Halloween** is seasonal, commercial and graphic: flat orange-black, costumes, plastic decor, spooky-fun party energy. Witchcore is a year-round lived practice in muted natural tones, where orange appears only as flame, amber glass and autumn leaf, and every object looks owned rather than stocked.
- Near siblings worth noting: Whimsigothic overlaps on celestial tapestries and velvet but centers nineties bedroom maximalism without craft tools; Cottagegore borrows witchcore's objects but corrodes them toward horror.
