# Dau al Set

Dau al Set — Catalan for "the seventh face of the die" — names both an avant-garde group and its magazine, founded in Barcelona in 1948 by Joan Brossa, Antoni Tàpies, Joan Ponç, Arnau Puig, Modest Cuixart and Joan-Josep Tharrats, with Juan-Eduardo Cirlot and, in orbit, Joan Miró and Salvador Dalí. It was the first organized aesthetic revolt against the conservative culture of Francoist Spain after the Civil War: a hermetic, mystical, Dada-and-Surrealism-fed practice of dreams, magic and the irrational, published on paper and painted on canvas until the group dissolved (magazine closed 1951, movement ended 1956). Its visual DNA is that of a secret language: sparse occult signs on pale paper, fantastic creatures stalking earthy plains, distorted faces colonized by stars and arrows, and — as members drifted toward Art Informel — dark scratched matter out of which forms barely surface. Everything is coded: non-figurative or half-figurative expression stood in for speech that censorship forbade.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: bone paper, dark earth, isolated bright accents
The wiki's colour note is "varies by artist, often dark and earthy," and the corpus confirms two poles with a strict accent rule between them. Pole one: monochrome works on bone-white to cream paper (#F2EDDE–#DFD6C0) in black ink (#1A1613) with sepia or cool grey photographic tones (#8C857B). Pole two: dark, earthy grounds — umber, tan, ochre, olive, soot (#6E4E30, #9A7B4F, #B08D5E, #3E3A30, down to near-black #23201B). Into either pole enter a small number of *contrasting* vivid accents, never a full spectrum: vermilion (#C63B22), cadmium yellow (#E7B71E), cobalt blue (#2E4A86), occasionally deep green (#47703A) or a pale rose wash (#E5C6C2). The rule: accents appear as isolated discs, masks, flowers or letters against a muted field — a red sun on cream, a yellow eye on umber — so contrast, not harmony, organizes the colour.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence
1. **Dreamlike, hallucinatory Surrealist imagery** — depictions of dreams, the subconscious, mythology; the irrational as subject.
2. **Fantastic and mythological creatures** — hybrid bodies: horned or cat-eared masked heads, bird-persons, insectile spindly limbs, skeletal familiars.
3. **Heavy symbolic and magical imagery** — an esoteric sign-scatter: four- and eight-point stars, crescent moons, sun-discs with spiky or splattered rays, open disembodied eyes, crosses, arrows, dots, spirals, astral and alchemical glyphs, thin trajectory lines connecting them like occult diagrams.
4. **Automatism** — spontaneous, trembling calligraphic pen line; scribbled handwritten Catalan text and signatures woven into the image as visual matter.
5. **Distorted human figures** — elongated, split or annotated bodies; portraits whose faces are overlaid with dots, stars and arrows.
6. **Abstract elements, landscapes and textures** — the late bridge toward Art Informel: scratched, incised, scrubbed non-figurative matter.

Recurring elements the surviving prints, covers and paintings corroborate (secondary to the text's list, but constant): black-and-white **checkered harlequin grids** on creature bodies and skirts; **numerals, dice and the number seven** and scattered masthead letters ("DAU AL SET") used as picture elements; **portrait photographs intervened with ink symbols**; tiny architectural glyphs (towers, pedestals, castles); thorn-like black flora and magical gardens of eyes and flowers.

### Materials and textures
Pen and ink on paper; ink drawn directly over grey or sepia photographic portraits; lithograph and letterpress print on cream wove paper, sometimes showing halftone dot; oil on canvas with streaky, scrubbed, dry-brushed grounds; scratched and incised impasto matter in the Informel-leaning pole; matte aged paper throughout. No varnish gloss, no smooth finish — the surface always reads as hand-worked paper or worked-over paint.

### Recurring subjects
Hybrid creatures and masked heads; annotated portrait heads (often young men) surrounded by astral signs; magical gardens of black thorns, eyes and poppy-like blooms; dreamlike landscapes split by a low horizon; esoteric diagrams and talismanic arrangements; magazine covers, mastheads and poster pages with serif lettering, pictograms and number sequences.

### Composition and lighting
Flat, frontal fields with no perspectival depth. Composition ranges from a sparse calligraphic constellation of signs on open pale ground, to an all-over dense tangle of black flora and creatures, to a two-register band (pale sky over ochre earth) populated by scattered beings, to poster grids where type and pictograms share the field. Figures and glyphs sit on the picture plane as symbols, not volumes. Lighting is essentially non-directional: even paper light for drawings and prints; flat archival grey light where photographs appear; in the dark matter paintings, forms emerge from gloom as if under very low ambient light, with no cast shadows and no modeled chiaroscuro anywhere.

## Mood & Values

Oneiric, hermetic, ominously playful. The line may be childlike while the content is sinister: masked familiars, skeletal bodies, faces marked like star charts. The style carries avant-gardism, artistic freedom, anti-Francoism, Catalan cultural revival, philosophical exploration and mysticism — magic and the irrational as refuge and resistance, existentialism inherited from its Dadaist first hour (the magazine grew out of the earlier *Algol*). Mood legitimately spans mischievous game-play, occult dread, and melancholy introspection; what never changes is the sense of a coded message addressed to initiates.

## Era & Origins

Barcelona, Catalonia, 1948–1951 as magazine, to 1956 as movement; born in the repressive post-Civil-War Francoist landscape as the first major challenge to the regime's conservative aesthetic. It digests Dadaism and Surrealism, evolves through existentialism, and ends by feeding post-war Spanish modern art via the textural abstraction of Art Informel. The magazine — named one of the twentieth century's best avant-garde magazines by MoMA — remains the style's primary platform, which is why typography, numerals and print texture belong to the style's core, not its margin.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Build from the sign, not the scene: assemble a vocabulary of stars, crescents, eyes, sun-discs with rays, crosses, arrows, dots and thin connecting lines before any subject.
- Keep the field flat and matte; depth is symbolic overlap, never perspective.
- Budget accents: at most two or three vivid hues, placed as isolated discs, masks or letters against bone paper or dark earth.
- Let line misbehave: trembling pen, calligraphic automatism, handwritten words entering the image.
- Mix registers freely: drawing over photograph, type over drawing, print dot over ink.
- Choose a ground from the two poles — cream paper or earthy gloom — and commit to it.
- Suggest, never narrate: content should feel encoded, talismanic, half-legible.
- Age the surface: matte paper, scrubbed paint, scratched incisions, print grain.

**Variation axes.** Subject: hybrid creature, annotated portrait, magical garden, horizon dreamscape, esoteric diagram, typographic poster page, or near-abstract scratched matter. Composition: sparse constellation, dense all-over tangle, two-register horizon, poster grid, centered portrait with marginal glyphs. Colour handling: pure monochrome ink; earth ground with one vermilion or yellow accent; bold primaries on flat cream (the Miró-adjacent edge of the style). Medium look: pen on paper, ink over photograph, lithograph, oil on canvas, matter painting. Mood: playful, dread, hermetic-melancholic. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image** — a still life of dice annotated with stars, or an abstract scratched field with one red disc, is as valid as a creature scene.

**Avoid**
- Photorealism, 3D/CGI render sheen, airbrushed gradients, glossy varnish.
- Full-spectrum, neon or pastel-kawaii palettes; evenly distributed colour harmony.
- Modeled chiaroscuro, cast shadows, one-point perspective, volumetric lighting.
- Dalí-style hyperreal illusionistic dreamscapes with rendered skies and melting objects.
- Francoist academic realism, religious kitsch, sentimental illustration.
- Clean vector minimalism, symmetrical corporate layout, hard-edge geometric abstraction with no organic line or glyph.
- Mechanistic Dada collage satire, readymade objects, steampunk or Art Nouveau ornament.
- Legible narrative illustration: if the picture tells an obvious story, the code has broken.
