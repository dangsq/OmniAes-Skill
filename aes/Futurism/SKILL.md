# Futurism

Futurism was an avant-garde art and social movement launched in Milan in 1909 when the poet Filippo Tommaso Marinetti published the Manifesto of Futurism. It aggressively rejected the past ("passatismo") — museums, libraries, academic tradition — and glorified modernity: speed, technology, youth, violence, industry, with the car, the airplane and the city as its new icons. In painting and sculpture it borrowed Cubism's fragmented forms but bent them to a different end: not the static analysis of an object, but the sensation of movement, "dynamism." Umberto Boccioni, Giacomo Balla, Carlo Carrà, Luigi Russolo and Gino Severini used rhythmic repetitions, blurred forms and radiating "lines of force" to show objects moving through and merging with their environment, with a bright, vibrant palette inherited from Italian Divisionism. The movement ran through distinct visual phases — fractured dynamism in the 1910s, a more mechanical and decorative aesthetic in the 1920s (Second Futurism, including fashion and theatre), and aerial imagery ("aeropittura") in the 1930s — and ended with Marinetti's death in 1944. A Russian offshoot, Cubo-Futurism (Goncharova, Larionov, Burliuk, the young Malevich, Mayakovsky), fused the same breakdown of form with contrasting textures and manifesto lettering.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)
The text's core directive is **bright, vibrant, jarring color**: complements placed side by side so they vibrate, light treated as prismatic energy rather than ambient tone. The working range:

- **Sky and velocity blues:** cobalt `#1E4FA8`, azure `#2F74C0`, teal `#2E8C99`, high-altitude pale cyan `#BFD8E6`.
- **Machine metals:** chrome silver `#C3CCD4`, gunmetal `#5A6672`, near-black shard `#17181A`.
- **City and earth warms:** ochre `#C9903E`, sienna `#A65A2A`, terracotta `#B4472E`.
- **Signal accents:** vermilion `#D93A2B`, signal orange `#E8762C`, chrome yellow `#F2C230`, traffic-light green `#3E7A3A`.
- **Lyrical / textile branch:** lavender and violet `#8E6FB0`, `#C9B8E0` on off-white grounds `#F1EDE2` (the Depero/Balla fashion and late abstract strain).

Rule of thumb: at least one clashing complementary pair (blue/orange, red/green) must be active in the frame, and light arrives as colored shafts and discs, not as neutral illumination. A muted ochre-grey-brown handling is acceptable only in the Cubo-Futurist Russian branch, where fragmented forms carry the dynamism instead of color.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence
1. **Dynamism itself:** "lines of force" radiating from every moving body, speed lines, blur, and chronophotographic repetition — the same wheel, limb, propeller or cone restated in offset sequence (inspired by Marey's motion studies).
2. **Machines of speed:** airplanes and propeller spirals (dominant in Second Futurism and aeropittura), cars, trains, ships, turbines, factories, artillery.
3. **The city:** rooftop grids, streets, chimneys, cranes; the street and the sky interpenetrating in one continuous fractured field.
4. **Aerial vision:** steep bird's-eye views straight down onto city or countryside, cockpit vantages, diving aircraft, spiralling horizons, the landscape fused with the pilot's face or instrument panel.
5. **War, violence, patriotism:** battle scenes, artillery, war casualties, target roundels and national-color hints; war framed as exhilarating, never mournful.
6. **Futurist fashion and textile:** asymmetric suits, vests and dresses built from faceted color planes, zigzag beams and "speed lines" on fabric; deliberately garish, anti-black-suit clothing.
7. **Typography as image:** manifesto lettering, onomatopoeic words-in-freedom, and (Russian branch) stencilled Cyrillic integrated into the fractured field.
8. **Sculptural dynamism:** striding figures whose bodies dissolve into flame-like flowing planes (Boccioni's bronze) — the same motion rules translated into polished metal. Evidence here is thinner than for painting; treat it as one legitimate medium, not the default.

### Materials and textures
Oil paint is the dominant medium: hard-edged faceted planes whose interiors carry smooth, almost airbrushed gradients (aeropittura), or finer Divisionist filament strokes in earlier work. Bronze with polished, flowing muscular surfaces for sculpture. Wool, felt and appliquéd geometric patches for fashion; lithographic poster surface and paper collage elements for the Russian branch. The surface should read as *made by hand in motion* — crisp wedges, streaked blur — never as photography or seamless digital render.

### Composition
Diagonal thrust over horizontal rest. Recurring armatures: the vortex or spiral that sucks planes inward; cones and wedges converging on a moving core; rhythmic sequences of repeated identical shapes offset along a trajectory; **simultaneity** — interior and exterior, object and environment, memory and sensation fused in one field (a face merged with the terrain below it, a street merged with the sky above it). Vantage points are extreme: straight down from a cockpit, straight up at a climbing fuselage, or plunging between buildings. Horizons tilt, curve or spiral away; there is no stable eye-level. Lines of force extend from every energetic body into the surrounding space.

### Lighting
Light behaves as energy, not atmosphere: raking beams and searchlight cones, sunburst rays, prismatic shafts cutting across facets, white glints shattering off metal, luminous high-altitude sky set against dark machine masses. There is no domestic interior light, no candlelit chiaroscuro, no soft overcast diffusion — even shade is faceted and directional.

## Mood & Values

Machine euphoria and aggressive optimism: the ecstasy of velocity, faith in progress and mechanization, youth as a weapon, violence and war glorified as "the world's only hygiene," militant patriotism, and contempt for everything archived and antique. The emotional register is urgent, loud, forward-thrusting; even aerial landscapes carry awe mixed with adrenaline. Nostalgia, melancholy, repose and reverence for the past are structurally absent — an image that feels quiet, contemplative or antique has left the style.

## Era & Origins

Milan, Italy, 1909–1944; founded as a literary avant-garde of manifestos that colonized painting, sculpture, architecture, fashion, music (Russolo's intonarumori noise instruments), theatre (Prampolini's non-naturalistic scenography of color, geometric form and electric light) and even cooking. Formed in reaction against academic tradition and "passatismo"; visually formed by Italian Divisionist color, Marey's chronophotography, and the 1911 Parisian encounter with Cubism. Phases: 1910s fractured dynamism → 1920s mechanical/decorative Second Futurism → 1930s aeropittura. Politically entangled with Italian Fascism, which shapes its rhetorical aggression more than its palette. Russian Futurism/Cubo-Futurism runs roughly 1910–1916 in parallel.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles (apply these rules, in this priority):**
1. Paint the *trajectory*, not the object: every moving body leaves repeated echoes, blur trails or radiating lines of force.
2. Fragment with purpose: facets exist to multiply viewpoints *in time*, so planes interpenetrate and overlap instead of describing a solid at rest.
3. Arm the composition with a motion engine: diagonal thrust, spiral vortex, cone of light, or sequenced repetition.
4. Choose a machine-age subject or apply machine logic to any subject — a dancer, a crowd, a dog, a cyclist becomes a fan of limbs and streaks.
5. Keep color jarring: complementary clashes, prismatic light shafts, metal glints; reserve muted earth tones for the Cubo-Futurist lettering branch.
6. Destabilize the vantage: dive, soar, look straight down or straight up; tilt or spiral the horizon.
7. Optionally let type enter the image as a shouting element: manifesto capitals, onomatopoeia, stencilled words.

**Variation axes (the acceptable spread):**
- *Subject:* airplanes, cockpits, aerial views of city or countryside, factories, trains, ships, cars, bicycles, crowds, battle and artillery, urban streets with signals and grilles, fashion garments and textiles, stage sets of geometric forms and electric light, striding sculptural figures, abstract force-compositions with no identifiable object at all.
- *Composition:* vortex, diagonal plunge, top-down aerial grid, frontal machine portrait, sequenced repetition across a flat field, fused simultaneity (face + landscape, street + sky).
- *Lighting:* searchlight cone, sunburst, high-altitude luminous sky, night beams over dark city, prismatic shafts inside a faceted interior.
- *Color handling:* full Divisionist-bright prismatic; crisp enamel-like Second Futurism blues and silvers; lyrical lavender/violet textile mode; muted ochre-grey Cubo-Futurist mode with inserted lettering.
- *Mood:* euphoric, warlike, lyrical-aerial, or purely abstract-forceful.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A Futurist painting of a sewing machine, a football match, a harbor crane or a staircase is entirely valid if it obeys dynamism, lines of force, fractured interpenetration and jarring color — none of which requires copying an airplane or a suit from the historical corpus.

**Avoid (style-breakers):**
- A single frozen instant with no trail, repetition, blur or radiating force.
- Stable one-point perspective, level horizon, centered symmetrical subject.
- Analytic-Cubist monochrome beige/grey faceting with still-life stillness.
- Art Deco symmetry, zigzag chevrons, gilded ornament, streamlined luxury nostalgia.
- Constructivist flat diagrammatic grids, photomontage, utilitarian red/black/white typography.
- Vorticist hard-edged static monumentality; Suprematist/De Stijl pure geometry without motion.
- Dada irony, readymades, chance collage, anti-art absurdity.
- Pastoral calm, Impressionist leisure, ruins or classical statues treated with reverence.
- Seamless photoreal or generic sleek sci-fi digital rendering without fracture and painterly surface; pastel decorative prettiness; rustic handcraft texture.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Cubism:** closest neighbor and shared DNA (fragmented planes, multiple viewpoints). Test: *do the facets analyze or accelerate?* Cubism keeps a static subject (guitar, bottle, seated figure), a restrained ochre-grey-green palette and closed, table-top space. Futurism adds velocity — streaks, sequenced repetition, blur — plus bright Divisionist color and machine/urban subject matter, and opens the space to sky, street and crowd.
- **Vs Art Deco:** Deco is ornament and status: symmetry, stepped zigzags, sunburst motifs as decoration, luxurious materials, streamlined elegance at rest. Futurism is agitation: asymmetry, clashing color, felt speed; its spirals and cones are forces, not patterns.
- **Vs Constructivism:** Constructivism builds utilitarian communication from real materials — flat planes, photomontage, diagrammatic clarity, red/black/white, the artist as engineer. Futurism stays painterly and illusionistic, with depth, atmospheric sky, brushed gradients and emotional frenzy rather than cool programmatic layout.
- **Vs Dadaism:** Dada mocks art itself through irony, chance, readymades and monochrome photo-collage. Futurism is deadly sincere: it glorifies, celebrates and shouts; its chaos is choreographed motion, not anti-art provocation.
- **Vs Vorticism:** the British cousin shares the fractured machine vocabulary but freezes it into hard, angular, monumental stillness with a restrained palette — the machine as idol. Futurism refuses stillness: blur, repetition and lines of force keep everything in transit, and its color stays Mediterranean-bright.
- **Border case:** Cubo-Futurism (Russian branch) legitimately mixes Cubist muted faceting with Futurist motion and inserted lettering; accept it as Futurism only when dynamism and textual intrusion dominate the static analysis.

*Evidence note:* painting and aeropittura are densely documented; sculpture, fashion and stage design are attested but thinner, and Futurist music/theatre are non-visual domains whose only transferable cue is Prampolini's geometric, electric-light scenography. Do not over-weight garments or bronze figures as the style's default face.
