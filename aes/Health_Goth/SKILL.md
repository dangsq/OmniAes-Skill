# Health Goth

## Overview

Health Goth is a fashion-and-image aesthetic that emerged in the early 2010s out of Facebook and Tumblr communities, named in 2013 by Portland artists Mike Grabarek, Jeremy Scott (Magic Fades) and Chris Cantino. It dresses the body in high-performance technical sportswear — mesh, compression fabric, latex, PVC, technical sneakers from Nike, Adidas, Puma, Under Armour, Y-3 — but strips athletic culture of warmth, sweat and effort, presenting it instead inside a sterile, rendered, almost laboratory-like futurism. Despite its name it has no connection to the music-based Goth subculture and none of its romantic or macabre themes; its "goth" is a tonal darkness, a subversive reading of sportswear advertising in which something cold and faintly sexual lurks under the clean surface. Its ethos is transhumanist: bio-enhancement, anti-aging, prosthetics, the body as apparatus, "pursuing perfection." The creators insist it is not a lifestyle but an exercise in aesthetics, and its image world reflects that — lookbooks, product still lifes, packaging macro shots, 3D renders with no people at all, and net-art object studies sit equally alongside portraits.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is monochrome by doctrine, not by tendency: clothing is "fully black and white, usually used as contrast to show a brand or pattern," with grey as the mediating field and chrome/silver as the metallic extension of white.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Void black | `#050505`–`#141414` | Garments, logo blocks, shadowless darks |
| Paper / lab white | `#F2F2F2`–`#FFFFFF` | Backgrounds, voids, ceilings, packaging |
| Cool grey | `#6E6E6E`–`#D9D9D9` | Floors, concrete, mid-values, desaturated grade |
| Chrome / specular silver | `#B9BEC2`–`#E6E9EB` | Mylar, polished steel, foil curtains; carries highlights, not hue |

Rule: chroma is held at or near zero. Contrast is carried by value (black-on-white logo blocks, white type on black shell), never by hue. The only tolerated color is incidental human skin and hair, and at most a faint clinical cool tint in the grade. Saturated color of any kind breaks the style.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Monochrome technical sportswear with brand graphics** — mesh jerseys, compression gear, performance leggings, technical sneakers; logos (Nike swoosh, Adidas trefoil/stripes, box logos) deployed as flat monochrome contrast, often oversized or boxed.
2. **Rendered and sterile environments** — white voids, fitting rooms, lab-like retail interiors, angular minimal architecture, 3D-rendered spaces with no people; the Tumblr feed of renders is a declared source.
3. **Mesh and moisture-wicking fabric; transparent clothing** — perforation, see-through layers, garments sealed in frosted or vacuum-packed plastic.
4. **Futuristic graphics and typography** — extended, angular, blade-like sans-serif type printed on translucent packaging or overlaid on images; tribal-pattern blades as net-art objects.
5. **Transhumanist elements** — body-enhancement tech, prosthetics, BioWare, apparatus around the body; the body treated as mechanism.
6. **Material fetish garments** — latex, PVC, form-fitting bodysuits, futuristic jackets with strong silhouettes, militaristic cold wear, combat and tactical gear.
7. **Rainwear, chains and light weaponry** — umbrellas and shell jackets as sleek props; hardware as jewellery-adjacent detail.
8. **Elemental aesthetics, corporeal mortification, shoe dipping** — conceptual keywords from the creators' sourcing list; visually thin, appearing as mood (elemental light panels, truss structures, restrained bodily tension) rather than fixed props.
9. **Net-art object studies** — close macros of mesh, a blade with a tribal pattern beside a fern: object-as-image with no wearer at all.

### Materials and textures

Perforated mesh and moisture-wicking knit; glossy PVC and latex with liquid, rolling specular highlights; matte compression Lycra; frosted translucent TPU/PEVA zip bags and vacuum-sealed polyurethane wrapping dark garments; chrome and mylar foil curtains; polished tubular steel (racks, chairs, rails); matte rubber outsoles; ripstop and coated technical shell fabric; smooth rendered surfaces with soft ambient occlusion. Texture interest comes from surface physics — specular, translucency, perforation — never from weave warmth, patina or handcraft.

### Recurring subjects

Models and athletes in head-to-toe monochrome performance wear, often anonymized (cropped heads, turned faces, masked or prosthetic hints); garment still lifes on chrome rails or chairs; packaging and sealed-bag still lifes; sterile interiors (fitting rooms, lab benches, retail voids, white installations with mechanical arms); personless architectural renders; technical sneaker and fabric macro shots; lookbook grids of standing figures; light panels, trusses and industrial rigging used as elemental staging.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends toward product-like isolation and clinical symmetry: centered single objects or figures in a void, one-point perspectives down sterile rooms, panel/grid lookbook layouts, extreme macro crops of fabric and type, generous negative space. Bodies are posed statically — standing, seated, slouched with attitude — never mid-exercise. Lighting is either shadowless diffuse lightbox/render illumination (soft, cool, ambient-occluded) or flat direct on-camera flash against a white wall, the Tumblr snapshot vernacular; gloss surfaces provide the only drama via specular streaks. Grades are desaturated and cool. No golden hour, no colored gels, no chiaroscuro.

## Mood & Values

Clinical futurism, minimalism, performance, technology, clean aesthetics, transhumanism. The emotional register is an anti-nostalgic dystopian present: sterile, controlled, faintly unsettling, blurring "things that are transcendental and taboo" — a perfection-pursuit that admits its own fears and doubts. There is a deliberate cold eroticism (the founders saw "something dark or sexual" in sportswear ads that wasn't intended) and a rejection of retro-fetishism, coziness and the "conventional and twee." Above all it values aesthetics over lifestyle: imagery is the point, not working out.

## Era & Origins

Early 2010s, online-first: the Health Goth Facebook community started 2013 by Grabarek, Scott and Cantino in Portland, fed by Tumblr's net-art and 3D-render feeds. Declared influences include HOOD BY AIR, Cottweiler, Whatever 21 and A D Y N, plus athletic wear advertising read against the grain. A fitness-oriented variation was started separately by Los Angeles personal trainer Johnny "Deathface" Love. The style went viral in 2014 — the #2 most Googled fashion trend behind Normcore — with coverage from Vice, i-D, The Guardian, Vogue, Complex, GQ, The Fader (Adam Harper's post-genre analysis) and others, to the founders' irritation at lifestyle readings.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Monochrome discipline: black, white, grey, chrome. Value contrast does all the work; logos and patterns exist as black-on-white or white-on-black graphics.
2. Synthetic material honesty: every surface should read as engineered — mesh perforation, gloss PVC/latex speculars, frosted translucency, vacuum-sealed plastic, polished steel.
3. Sterile staging: voids, lightboxes, lab-like rooms, rendered architecture, packaging tables. The environment is clinical or artificial, never domestic or naturalistic.
4. Body as apparatus: compression fit, strong silhouettes, prosthetic or biotech hints, anonymized or cropped figures; attitude without exertion.
5. Graphic futurism: angular extended type, tribal-blade geometry, boxed brand logos treated as pure design elements.
6. Restraint: few props, heavy negative space, static poses, cool desaturated grade.

**Variation axes (allowed range)**

- *Subject*: garment still life, sealed-packaging macro, personless render, lookbook panel, portrait in sportswear, sterile interior, object net-art study (blade, fern specimen, mesh close-up), sneaker or fabric detail, light-panel installation.
- *Composition*: centered symmetry, one-point sterile perspective, grid/panel layout, macro crop, wide empty interior with a single small figure.
- *Lighting*: shadowless diffuse lightbox, flat direct flash snapshot, cool render ambient; speculars on gloss surfaces.
- *Color handling*: pure black/white, grey-dominant, chrome/metallic-led, translucent-white led; incidental skin tone only.
- *Mood*: from calm clinical minimalism to unsettling taboo edge (restraint, tension, faint erotic coldness).

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A rendered corridor with no clothing, a flash snapshot of a branded tee, and a macro of frosted packaging are equally valid Health Goth outputs.

**Avoid**

- Saturated color, neon, colored gels, warm grading.
- Earth tones, fleece, trail gear, camping hardware, mountain or forest settings (Gorpcore territory).
- Occult or romantic Goth props: crosses, lace, velvet, candles, fishnets, Victorian detail, band tees, macabre symbolism.
- Neon-soaked night cities, rain-slicked streets with dense signage, cyan/magenta glow (Cyberpunk).
- Y2K rave maximalism: chrome jewellery, buffed nails, UV accents, logomania in color, glitter (Cyber Ghetto).
- Multicolor sneaker-collab flexing, warm lifestyle photography, resale-hype staging (Hypebeast).
- Vintage film grain, retro styling, nostalgia of any kind — retro-fetishism is explicitly rejected by the founders.
- Natural materials and handcraft: wood, linen, wool, wicker, patina, plants used decoratively (a fern may appear only as a cold net-art specimen).
- Actual athletic exertion: sweat, mid-run action, gym-clutter realism.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | How Health Goth differs |
|---|---|
| **Gorpcore** | Gorpcore is outdoor-utility: fleece, shell jackets in earth or color-block tones, trail shoes, nature context, comfort. Health Goth is urban/sterile, strictly monochrome, synthetic-glossy, laboratory-staged. |
| **Cyber Ghetto** | Cyber Ghetto is maximalist DIY rave futurism: chrome nails and jewellery, neon/UV, Y2K web glamour, colored logomania. Health Goth is austere, minimal, monochrome, clinically lit. |
| **Cyberpunk** | Cyberpunk is narrative dystopia: neon night, wet streets, dense signage, saturated cyan/magenta, clutter. Health Goth is daylight-white or flat-grey sterility, sparse composition, no neon, no story clutter. |
| **Goth** | Traditional Goth is music-subcultural: romantic/macabre themes, lace, velvet, leather, occult silver, black-only palette. Health Goth shares only tonal darkness; it uses performance synthetics, includes white and grey equally, and rejects romance and the macabre. |
| **Hypebeast** | Hypebeast is sneakerhead consumer flex: colorful collabs, loud branding in color, warm street lifestyle shots. Health Goth uses brand logos only as monochrome graphic contrast inside cold, art-directed, sterile frames. |

**Quick tell:** if an image looks like a sportswear advertisement re-photographed inside a render — monochrome, mesh and gloss plastic, chrome and white voids, angular type, body as machine — it is Health Goth.
