# Too Cool (土酷 / "Tacky Cool")

Too Cool is a kitschy movement and fashion trend from 2010s China that takes everything urban taste dismissed as "tu" (土, "soil" — rural, tacky, outdated) and wears it as a crown. It reclaims the visual culture of rural towns and older generations — devotional posters, studio portrait conventions, street-sticker ephemera — and fuses it with early-internet clip-art gloss and ironic modern twists. The result is neither parody nor pure sincerity: it is a proud, excessive, self-authored glamour built from materials the mainstream called tasteless. Its name is a phonetic pun — 土酷 sounds like "too cool" — and the emphasis falls on the *Too*: coolness pushed past propriety into excess.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is **saturated clash, never restraint**: at least three unrelated hue families coexist at full chroma, and empty background is always filled with sky, foliage, checkerboard or gradient. Typical ranges:

| Family | Hex range | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| Jade / teal | `#00A896`–`#38D9C0` | water, waterfalls, garments |
| Sky / azure blue | `#3FA9F5`–`#8ED0F8` | backdrop skies, water splashes, ad fields |
| Hot pink / magenta | `#FF4D9E`–`#FF9ECD` | hearts, fleeces, flowers, gradient orbs |
| Lucky red / vermilion | `#D93025`–`#FF6650` | robes, drapes, seals, brushstroke signatures |
| Foliage green | `#4CAF3F`–`#8FE06A` | leafy backdrops, checkerboard tiles |
| Ingot gold / yellow | `#F5C542`–`#FFE08A` | coins, money trees, glyph outlines, frames |
| Cloud lavender / lilac | `#B9A7E8`–`#E6D9F7` | dreamy cloud skies behind figures |
| Chrome / CD silver | `#C0D8E8`–`#F0F8FF` | skeuomorphic orbs, glossy highlights |

Muted, tonal or monochrome handling is outside the style; even "quiet" variants keep gold or pink accents at poster saturation.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how strongly the text documents them)

1. **Traditional Chinese and Buddhist iconography**: deity figures (wealth-god type bearded officials in red robes and ornate headdresses), female Buddhas, temples, money trees strung with coins, gold ingots (also printed on stockings), dragons, tigers, eagles, auspicious proverbs and calligraphic glyphs.
2. **Waterfalls and flowers as backdrop**: cascading waterfalls, lotus ponds, plum blossoms, peonies and daisies used as photoshoot settings, poster scenery, or wreath-like floral borders framing the whole image.
3. **Vernacular typography**: rainbow-gradient text, glossy beveled/skeuomorphic lettering, heavy black-outlined calligraphy, vertical Chinese text columns, mixed Chinese–English strings, old-poster proverbs and "办证" (get-certificates) sticker lettering; brand-style wordmarks slapped across the center are in-play.
4. **Garments**: qipao (cheongsam) in vibrant colors printed with deities, temples or eagles; bellybands (dudou), Chinese tunic suits, embellished stockings; modern streetwear (fleeces, hoodies, caps) colliding with these traditional pieces.
5. **Rural and older-generation ephemera**: gold picture frames, pavilions and landmarks (Great Wall type), resort-courtyard snapshots, old residential stickers, printed poster halftone.
6. **Early-internet clip-art gloss**: chrome/CD orbs, glossy pink hearts with text inside, starburst stickers, checkerboard tiles, cut-out water splashes, inset sub-photos pasted at odd scales.
7. **Performative body**: exaggerated poses (crouches, leaps, tilted heads, arms raised), dramatic makeup, sunglasses indoors, theatrical staging against obviously synthetic scenery.

### Materials and textures

Glossy skeuomorphic plastic sheen on text and orbs; halftone/moiré print backgrounds; low-resolution JPEG compression and composite seams left visible; gold-leaf frames; satin and brocade qipao against teddy-fleece and polyester; clip-art water with hard cut edges; direct-flash skin shine. Everything reads as **assembled**, not photographed: layers keep their own lighting, scale and resolution.

### Composition and lighting

Horror vacui: negative space is filled with text blocks, stickers, insets or border flora. Subjects sit frontal and centered against a backdrop that demonstrably comes from elsewhere; scale mismatches (giant lotus, tiny figure, huge glyphs) are normal. Decorative containment recurs — floral wreath borders, gold frames, checkerboard margins. Lighting is flat and frontal: direct flash or even studio light on the subject, while the backdrop keeps its own daylight or neon glow; optional bloom/glow on props. There is no unified light source, no cinematic chiaroscuro, no atmospheric perspective.

## Mood & Values

Defiant joy and rural pride: tackiness wielded as avant-garde. The mood is celebratory, theatrical and self-aware — excess performed with a straight face and a wink simultaneously. Nostalgia here is not longing but recontextualization: homage to a period of rapid Chinese modernization, and a claim that copying and recombining is itself creativity. The style laughs *with* rural culture, never down at it; its kitsch is empowerment, DIY self-authorship, and cultural pride dressed in gold ingots.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s China, popularized from early 2018 by influencer Liu Min ("Princess Butterfly") on Weibo and Instagram, and pushed into mainstream consciousness in mid-2020 when Balenciaga's Qixi advertisement borrowed the look (sparking appropriation debates). It descends from the 2000s Shamate subculture and reacts against three pressures: the urban contempt embedded in the word "tu", the "Made in China / shanzhai knock-off" stigma, and Western stereotypes of Chinese art as either exotic Orient or propaganda cliché. Its DIY digital nostalgia parallels overseas Xpiritualism.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Collage over camera**: build the image as stacked layers — subject, backdrop, text, stickers, insets — each with its own lighting and resolution; let seams show.
2. **Fill the frame**: no breathable minimalism; borders, wreaths, frames or text blocks occupy every margin.
3. **Text is ornament**: include Chinese glyphs and/or English strings in rainbow gradient, glossy bevel, or heavy black outline; vertical columns and centered wordmarks both valid.
4. **Clash registers**: put sacred/folk iconography (deity, ingot, lotus, temple, proverb) in the same layer stack as consumer clip-art (chrome orb, heart, starburst, handbag, fleece).
5. **Gloss everything**: skeuomorphic sheen on type and props; saturated chroma throughout.
6. **Pose performatively**: exaggerated, theatrical, deadpan-glamorous body language against obviously synthetic scenery.
7. **Present as an artifact**: studio portrait, street poster, framed print, or social-media ad graphic — never a "candid fine-art photograph".

**Variation axes (allowed range)**

- *Subject*: fashion portrait, deity/devotional poster, product or brand ad parody, landscape poster with figures, rural courtyard snapshot, still-life of ephemera.
- *Composition*: centered frontal portrait, full-bleed collage poster, framed studio photo, ad layout with overlaid wordmark, bordered wreath layout.
- *Lighting*: direct flash flat, even daylight, neon/glow bloom on props; subject–backdrop lighting mismatch encouraged.
- *Color handling*: teal-pink-dominant, red-gold devotional, green-blue checkerboard, lavender-cloud romantic — all inside the saturated palette table.
- *Mood*: ironic glamour, sincere celebration, devotional kitsch, playful commerce.

**A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A portrait of a stranger in a tunic suit before a checkerboard sky, or a poster of a money tree with no human at all, is equally valid Too Cool if the collage logic, palette and typography rules hold.

**Avoid**

- Minimalist negative space, restrained luxury-editorial styling, neutral beige/gray palettes.
- Matte finishes, film-grain analog purism, desaturated "cinematic" grading.
- Unified single-point perspective and physically consistent lighting across all elements.
- Absence of typography or of any Chinese vernacular anchor.
- Goth/emo/visual-kei hair and hardware (that is Shamate), pastel dreamy softness (Yabi), painterly pastoral serenity (Romantic Chinese Village), earthy handmade warmth (Cottagecore), clean vector UI flatness or chrome-only futurism (Frutiger Aero/Webcore proper), night-neon websurfing cityscapes (Fotonight Web).
- Contemptuous mockery of rural people: the irony targets taste hierarchies, not the countryside.
