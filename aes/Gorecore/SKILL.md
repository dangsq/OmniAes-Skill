# Gorecore

Gorecore is a compound aesthetic of the graphic and the disturbing: blood, death, murder, mutilation, organs, bones and corpses, filtered through slasher-film imagery and goth-punk fashion. It is deliberately unsettling but almost always *stylized* — the blood is fake blood, the wounds are SFX makeup, the corpses are drawings or duotone graphics. The style lives equally in three registers: wearable DIY fashion (spiked chokers, skeleton knits, blade charms), graphic design (dripping red-on-black posters, tiled moodboards, naive threatening handwriting), and photographic or illustrated tableaux of bloodied body fragments. A lighter, social-media-safe branch substitutes fake and animated gore for explicit imagery, and the aesthetic openly courts overlap with cute registers — hearts, smileys, anime softness — precisely to sharpen the transgression. Culturally it positions itself against conventional beauty standards and, for some practitioners, against the male gaze: bodies appear fragmented, menacing, or decayed rather than offered.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

A three-role system with one optional accent: **blood red is the protagonist, black is the stage, bone-white and pale flesh are the contrast**, and cold steel silver appears only as hardware. Reds span a wide but bounded range — bright poster scarlet (around `#E40000`–`#C00000`), crimson and oxblood (`#8A0303`–`#6B0F0F`), down to dried maroon (`#5C0A0A`–`#3F0708`). Blacks run `#000000`–`#141414`; contrast whites are bone and paper (`#EDE6DE`, `#E7D8CF`) plus pale desaturated skin (`#EFD9D3`). Metal reads as unpolished silver/steel (`#A8A8AD`, `#6E6E73`). A second, quieter handling exists for the painterly/animated branch: a muted crimson field (`#A63A3A`–`#C46A6A`) with dusty rose bones (`#D9A0A0`) and near-black figures. Two-tone duotone treatments (red-on-black, red-on-bone) are canonical. What never leads: pastels, warm cozy neutrals, gold, rainbow multicolor, or naturalistic green/blue environments.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Blood, explicitly.** Drips with clean viscous edges, smears across pale knuckles, pools, soaked fabric, wax-like runs down chokers and candles. Blood is the load-bearing element; an image without it (or its skeleton/blade proxies) is not Gorecore.
2. **Blades and sharp hardware.** Bloody knives, razor blades dangling from chokers, spikes, studs, chains, knife-themed hair accessories (the knife-through-the-head headband), pointed nails, abstract sharp shard shapes.
3. **Skeletons and bones.** Ribcage intarsia across knit sweaters, bone appliqué on black wool, skulls floating in red fields, graveyard crosses and tombstones as set dressing.
4. **Corpses and mutilated bodies**, usually implied through fragmentation — a cropped neck, a bandaged bleeding arm — rather than whole explicit bodies.
5. **Organs.** Entropic all-over intestine textures, guts as pattern and prop, always saturated red rather than butcher-beige.
6. **Slasher-film imagery.** Grainy screenshot crops, flash-lit snapshot framing, VHS-era contrast.
7. **Creepy children's drawings and naive text.** Scribbled line-art creatures on scratched red grounds, handwritten threats paired with smileys, dripping poster capitals, band-flyer lettering.
8. **Goth-punk fashion apparatus.** Chokers, mesh and fishnet, split-dye hair (two colours, centre part), red lipstick and eyeshadow, dark plum lips, layered chain necklaces.
9. **Cute symbols as counterpoint** (hearts, roses, coffins, smileys, soft anime faces) — a juxtaposition layer, never the lead.

### Materials and textures

Knit wool with intarsia or appliqué skeleton motifs; black leather straps and belts; silver-tone metal (studs, spikes, blades, chains, cross pendants); mesh and fishnet; glossy fake blood with paint-like viscosity and hard drip silhouettes; SFX wax and gelatin wounds; grain registers including flash-photo noise, dithered poster grain, flat vector shapes, matte digital paint, and naive pencil/crayon scribble; paper backing cards for earrings and patches.

### Recurring subjects

Body fragments (neck, hand, waist, chin); accessory and garment product shots (chokers with dripping blood or hanging blades, earring cards, skeleton sweaters); weapons as still-life props; candles running red wax; skulls; bare-tree and graveyard silhouettes against red sky; figures floating or drowning in red liquid; tiled moodboard grids; abstract compositions of black shards on red.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Fragment cropping:** frames cut the body at neck, wrist or waist; the crop anonymizes and unsettles.
- **Centered object on plain ground:** product-shot isolation on white or grey with even, shadowless light.
- **Full-bleed duotone poster:** red-on-black fields, decorative borders, dripping caps typography, silhouetted figures.
- **Collage grid:** square tiles separated by white gutters, mixing photo, scan, text and drawing.
- **Low-key snapshot:** near-black scene, one red accent, harsh direct flash falling on pale skin, heavy vignette.
- **Flat illustration:** shadowless muted-red fields, floating figures, scattered skulls, vein-like branching linework.
Lighting always serves legibility of red: either flat and even (product, vector, illustration) or harsh and singular (flash, low-key spotlight). No golden hour, no soft ambient warmth, no atmospheric fog without a red anchor.

## Mood & Values

Confrontational, transgressive, and craft-proud. The mood ranges from menacing (blade close-ups, threatening handwriting) through ironic-cute (smiley faces beside spiked clubs, heart-shaped earring cards holding coffins and blood drops) to melancholic (a girl drifting quietly among skulls in a red sea). Romanticized decay is a virtue — rot, rust-red skies, "shine when you're dead" energy. The DIY punk ethos shows in handmade patches, chains and badges. Artificiality is honored: fake blood, SFX makeup and animated gore are the point, not a compromise; the skill on display is the convincing stylized wound. Underneath, the style asserts autonomy over the body — fragmented, armed, decayed, unappealing to the gaze it subverts.

## Era & Origins

The source material does not pin a founding date, and honestly the named taxonomy is a product of 2010s–2020s internet aesthetic-wiki, Tumblr and Pinterest culture; treat the *label* as recent and online-native. Its visual vocabulary is older and borrowed: late-20th-century slasher cinema supplies the screenshot language; 1970s–80s punk and goth fashion supply spikes, studs, chains and black leather; early-2000s flash animation and gore-physics games (the *Happy Tree Friends*, *Mortal Kombat*, *People Playground*, *Gorebox*, *Pretty Blood* lineage) supply the animated, stylized-violence branch. The style reacts against polished mainstream beauty norms and sanitized social feeds — its lighter branch exists precisely to smuggle gore past platform moderation via fake and animated blood. Neighboring currents it touches: Pastel Gore, Cutecore/Gurokawa cuteness, Vampire's red-black romance, Goth Punk hardware, Weirdcore's uncanny collage logic.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Red leads, black stages, bone/pale flesh contrasts; keep the palette inside the bounded range above.
2. Stylize every wound: paint-like drip silhouettes, SFX gloss, duotone blood, animated gore — never clinical photorealism.
3. Fragment the body: crop to neck, hand, waist; pale skin against dark cloth; let accessories read as weapons.
4. Juxtapose sweet and deadly: a heart, a smiley, a cute typeface, an anime-soft face placed against blade, blood or bone.
5. Choose exactly one texture register per image (flash grain, dithered poster grain, flat vector, matte digital paint, knit/leather product photography, naive scribble) and commit to it.
6. When type appears, make it a prop: dripping capitals, band-flyer stacks, or childish handwriting with an incongruous smiley.
7. Keep metal cold and silver, never gold; keep light harsh or flat, never warm and ambient.

**Variation axes.**
- *Subject:* garments and jewelry; body fragments; weapon still-lifes; skulls, organs, candles; graveyard or red-sea scenes; floating illustrated figures; collage moodboards; abstract shard graphics; creepy drawings. Any subject that can carry blood, blade or bone logic is admissible.
- *Composition:* centered product shot; tight fragment crop; bordered poster; tiled grid; full-bleed duotone scene; shadowless illustration.
- *Lighting:* even studio flat; harsh direct flash on pale skin; low-key single-accent; backlit silhouette against red sky; no directional light in illustration.
- *Color handling:* saturated scarlet duotone; red-on-bone; oxblood-and-black knitwear; muted crimson painterly field with dusty rose bones.
- *Mood:* menacing, ironic-cute, melancholic, confrontational-DIY.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A duotone poster of a church step, a knitwear catalog photo, an animated drowning scene and a flash snapshot of a stained hand are equally Gorecore when they obey the palette, stylization and motif rules.

**Avoid.**
- Pastel-dominant palettes or kawaii softness leading the image (that crosses into Pastel Gore).
- Photorealistic clinical viscera, documentary violence, or forensic wound detail.
- Butcher-shop meat: raw slabs, mince, wrapping plastic, refrigerated pink-beige (Meatcore's territory).
- Demonic or occult centrality: pentagrams, horns, hellfire, devotional inverted iconography (Devilcore).
- Eating, biting, feasting on flesh (Cannibalcore).
- Bloodless atmospheric dread, monsters or jumpscares carrying the image (generic Horror).
- Cuteness with no violent counterweight (Cutecore).
- Warm golden light, cozy autumn tones, gold jewelry, neon cyberpunk, rainbow color.
- Sexualized pin-up framing or inviting poses; the style's bodies menace or decay, they do not offer.
- Clean corporate minimalism or high-fantasy ornament.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Cannibalcore:** look for the mouth. Cannibalcore centers consumption — teeth, biting, feasting on flesh, often playfully. Gorecore centers bloodshed and injury: blades, drips, skeletons; mouths appear only as dark-lipstick fragments or as wounds.
- **Vs Devilcore:** look at what the red means. Devilcore's red is hellfire serving demonic iconography (pentagrams, horns, ritual blacks). Gorecore's red is blood serving blades, bones and SFX wounds; its crosses are graveyard props and pendant hardware, not satanic devotion.
- **Vs Horror:** Horror is broad atmospheric dread and may be entirely bloodless — shadows, monsters, suspense, cold desaturated palettes. Gorecore is graphic and blood-forward by definition, and leans on fashion, poster design and collage conventions that Horror does not need.
- **Vs Meatcore:** look at the flesh's context. Meatcore treats raw meat as material — slabs, ground meat, butcher pinks and beiges, plastic wrap, fluorescent chill. Gorecore's organs are saturated red, stylized, and bound to a violence narrative (wound, blade, corpse), never to a food chain.
- **Vs Pastel Gore:** the motifs overlap (blood, wounds, cute juxtaposition) but the palette decides. Pastel Gore bathes gore in soft pink, lavender and mint with gentle gradients; Gorecore keeps hard contrast, black grounds, and saturated or oxblood red. If the field reads as candy first and blood second, it is Pastel Gore.

**Quick recognition checklist:** dominant red-in-range plus black plus bone/pale flesh; visible blood with viscous stylized drip edges; blade/spike/bone hardware or skeleton motif; fragmented or anonymized body; one committed grain/texture register; optional cute element placed ironically against the violence. Meet most of these and the image is Gorecore even if it resembles no reference example at all.
