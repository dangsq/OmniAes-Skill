# Vulture Culture

## Overview

Vulture Culture is a contemporary hobby and internet subculture, born in the 2010s on Tumblr, Instagram and Reddit, centered on the collection, processing and preservation of animal remains. Its practitioners — "vultures" — collect only from animals that died of natural causes: roadkill, forest finds, weathered bones. The subculture's founding ethic is ethical sourcing and explicit opposition to sport or profit hunting, and this ethic leaks into the imagery: specimens are presented as rescued, cleaned, studied and honored objects, never as trophies of a kill. The hobby spans skeleton cleaning and articulation, taxidermy, pelts, pinned insects, and wet specimens made through diaphonization, and its output becomes art, jewelry and educational curiosity collections shared and traded within the community.

Visually this produces a hybrid genre: part natural-history documentation, part handmade craft photography, part cabinet-of-curiosities portrait. The specimen is always the protagonist, and almost always there is a trace of the practitioner — a palm holding a find, wire wrapped around a bone, a handwritten label, a shelf packed to overflowing — which is what separates this style from pure nature photography or from gothic décor.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette rule: **light specimens on dark or neutral grounds**, with earth tones as the bridge and only small, intentional accents of saturated color. Bone carries the highest value in the frame; the ground (black cloth, dark wood, maroon fabric, shadowed forest) carries the lowest; fur, paper, dried flora and jar liquids occupy the mid tans and browns; greens enter through moss, lichen, foliage and verdigris. Saturated hues appear only as *craft decisions* — dyed or patinated bone, iridescent chitin, alizarin-stained diaphonized specimens — never as ambient décor color.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Animal skulls and bones** — the core motif; cleaned, degreased, sun-bleached or macerated crania of any species, from shrew jaws to livestock skulls, alone or in multiples.
2. **Taxidermy and pelts** — mounted heads, study skins, fur feet and tails, often small-scale and hand-finished.
3. **Pinned insects** — spread-wing butterflies and moths, cicadas, beetles, arranged around a skull or in rows; chitin sheen against matte cloth.
4. **Wet specimens and glass jars** — diaphonized (translucent, stained) specimens and preserved parts in ethanol or formalin; amber-tinted liquid, glass refraction, cork or metal lids. (Strongly documented in the text; under-represented in sampled imagery, so treat jars as fully canonical even when rare.)
5. **Feathers** — loose feathers, wing mounts, feather-and-bone assemblages.
6. **Natural decay flora** — dead trees, wildflowers, mushrooms, moss; the forest floor as both sourcing ground and staging ground.
7. **Curiosities and curio cabinets** — crowded shelves, drawer collections, wall grids of specimens; abundance as portrait of a practice.
8. **Forests** — the sourcing landscape; in-situ finds among leaf litter and fungi.

Craft derivatives documented as community activity: **bone jewelry** (wire-wrapped feet, claws, vertebrae; silver or copper coil binding), **dyed, painted or crystallized bones**, **articulated skeletons**, and **educational plates and prints** (vintage natural-history illustration style, cream paper, grid of labeled skulls).

### Materials and textures

Porous matte bone with visible sutures, tooth rows and nerve foramina; directional soft fur; glossy laminated chitin; dry papery moth wings; velvet or coarse cotton backing cloth; glass with liquid meniscus and refraction; tarnished silver or copper wire; aged paper and ink; raw wood shelving; waxy fungus caps; crumbly bark. Texture contrast is a signature: matte bone against sheen of beetle wing, soft fur against hard coil of wire.

### Recurring subjects

The specimen itself; the **hand as presenter** (open palm offering a small find, fingers holding a skull at arm's length — scale-giving, intimate, snapshot-honest); the **collection as self-portrait** (shelves, cabinets, drawers packed with bone); the **process** (jars, drying racks, works-in-progress); the **find in situ** (skull nestled in moss or crowned by mushrooms, outdoors); the **assemblage as artwork** (skull framed symmetrically by insects or feathers).

### Composition tendencies

- **Palm-up offering close-up:** specimen small in an open hand, shallow depth of field, casual framing.
- **Symmetrical flat-lay / mandala:** central skull ringed by pinned insects or feathers on black cloth, near-axial symmetry, top-down.
- **Crowded cabinet grid:** shelves or fabric walls filled edge-to-edge with specimens; horror-of-abundance, no negative space.
- **Scientific plate layout:** cream paper, rowed or gridded specimens, small type captions.
- **Single-specimen portrait:** one skull or pelt centered on a plain or dark ground, even light.
- **In-situ forest floor:** specimen among moss, mushrooms, leaf litter, dappled or overcast light.

### Lighting tendencies

Diffuse, honest, documentary light. Soft window daylight for handheld snapshots; even neutral light for flat-lays and plate scans; dim warm interior light for cabinet interiors; overcast or dappled forest light for finds. Shadows are soft and short. There is no theatrical rim light, no fog, no candle-flame chiaroscuro — light serves identification and texture, not drama.

## Mood & Values

Reverence without horror. The emotional register is quiet pride in craft, tender documentation, and curiosity-cabinet wonder — memento mori handled by someone who cleaned the bone by hand and can tell you the species. Values expressed: ethical sourcing (death found, never caused), respect for the deceased animal, preservation against waste, anti-consumerism (found, traded and handmade objects rather than bought décor), and shared curiosity (labels, plates and educational framing). Abundance reads as devotion, not hoarding; a single specimen reads as study, not loneliness.

## Era & Origins

Emerged in the 2010s as an online community on Tumblr, Instagram and Reddit, where members share finds, exchange preservation advice and trade specimens. It reacts against two things: disposable consumer décor (its objects are rescued and processed, never factory-made) and sport hunting (its ethics forbid killing for specimens). It sits adjacent to Goblincore, Crowcore, Cottagegore, Dark Academia, Strega and Forestpunk, but unlike most of those it is defined by an actual *practice* — maceration, articulation, taxidermy, diaphonization — rather than by a décor mood.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. The specimen is the protagonist and it is *processed*: cleaned, dried, pinned, articulated, jarred or wrapped — never fresh death.
2. Imply a practitioner: a hand, a wire coil, a label, a jar, a shelf, a workbench. Someone cared for this object.
3. Keep palette discipline: bone-light on dark/neutral ground, earth mid-tones, saturated color only as deliberate craft accent (dye, patina, chitin, stained wet specimen).
4. Honor texture: porous matte bone, directional fur, chitin sheen, glass refraction, cloth weave, paper grain.
5. Light as documentation: diffuse, even, soft-shadowed; brightness serves legibility of anatomy.
6. Choose a composition family (palm offering, symmetrical flat-lay, crowded cabinet, plate grid, single portrait, forest-floor in-situ) and commit to it.
7. Mood is reverence and curiosity; eeriness may exist but never menace.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- *Subject:* any species and any preserve type — mammal skull, bird foot, reptile skeleton, fish or amphibian diaphonization jar, pelt, feather mount, pinned beetle or moth, articulated full skeleton, dyed or crystallized bone, vintage-style anatomical print.
- *Scale:* from a shrew mandible in a fingertip to a livestock cranium on a shelf.
- *Composition:* any of the six families above, or hybrids (a jar held in palm; a flat-lay on moss instead of cloth).
- *Lighting:* window daylight, overcast outdoors, even studio light, dim cabinet lamp, forest dapple.
- *Color handling:* untouched bone-white; or intentionally altered bone (dye reds, verdigris patina, crystal crust); or translucent stained wet specimen; grounds from velvet black to maroon fabric to raw wood to living moss.
- *Mood:* clinical-educational, intimate-reverent, collector-abundance, melancholy-decay.

**Avoid**

- Fresh gore, blood, raw carcasses, maggots or fly-blown decay — the style begins after cleaning.
- Sport-hunting iconography: antler plaques, lodge trophy mounts, camouflage, weapons.
- Halloween or occult props: plastic bones, candles-and-roses altars, pentacles, cobwebs, fog machines (drifts to Cottagegoth/Strega).
- Human skeletal or anatomical remains — the community is animal-only.
- Glossy saturated commercial product photography, neon colors, smooth plastic surfaces.
- Bones as generic boho/minimalist wall décor with no craft or collection context.
- Cute live-pet photography; mud-smeared chaotic clutter with live frogs and snails (Goblincore); corvid-only occult framing (Crowcore); candlelit libraries and tweed (Dark Academia); pristine wilderness with no human trace (Dark Naturalism).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Vulture Culture's counter-tell |
|---|---|---|
| Crowcore | Corvid-exclusive: black feathers, crow skulls, scavenged shiny trinkets, folk-magic scavenger persona | Taxonomically omnivorous; process evidence (jars, wire, labels, shelves) over occult persona |
| Goblincore | Mud, live snails and frogs, chaotic "ugly-cute" hoarding, playful dirt | Cleaned, arranged, labeled specimens; order and care, not gleeful grime |
| Cottagegoth | Spooky pastoral set design: rotting pumpkins, haunted cottage, black candles, autumn décor | Real specimens and real craft; no staged narrative spookiness |
| Dark Academia | Books, tweed, candlelit libraries, classical busts; skull as memento mori prop | No study-of-canon props; fur, chitin, jars and processing tools present |
| Dark Naturalism | Field-guide or chiaroscuro wilderness realism; nature untouched by hands | A practitioner's hand, home, shelf or tool always implied |

**One-line test:** if the image shows an animal remain that has been cleaned, preserved or assembled by a caring human hand, lit honestly and framed with reverence or documentary pride, it is Vulture Culture; remove the human trace and it becomes nature photography, add candles and it becomes goth décor, add mud and frogs and it becomes Goblincore.
