# Scene

Scene is the loud, glittery child of emo: a youth subculture that formed in the early 2000s United States out of emo and hardcore punk, peaked on MySpace from roughly 2005 to 2012, and swapped emo's introspective angst for color, play and internet fame. Where emo looked inward, scene looked at the camera — at a webcam, in a mirror, on a profile page covered in glitter GIFs. Its visual DNA is a deliberate collision: hardcore punk signifiers (band tees, studs, skulls, eyeliner) fused with rave brightness, Harajuku street color and childlike franchise kitsch (Hello Kitty, Gloomy Bear, Invader Zim, dinosaurs, unicorns). The result reads instantly as a specific moment in internet history — point-and-shoot snapshots, overexposed flash, teased neon hair, and a peace sign or clawed "rawr" hand aimed at the lens.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

The governing color law is **solid black contrasted with a limited set of saturated neons**, not an even rainbow. Black (#0D0D0D–#1C1C1C) anchors the outfit or the graphic field — typically the tee, the jeans or the hair base — while one to three neons fire against it:

- hot pink / magenta: #FF2D95 – #FF69B4
- electric blue / teal: #00B7EB – #00E5CC
- lime / neon green: #9EFF00 – #32CD32
- grape purple: #7B2FBE – #9932CC
- lemon yellow: #FFE135 – #F5D000

Platinum bleach-blond (#F2E8D5) and stark white act as the neutral "bright" in hair and checkerboards. In **graphic** work (splatter art, Blingee edits, typography posters) the palette is allowed to escalate to full rainbow paint-splat (#FF0000 through #FF00FF plus orange and green drips), but even then black remains present as line art, silhouettes or drips. A scene image whose colors are muted, earthy, pastel-without-black, or monochrome-without-neon has left the style.

### Hair and makeup (the single strongest identifier)

Hair is the centerpiece and the most text-documented motif. Rules: asymmetric, choppy, layered cuts; long side-swept fringe ("swoop") often covering one eye; hair flat-ironed straight then **heavily teased or spiked for volume** — big, opaque, helmet-like mass rather than soft waves. Color: unnatural dye applied as streaks, sections, or horizontal **"raccoon tail" / "coon tail" stripes** via extensions — pink, red, green, vivid blue, or bleach-blond bands running through a black base. Animal-print (cheetah/leopard) extensions count as a raccoon-tail variant. Makeup: **thick black "raccoon" eyeliner** rimming the eye, sometimes black or neon eyeshadow, tinted chapstick; pale or matte complexion in photos. Piercings (septum, lip studs, eyebrow) appear frequently but are optional. Both genders wear essentially the same hair — androgyny is a structural feature, not an exception.

### Wardrobe and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Skinny jeans**, tight, in black or bright colors, or patterned (stripes, plaid); also shorts over fishnet tights.
2. **Band T-shirts** — metalcore, crunkcore, pop-punk, or franchise prints (Invader Zim, Hello Kitty, Gloomy Bear); screen-printed graphics, often skulls or cartoon characters.
3. **Studded belts and studded jewelry** (wristbands, chokers); white belts in the early fashioncore-inflected phase.
4. **Bright accessories**: hair bows, tiaras, plastic pearl necklaces, stacked bracelets; **kandi** bead cuffs borrowed from rave culture appear often but are *not* universal — treat as common optional, never mandatory.
5. **Eyewear as prop**: oversized "nerdy" thick-rim glasses and **shutter shades**.
6. **Arm warmers, fingerless gloves, striped leg warmers / knee-high socks**; tutus and tulle skirts layered over jeans.
7. **Sneakers**: Converse (especially knee-high), Vans, DC, Osiris.
8. Secondary props: beanies (sometimes with animal ears), furry wolf-hood hats, snapbacks, plushies, energy-drink cans, lanyards, mirror-selfie cameras held visibly in frame.

### Pattern and graphic language

Recurring flat patterns: **checkerboard** (black/white or pink/black), **horizontal stripes**, **animal print**, **polka dots**, plus icon motifs of **skulls, hearts, stars, bows and splatters**. In digital graphics the style adds: paint-splat and drip vectors, glitter and star stickers, jagged splatter frames, bubble or gradient rainbow typography, and text conventions borrowed from band names — **words run together with no spaces** and **exclamation marks** ("rawr!", "xxdeadb4idiedxx"-style handles with x's and underscores). The shared **Scene art style** for illustration: sketchy, jagged pencil-like line art; thin or stick-figure bodies with caricature heads; huge circular "blank" chibi eyes or anime eyes; characters wearing the same uniform — straightened high-volume opaque hair, black tee with neon splashes, striped details, low-slung bottoms, sneakers.

### Medium, composition and lighting

Scene's native media are lo-fi and self-made: point-and-shoot snapshots, webcam and mirror selfies, bedroom photos, band promo shots on plain seamless backdrops, outfit flat-lay collages, MySpace/Blingee composite edits, chibi vector art, and typography posters. Composition tendencies: high-angle self-shot looking up into the face; tight head-and-shoulders crops that make the hair the subject; full-body outfit documentation; visible camera or phone in mirror; clawed "rawr" hands, peace signs, or heart-hands gestures; bedrooms with poster-plastered walls, animal-print bedding and pink clutter as backdrop. Lighting is characteristically **harsh direct flash or overexposed daylight** — blown highlights, pale skin, slight color wash — or flat amateur indoor light; graphics are flat-saturated digital color with no realistic lighting at all. Grain, compression artifacts and sticker overlays are period-correct texture, not flaws.

## Mood & Values

Playful rebellion: hardness (studs, skulls, eyeliner, hardcore band tees) worn with softness (bows, kitties, plushies, tutus) and a wink. Values named by the culture itself are individuality, self-expression, internet culture and music-scene belonging (Warped Tour, MySpace top-friends lists). The emotional register is optimistic, party-oriented and performative — "rawr!" as irony-flavored childlike aggression — explicitly rejecting emo's introspective melancholy. Attention-seeking is a virtue: the look is built to be photographed, posted and ranked. Androgyny and gender-blurring presentation are celebrated rather than tolerated.

## Era & Origins

Early 2000s United States, out of emo and hardcore punk. Direct ancestors: **Fashioncore** (Eighteen Visions — eyeliner, tight jeans, straightened hair, white belts, crafted in reaction to late-90s hardcore hypermasculinity) and **sasscore**'s flamboyant homoerotic presentation; the A-line "razor haircut" evolved from there into ultra-short crowns and asymmetric bangs. The term "scene queen" began circa 2002 as an insult and became a title for MySpace trendsetters (Audrey Kitching, Jeffree Star, Hanna Beth, Kiki Kannibal). Mainstream peak mid-2000s to early 2010s with metalcore, crunkcore and electropop (Brokencyde, Attack Attack!, 3OH!3, Metro Station, early Bring Me the Horizon); decline around 2014; revivals from 2019 (#20ninescene, "Rawring 20s", SpaceHey) and the TikTok-era exaggeration called Scenecore. Global cousins: China's Shamate, Argentina's Floggers, Brazil's Coloridos, Chile's Pokemón.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Anchor with black, puncture with neon: keep black as the dominant mass and let 1–3 neons (pink, electric blue, lime, purple, yellow) act as loud accents; reserve full-rainbow splatter for graphic backgrounds, always with black line work or drips present.
2. Make hair the hero: straightened shine plus teased volume, asymmetric swooped fringe, unnatural streaks or horizontal raccoon-tail banding; the silhouette should be big, choppy and geometric, never soft or natural.
3. Stack the accessories: bows or tiaras plus studs plus pearls or kandi plus thick-rim glasses or shutter shades — abundance is correct, minimalism is not.
4. Mix hard and soft signifiers in one frame: skull band tee with a pink tutu; studded belt with Hello Kitty print; clawed "rawr" hand with a heart sticker.
5. Use period media logic: snapshot flash or overexposed daylight, slight blowout and grain, mirror-selfie framing, glitter-sticker and splatter overlays, no-space exclamation-mark typography when text appears.
6. Keep bodies and faces androgynous-leaning and youthful; both masculine and feminine subjects wear the same haircut and skinny silhouette.
7. In illustration mode, switch to sketchy jagged linework, stick-thin bodies, oversized heads with huge blank chibi eyes, and splatter-drip rainbow fields.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be any of: a self-portrait or selfie of any gender; a group snapshot at a festival or convention; a band promo on a seamless backdrop; a bedroom or mirror environment shot; an outfit flat-lay collage; a chibi cartoon avatar; an album cover; a pure typography/glitter graphic. Composition may range from extreme high-angle face crop to full-body documentation to centered character-on-splatter graphic layout. Lighting may range from harsh on-camera flash, to blown-out outdoor daylight, to flat indoor amateur light, to no lighting at all in vector art. Color handling may slide from black-dominant-with-one-neon to neon-dominant-with-black-anchor to full splatter rainbow in graphics. Mood may slide from party-optimistic and goofy ("rawr") to soft pastel-girly (pink rooms, bows) to harder hardcore-leaning (studs, skulls, darker base) while keeping the black-plus-neon law and the hair silhouette. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Muted, earthy or beige palettes; quiet minimalism; capsule wardrobes; natural makeup; beach waves or balayage hair.
- Baggy jeans, cargo shorts, bootcut denim, flannel grunge layers, thrifted gray distressing.
- Goth signifiers: Victorian lace, corsets, black-on-black with silver occult jewelry, romantic darkness.
- E-girl/e-boy specifics: curtain bangs with tiny face-framing clips, split-dye underlayers only, blush across the nose with stamped hearts, chain wallets, ring-light glow, TikTok-UI framing.
- Emo specifics used alone: flat straight black fringe with no tease and no neon, subdued red/plum accents, introspective downcast mood.
- Raver-only looks: all-over neon with no black anchor, PLUR gear as the whole outfit (scene borrows kandi but keeps band tees and black).
- Modern streetwear: hype sneakers, bucket hats, techwear, oversized hoodies as the silhouette (scene silhouettes are tight).
- Professional editorial lighting: softbox glamour, cinematic teal-orange grades, clean 2020s Instagram polish.
- Chrome cyberfuturism or vaporwave pastels; neither belongs to scene's mall-goth plastic-and-glitter material world.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Scene |
|---|---|
| **Emo** | Emo keeps color subdued: black hair, straight flat fringe, black tee, skinny jeans, Buddy Holly glasses, cardigans; mood introspective, palette black with at most deep red or plum. Scene adds teased volume, neon streaks/raccoon tails, bows and kitsch, and a party mood. Flat dark fringe + angst = emo; big colored tease + play = scene. |
| **Fashioncore** | The precursor: eyeliner, tight jeans, straightened hair, white belts, collared shirts on hardcore musicians; masculine-band context, no neon splatter, no franchise kitsch, no glitter graphics, less backcombed volume. Scene is fashioncore plus color, kitsch and MySpace gloss. |
| **E-Girl / E-Boy** | Late-2010s TikTok descendants: softer gradient or split-dye hair with curtain bangs and face-framing strands, heavy blush and graphic winged liner, oversized tees with pleated skirts, chain wallets, beanies, ring-light sheen and digital-native poses. Scene's liner is a thick raccoon smudge, its hair is teased and streaked, its light is flash-blown, its overlays are glitter stickers and splatter PNGs, not clean UI. |
| **Bro Hoe** | Rural party style: camo, cutoff denim, flip-flops, tank tops, American flag and beer branding, no eyeliner, no dyed hair, no skinny silhouette. Palette (camo green, flag red-white-blue) and gender presentation are opposite to scene's androgynous neon-on-black. |
| **Kandi Raver / Scenecore** | Kandi Raver goes all-neon with PLUR beads and no black anchor; Scenecore is the 2020s TikTok exaggeration of scene with digicore graphics and even louder candy color. Classic scene sits between: black-based wardrobe, band tees, snapshot flash, 2000s platform artifacts (MySpace layouts, Blingee glitter). |

When in doubt, run the three-part test: (1) is black the base with selective neon punching through it? (2) is the hair straightened, teased, asymmetric and streaked or banded? (3) does the image carry 2000s internet snapshot or glitter-splatter graphic logic? Two of three plus a hard/soft motif clash places the image firmly in Scene.
