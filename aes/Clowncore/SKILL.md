# Clowncore

Clowncore surrounds clowns, mimes and jesters and everything orbiting them — carnivals, fanfare, big tops, balloon animals — filtered through childhood nostalgia. It is an internet-native reclaiming of the clown from horror typecasting: where coulrophobia sees a threat, clowncore answers with vibrant humour, cream pies and stickers everywhere. The style lives on the body and in the hands more than in the environment: face paint, dyed wigs, rainbow tights, enormous shoes, wrist-stacks of candy-letter beads. Its default register is bright, happy and childish (it sits in the Kidcore/Decora family), but it tolerates a creepy-cute and a fully dark gothic register without losing the clown signifiers that hold it together.

## Visual Identity

### Color language

The palette is anchored by a **primary triad — red, yellow, blue** — treated as paint, not dye: saturated, flat, slightly glossy, laid over a **white or pale ground** (face paint, white tee, cream skirt, plain wall) so every color reads as applied pigment or molded plastic. Multi-color passages are organized as **ordered rainbow stripe sequences** (red–orange–yellow–green–blue–violet bands on socks, sleeves, laces, nails, stickers) rather than random confetti. Recurring accent pops: hot magenta-pink (`#FF4FA3`–`#FF77C0`) and turquoise/teal (`#17BECF`–`#1FC4D4`) for lips, noses and tears; fire red (`#E60023`–`#FF2A2A`), sun yellow (`#FFD400`–`#FFE94A`), cobalt (`#0047AB`–`#2A6BFF`), plus orange (`#FF8A00`), grass green (`#3CB44A`) and violet (`#7B4FBE`) inside rainbow runs. **Black-and-white is the structural neutral**, appearing only as harlequin diamond checks, mime stripes and platform-boot leather. Two documented shifts: **Pastel Clowncore** desaturates to baby pink, mint, lilac, butter and cream (`#FFD9E8`, `#BFF5E3`, `#D9C7F2`, `#FFF6E5`); **Dark Clowncore** grounds in black, deep purple and oxblood (`#0D0D0F`, `#3B1F4E`, `#6E0F1A`) while keeping one or two saturated clown pops (teal lips, red nose) intact.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **The clown face as canvas**: white or pale base, blended pink or rouge halo around the eyes, colored nose tip, diamond or teardrop marks under the eyes, overdrawn colored lips, thick drawn or shaved-and-repainted brows. Jester and mime faces count; so do stitched, cracked or bleeding versions in the dark branch.
2. **Rainbows** in stripe or sticker form, repeated serially across a surface (forehead, cheeks, nails, phone case).
3. **Circus and carnival apparatus**: tents, rides, confetti, balloons, pom-poms, cotton candy, gumballs, ice cream, birthday cake, circus animals such as elephants, the clown emoji, worm-on-a-string toys.
4. **Childlike plastic maximalism** (Decora-adjacent): bead bracelets spelled with candy letters, slogan badges ("I jump in puddles", "I got dressed today"), flower and food charms, hair clips, stickers applied everywhere.
5. **Costume grammar**: large round-toed colorful shoes and platform boots with rainbow laces; party or jester hats; ruffled pierrot collars; overalls or suspenders; circus tights; harlequin diamond patterns; mismatched pairs (one striped sock, one rainbow; split-color jumpsuits); brightly dyed hair or wigs.
6. **Soft toys and handmade craft**: plush dolls, polymer-clay clown earrings, ball-jointed dolls, balloon animals.
7. **Sub-variant markers**: clown-print Lolita coords (pastel branch); berets, white gloves and black-white stripes (mimecore); streetwear, metal and E-girl layers under stylized makeup (dark branch).

### Materials and textures

Glossy injection-molded plastic, polymer clay, felt and plush pile, candy-glaze shine, creamy face paint, cotton jersey and opaque tights, patent or leatherette shoe uppers, frilled cotton collars, printed sticker paper, bead-string cord. Surface treatments include film grain and orange date stamps on nostalgic selfies, and soft shadowless light on craft macros. Matte luxury materials (silk, velvet, tailored wool) are foreign to the style except as ironic contrast.

### Recurring subjects

People in makeup and costume, usually cropped to the expressive zone: full-face makeup portraits, top-down feet shots, wrist stacks, nail art. Crafted objects photographed as still lifes or flat-lays. Flat vector and pixel illustration of clown characters on geometric or solid grounds. Sweets and party food. Environmental carnival scenes (tents, rides) are text-documented but comparatively rare in practice; the style's center of gravity is personal adornment and small objects.

### Composition and lighting

Close crops that fill the frame with face, feet, wrists or nails; grid-like flat-lays of many small plastic objects on pastel or novelty grounds (cow print, lavender); centered half- or full-body costume portraits against plain walls; illustration centered on triangles, stars or flat color fields. Lighting is bright and even — direct flash, daylight, shadowless studio — or entirely flat in illustration. The dark branch dims the room, cools the cast and adds grain, but keeps the makeup legible.

## Mood & Values

Joy, humor, positivity. Childhood nostalgia worn sincerely, not ironically: the style values regressiveness as comfort, DIY craft as identity (molded charms, painted faces, sewn coords), and play over polish. It is a reclaiming — answering the creepy-clown trope with affection and puns. The dark branch keeps community and self-expression (strongly shaped by Latino/a creators, and by goth, E-girl and cholo scenes) while trading sunshine for eerie intimacy; creepy-cute overlap with Pastel Goth and Morute is accepted. A minor adult suggestive edge ("clussy") is documented in the source culture but is not central to the visual language.

## Era & Origins

Lineage is claimed from ancient Greek and Roman theatre fools and from 1860s circus clowning; the modern aesthetic was coined on 2010s Tumblr and bloomed on 2020 TikTok alongside Kidcore, Decora, Scene and Visual Kei. It reacts against coulrophobia and against the clown's horror typecasting by re-embracing the figure; its dark branch instead absorbs comic-book villain imagery (Joker, Harley Quinn) and alternative subcultures. Media touchstones include *Popee the Performer*, *Homestuck*, *Killer Klowns from Outer Space* and *The Amazing Digital Circus*.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles (abstract rules, not scenes):**
1. Include at least one unmistakable clown/circus semiotic: makeup geometry (white base, colored nose, diamond tears), ruff collar, jester hat, harlequin diamonds, oversized shoes, tent, balloon animal.
2. Anchor with the red-yellow-blue triad; render multi-color passages as ordered rainbow stripes, never as unordered sprinkle color.
3. Keep a pale or white ground so saturated hues read as paint and plastic.
4. Maximalize childlike detail: stack bracelets, pile badges, repeat stickers; blank surface is unused canvas.
5. Mismatch on purpose: asymmetrical socks, sleeves, tear colors, split-color garments.
6. Play scale: enormous shoes or hats against tiny beads and charms.
7. Favor glossy plastic, clay, felt, plush and candy shine over matte luxury fabric.
8. Light brightly and evenly (flash, daylight, flat illustration color); grain signals nostalgia, not gloom — except in the dark branch, where dim cool light is correct but makeup stays legible.
9. Keep affection visible: even eerie versions stylize horror (diamond tears, stitched smiles) rather than wallowing in grime.

**Variation axes.** Subject: makeup portrait, extremity close-up, charm flat-lay, doll or clay craft, flat/pixel illustration, decorated dessert, nail art, carnival scene. Composition: frame-filling close-up, top-down detail, grid flat-lay, centered figure on flat ground, geometric backdrop. Lighting: flash, soft daylight, shadowless studio, flat color, grainy film with date stamp, dim cool light (dark branch). Color: full primary saturation, pastel kawaii, dark gothic with saturated pops, black-and-white mime. Mood: exuberant, tender-nostalgic, creepy-cute, eerie. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid:** muted earth tones, beige minimalism, Scandinavian interiors; generic party imagery with balloons but no clown semiotic; seductive burlesque styling (corsets, fishnets, boudoir light) as the dominant read; Juggalo black-white warpaint with hip-hop streetwear and aggression; raw slasher gore without clown makeup geometry; sepia vintage-circus scenery with no personal adornment; monochrome grayscale without mime or harlequin intent; neon rave glow without clown signifiers; matte luxury fashion photography; documentary circus labor without stylization.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shares | Clowncore tells them apart |
|---|---|---|
| Kidcore | Bright primaries, plastic charms, childhood nostalgia | Clowncore adds clown semiotics: face paint, colored nose, ruffs, jester hats, harlequin diamonds, big shoes, tents |
| Burlesque | Theatrical costume, face emphasis | Burlesque is vintage striptease glamour — sequins, corsets, feathers, warm boudoir light, adult seduction; clowncore is plastic, daylight, childish silliness |
| Juggalo | Clown face paint | Juggalo paint is black-and-white warpaint over streetwear with aggressive posture and gritty palette; clowncore paint is multicolor over playfulness |
| Dark Cabaret | Circus-adjacent theatricality, dark register | Dark Cabaret is Weimar cabaret: corsets, fishnets, candlelight sepia, piano-ballad gloom; dark clowncore is modern E-girl/goth streetwear with saturated clown makeup |
| Dark Circus | Creepy circus imagery | Dark Circus is environmental and vintage — fogged big tops, rusted rides, aged photography; clowncore stays centered on personal adornment and internet-selfie stylization, even when eerie |

Internal borders: **Mimecore** (beret, gloves, strict black-white) is a subset, valid only with mime grammar; **Pastel Clowncore** keeps all clown motifs but swaps saturation for baby pastels and Lolita coords; if an image has charm stacks and rainbows but no clown signifier at all, it is Kidcore or Decora, not clowncore.
