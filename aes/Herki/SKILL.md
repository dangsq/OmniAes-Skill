# Herki (Херки)

Herki — singular *herka*, self-styled *gotka/gotyushka* — is the Russian youth subculture of the late 1990s and early 2000s in which teenagers, mostly girls aged 12–17, wore a simplified, commercially accessible version of goth fashion. The name began as a joke about the female fanbase of the Finnish band HIM (released in the US on the "HER" label), and the aesthetic never shed that origin: it is goth imagery filtered through musical fandom, adolescent earnestness, and the material reality of post-Soviet apartments. Its defining visual tension is dissonance — expensive-feeling dark romance staged inside mundane, "low-brow" surroundings: a black-tulled silhouette in front of grandmother's wool carpet, corpse-pale makeup under a stairwell's green oil paint. "True" Russian goths used the term as a pejorative for poseurs, and the photos carry both the satire and the sincerity of that position.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is a black mass punctured by one pale face and one subdued accent; the background supplies drab post-Soviet neutrals that the black costume clashes against.

| Role | Hex range | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Costume black | `#0b0b0b`–`#242424` | coats, mesh, tulle, beanies, chokers; 60–80% of figure area |
| Powdered skin | `#e9e4d8`–`#f4f1e8` | chalky pale face, hands; sometimes full white face paint |
| Accent red / burgundy | `#6e0f14`–`#a31220` | tights, stripes, skirt under tulle, lips |
| Accent plum / violet / dusty pink | `#4a2350`–`#6d3b6e`, pink note up to `#c46a86` | skirts, tights, eyeshadow bleed |
| Backdrop neutrals | beige `#cfc4ab`, olive oil-paint `#5c7248`–`#74865c`, carpet rust `#7d4f2e`, overcast grey `#a7abad` | wallpaper, stairwell wainscot, wall carpets, sky |

Accents stay *subdued* in面积 and saturation: a stripe of pink tight, a burgundy skirt swallowed by black tulle, a plum eyeshadow smudge. A frame with no accent at all (black + white face + drab wall) is still correct.

### Makeup and face

Text-ranked first: aggressive black eyeliner applied heavily around the whole eye, "panda"-wide, sometimes drawn downward as tear streaks under the eye; white powder or foundation for a pale, chalky complexion mimicking black-metal corpse paint (which adherents mislabelled "Goth"). Dark plum-to-black lipstick. In theatrical variants, full white face paint with blacked-out eyes. The face is a mask worn by a child — youth showing through the makeup is characteristic, not a flaw.

### Clothing, materials, textures

A cheap, accessible goth kit: black mesh or netting sleeves and tops; violet or burgundy skirts wrapped in black tulle; long black pseudo-Victorian dresses (earlier, cumbersome); black knit beanie in the Ville Valo style ("шапка-виллевалка"); black wristbands; studded faux-leather chokers; simple gothic ornaments and thin silver-tone pendants, above all the **ankh**. Textures are bargain-bin and tactile: synthetic net, scratchy tulle, acrylic knit, thin velvet ribbon, tarnishing alloy jewelry. Subgenre dials (documented in text, rare in surviving photos): cyber-herki add big platform boots, latex skirts, brightly colored hair streaks, gas masks or respirators; deathrock-herki swap dresses for ripped tights and a temporary mohawk.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Exaggerated black eyeliner + pale powder** (the face as logo).
2. **Soviet-era interior backdrop**: floret-patterned wallpaper, the wall-hung "grandmother's carpet" (бабушкин ковёр), sofa-beds, an ankh pinned to the wall; also stairwells and corridors with two-tone olive-green oil paint. The text names this juxtaposition the main visual motif of the aesthetic in its Russian context.
3. **Ankh symbolism** as jewelry and wall decor, revered as "especially gothic."
4. **HIM fandom imagery**: band posters, disc covers, the heartagram-style broken-heart emblem; the Ville Valo beanie as wearable fandom.
5. **Black mesh/netting + red accent + tulle-wrapped skirt** as the uniform formula.
6. **Cemetery and decay outings** (stalker-goth subtype): benches among graves, cemetery railings, abandoned factories, flowers laid over a coffin-like black surface.
7. **Snapshot props observed in the photo record** (secondary to text): a lit cigarette held with black-nailed fingers, an acoustic guitar, blue choir folders or books held by a black-coated group, claw-hand gestures.
8. **VK/LiveJournal photo overlays**: handwritten Cyrillic captions, scrawled X marks and hearts directly on the image.

### Composition, lighting, medium

Amateur snapshot grammar, circa 2003–2008 compact digital camera: 4:3 frames, low megapixel softness, visible noise, slight color cast. Lighting is whichever was available — harsh direct on-camera flash throwing a hard shadow on the wall behind; flat overcast daylight in cemeteries and pine forests; warm raking sun in a stairwell; night flash outdoors. Composition is centered, sometimes awkwardly cropped: head-and-shoulders portraits turned to profile to show the liner; figures squeezed together on a couch or bench; full-body seated rows; a lying pose among flowers; groups clustered tight in a corner. Nothing is styled by a professional — clutter stays in frame (blurred party-goers, peeling paint, a door latch).

## Mood & Values

Performed sadness and alienation ("nobody understands me"), romanticized death, and total musical devotion to HIM, Lacrimosa, The Rasmus, Evanescena-adjacent melodic darkness. The values are adolescent sincerity and superficiality at once: grief staged with flowers on a piano lid, claw hands in a wallpapered bedroom, white face paint in front of a wool carpet. There is irony baked in — the culture was satirized even by its own practitioners — but the photos read as earnest self-documentation. Authenticity anxiety ("tru" gothic level) hums under everything: the ankh worn proudly, the eyeliner applied thicker than anyone's budget for understanding.

## Era & Origins

Late 1990s–early 2000s, Russia; born from the Russian fanbase of HIM and spread through Vkontakte and LiveJournal photo albums. It reacts to nothing deliberately — it is a commercial simplification of imported goth imagery, made from what a provincial teen could buy: mesh, tulle, a knit hat, an ankh pendant. Its neighbors are Emo, Goth, Mallgoth and Soviet Nostalgia, and it is defined partly by how each of those communities rejected it.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Black first: the costume reads as one dark mass; accents are small and subdued.
- The face is a mask: pale powder, panda-wide black liner, optional drawn tears; youth visible beneath.
- Cheap goth, not couture: mesh, tulle, wristbands, choker, ankh, beanie — accessible items, slightly ill-fitting.
- The backdrop contradicts the costume: Soviet apartment interior (wallpaper, wall carpet, sofa), green-painted stairwell, overcast cemetery, derelict factory, grey courtyard.
- The camera is a character: compact-flash harshness or flat overcast light, low-res noise, awkward crop, optional handwritten Cyrillic overlay.
- Poses perform feeling: downcast gaze, profile turn, cigarette, claw hands, lying among flowers, huddled group intimacy.
- Fandom leaks in: HIM imagery, the beanie, a guitar, song-title melancholy.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo portrait, couple in a pine forest, friend group on a bench or couch, choir-like cluster with folders, cemetery mourner, stairwell smoker, guitar player at night, white-face theatrical trio. Composition: close headshot to full-body seated or lying; centered snapshot framing preferred but any amateur-plausible angle works. Lighting: direct flash, overcast daylight, warm stairwell sun, night flash. Color handling: accent hue may slide red ↔ burgundy ↔ plum ↔ dusty pink, or vanish entirely; backdrop neutrals may be beige, olive, rust or grey. Mood: earnest grief, playful spookiness, corpse-paint theatrics, deadpan boredom. Subgenre dial: core ↔ cyber (respirator, latex, bright hair) ↔ stalker-goth (ruins, graves) ↔ deathrock (ripped tights, mohawk). **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A boy tuning a guitar in a painted corridor, girls sharing a bench between graves, a trio in white face paint by a carpet — all valid, none required.

**Avoid**

- Neon emo palette (hot pink/green accents), fringe-over-one-eye as the sole signifier, MySpace-angle bathroom selfies.
- American mall retail, food courts, suburbia; Hot Topic tripp pants.
- Modern smartphone photography: HDR gloss, computational bokeh, flawless skin, vertical social-media crop with clean type.
- Professional studio lighting, fashion-editorial styling, haute goth couture, adult practitioners with crafted fetishwear.
- Warm, affectionate Soviet-kitsch framing where the carpet and clutter are the beloved subject (that is Soviet Nostalgia).
- Anime/streetwear branding, logo crews, 2020s phone-flash teen gang looks.
- Tidy minimalist interiors, white walls, Scandinavian furniture, golden-hour lifestyle light, saturated tropical color.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell |
|---|---|
| **Mallgoth** | Same superficial goth consumption, but staged in North American malls and suburbs with retail brand gear; no Soviet interiors, no ankh-as-treasure, no Ville Valo beanie, no cemetery benches. |
| **Emo** | Mid-2000s, fringe over one eye, skinny jeans, studded belts, neon accents, emotional direct-to-camera confession; Herki predates/parallels it with powdered pallor, panda liner, mesh-and-tulle dresses and fandom iconography. |
| **Goth** | Older practitioners, crafted historical/fetish wardrobe, club settings, philosophical self-image; Herki is teenage, cheap, domestic, and photographed at home or at graves rather than in clubs. |
| **PMC Ryodan** | 2020s Russian teen crew style from anime/streetwear signifiers and smartphone-era imagery; Herki is 2000s, goth-derived, compact-flash, ankh and beanie instead of logos. |
| **Soviet Nostalgia** | Celebrates USSR material culture warmly — carpet as subject, cozy clutter, fond film tones; in Herki the carpet and oil paint are only the ironic stage for a black-clad teen, and black dominates the frame. |

Evidence note: the cemetery, stairwell and carpet settings dominate the surviving photo sample and match the text's ranking; the cyber- and deathrock-subtype wardrobes are documented in text only and should be treated as licensed variation rather than core imagery.
