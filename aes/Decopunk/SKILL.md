# Decopunk

Decopunk, also called Ottensian Dieselpunk, is the optimistic, deluxe branch of the dieselpunk family: a retrofuturist style that takes the Art Deco and Streamline Moderne language of the 1910s–1940s and projects it into a counterfactual "world of tomorrow" that never met the 1929 crash or the world wars. Where dieselpunk keeps the soot, Decopunk keeps the champagne. Its canonical touchstones are the original *BioShock*, *Sky Captain and the World of Tomorrow*, *The Rocketeer*, the Flash Gordon serials, *Batman: The Animated Series*, *Dick Tracy*, *Grim Fandango* and *Skullgirls* — a lineage that runs from pulp serials through animated noir to game worlds of fallen or flourishing Deco metropolises. The style's emotional contract is machine-age confidence: speed, symmetry, luxury and order, rendered in polished metal and theatrical light.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

Decopunk color is never muddy and never rainbow: each image restricts itself to one of a few documented palette modes, and the metallics are meant to read as light reflecting off polished surfaces rather than as flat pigment.

| Mode | Range (hex) | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Poster triad | vermilion `#C8372D–#E14B3B`, ink black `#0D0D0F–#1A1A1C`, cream white `#F4EEE1`, cool grey `#A9ADB3`, one acid accent (jade/lime `#7CBF3F` or teal `#1E6E64`) | flat graphic fields, hard edges, no gradients |
| Metallic warm | brass/gold `#B08D3E–#D9B44A`, bronze `#8C6A2F`, deep teal/verdigris `#14655C–#3FA08F`, champagne cream `#EFE6D0` | ornamental, jewelled, book-cover and lobby richness |
| Sepia / monochrome | umber `#4A3524–#8A6B4F`, warm grey `#6E675E–#C9C2B8`, or neutral grayscale `#1A1A1A–#E8E8E8` | aged-photograph monumentality, dramatic cloud |
| Painted cityscape | ivory stone `#E8DCC3`, pale sky blue `#B8C6CE`, amber window light `#E8A33D`, dusk orange `#D97A28`, slate shadow `#4E5A66` | gouache matte-painting utopia or amber dusk |

The rule: choose one mode per image; keep saturation controlled everywhere except the poster mode's red; let gold, chrome and teal behave like highlights, not hues.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the text documents them

1. **Streamline Moderne elements** — the style's single named key motif: teardrop and lozenge forms, horizontal speed ribbing, rounded corners, chrome trim bands, porthole windows, aerodynamic fairings on anything that moves.
2. **Art Deco architecture and ornament** — stepped-setback ziggurat towers with spires and mooring masts; sunburst and radiating ray fans; zigzag, chevron, fret and key patterns; fluted pilasters; symmetrical grillework; sculpted winged figures and reliefs on facades.
3. **Retrofuturist machine age** — streamlined rockets, airships moored to tower masts, monorail viaducts threading between skyscrapers, aerodynamic locomotives and cars; raygun-adjacent gadgets (its Raygun Gothic cousin).
4. **Period high fashion** — zoot suits, double-breasted coats, bias-cut dresses, cloche hats, fedoras, wide tilted brims, gloves, T-strap heels; the text is explicit that Decopunk dress is fashionable and classically presentable, never workwear-grimy.
5. **The counterfactual metropolis** — impossible densities of Deco towers, suspension bridges and elevated rails stacked in haze, billboards carrying Deco-styled faces and lettering; the "world of tomorrow" of Ottensian speculation.
6. **Anachronistic modern technology** — the style's signature temporal slip: slightly-too-modern devices such as VHS tapes recast in period cabinets. The text names this clearly; reference imagery shows it rarely, so treat it as optional spice rather than a required element.
7. **Noir-adjacent staging (peripheral)** — fedora silhouettes, long clean shadows, mysterious masked or coated figures; the text files noir (*Casablanca*, *L.A. Noire*, Lovecraft) under "outskirts," so borrow noir's lighting, not its grime.
8. **Deco graphic design** — geometric display capitals, inline and outline lettering, sunburst logotypes, symmetrical chevron or lotus-fret border frames on posters, covers and signage. (Inferred from the Deco grammar and the corpus's poster/cover work rather than spelled out in the text.)

### Materials and textures

Polished chrome and stainless trim; brass and bronze grillework; lacquered and marquetry wood; marble and terrazzo floors; gleaming limestone and cast stone; frosted and etched glass; bakelite and enamel signage. Surfaces are smooth — brushed, polished, lacquered — and carry specular highlights. The only acceptable "texture" beyond that is photographic aging: soft grain, vignetting and cloud-mottled sky in the sepia mode. Dirt, rust and pitting are out of vocabulary.

### Recurring subjects

Monumental cityscapes and tower hero shots; streamlined vehicle portraits (rocket, airship, monorail car, aerodynamic automobile); glamorous fashion figures posed with period posture; interior glamour — lobbies, ballrooms, offices, nightclubs with lamplight on polished stone; poster and cover graphics with symmetrical ornament; still lifes of Deco-cabinet gadgets.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical monumentality: worm's-eye views up setback towers, spires cropping the frame. Symmetrical frontal axes for ornament, posters and ceremonial interiors. Sunburst backlight radiating from behind a central emblem or tower; searchlight beams raking the sky; a low amber sun burning between silhouetted towers; layered depth of bridge, viaduct, mid-ground towers and haze-faded spires. Interiors use theatrical pools of warm light on reflective surfaces. The poster mode replaces all this with flat, even light and hard shadow shapes. Shadows are long, clean and graphic — never cluttered or smoky.

## Mood & Values

Decopunk expresses optimism as luxury: faith in progress, speed and the machine age, dressed in Roaring Twenties decadence. It is utopian retrofuturism in the solarpunk sense — a future that went right, or at least went glamorous. The "-punk" edge survives as an optional ominous undercurrent: a masked figure, an empty ballroom, a fallen-utopia frisson in the *BioShock* manner — but even then the surfaces stay sleek and the geometry stays proud. Values on display: order, symmetry, elegance, civic grandeur, grooming and poise; glamour treated as a civic virtue rather than a private indulgence.

## Era & Origins

The visual grammar is interwar: Art Deco and Streamline Moderne of the 1910s–1940s, with the Roaring Twenties as the emotional core. The fiction is counterfactual — a timeline uninterrupted by Black Tuesday and the world wars, extrapolated forward. The label itself is a product of online retrofuturist taxonomy, the optimistic "Ottensian" half of the dieselpunk spectrum, grouping media that span from the 1936 Flash Gordon serials and *Metropolis* through *Batman: The Animated Series*, *BioShock* and *Sky Captain* to recent revivals like *Lackadaisy* and *Superman* (2025). It is therefore a retrospective style: modern hands painting a future that the 1920s imagined.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Build from the grammar, not from a scene: setbacks, sunbursts, chevrons, streamline curves, bilateral symmetry. Any subject wearing this grammar reads Decopunk.
- Keep it sleek. Every metal and stone surface should catch a highlight; polish is the style's fingerprint.
- Restrict the palette to one documented mode and let metallics behave like light.
- Play monumentality against the human: tiny figures at the base of a ziggurat, or a single glamorously lit figure owning an interior.
- Add at most one speculative or anachronistic note — a mooring mast, a rocket pad on a crown, a VHS slot in a lacquered console — to signal counterfactual rather than documentary.
- Light theatrically: sunburst backlight, searchlights, low amber sun, lamplit pools. No flat documentary light except in the deliberate poster mode.
- Commit to one finish mode (flat vector poster, gouache matte painting, sepia photograph, clean digital render, animation cel) and keep it consistent across the image.
- Use in-world Deco typography — geometric capitals, inline lettering, sunburst logotypes — on signage and borders when text appears.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: cityscape, tower, vehicle, fashion figure, interior, gadget still life, poster/cover graphic, masked or noir-tinged character study.
- *Composition*: worm's-eye vertical, symmetrical frontal, layered city depth with bridges and viaducts, flat poster planes, intimate interior framing.
- *Lighting*: bright utopian daylight, amber dusk, searchlit night, theatrical interior pools, noir-adjacent but clean shadow, flat graphic light.
- *Color handling*: any of the four palette modes; monochrome and sepia are fully in-style.
- *Mood*: triumphant optimism, glamorous mystery, subdued fallen-utopia menace — sleekness constant across all three.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A Decopunk picture of a seaside bathhouse, a radio studio, or a garden terrace is perfectly valid if it carries the grammar, the gleam and the palette.

**Avoid**

- Rust, soot, grime, oil stains, smog, distressed metal — that is dieselpunk's territory.
- Wartime austerity, camouflage, military hardware as the focus.
- Victorian/Edwardian brass, clockwork, goggles, corsets, steam pipes — steampunk.
- Googie roofs, tailfins, atomic boomerangs and orbit motifs, diner neon — atompunk.
- Exposed ducts, pipes and structural trusses of late-20th-century high-tech architecture — Factory Pomo.
- Seamless modern glass curtain walls without setbacks or ornament; contemporary digital UI minimalism.
- Cyberpunk neon rain, holograms, chrome-and-black dystopian grime.
- Sinuous organic Art Nouveau whiplash ornament; handcrafted folk or cottagecore textures.
- Casual contemporary clothing and grooming; sportswear; distressed denim.
- Muddy desaturated "gritty realism" grading and heavy photograin used as dirt rather than as period photograph aging.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Art Deco (historical):** same ornamental grammar, but historical Deco is documentary — real buildings, real objects, no counterfactual layer. Decopunk adds the -punk: impossible metropolis scale, speculative machines, anachronisms, staged fictional-world glamour, and stylized finishes (poster flatness, sepia render, animation cel) that a period photograph would never show.
- **Dieselpunk:** shared era, opposite skin. Dieselpunk is gritty and dark — soot, rust, smog, wartime hardware, exhausted people. Decopunk is sleeker and shiny: polished chrome, champagne glassware, utopian gleam. If the metal is rusty and the air is smoky, you have left Decopunk.
- **Atompunk:** 1950s–60s atomic age — Googie coffee shops, tailfins, boomerang and starburst-orbit motifs, suburbia, space-race white-and-red finned rockets. Decopunk is pre-war: ziggurat setbacks, radial sunbursts (not atomic orbits), streamline curves, fedoras instead of boomerang prints.
- **Steampunk:** Victorian machine fantasy — steam, clockwork, brass goggles, wood and leather, corseted silhouettes. Decopunk is the electric machine age: chrome, bakelite, mass media billboards, serial-hero rockets, 20th-century tailoring.
- **Factory Pomo:** postmodern industrial high-tech architecture with exposed services, structural expressionism and primary-color ducts; ornament-free and narrative-free. Decopunk hides its machinery behind sleek cladding and loads its surfaces with applied, symmetrical, historicist ornament.
- **Raygun Gothic (adjacent):** pulp tin-toy gadgetry — bulbous helmets, finned toy rockets, serial props. Decopunk is broader and more elegant: it includes fashion, interiors, cityscapes and poster design, and keeps Streamline Moderne poise rather than pulp crudeness.
- **Hardboiled noir (outskirts):** Decopunk borrows noir's fedora silhouettes and long shadows but keeps warm metallic palettes and polished surfaces; classic noir is greyer, grainier and cynically lit. Noir in Decopunk is a costume, not a worldview.
