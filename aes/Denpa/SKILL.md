# Denpa

Denpa (電波, "electromagnetic waves") is the visual arm of a Japanese subgenre of psychological horror born in visual novels. Its images stage a single governing idea: an ordinary, even banal environment — a street corner of utility poles, a classroom after hours, a rooftop, a bedroom lit by monitors — invaded by a subjective distortion, so that the viewer cannot be sure whether the intrusion is in the world or in the head. The name comes from the delusion of being controlled by radio waves; the aesthetic keeps that literal vocabulary of wires, signals, static and interference as its picture-language. Crucially, denpa is unified by *visual subjectivity* rather than one house style: the art shifts to mirror a destabilized mind, which is why the same genre can appear as grey ink-on-paper graphics, as night-blue anime, as blinding summer pastel, or as corridors of meat.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette.** Any given image commits to one of five registers, and the tension is carried by content rather than by mixing palettes:

- *Static grey and ink:* paper and concrete greys (#B9BDBF, #8E9496, #6E7476) with pure ink black (#0B0B0C) line work — the graphic-design register of wires silhouetted on overcast paper.
- *Night blue:* near-black navy through cold steel blue (#0A1220, #14263F, #1E3A5F, cloud #3E5C76), often with one pale light source or a huge moon.
- *Sickly teal-green and olive:* desaturated greens and sepia-olive interiors (#2F4F4A, #4A6B62, #6B6B4A) for rooms, stairwells, classrooms.
- *False paradise / moe façade:* deceptively bright pastels and candy saturation — sky blue #4DA6E0, sunflower #F2C94C, pink #F7B9D4, streamer magenta #FF4FA0 — used straight, with the wrongness supplied by context, a glitch bar, or a dissonant line of text.
- *Flesh and alarm red:* visceral reds (#8E1A1A, #C0392B, #D94F5C) flooding black or white fields for hallucinatory breakdown.

Vermilion kanji (#D7263D) and small monospace black type are permitted accents in every register. Desaturation is the default weather; saturation is always a mask or a scream.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the tradition documents them.** First and most ubiquitous: tangled telephone wires and utility poles webbing the sky, and their monumental cousin the transmission tower, read as the physical body of the "network" that connects and controls. Second: the mundane-setting-with-distortion juxtaposition itself — a normal room, street or rooftop carrying one impossible element. Third: high-contrast shadow and chiaroscuro, noir lighting falling across faces and walls. Fourth: signal degradation — static, glitch blocks, CRT scanlines, low-resolution dither, corrupted strings of text. Fifth: urban isolation in liminal non-places — rooftops (the rooftop-as-antenna is foundational), empty streets, train platforms, stairwells, air-conditioner units on blank façades. Sixth: the cluttered dim bedroom of monitors, figurines and trash, but only ever as a symptom of withdrawal, never as cozy décor. Seventh: the idyllic summer rooftop under blue sky with sunflowers, deployed as false paradise or looping purgatory. Eighth: the hyper-saturated streamer-interface and moe presentation as disarming façade. Ninth: grotesque organic distortion — walls of meat, slime, decay — standing in for agnosia and perceptual collapse. Tenth, the fan-born "layered reality" edit: rough 2D line art or manga-panel figures superimposed semi-transparently over gloomy lo-fi photography, blended into concrete and cloud like ghosts, often accompanied by typewriter or monospace text fragments and visual-novel dialogue boxes.

**Materials and textures.** Paper grain and photocopy toner; pen ink and halftone dots; CRT phosphor glow and scanlines; pixel dither and compression smear; concrete, vinyl siding, copper wire, cloud. Surfaces should feel transmitted, copied, or remembered — never freshly rendered.

**Recurring subjects.** A single isolated adolescent or young adult, in school uniform or plain clothes, seen from behind, half-transparent, or staring past the camera; a hikikomori among screens; a figure balanced on a rooftop edge; an empty classroom or corridor; suburban houses under a sky that occupies two-thirds of the frame; the moon; wires, always wires.

**Composition and lighting.** Worm's-eye views up into the wire web; wires crossing the frame as diagonals that cage the sky; tiny figures dwarfed by infrastructure or weather; collage layouts of photo fragments, flat color rectangles and typed text blocks; visual-novel framing with a dialogue box and name plate anchoring the bottom; centered symmetry of empty liminal space. Light is overcast and flat, or nocturnal with a single practical source, or harsh noir chiaroscuro, or the cold glow of a monitor in a dark room, or deceptively bright noon with hard shadows — always emotionally charged, never neutral.

## Mood & Values

Dissociation, derealization, the feeling of being watched or steered by invisible signals; the paradox of the relationless society (*muen shakai*) — dense cities, total disconnection. Denpa values subjective truth over objective reality: the distorted view is the honest one. It is bleak, pessimistic, introspective, and quietly tender toward its deluded subjects, because the delusion is also a refuge. Escapism, conspiracy thinking, and the commodified fantasy world are treated with simultaneous sympathy and critique.

## Era & Origins

The slang derives from the 1981 Fukagawa street killings, whose perpetrator claimed "poisonous radio waves" drove him; the term matured in 1990s Japan around anxieties of technology and withdrawal. The visual language crystallized 1996–2001 in early visual novels and in anime such as Serial Experiments Lain, under the influence of film noir, Kiyoshi Kurosawa, and David Lynch, on PC-98 and early Windows hardware whose dither and scanlines became stylistic DNA. A 2010s–2020s resurgence (Subarashiki Hibi, NEEDY STREAMER OVERLOAD, Milk inside a bag of milk...) extended it into streamer-interface and indie-game registers.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Anchor in the mundane first: a real, ordinary Japanese urban or domestic space, rendered specifically (poles, vents, siding, desks, rails).
2. Add exactly one layer of wrongness — a translucent sketch figure, a corrupted text line, a fleshy wall, a doubled object, an impossible sky. Restraint is the horror.
3. Let wires and signals structure the composition whenever the scene allows sky.
4. Treat text as a diegetic object: monospace fragments, glitched strings, dialogue boxes, kanji stamps.
5. Degrade the signal: grain, scanlines, dither, paper texture, ink line — choose a fidelity register and hold it.
6. Keep the figure isolated; crowds appear only as blurred, indifferent masses or as irony beneath a caption about connection.
7. Commit to one palette register; dissonance comes from the event, not from palette soup.

Variation axes: subject (street, rooftop, bedroom, classroom, streamer UI, flesh dreamscape, pure graphic collage); composition (worm's-eye wire web, liminal centered wide, collage layering, VN box framing); lighting (overcast, nocturnal single-source, chiaroscuro, CRT glow, false noon); color (any of the five registers); mood (quiet dissociation, overt hallucinatory terror, weaponized cheer). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: neon cyberpunk futurism, chrome and holograms; glossy modern 3D or clean gradient anime rendering; sincere cute moe with no uncanny counterweight; merch-cluttered rooms played as cozy hobby dens; generic surrealism with no mundane anchor; romanticized lo-fi "sad bedroom" comfort; random glitch spam without narrative logic; warm nostalgic retro; social warmth and connection imagery played straight; high-fidelity photography with no degradation layer in the layered-reality register.

## Discrimination Cues

Against **Akibapop**: Akibapop is the same name's music cousin — candy-bright, high-pitched, sincerely cute. Denpa borrows that cuteness only as a façade; look for the crack in the sugar (glitch, dissonant text, empty eyes, wires behind the pastel). Against **Divine Machinery**: both love cables, but Divine Machinery frames infrastructure as sublime, sacred, monumental, often warm-rusted and awe-filled; denpa's wires are mundane, oppressive, paranoid, tied to a suffering individual. Against **Otaku**: the merch-filled room alone is Otaku or Animecore self-portraiture; denpa requires the room to read as symptom — distress, interference, delusion pressing in. Against **Corporate Grunge**: shared desaturated grime, but Corporate Grunge satirizes institutions (offices, copiers, beige bureaucracy); denpa stays inside one psyche in domestic and street liminal space. Against **Cyber Grunge**: Cyber Grunge is street-level futurity — neon, rain, chrome, modded hardware; denpa's technology is deliberately dated (CRT, telephone copper, PC-98 dither) and its dread is psychological, not socioeconomic. The final tell across all neighbors: denpa images feel like a transmission from someone who is not sure you, or they, are real.
