# Oi! Skinhead

Oi! Skinhead is the "Second Wave" of the British skinhead subculture, born around 1977–1979 in London's East End and named by journalist Garry Bushell after the Cockney Rejects' stage greeting. It is a deliberately unpolished, durable, intimidating uniform designed for streets, pubs and football terraces: where the 1969 Trojan skinhead looked to Mod polish and Jamaican rocksteady, the Oi! skin looked to punk rawness, industrial workwear and surplus military gear. The aesthetic prizes hardness as honesty — "dirty, grimy, ugly" over plastic polish — and carries its politics on the sleeve of class pride rather than art-school theory. Modern documentation (the 2010s "Third Wave" revival in Poland, Spain, France, the USA) keeps the 1980s rules but widens the cast: skingirls/bootgirls, mixed crews, varied ethnicities, lookbook-style catalog shots alongside estate snapshots.

## Visual Identity

Four anchors carry every correct image, with one accent family:

| Role | Range | Typical carrier |

| Jet black | `#0D0D0D` – `#1C1C1C` | bombers, polos, tees, boots, tights, bags |
| Bleached denim | indigo ground `#2E4A62` – `#35506B`, bleach clouds `#D8DEE4` – `#F4F5F1` | splattered "snowflake" jeans, white denim variants |
| Olive drab | `#55603A` – `#77804F` | MA-1 nylon bombers, camo trousers |
| Accent scarlet / red | `#B01E24` – `#D2232E` | braces, sock bands, tartan, vinyl bags, headbands |
| Support neutrals | off-white polo `#E9E8E2`, concrete grey, brick brown-red, dull park green | shirts, environments |

Rule: mid-to-low saturation everywhere; red behaves as hardware (straps, socks, check lines, bag gloss), never as a garment field except a tartan skirt or lining; no neon, no pastel, no metallic sheen beyond boot polish and vinyl.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual authority

1. **Shaven head** — #0 to #1 shave, bald or stubble; the single defining marker versus every neighbouring style.
2. **Bleached denim** — dark jeans splattered with bleach into snowflake or camo clouds, worn tight ("drainpipes") and cuffed so the boot shaft shows.
3. **MA-1 flight jacket** — nylon bomber in olive green or black (Alpha Industries lineage), ribbed knit collar/cuffs/waistband, optionally patched with band, brand or flag cloth.
4. **High-lace Dr. Martens** — 10-hole or higher, shined to parade gloss, black or cherry; grooved sole edge and yellow welt stitch; lace colours sometimes code identities but historically inconsistently, so treat lace colour as free variation.
5. **Thin braces (suspenders)** — worn over T-shirts or polos, frequently scarlet, sometimes hanging off the shoulders or hooked at the waist with thumbs through.
6. **Polo shirts and band T-shirts** — piqué polos with tipped collars (Fred Perry lineage); plain or circular-logo screen-printed tees (street-punk band graphics, Trojan-helmet-style emblems).
7. **Donkey jacket** — traditional British industrial workwear wool coat with leather shoulder panel; text-documented proletarian signal, rare in surviving imagery.
8. **Visible tattoos** — neck, hands, face per the text; sleeves and forearms in practice.
9. **Tartan accents** — red tartan skirts, jacket linings, headbands: image-documented revival and skingirl crossover accent, silent in the text; treat as legitimate secondary spice, never the main garment field for men.
10. **Terrace/street props** — vinyl shoulder bags (Lonsdale, Warrior Clothing lineage), pub bench bottles, crew clusters; football terrace and pub forecourt as habitat.

### Materials and textures

Nylon twill with a dull sheen and ribbed knit trim; stiff denim with bleach-corroded crust and whiskering; smooth bovine leather buffed to mirror gloss on boot shafts; piqué cotton knit; wool tartan; black opaque tights; glossy PVC bag surfaces; environment of rough brick, spalled concrete, asphalt, painted metal stair rails and weathered wood panelling. Wear is mandatory: creased elbows, scuffed toe caps, faded knees.

### Recurring subjects

Solo full-length portraits; paired or trio crew shots with arms over shoulders; mixed-gender crews and skingirls (polo or tee, braces, tartan skirt or denim shorts, tights, boots); adolescents to forties; detail studies of boot shafts, laces and cuffed hems; group rows in alleys or yard gates. Band-promo stiffness and casual bench candour both belong.

### Composition, lighting, medium

Composition obeys the **cuffed-hem rule**: framing routinely runs head-to-toe so the boot shaft, sock band and cuff read clearly; straight-on eye-level documentary stance, weight on one leg, hands in pockets or thumbs in braces; groups arranged in a tight row or wedge, bodies touching. Detail crops isolate boots against brick or pavement. Backdrops are working-class exterior: council-estate yards, brick alleys, terrace steps, park benches, metal stairwells, pub forecourts — or a plain panelled interior wall for lookbook-flat catalog poses. Lighting is flat and honest: overcast north-light daylight, open shade, occasional on-camera flash in dim interiors, even indoor panel light for catalog work, and a harsh high-contrast monochrome mode for promo/gig mood. Medium reads as amateur 35mm or early-digital snapshot: mild grain, slight colour cast, unretouched skin, no bokeh theatre, no glamour key light.

## Mood & Values

Defiance worn as dignity. The style expresses class pride, street authenticity, territorial loyalty and a "boot-to-the-bollocks" rage against unemployment, police harassment and condescension; its anti-intellectualism is a stance of directness, not stupidity — gang vocals shouting together, crews linked arm-over-arm, the collective voice over the solo virtuoso. Hardness signals honesty: polish on boots yes, polish on image no. Beneath the glare sits mourning for deindustrialized streets, and a stubborn communal joy (bench lager, terrace chants, kids-united solidarity).

## Era & Origins

Late 1970s United Kingdom, London East End (c. 1977–1979), coalescing as the first Trojan wave faded into Suedehead/Smoothie styles and street youth rejected punk's perceived art-school drift. Named in 1980 by Garry Bushell; soundtrack of Sham 69, Cockney Rejects, The 4-Skins, Cock Sparrer, Angelic Upstarts. Thatcher-era realities feed the lyric sheet and the look. The 1981 Southall riot and the *Strength Thru Oi!* compilation fused the aesthetic to far-right imagery in public memory, though the scene was always fragmented — anti-racist and left-wing wings (Angelic Upstarts, later SHARP, Redskins) are equally part of its history. A meticulous, ethnically diverse Third Wave revival from the 2010s (Poland, Spain, France, USA; bands like Crown Court) supplies much of today's visual documentation.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Build the silhouette top-down: shaven or stubble head → utilitarian top (olive/black nylon bomber or donkey jacket; or polo/band tee with thin braces) → tight cuffed bleached or black drainpipe denim → polished high-lace boots. The boots must be visible; the cuff exists to show them.
2. Keep colour inside the anchor palette; spend red only on braces, socks, tartan, bags.
3. Prefer texture over finish: nylon sheen, bleach crust, leather gloss, brick dust, knit rib.
4. Set scenes in working-class habitat or flat catalog interiors; never glamour locations.
5. Pose documentary: squared stance, thumbs in braces, hands in pockets, arms over shoulders, seated slump with boots forward.
6. Light flat: overcast day, shade, even indoor panel light, or harsh monochrome contrast.
7. Render as snapshot medium: grain, cast, real wear, no airbrush.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo male or female, mixed crew, teen to middle-aged, any ethnicity (Third Wave reality), boot detail crop, terrace crowd, bench candid, catalog lookbook pose. Composition: full-length straight-on, three-quarter, boot close-up, group row, wedge cluster, seated bench. Lighting: overcast daylight, flat indoor, flash-in-dark, high-contrast black-and-white. Colour handling: full snapshot colour, muted desaturation, monochrome. Mood: defiant stare, laughing crew, bored slump, chant-time roar. Era inflection: 1979–84 grainy film vs 2010s crisp digital revival; feminine variants (Chelsea-style crop, short bob with shaved sides, headband, tartan skirt, tights) sit inside the revival range. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Mohawks, liberty spikes, dyed crests, safety pins, ripped bondage trousers, studded leather biker jackets (70s punk).
- Crombie overcoats, Harringtons with straight unwashed 501s, pork-pie hats, visible mod crops (Trojan/Suedehead).
- Designer sportswear logos, trainers, fleeces, bucket hats (Casuals).
- Tracksuits, Air Max soles, neon or fluorescent accents, rave context (Gabber).
- Long flowing hair, glam makeup, silk ties, tailoring, luxury fabrics.
- Neon or pastel hues, gradient fades, airbrushed skin, studio rim light, golden hour, dreamy bokeh.
- Factory-new garments without crease, scuff or fade.
- Hate insignia of any kind: swastikas and far-right emblems are outside the style and prohibited; the subculture's documented anti-racist wing (SHARP, Redskins) is as authentic as its controversies. Patches stay band logos, brand labels or generic flags.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Punk (1976 art-school):** punk destroys clothes (rips, pins, studs, collage tartan) and keeps hair as sculpture (mohawks, dyed crests); Oi! skinhead builds a durable uniform (bomber, denim, boots) and removes hair entirely. Punk sneers and performs; skin stands squared and still.
- **Vs Suedehead / Trojan Skinhead (1969):** those keep a #2–#4 crop, Harringtons, Crombies, sheepskin, straight Levi's 501 or Sta-Prest, 8-hole boots and mod polish; Oi! answers with bald stubble, nylon MA-1, bleached splattered drainpipes, 10-to-20-hole parade-gloss boots and punk band graphics instead of reggae references.
- **Vs Casuals:** same terraces, opposite wardrobe — Casuals wear European designer sportswear (Fila, Stone Island, Adidas) and trainers with casual hair; Oi! wears workwear, military nylon and boots, and the shaven head is non-negotiable.
- **Vs Gabber:** gabber shares the shaved skull but pairs it with Dutch rave sportswear — tracksuits, Air Max, fluorescent flashes, pacifier props, hardcore-logo tees — in rave or industrial-party context; Oi! pairs it with denim, leather gloss and nylon bombers in street, pub or terrace context.
- **Vs generic/first-wave Skinhead:** look for the Second Wave tells — bleach-splattered denim, higher boot shafts, more visible tattooing, band-logo tees and a harder, punk-derived posture; the first wave reads smoother, modder, reggae-adjacent.

**Evidence note:** the donkey jacket and face/neck tattoos are text-documented but scarce in surviving imagery; lace colour-coding is textually flagged as inconsistent, so treat it as free detail; tartan skirts, vinyl shoulder bags and feminine crop haircuts are image-documented revival accents the text does not list — legitimate variation, not canon.
