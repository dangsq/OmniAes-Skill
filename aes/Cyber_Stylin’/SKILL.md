# Cyber Stylin’

## Overview

Cyber Stylin’ is a blanket term for the family of art styles that populated girl-targeted internet spaces of the 2000s and 2010s: Flash dress-up games, avatar creators, pixel dolls (Dollz), Korean dollmakers, and standalone fashion illustrations of girls. The "Cyber" denotes origin — online, digital, customizable — not futurism. What binds the family is not one line style but a shared purpose: the figure is a *doll*, a fashion mannequin for identity play, rendered in clean digital gloss and dressed in the Y2K consumer wardrobe of the period. Regional and authorial variants exist (Western Bratz-flavored vector glamour, Korean airbrushed Enakei, tiny pixel Dollz sprites, detailed Candybar-style dollmaker renders), and all belong here. The aesthetic died down as Flash platforms went defunct around 2020, which is exactly why its surviving visual language now reads as nostalgic.

## Visual Identity

### Medium and surface
Three surface treatments cover the family, and an image commits to one:
1. **Hard-edge vector cel**: flat color fills, one crisp shadow step per form, white specular streaks on hair, lips and patent accessories; no outlines or only thin clean ones.
2. **Soft airbrush** (the Enakei / Jennie Enakei line): smooth gradient blending, porcelain or softly blushed skin, diffuse glow, still perfectly clean — no brush texture.
3. **Pixel doll sprite**: small, limited-palette figures with visible pixel stepping, presented like game assets on plain grounds.
What never appears: painterly strokework, paper or canvas grain, watercolor bleed, photographic grain, 3D-render shading.

### Palette rule
Every image draws from one hue family — pink, purple/lilac, white, brown, blue (denim to teal), black — with gold as the metallic accent and mint/aqua as a frequent cool counterpoint. Within that family an image picks one of two regimes: a **pastel sorbet** scheme (bubblegum and blush pinks around #F781B9–#FBD3E0, lilac #E6D7F7, cream white, mint #BFE8E0) or a **bold saturated** scheme (fuchsia, teal, canary, cherry red on black or brown grounds, gold #D4AF37 hardware). Brown carries real weight — chocolate hair, tan trench coats, bronze skin — which keeps the palette from collapsing into pure pink. Skin ranges from porcelain to deep brown, with tanned/bronze very common; hair glosses in brown, blonde, black or fantasy tints.

### The doll figure
Proportions are fashion-illustration elongated: long legs, narrow waist, small hips, oversized head-to-body emphasis in the kawaii variants. Faces carry either large glossy eyes (cute East Asian line) or narrow stylized eyes with heavy lid shadow (glamour line); lips are full and glossed. Hair is a mass with highlight bands, never flyaway-realistic. Poses are display poses — hand on hip, accessory held forward, weight on one leg — because the figure’s job is to show the outfit head to toe.

### Wardrobe, bling and props (ranked by textual prominence)
The style’s core documented vocabulary is the Y2K closet: low-rise jeans, mini skirts, short shorts, crop tops, tank tops, strapless tops, baby tees; then bling — necklaces, earrings, bracelets, belts and waist chains; then headwear and carry — baseball caps, newsboy caps, barrettes, beanies, handbags, sunglasses. Fur trim, Uggs-style boots, tiaras and layered pearl strands sit just behind these. Props such as lap cats, plush bears, parfaits, scooters, snowboards and flip phones appear frequently as accessory-props that extend the outfit’s story; they are secondary to the clothing grammar. Seasonal dress-up sets (ski gear, fur-hooded winter coats, mittens) are a legitimate subset, inherited from dress-up game category tabs.

### Composition and framing
Composition follows doll-asset logic rather than scene logic. Typical frames: a single full-body doll centered on white or a flat tinted ground; a parallel-to-picture-plane lineup of two to five dolls; an avatar bust or face close-up cropped for a banner or signature; a grid of faces; occasionally a soft romantic pair portrait in the airbrushed line. Backgrounds, when present, are wallpaper not world: flat stripes, heart or smiley repeats, halftone dots, palm or floral silhouettes, soft gradients. Depth, perspective rooms and rendered environments are foreign to the style.

### Light, gloss and sparkle
Lighting is decorative, not physical. The vector line uses one hard shadow step plus white glints on hair, lips, jewelry and patent leather; the airbrushed line uses even diffuse glow with no cast shadows at all. Four-point sparkle stars punctuate bling and hair. There is no sun direction, no golden hour, no chiaroscuro.

### Type and graphics
Wordmarks in script, bubble letters or novelty gothic fonts ("Girl", "believe", brand and site names) often share the frame, along with glitter glints, hearts and star bursts — the Glitter Graphics adjacency. Watermark-style site URLs in a corner are period-authentic.

## Mood & Values

The style expresses self-love, self-care, personal expression and femininity as play. Dressing the doll is identity work: confident, aspirational, best-friend-coded (squads matter), never ironic or cynical. Youth and nostalgia saturate it — it feels like a remembered bedroom computer screen. Cozy kawaii warmth, glam diva confidence and punk-lite "power babe" energy are all valid emotional registers inside the same feminine frame.

## Era & Origins

Roots run to Dollz bases of 1997 (The Palace) and the late-1990s pixel doll communities; the look consolidated in the early-to-mid 2000s on Flash dress-up sites (Doll Divine, Stardoll, Candybar Dollmaker, Chicaloca, Sugar Luxe, Miss Bimbo, My Scene, Bratz and Barbie Flash games) and in Korean digital illustration (Enakei from around 2010–2012). It mirrors the McBling-era consumer wardrobe (Pink, Bebe, Claire’s, Justice, Victoria’s Secret, plus luxe-logo aspiration) and shojo manga’s fashion obsession (Paradise Kiss, Nana). Flash’s discontinuation in late 2020 froze the style as nostalgia, with revival platforms (Everskies, Fashion Dreamer) extending it.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Render a stylized fashion doll, not a person: clean digital surface, gloss highlights, display pose. Dress her in the documented Y2K grammar with at least two bling or headwear accents. Commit to one palette regime (pastel sorbet or bold saturated) inside the pink-purple-white-brown-blue-black family plus gold. Keep the ground flat or wallpaper-like. Light decoratively: cel shadow steps or airbrushed glow, sparkle glints on metal and hair. Optionally integrate a script/bubble wordmark or glitter graphics as period framing.

**Variation axes.** Subject: single doll, squad lineup, avatar bust, face grid, doll with pet/plush/dessert/vehicle prop, seasonal set, soft pair portrait. Medium: vector cel, airbrushed Enakei, pixel sprite, detailed dollmaker render. Composition: isolated asset, patterned wallpaper ground, banner with logo, lineup, grid. Color: pastel, bold, or brown-black-gold glamour. Mood: playful, cozy, glam, punk-lite, dreamy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style’s rules, not any reference image — a doll on a scooter, a winter set, or a face grid none of which match a reference can all be perfectly on-style.

**Avoid.** Literal cyberpunk or sci-fi reading of "Cyber": neon grids, chrome bodies, circuitry, holograms. Chrome/liquid-metal Y2K Futurism as the dominant material. Photorealism, camera grain, depth of field, naturalistic light direction. Painterly texture, watercolor, paper grain, risograph noise. 2020s silhouettes: high-waisted wide-leg denim, athleisure, quiet-luxury minimalism. Grunge desaturation or earthy boho. Perspective environments and rendered interiors. Manga inking with screentones and speed lines as the surface language. Matte modern flat-design minimalism without gloss or bling. Any loss of the feminine, girly register that the style treats as its constant.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Bubblegum Bitch** shares pink attitude but is a 2010s internet mood aesthetic, often photographic or meme-graphic, with pink monoculture; Cyber Stylin’ is doll illustration with a full multi-hue Y2K wardrobe grammar.
- **Curly Girly** is soft-romantic: curls, florals, vintage tenderness, natural texture; Cyber Stylin’ is glossy digital streetwear bling with caps and waist chains, not flower crowns.
- **Barbiecore** is fuchsia monoculture tied to the Barbie brand in any medium; Cyber Stylin’ keeps brown, black, blue and purple in play, favors tanned Bratz-like dolls, and is bound to the doll-art medium.
- **Gyaru** is a photographed real-world Japanese street fashion; Cyber Stylin’ may depict gyaru-flavored tanned dolls but remains illustration and game art, never street photography.
- **McBling** is the photographic celebrity-consumer surface of the same years (rhinestone logos, velour, flip phones); the wedge is medium — McBling is photo and media imagery, Cyber Stylin’ is the vector/pixel doll interpretation of that wardrobe.
- Broad vector-web families (Vectordelia, Old Web graphics) contain this style but lack its specific content: the customizable feminine fashion doll.

**Evidence note.** The five Dollz substyle names (Preps, Minis, Wonderkins, Silents, Diva) and the Enakei/Jennie Enakei split are documented in text but visually underspecified here; treat them as lineage labels, not visual rules. Pixel-sprite resolution conventions beyond "small stylized base" are likewise thin in the evidence and should not be over-specified.
