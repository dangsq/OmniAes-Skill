# Darksynth

## Overview

Darksynth is a microgenre of electronic music that coalesced around 2012, primarily in France and the United States, as the darker, heavier, more aggressive offshoot of Synthwave. Where its parent genre Outrun celebrates bright 80s pop nostalgia, Darksynth abandons optimism entirely: its visual language is a deliberate break, built from 1970s–80s horror and science-fiction film imagery (John Carpenter above all), cyberpunk dystopia, occult and satanic symbolism, and themes of ultra-violence. The result is a cohesive "retro-noir" or "cyber-doom" atmosphere — analog-degraded, neon-lit, menacing. The style's natural carriers are album covers, music videos and artist branding, so square cover formats with a dominant logotype are common, though the aesthetic extends freely to posters, illustrations and geometric sigil graphics. Its producers came largely from the metal scene, and that lineage shows visually: chrome beveled logotypes, skulls and occult emblems borrowed from heavy metal sleeve art, rewired through synthesizer neon and VHS decay.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The ground is always near-black: `#050508` to `#14141c`, sometimes crushed to pure black at the edges. Onto that void, saturated neon is applied as light, not as decoration. The working accent range is:

- **Deep red / blood crimson** `#7a0000`–`#d40022` — the occult and violence register; used as full-scene flood light (cathedral interiors, demon crowds) or as sigil color.
- **Magenta / hot pink** `#ff2a6d`–`#ff4fd8` — the genre's default glow; skies, rims, script neon.
- **Violet / purple** `#5b21b6`–`#a020f0` — bridge tone between magenta and black; haze, gradients, occult interiors.
- **Electric blue / cyan** `#00d4ff`–`#3a6bff` — the technological register; city windows, grids, chrome reflections.
- **Sparing toxic accents**: acid green `#39ff14` for glowing eyes and visors, rare ember orange for fire.

Rule of thumb: two or three neon accents maximum against black, each behaving as an emissive source with bloom and fog around it. Pastels, daylight whites, earth tones and warm natural skin lighting are outside the palette. Monochrome-flood variants (almost entirely red-on-black, or magenta-on-black) are fully in style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Cyberpunk dystopia / neon noir cityscape** — monumental skylines of black spires, rain-slick streets, tiny silhouetted figures dwarfed by architecture, neon perspective grids receding to a horizon.
2. **Occult and satanic symbolism** — pentagrams and inverted crosses, sigil geometry (circles, triangles, polygon frames enclosing an emblem), demonic figures, horned masks, cathedral and ritual-chamber interiors bathed in red.
3. **80s horror / sci-fi iconography** — slasher-mask killers, hooded or helmeted figures with glowing eyes, androids and cyborgs in chrome bodysuits, visors and headphones, panthers and monstrous animals, Carpenter-esque dread.
4. **Skulls** — chrome or bone skulls as central emblems, skull masks, bone piles; often fused with tech (wires, implants) or occult tentacles.
5. **Ultra-violence** — present as menace and threat: blades, demonic crowds, bound or imperiled figures. In practice the sampled artwork stylizes violence through illustration and implication rather than photographic gore.
6. **Neon noir lighting itself as motif** — glow, fog, rim light, CRT bloom.

Carrier motifs inherited from the genre's metal and tape-culture lineage: chrome or steel beveled script logotypes (metal-band lettering), neon-tube script titles, pixel/OCR block type, distressed stencil captions; and analog degradation — VHS noise, scanlines, film grain, halftone print dots, cracked and worn poster surfaces, chromatic aberration.

### Materials and textures

The dominant medium is digital illustration emulating 1980s airbrush poster and comic-book art: ink linework, halftone dots, smooth airbrush gradients on latex, chrome and flesh. Secondary registers include flat neon linework sigils on black (geometric, almost vector-occult), pixel-type graphics, and distressed printed-poster surfaces with torn or cracked edges. Chrome and polished metal recur as materials — mirrors, masks, bodysuits, logotype metal — always reflecting neon rather than daylight. Fog, haze and smoke carry the light volumetrically.

### Recurring subjects

Masked or hooded killers; android and cyborg women in chrome or latex; hooded figures with luminous eyes; demons and occult congregations; chrome skulls; dystopian cities and cathedral interiors; neon grids and sigil diagrams; weapons and machines; band logotypes as the sole subject. Any of these can carry an image alone.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Symmetry is common: central emblem or altar arrangements, flanking figures mirroring each other, sigil geometry centered on black. The cover-style stack — logotype at top, title at bottom, emblem or figures between — is a frequent skeleton but not mandatory. Equally characteristic is the cinematic wide shot: a lone small silhouette against a vast city, spire field or ritual hall. Lighting is low-key chiaroscuro where neon is the only source: rim light carving chrome and latex, backlit fog, a single-hue flood saturating an interior, glowing eyes or sigils acting as key lights, horizon grids emitting from below.

## Mood & Values

Aggression, darkness, intensity, rebellion. Darksynth sells cathartic menace: the visceral fast-paced action of *Hotline Miami*, the dread of Carpenter's corridors, the transgression of occult imagery. Its nostalgia is specifically for *dark* 80s media — slashers, dystopias, tape-traded horror — and its posture is anti-optimist, a rejection of synthwave's sunny cruise. Power, threat and ritual dominate; tenderness and humor are absent or ironic.

## Era & Origins

Early 2010s, Europe (primarily France) and the United States. Pioneered by Perturbator, Carpenter Brut and Mega Drive, many from metal backgrounds; mainstream exposure via the *Hotline Miami* soundtracks (2012, 2015); consolidation through the extreme metal label Blood Music from Perturbator's *Dangerous Days* (2014) onward. Musically it fuses vintage synthesizers (Roland 808 drum sequences) with distorted production, heavy basslines and metal-influenced structure, later branching into electro-industrial, metal-guitar electro house, and black-metal-inflected paths — a breadth the visual style absorbs without breaking.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Black first: the void is the canvas; neon exists as emissive light with bloom and fog.
2. Fuse two lineages in one frame: cyberpunk tech (chrome, visors, grids, cities) and occult metal (sigils, skulls, demons, chrome logotypes). Either may dominate, but the other usually whispers.
3. Degrade the signal: grain, scanlines, halftone, cracked print, chromatic fringing — the image should feel like a worn 80s tape or poster, not a clean render.
4. Illustrate, don't photograph: airbrush-comic shading and ink line, or flat neon linework; avoid photoreal cleanliness.
5. Menace over gore: threat conveyed by mask, glow, posture, scale.
6. Typography, if present, is chrome metal script, neon tube script, pixel block or distressed stencil — never corporate sans.

**Variation axes.**
- *Subject*: cityscape, cathedral or ritual interior, sigil diagram, skull emblem, android/cyborg portrait, masked killer, hooded figure, demon crowd, machine, pure logotype piece, abstract grid horizon.
- *Composition*: symmetrical emblem/altar; cover stack; cinematic wide with tiny silhouette; close bust portrait; flat centered sigil; chaotic crowded scene.
- *Lighting*: single-hue flood; neon rim on black chiaroscuro; backlit fog; sigil or eyes as key; under-glow from grids.
- *Color handling*: magenta+cyan duotone; red/black monochrome flood; violet+acid green; full neon spectrum on black; near-monochrome with one accent.
- *Medium register*: painterly airbrush comic; flat geometric neon linework; pixel/OCR graphic; distressed printed poster.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A black cathedral flooded in red with no figure, a flat pentagram linework diagram, or a chrome skull on a grid are all valid Darksynth even if no reference looks exactly like them.

**Avoid.**
- Outrun/synthwave optimism: peach-orange sunsets, teal-and-pink pastels, palm trees, beaches, daylight chrome sports cars, cheerful Miami nostalgia.
- Daylight, white or cream backgrounds, soft natural light, overcast realism.
- Clean corporate minimalism, flat UI vector without occult/neon content, modern sans-serif branding.
- Photoreal clean CGI with no analog degradation.
- Victorian Goth: lace, candles, rosaries, cemeteries in desaturated plum and gray.
- Industrial utilitarianism: rust brown, olive drab, bare concrete, stencil-only machinery without neon or occult content.
- Generic horror gore: photographic blood and decay without the retro-neon frame.
- Metal fantasy realism: dragons, knights, parchment, flame-lit battle scenes without synthesizer neon.
- Vaporwave pastels, steampunk brass, medieval or folk handcraft materials.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Darksynth |
|---|---|
| **Cyberpunk** | Cyberpunk leans near-future urban realism: rain-slick streets, corporate HUDs, teal-orange blockbuster grading, clean CGI. Darksynth keeps 80s analog decay, illustrated airbrush surface, occult/metal iconography and neon-on-black; no clean interface aesthetics. |
| **Goth** | Goth is antique and romantic: lace, velvet, candlelight, stone cemeteries, desaturated plum. Darksynth is synthetic: chrome, latex, neon tubes, grids, VHS grain; no Victorian ornament. |
| **Horror** | Generic horror uses naturalistic or desaturated light and contemporary gore. Darksynth horror is period-stylized slasher/sci-fi filtered through neon palette and retro print texture; the neon grid and chrome logotype are absent from plain horror. |
| **Industrial** | Industrial favors rust, concrete, exposed machinery and utilitarian stencil type in gray-brown-olive. Darksynth borrows machine chrome but always powers it with neon glow, fog and sigils. |
| **Metal** | Metal sleeve art shares skulls and chrome logotypes but paints fantasy realism with fire and leather, often flame- or daylight-lit. Darksynth grounds the same emblems in synthesizer neon, magenta/cyan accents, perspective grids and tape decay. |
| **Synthwave / Outrun (parent)** | Same retro hardware and grids, opposite soul: Outrun is sunset pastel and cruise optimism; Darksynth is black, blood red and magenta, menace and ritual. |

When in doubt, apply the litmus: near-black ground + emissive 80s neon + analog degradation + (occult or cyber-doom subject) = Darksynth. Remove any one of those four and the image drifts toward a neighbor.
