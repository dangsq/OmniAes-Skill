# Mermaidcore

Mermaidcore (also "Mermaid Style") is a fashion and lifestyle microtrend popularized on TikTok, Pinterest and Instagram in the early 2020s, inspired by the mythology and visual representation of mermaids, sirens and other aquatic life. It is escapist by design: a fantasy of magical life under the sea, filtered through Y2K-era nostalgia and modern high-fashion interpretation. The aesthetic spans a range from whimsical and romantic to a darker, more menacing "telchine" siren register, but both poles share one physical language — shimmer, scale and wetness.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** Think of the color space as a tide line with three registers, and place every image somewhere along it. (1) *Pearl register*: iridescent whites and silvers — pearl ivory around #F5F2EC, cool silver #E9EDF0, with a holographic shift that flashes pink #FADCE5 and mint #D9F2E6 depending on angle. (2) *Shallow-water register*: aquamarine #7FFFD4, seafoam #9FE2BF, teal #0E7C86, bridged by pastel lilac #C9A7E8, coral pink #FBCFC8 to #F88379, and peach-sunset tones #F6D8C0. (3) *Deep-sea register*: jewel tones — sapphire #0F52BA, amethyst #9966CC, near-black teal #0B3C49. The working rule: a base water tone from any register, plus at least one luminous carrier (scale shimmer, pearl, wet-skin specular), with pastels acting as the bridge between registers. Iridescence is a hue-shift, not a color: scales and paillettes should read pink-green-blue at different angles. Fully pastel, fully jewel-toned, and near-monochrome pearl images are all valid; what fails is a dry, matte, warm-neutral palette with no shimmer.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.** First, fish scales rendered as shimmer: sequins, large paillettes, scale-textured fabric, and the beauty technique of pressing powder or eyeshadow through fishnet to stencil a scale pattern onto skin. Second, seashells — shell cups on bralettes, shell-strung necklaces and belts, shell prints, shell hair clips, scallop shells as props. Third, pearls: strands, earrings, face adornments, pearls scattered on skin and tail. Fourth, iridescent texture itself — fin membranes, pleated translucent flukes, holographic scale fields. Fifth, sheer water-mimicking fabrics (tulle, organza, chiffon) and net-like crochet and fishnet. Sixth, coral and starfish as accent ornaments. Seventh, waves, water surfaces, sun-glitter, bubbles and droplets. Eighth, sea glass in jewelry. Structurally, the mermaid silhouette — fitted through the body, flaring at the knee — is the style's signature cut, echoed in gowns and in tail forms alike.

**Materials and textures.** Overlapping rounded scales with a pearl luster; oversized paillettes catching light individually; sheer organza and chiffon drifting like suspended water; open crochet and fishnet mesh; strung pearls and shell beads; wet skin and wet-look hair with high-shine finish; soap bubbles and water droplets as ephemeral props; damp sand; underwater caustic light patterns rippling across bodies.

**Recurring subjects.** Mermaid figures and tails (whole or fragmentary); bathers and swimmers at the shore or submerged; beauty close-ups with scale-stencil makeup, glitter, face gems and starfish or pearl adornments; fashion figures in sequined scale garments and shell-embellished corsets or bralettes; mermaid-ombre hair (teal, green, lavender gradients); object still lifes of shells, pearls, coral, bubbles and elaborate baroque shell-and-pearl headpieces; macro texture fields of scales and fin pleats; painterly mermaids in the Pre-Raphaelite lineage combing hair on rocks.

**Composition and lighting.** Fragment crops are as canonical as full figures: a tail coiled on sand, a shoulder armored in scales, a hand holding a shell, hair filling the frame. Macro texture fields that abandon the body entirely are equally at home. Underwater compositions use vertical depth, light rays from the surface and caustic ripple. Shore and studio work favors soft diffuse daylight, high-key pastel overexposure, or film-snapshot warmth with sun-glitter speculars dancing on water and wet skin. A quieter painterly pole exists — muted chiaroscuro, still-life intimacy, classical oil handling — in which the motifs (shell, bubble, pearl, mermaid) carry the style even when the palette goes earthy and dim.

## Mood & Values

Mermaidcore values escape, transformation and tender nostalgia: the wish for a magical life below the surface, and affection for the early-2000s media that first pictured it. Its romantic pole is whimsical, soft and playful — candy pastel gradients, shells arranged like sweets, bubbles balanced on a palm. Its dark pole is the siren: alluring, slightly menacing, luminous in gloom. Across both, the style prizes adornment and handcraft (beading, pearl-stringing, shell jewelry), communion with the sea, and ephemerality — the bubble that will pop, the sheen that will dry, the tide that will erase the tail's drag mark.

## Era & Origins

Internet-native to the 2020s, Mermaidcore gained runway traction in the post-pandemic era — Versace's Spring/Summer 2021 shell-and-starfish prints are an early high-fashion marker, followed by designers such as David Koma, Blumarine and Louis Vuitton — and peaked in 2023 on the wave of Disney's live-action *The Little Mermaid*, with enormous TikTok hashtag engagement and a several-hundred-percent rise in Pinterest searches. Its emotional fuel is nostalgia for early-2000s media like *Aquamarine* (2006) and *H₂O: Just Add Water*; its deeper lineage reaches back to the mermaid-silhouette gown of 1930s couturier Marcel Rochas and to nineteenth-century mermaid painting such as Waterhouse's *A Mermaid*. Iconic figures include Halle Bailey and Dua Lipa.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Build the image from the tide-line palette rule: pick a register (pearl, shallow pastel, deep jewel) and keep at least one luminous carrier in frame.
- Always include a scale, shell, pearl or iridescence carrier; the style is announced by material, not by logo or setting.
- Establish a water relationship: submerged, just-emerged wetness, shore edge, or materials that behave like water.
- Let texture lead: shimmer, sheen, translucency, netting, caustics.
- Keep light aqueous: diffuse softness, specular sun-glitter, underwater rays, or — in the painterly pole — dim museum chiaroscuro.
- If a face is present, apply the beauty code: wet-look hair or beachy waves, glossy lips, iridescent blue-green-purple lids, glitter, face gems, pearl adornments, optionally fishnet-stenciled scales.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any of mermaid figure, bather, beauty close-up, fashion figure, hair study, still life, texture macro, doll or figure, painterly narrative scene. Composition: from full-body underwater depth to body-fragment crop to frame-filling macro to tabletop still life. Lighting: high-key pastel studio, sun-glitter shore daylight, underwater caustics and god rays, or muted painterly dimness. Color handling: near-monochrome pearl, candy pastel gradient, saturated jewel deep-sea, or earthy classical tones when motif carries the read. Medium: glossy digital photography, grainy film snapshot, underwater photography, fantasy digital painting, classical oil, doll photography. Mood: whimsical-romantic through serene to dark siren "telchine." A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.**
- Sailor-uniform code: navy wool, white piping, anchors, rope, brass buttons, crisp red-white-blue stripes (that is Nautical).
- Storm-desaturated blacks and greys with ship hulls and rigging and no iridescence (Dark Nautical); the dark mermaid pole keeps jewel-teal gloom *with* shimmer.
- Neon acid cyan-magenta, chrome, glitch effects, dolphin or trident clipart irony (Seapunk).
- Warm tan-and-cream boho beachwear, raffia, coconut and hibiscus with matte sun-dried textures (Coconut Girl); mermaidcore crochet is cool-toned netting over shimmer.
- Generic high fantasy: castles, armor, wings, torchlight, non-aquatic creatures.
- Matte flat fabrics, matte makeup, dry powdery skin with no sheen or sparkle anywhere.
- Inland urban or studio settings with neither water logic nor a shell/scale/pearl carrier.
- Sporty surf gear, neoprene, tiki tropicalia.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Coconut Girl** shares the beach but not the physics: it is warm, dry, matte and woven (raffia, terry, cream and brown); Mermaidcore is cool, wet and luminous, with scales and pearls where Coconut Girl has straw and tan lines.
- **Dark Nautical** is tailored and maritime-industrial — navy black, brass, rope, rigging, storm light; Mermaidcore's dark "telchine" pole is organic and bioluminescent, glowing skin, pearl and scale shimmer inside jewel-teal gloom. Note honestly: this dark pole is named in the style's writing but thinly represented in surviving imagery, so treat it as documented but rare.
- **Fantasy** builds worlds (castles, spells, armor); Mermaidcore's magic is material — iridescence, wetness, scale — and its only creatures are sea life.
- **Nautical** is graphic and crisp: stripes, anchors, sailor collars, matte cotton primaries; Mermaidcore is textural and luminous, with no stripe code at all.
- **Seapunk** is 2010s digital irony: neon renders, chrome type, glitch, clipart tridents; Mermaidcore is soft, tactile, photographable or painterly, pastel-to-jewel, never glitched.

When in doubt, ask: does the surface behave like it is wet, scaled, or pearl-lined? If yes, with an oceanic palette, it is Mermaidcore.
