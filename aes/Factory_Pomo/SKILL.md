# Factory Pomo

Factory Pomo (Factory PoMo) is the postmodern machine style of the late 1980s through mid-1990s, born from the Memphis Design explosion and filtered through the first generation of CAD computer graphics. It takes the machine-age vocabulary of the early twentieth century — Art Deco, Constructivism, Bauhaus, Raygun Gothic, Streamline Moderne, WPA poster work — and restages it as playful, family-facing spectacle: the factory as amusement park, the gear as mascot. Its natural habitats are industrial and graphic design of the period: theme-park lands and arcade interiors, television title cards and studio sets, video-game box art, logos, packaging, signage and exhibition booths. The mood is techno-optimist edutainment — loud, symmetric, pristine, and slightly winkingly self-aware.

## Visual Identity

### Color

The palette rule is **contrast pairing, never harmony**. Complementary collisions dominate: magenta against acid green, orange against violet, red against teal, primary yellow against cobalt. These saturated accents sit on one of three grounds: paper white (graphic and illustration work), signal black (logos, signage, night spectacle), or grayscale metal (halftone gear murals, brushed steel, gunmetal ridging). A fourth "night mode" appears in built environments: violet-to-magenta dusk skies washed with neon pink, green and orange gel light.

Working ranges: magenta `#E6007E–#FF4FD8`, violet `#4B2E83–#8A63D2`, teal `#009B9B–#3FD8C7`, orange `#FF5A00–#FFA028`, yellow `#FFC400–#FFE867`, red `#D90000–#FF3B30`, cobalt `#0046AD–#2E7FE6`, acid green `#59E600–#B8FF5C`, machine grays `#6E6E6E–#D9D9D9`, black `#0A0A0A`, paper `#F4F4F2`. Color inversion is structural, not decorative: the same shape or half of a composition reappears with figure and ground swapped.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Gears and cogs** — the defining element. Oversized, toothed, often clustered in grayscale halftone murals, cut into portals and archways, or set spinning behind a logo.
2. **Metal ridging and industrial skin** — corrugation, rivets, pipes, tire-tread rings, brushed and checker-plate surfaces, all clean and freshly painted.
3. **Simple hard-edged shapes** — triangles, diamonds, circles, chevrons, sunburst/starburst rings used as frames, backplates and emblem grounds.
4. **Symmetry with inversion** — bilateral elevations, mirrored halves, repeated modules in swapped palettes.
5. **Early-CAD mannerisms** — extruded and beveled forms, precise vector edges, airbrush gradients fading to white, chrome-bevel lettering.
6. **Machine-age quotations** — Deco sunbursts and speed lines, Constructivist diagonal type bars, Bauhaus primary geometry, WPA-style angular figuration.
7. **Diagrammatic annotations** (secondary, image-confirmed): dashed trajectory lines, arrows, spec-sheet grids of circles and half-circles, tiny caption blocks.
8. **Collage cut-outs** (secondary): photographic figures and objects scissored onto white grounds among vector shapes; checkerboard and radial-stripe fills.

### Materials and textures

A three-part texture system: flat vector fills; airbrush gradients with visible spray grain; and grainy pattern fills — granite speckle, photocopy noise, halftone screen. Metal reads as grayscale photographic halftone or brushed silver, never rusted. Built environments show painted MDF set walls, laminated signage, vacuum-formed plastic, neon tube and gel-lit fixtures, carpeted convention floors, CRT scanlines on title cards.

### Recurring subjects

Theme-park gates and land entrances at dusk; arcade and entertainment-center interiors; science-show and game-show sets and title cards; video-game box art; sports and entertainment logos; soda cans and packaging; trade-show booths and gear-mural walls; poster and spec-sheet graphics. Human figures, when present, are angular WPA-derived cut-outs or excited hosts, never lifestyle models.

### Composition and lighting

Compositions cluster into five types: the frontal symmetric elevation (gate, booth wall, title card); one-point perspective corridors converging on a machine centerpiece; the Constructivist diagonal with type bars on white; the centered emblem lockup on a diamond or sunburst; and the scattered collage on open white. Lighting has three modes: flat, shadowless graphic light for print and illustration; festive neon gel washes under violet dusk skies for exteriors; and pooled halogen spots throwing specular streaks off metal for interiors.

## Mood & Values

Factory Pomo expresses machine-age wonder democratized for mass entertainment: the factory as playground, the computer as friendly co-host. It values optimism, legibility, spectacle and smart fun — education delivered as game show, technology as birthday party. Its postmodern wink keeps the machinery toy-like rather than monumental; nothing here is ominous, worn, or solemn.

## Era & Origins

Prevalent roughly 1988–1996, coined as a microlabel by Evan Collins. It emerges from the 1980s postmodern reaction against modernist restraint, merging Memphis Design's clashing play with emerging CAD graphics and early-twentieth-century industrial iconography. It overlaps Early Cyber, Cyberdelia, Silicon Dreams, Memphis Lite, Decoplex and Wacky Pomo, and was deployed at scale in 1990s theme-park lands and entertainment centers, console-era box art, children's science television, and mid-90s blockbuster production design.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Make the machine structural: at least one gear, cog, ridged panel or sunburst acts as architecture (portal, frame, backplate), not ornament.
- Build from simple primitives with CAD-clean edges; extrusions and bevels welcome, soft rounded corners not.
- Treat contrast as a system: pick a complementary pair and invert it across mirrored halves or between shape and ground.
- Combine the texture triad: flat fill + airbrush gradient + grainy fill (speckle, halftone, photocopy noise); metal stays grayscale and pristine.
- Use period typography: bold condensed or geometric sans inside bars and shapes, chrome-bevel or single-color, optional script accent for science-show warmth.
- Keep everything new: painted, plastic-coated, freshly lit.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be any machine-age playful artifact — poster, logo, can, set, park gate, arcade, booth, mural, title card, box art, diagram sheet. Composition may range across symmetric elevation, one-point interior, diagonal collage, centered emblem. Lighting may be flat graphic, dusk neon, or interior spot pools. Color handling may be white-ground primaries, black-ground neons, grayscale-plus-one-accent, or full rainbow saturation. Mood may run from diagrammatic deadpan to night-time spectacle. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Rust, grime, decay, grunge distressing (machines here are brand-new).
- Steampunk brass, Victorian ornament, patinated clockwork.
- Memphis terrazzo pastels and squiggle lines without machine content.
- Chrome globes, lens flares and blue grid horizons of corporate cyberspace tech-sublime.
- Rain-slick cyberpunk noir; Factory Pomo neon is festive, never ominous.
- Earth-tone beige, burgundy and muted lifestyle palettes.
- Modern flat-UI minimalism, soft gradients of contemporary startup design, photoreal global-illumination CGI.
- Handmade craft textures: wicker, macramé, raw wood grain.
- Biomorphic organic curves as the primary language; curves appear only as circles, gears, arches and spheres.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Decisive tell |
|---|---|
| Corporate Cyberspace | Chrome globes, lens flares, blue network grids; Factory Pomo swaps the network sublime for toothed gears, primaries and symmetry. |
| Memphis Lite | Pastel squiggles, terrazzo, confetti asymmetry; Factory Pomo keeps the clash but adds machine iconography, metal texture and hard symmetry. |
| Decopunk | Brass, soot, noir dystopia and diesel grit; Factory Pomo is bright, clean, plastic-coated and family-facing. |
| Frasurbane | Muted teal-burgundy-beige lifestyle sophistication; Factory Pomo is loud, geometric and kid-smart. |
| Memphis Design | Italian original: laminate kitsch, asymmetrical collage, pastel-neon furniture scale; Factory Pomo is CAD-precise, industrial-motif, American corporate and theme-park scale. |
| Wacky Pomo | Festival-marketplace signage whimsy without machinery; Factory Pomo always retains gears, ridging or metal skin. |

Where Factory Pomo bleeds into Early Cyber or Cyberdelia (neon interiors, CRT glow), the tiebreaker is the gear: if the spectacle is mechanically toothed, symmetric and playfully pristine, it is Factory Pomo.
