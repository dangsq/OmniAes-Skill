# Pastel Gore

Pastel Gore is the internet's tenderization of violence: an art style from the late-2010s online art community (Tumblr, DeviantArt, Amino, Sony Sketch) that depicts blood, organs and dismemberment in a stylized, cartoonish manner using bright, desaturated colors and cute motifs, so the disturbing subject matter arrives wrapped in innocent presentation. It grew in fandom and "animation meme" circles as a house style for original characters and fan art, inherits its cute-plus-guro contrast from Japanese Gurokawa and from Pastel Goth, and counts the cartoon carnage of *Happy Tree Friends* as a thematic precursor. Its governing value is juxtaposition: horror de-realized into decoration, body horror reframed as artistic expression rather than shock.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (the rule, not a swatch copy)
The base register is pastel: high-lightness, low-to-mid saturation hues — baby pink (#FFD1DC–#FFB6C1), lavender (#E6E6FA–#C8A2C8), mint (#BFFCC8–#98E6C8), baby blue (#AEC6EB–#89CFF0), peach and cream (#FFDAB9, #FFF0F5), butter yellow. The defining move is that **gore itself takes these colors**: blood runs pink (#FF8FB8), lavender (#B78BEA) or bright blue (#6EC6FF); organs are rendered as a rainbow of soft distinct hues, one pastel per organ. Candy substitutes add confectionery tones — strawberry icing, pistachio, grape, citrus flesh. Two licensed saturation spikes exist as subgenre registers: **Bright/Neon Gore**, which pushes the same motifs into eyesore saturation (acid green #39FF14, hot magenta #FF2E93, cyan #00F0FF, rainbow concentric contour-line backgrounds), and **black-ground variants**, where a solid black or deeply saturated field makes the pastels glow. What never appears in the core register is realistic arterial red-brown.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them
1. **Pastel blood**: drips, pools, splatters and mouth/eye/wound runs in pink, lavender or bright blue.
2. **Exposed organs in rainbow-soft color**: hearts, lungs, intestines, brains visible through open torsos, split heads or peeled skin, each organ a different soft hue.
3. **Candy Gore substitution**: melting candy, colorful slime or goo standing in for blood; organs and bones as sliced fruit (orange, watermelon, kiwi), candy canes or desserts; wounds spilling sprinkles or stars.
4. **Disembodied eyeballs**, loose as props or scattered in borders, often with heart- or star-shaped pupils.
5. **Bones and skeletons**: ribcages, spines, skulls, skeleton animals, rendered in pastel or candy-stripe.
6. **Cute-and-sharp pairings**: bows with knives, ribbons with needles, plush props with cleavers; sutures, stitches and safety pins closing seams of skin.
7. **Body-horror elements**: mismatched or replaced eyes, extra limbs, melting faces, stitched-together parts, heads opening like containers.
8. **Weaponry**: knives, scissors, syringes, cleavers — drawn chunky and cartoonish, never photoreal.
9. **Kawaii garnish**: blush circles, sparkles, stars, hearts, clouds, checker and dot grounds, bubble-letter captions ("OOPS", "YUMMY"), halo-like rainbow contour outlines.

### Materials, textures, medium
Overwhelmingly flat 2D digital illustration: clean uniform line art (often colored ink — pink, blue or magenta outlines rather than black), smooth cel fills, minimal rendering. Wet surfaces (slime, drips, gumballs, eyeballs) carry glossy white specular dots and rounded viscous edges. Secondary registers: sticker sheets with matte white die-cut borders; all-over doodle or pattern wallpapers; occasional crayon/marker-on-paper grain; and a minor photographic register of makeup and cosplay (pastel face paint, fake pink blood, craft props) under even studio or flash light. No film grain, no painterly realism, no photoreal skin.

### Recurring subjects
Original characters and fandom fan art; semi-chibi humanoids with animal ears (bunny, cat); skeleton creatures and skeleton pets; personified desserts and fast food with skulls, eyeballs and drips; crying cuties; avatar busts; makeup looks. Characters often look sleepy, dazed or tearful rather than aggressive — the violence happens *to* them, or is worn casually as decoration.

### Composition and lighting
Lighting is essentially non-directional: flat, even, shadowless, with color doing all the work; the only "light" is candy gloss. Composition tendencies: centered bust or full-body character on a solid, patterned or rainbow-contour ground; scattered motif fields (sticker sheets, wallpaper doodles); dense clutter piles of candy, fruit and eyeballs; thin framed borders around a black ground; graphic text integrated as design. Depth is shallow and decorative — layers stack like paper cutouts.

## Mood & Values
The feeling is tender macabre: playful "oops!" irony, melancholic cuteness (tears plus pastels), surreal sugar-dream disorientation. The community values juxtaposition, cuteness and body horror as *artistic expression* — gore as character design, as sticker trade, as animation-meme costume, not as terror. Sweetness acts as armor: pain made holdable, vulnerability made shareable. This is why the style sits adjacent to Yami Kawaii and Traumacore without becoming them.

## Era & Origins
Emerged and peaked in the late 2010s in online art communities (Tumblr, DeviantArt, Amino, Sony Sketch), slightly after the broader 2014 Tumblr era; rooted in fandom and animation-meme culture. Influences: Japanese Gurokawa, Pastel Goth, and the cute-animal/extreme-violence contrast of *Happy Tree Friends*. Documented subgenre siblings: **Candy Gore** (candy/sugar/fruit substitution, any palette), **Pop Gore** (Pop Art techniques, bold graphic text, violent tableaux), **Bright Gore** (saturated eyesore fanart), **Neon Gore** (extravagant neon color). These are palette/technique extensions of the same motif set, not separate styles.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Juxtaposition first: pair at least one gore element (wound, organ, bone, blood) with at least one cute element (bow, blush, sparkle, dessert) in every image.
2. Color the violence: blood and innards take pastel or candy hues; realistic red is forbidden in the core register.
3. Keep it cartoonish: rounded shapes, simplified anatomy, big eyes, chunky props; de-realize every wound.
4. Use substitution logic: fluids → slime, goo, melted icing; organs → fruit flesh, confections; bones → candy canes, stripes.
5. Finish flat: cel fills, clean colored line art, glossy speculars on wet surfaces, no directional lighting or cast shadows.
6. Garnish per the motif list: eyeballs with heart/star pupils, stitches, sprinkles, stars, bubble-letter captions.
7. Choose a format freely: OC bust, full-body sheet, sticker sheet with die-cut borders, all-over pattern, framed black-ground panel, or (rarely) makeup/cosplay photo.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
- *Subject*: humanoids, animal creatures, skeleton characters, personified food, crying cuties, avatar busts, makeup portraits — any bearer of the motif set.
- *Composition*: centered bust, full-body, scattered sticker field, dense clutter pile, all-over doodle pattern, bordered panel.
- *Lighting/ground*: flat even light on pastel ground; black or saturated ground for glow; rainbow concentric contour ground in the Neon/Bright register.
- *Color handling*: core soft pastel; fruit-flesh Candy Gore; saturated Bright/Neon spike — as long as gore colors stay non-realistic.
- *Mood*: playful irony, melancholic tearfulness, surreal dream, spooky-fun.
- *Medium*: flat digital illustration (default), sticker sheet, paper-marker texture, cosplay/makeup photo (minor register; evidence is thin but real).

**Avoid**
- Realistic red/maroon blood, photoreal wounds, visceral texture, grime (that is Gorecore).
- Dominant black/deep-purple gothic palette with pastel only as trim (Pastel Goth).
- Horror-film lighting, grain, found-footage or candid framing.
- Cuteness with no gore at all (Cutecore, Candycore).
- Raw, unstylized trauma imagery or clinical medical realism (Traumacore, Medicalcore).
- Muted earth tones, beige minimalism, anatomical-study realism.
- Shock-for-shock extremity or sexualized gore; the style's violence is decorative and expressive, not assaultive.
- Heavy painterly rendering or 3D realism; keep the surface flat and cartoonish.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Pastel Gore |
|---|---|
| **Dokukawaii** | "Poisonous cute": unsettling, sickly-creepy characters and psychological unease without explicit organs, blood or bones. Pastel Gore always shows literal gore elements. |
| **Gorecore** | Photographic, realistic red blood, grime and shock value. Pastel Gore is cartoonish, pastel-colored and de-realized. |
| **Gurokawa** | Japanese guro-kawaii illustration/fashion lineage, often keeping red or darker blood and heavier erotic-guro tradition. Pastel Gore is the western fandom-cartoon branch with strictly pastel or candy blood and candy substitution. |
| **Candycore** | Sweets and sugar with no wounds, organs or eyeballs. In Pastel Gore, candy *is* the wound — it substitutes for bodily matter. |
| **Cutecore** | Pure innocent childlike pastel imagery, zero dark content. Pastel Gore imports gore into that palette. |
| **Yami Kawaii / Traumacore** | Sick-cute fashion and raw trauma doodle imagery centered on mental-health signifiers (pills, bandages, medical props) rather than stylized organs and candy gore; overlap exists but the motif core differs. |

When in doubt, apply the two-part test: is the gore explicit (organs, blood, bones, dismemberment), and is its color and rendering soft, sweet and cartoonish? Both yes → Pastel Gore.
