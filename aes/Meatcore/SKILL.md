# Meatcore

Meatcore is an obscure internet micro-aesthetic built on flesh, raw meat, organs, mutilation and blood — but centered on the *cute or artistic side* of that material rather than on violence. Its participants are lookers, not eaters: the practice is photographing the meat aisle or the butcher case and sharing the finds with mutuals, editing marbling into banners and icons, drawing sweet characters out of or beside slabs of beef. The philosophy is gently absurdist and unexpectedly tender: we are all just meat, and that is not necessarily a scary idea. The same flesh-and-blood sameness underwrites its body-positive strand, and its occasional feminist strand reads the packaged, priced, labeled cut as an image of bodies historically bought and sold. Values declared by the style itself are discordance, discomfort and disgust — held in the same frame as pastel stripes, heart shapes and lowercase melancholy captions.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

A three-family system. The rule: red always reads as *biological or commodity*, cream always reads as *fat or packaging*, pink always reads as *tray or cute register* — never as decorative neutrals.

| Family | Hex range | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Deep red / oxblood | `#4A0A0A` – `#7E1114` | shadowed muscle, dark case interiors, low-key backgrounds |
| Arterial / package red | `#A31621` – `#C4293A` | wet meat surface, blood motifs, flat illustration reds |
| Pink | `#E4699E` (tray hot pink) – `#F3B7CB` (pastel flesh pink) | plastic heart trays, kawaii character palettes, dithered fields |
| Reddish white / cream | `#E8D6CC` – `#F4EAE1` | fat marbling, styrofoam, label paper, pale skin tones |
| Found-print accents (tiny doses only) | barcode black, price-sticker yellow | labels, stickers, barcodes |

Low-key variants push the ground to near-black (`#120507`) lit only by red case-glow; dithered digital variants allow pale pink/lilac noise fields behind the flesh. Saturation may slide from saturated pack-red down to desaturated melancholy red, but the hue family never leaves red–pink–cream.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Raw meat as object** — steak above all, prized for its marbling; intact cuts, mirrored pairs, slabs and chunks.
2. **Supermarket packaging** — styrofoam and plastic trays, cling-film glare, price labels, barcodes, shelf rows, refrigerated case walls; the commodity frame is the style's signature domestication device.
3. **Organs and offal** — coils of small intestine, organ masses; as creature material, as background texture, as accessory forms.
4. **Blood as motif** — blood bags, stains, drips; worn and printed rather than spilled as evidence.
5. **Body horror and mutilation** — characters with extreme mutilations, flesh-as-body reflection; the dark register of the style.
6. **Creatures and objects made of meat** — flesh assemblages with faces, meat-replica jewelry (steak earrings, intestine necklaces), things built from cuts and organs.
7. **Meat texture as surface** — marbling closeups used as banner/icon backgrounds or as the fill pattern of text in image edits.
8. **Cannibalism motifs** — occasional, appearing in image posts as theme rather than narrative.
9. **Cute domestication** — the "cute side" the style centers on: heart-formatted trays and paired-cut hearts, valentine framing, chibi characters, stripes and soft roundness wrapped around abject material.

### Materials and textures

Wet sheen on cut muscle; intramuscular fat striations; cling-film specular glare and wrinkle; styrofoam dimple; matte paper label with thermal barcode print; coiled intestine rope; ground-meat granule. On the digital side: dithering and noise grain, flat cel color with simple line art, cut-and-paste collage edges, pixel-scale text.

### Subjects and composition

Subjects range from the literal (a packaged cut, a case shelf) to the constructed (a flesh creature, a mutilated or meat-adjacent character, a jewelry piece) to the abstract (texture field, text-over-marbling). Compositional tendencies: top-down or angled product framing of the tray as hero object; repetition grids of shelves and stacked packs; a single centered object or character on a void or flat ground; a small silhouetted figure dwarfed by a glowing wall of product; bilateral symmetry from mirrored cuts; banner/icon crops and text-pattern edits.

### Lighting and medium

Lighting runs from harsh on-camera flash with specular hits on plastic film, to flat fluorescent case light, to a dark room lit only by the red glow of the meat wall, to shadowless even light in illustration. Mediums are deliberately mixed: phone snapshot, product-photo cutout on transparent ground, chibi digital drawing, dithered pixel edit, surreal photo-collage, banner/icon graphic. Fashion exists but is highly individualized and mostly handmade — replica-meat jewelry, blood-bag and intestine accessories — so treat wearable artifacts as rare, not canonical.

## Mood & Values

Discordance, discomfort and disgust, kept in tension with tenderness. The emotional registers in circulation: deadpan documentary flatness (the tray simply *is*); kawaii sweetness (flesh drawn round and blushing); lonely melancholy (lowercase one-line confessions set over pink noise); absurd surrealism (a meat sphere with a sleeping face); quiet dread (a silhouette before the glowing case); and body-positive warmth (the same flesh in all of us). The style values looking over eating, anti-gourmet anti-polish honesty, and comfort in abjection.

## Era & Origins

An obscure web-native micro-aesthetic of blogger and mutuals culture, circulating on Tumblr, Instagram and DeviantArt and codified on the Aesthetics Wiki; the source material gives no founding date or physical scene, so treat the era as undocumented beyond platform traces (roughly 2010s–2020s internet). It branches from Gorecore's darkness while refusing trauma narrative, borrows amateur-surreal digital techniques from Weirdcore, and shares with Starrflesh the "animalistic side of humanity" thesis. Its ritual activity — walking the meat aisle to photograph, not buy — is its origin story in miniature.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Flesh is material, never meal: keep meat raw, uncooked, unseasoned, unplated.
- Commodity framing creates the tension: trays, film, labels, barcodes, shelves, prices.
- Pair the abject with the sweet or the deadpan: pastel, chibi, hearts, stripes — or flat snapshot neutrality.
- Hold the palette inside red–pink–cream; other hues only as tiny found-print accents or noise fields.
- Let texture carry the image: marbling, sheen, film glare, dither grain. No violence story required.
- Optional caption voice: short lowercase melancholy lines, or text filled with meat pattern.

**Variation axes**
- *Subject*: packaged cut → organ coil → flesh creature → human figure (mutilated, contemplating, wearing meat motifs) → replica jewelry → pure texture field.
- *Composition*: product top-down, shelf repetition, centered object on void, figure-vs-case silhouette, symmetric paired cuts, collage stack, banner/icon crop.
- *Lighting*: camera flash, fluorescent flat, case-glow low-key, shadowless digital flat.
- *Color handling*: saturated pack-red ↔ desaturated melancholy ↔ pastel dithered pink; cream proportion may dominate (fat, foam) or recede.
- *Mood*: deadpan ↔ kawaii ↔ melancholy ↔ absurd ↔ dread ↔ tender.
- *Medium*: phone photo, cutout, chibi vector, pixel-dither edit, photo-collage.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A dithered portrait of a character in an intestine scarf, or a flash photo of a paper-wrapped butcher parcel, is valid Meatcore even though no reference shows either.

**Avoid**
- Cooked food language: sear, grill marks, browning, glaze, steam, plating, garnish, cutlery, recipe context, golden-hour food photography.
- Trauma gore: wounds as injury narrative, weapons, crime-scene splatter, medical aftermath (that is Gorecore).
- Occult apparatus: pentagrams, horns, candles, black-metal type (Devilcore).
- Dentition centrality: enamel closeups, gum grids, bite marks as subject (Teethcore).
- Consumption narrative as the point: biting, chewing, feeding, human-as-meal (Cannibalcore).
- Dominant non-family hues: greens, blues, neon cyberpunk, sepia vintage; polished commercial CGI finish.
- Cuteness or red abstraction *without* flesh: pastel kawaii with no meat content, or red paint pours with no flesh/packaging cue, are not Meatcore.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Its center | Separating cue |
|---|---|---|
| Cannibalcore | The act of eating people; appetite, predation, mouths | Meatcore displays flesh as object/material/cute artifact; consumption motifs are garnish, not plot |
| Gorecore | Injury and violence; blood as evidence of harm | Meatcore's blood is motif and accessory; its dominant surface is intact packaged meat, its mutilation is body-horror art, not aftermath |
| Starrflesh | Flesh as feral, instinctual, creaturely identity (distinction thinly documented) | Meatcore stays grounded in grocery domesticity, commodity print and cute formatting |
| Devilcore | Occult transgression; infernal black-red, sigils, horns | Meatcore's red is biological, its white is fat and styrofoam; no occult gear |
| Teethcore | Dentition: enamel, gums, dental grids | Meatcore's whites are marbling and foam; its surface is wet muscle, not calcified enamel |
| Weirdcore | Liminal disorientation, amateur digital surrealism | Weirdcore supplies Meatcore's dither and cryptic-caption techniques; if emptiness is the point and flesh is absent, it is Weirdcore |

Quick recognition test: uncooked flesh as hero + commodity or cute framing + red/cream/pink discipline = Meatcore.
