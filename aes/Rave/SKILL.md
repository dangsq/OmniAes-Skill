# Rave

Rave is first a party and only second a look: an unlicensed, all-night electronic dance event that grew from Chicago acid house, Detroit techno, Ibiza after-hours and British warehouse squats into a global subculture and, later, a festival industry. Its visual identity is therefore the record of an experience — bodies in motion inside borrowed industrial space, saturated by machine light and fog — plus the ephemera that summoned people there: photocopied flyers, infoline typography, smiley emblems. Everything downstream (fashion, graphics, photography) carries that DNA: clandestine, communal, euphoric, deliberately excessive.

## Visual Identity

### Colour palette

The style operates in registers, not one fixed scheme:

- **Fluorescent-on-black (dominant).** Acid green (#39FF14–#00FF41), hot magenta (#FF00CC–#FF2E88), electric cyan (#00FFFF–#00E5FF), ultraviolet violet (#9D00FF–#BF00FF), laser tangerine (#FF6D00–#FF9500), highlighter yellow (#FFE500) — always read as *light sources* or as dye on synthetic fabric, absorbed by deep black (#050505–#0A0A0A) or by haze. Neon never appears as wall paint in a brightly lit room.
- **Pastel-chrome flyer gradient (secondary).** Baby pink, mint, lilac and chrome silver blended in airbrushed or early-CGI gradients on posters and flyer backgrounds.
- **All-black counter-pole.** Berlin techno and Belgian New Beat: head-to-toe black (#000000) punctuated by one cold accent — white strobe, a red lip, a single beam.
- **Daylight-festival pole.** Sun-bleached neon costume (holographic vinyl, fluorescent fur) against grass, dust and pale sky; colour survives but glows less.
- **Ground neutrals.** Concrete grey, rust, corrugated metal, kraft paper — the borrowed architecture that makes the neon read.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Smiley face** — the acid house emblem, on T-shirts, flyers, badges.
2. **Glowsticks and light-show props** — glowsticking, glowstringing, gloving; LED poi trails written onto black.
3. **Kandi** — plastic beaded bracelets and necklaces, stacked wrist-deep, traded hand-to-hand under the PLUR ethos (strongly US; thinner in Europe).
4. **Pacifiers and whistles** — mouth-held, candid, unselfconscious.
5. **Baggy silhouette** — phat pants, bell-bottoms, oversized jeans, quilted vests, tight nylon shirts, neoprene and flokati fur; bucket hats, bandanas, white gloves, feather boas, oversized round sunglasses.
6. **Laser beams, strobes, fog machines, projected visuals** and the speaker stacks / sound-system walls that anchor them.
7. **Found space** — warehouse, aircraft hangar, squat, power station, field; scaffolding truss, tarpaulin, pasted posters.
8. **DIY flyer graphics** — smileys, handwritten or chrome type, stacked info lines (date, venue, infoline), photocopy halftone.
9. **Retro sportswear pole** — Adidas tracksuits (gabber), and the New Beat goth uniform (cycling shorts, knee socks, bowler hat, rosary).
10. **Glitter-queen pole** — sequins, glitter makeup, holographic boots and bodysuits with mermaid/star/alien/fairy themes.
11. **Early costume pole** — protective suits, safety vests, gas masks, cyberpunk goggles (documented but historically narrow).
12. **Pagan/spiritual decor** — altars, sacred imagery over turntables (text-documented, rarely visible in imagery; treat as optional spice).

### Materials and textures

Nylon, neoprene, PVC and latex sheen; flokati fur and feather boa fluff; plastic bead gloss; screen-printed cotton jersey; truck-tarpaulin bags; sequin and holographic vinyl. Environmentally: raw concrete, corrugated iron, steel truss, plywood speaker stacks, paper flyers wheat-pasted over older paper. Surface treatment: 35mm compact-flash grain, flash hotspots and red-eye-era colour, motion blur and double-exposure smear, long-exposure light trails, volumetric fog, sweat sheen, photocopy halftone on print.

### Recurring subjects

Massed crowd seen from behind or above (raised arms, silhouetted heads under beams); one or two costumed dancers mid-gesture, grinning or peace-signing straight into the flash; a DJ bent over turntables with kandi-wrapped forearms; gloving hands weaving light; close-up of kandi-covered wrists being traded; the flyer itself as flat object; an empty hall of fog, rigging and lasers; speaker walls and generator clutter.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- Crowd-as-organism: dense heads filling the lower frame, light architecture above.
- Symmetrical light vistas: laser fans and truss grids converging on a vanishing point.
- Candid snapshot framing: off-centre, tilted, half-cropped figures, intimate distance.
- Light-writing on pure black for gloving/poi subjects.
- Flat centred flyer layout: emblem, headline, info block.
- Lighting modes: on-camera flash against dark ambient; beams cut through fog; UV blacklight making fluorescents bloom; strobe stutter-freeze; morning skylight bleeding into lingering haze; open-sky dusk at outdoor festivals.

## Mood & Values

Euphoria, communion and hedonism — the crowd as a single breathing body. PLUR (Peace, Love, Unity, Respect) governs the gesture language: traded kandi, hand signs, shared pacifiers and water bottles. Underneath the playfulness runs clandestine thrill: secret locations, infolines, pasted flyers, anti-establishment DIY. Rave values individuality expressed through maximal, handmade costume, and escape — from Thatcher-era unemployment, from workweek time, from ordinary architecture.

## Era & Origins

Late 1980s: Chicago TB-303 acid house, Detroit techno, the 1987 Ibiza pilgrimage that spawned London nights like Shoom, Future and Trip, Berlin's Ufo basement, Belgian New Beat. The 1988 "Second Summer of Love" and the early-1990s illegal warehouse and free-festival peak (Castlemorton 1992), met by tabloid moral panic and anti-rave laws. Post-Wall Berlin techno (Tresor, Bunker) hardened into the black counter-pole; the 2000s onward split between underground secrecy and commercial mega-festivals (EDC, Tomorrowland). Regional dialects persist: UK baggy acid house, US kandi/PLUR, Berlin black, Dutch/Italian gabber sportswear.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Light is architecture: darkness or haze first, then coherent coloured sources (lasers, strobes, glowsticks, UV) that carve the space.
2. Costume is DIY semiotics: layered baggy or tight synthetics plus meaningful accessories (kandi, pacifier, whistle, gloves, boa, bucket hat, round shades) — assembled, not styled.
3. Bodies in motion: dance, raised arms, gloving, trading gestures; sweat and joy over poise.
4. Space is borrowed, not built: warehouses, hangars, squats, fields with improvised rigging, speaker stacks, tarps, pasted paper.
5. The medium remembers: keep grain, flash flare, blur, halftone or haze — never sterile crispness.
6. Graphics speak smiley and infoline: emblem plus stacked practical text, photocopy or chrome-gradient ground.

**Variation axes.** Subject: crowd, solo dancer, duo snapshot, DJ, gloving hands, kandi close-up, flyer, empty rigged hall, speaker stack. Composition: symmetrical light vista, candid off-centre snapshot, flat flyer, macro detail, elevated crowd view. Lighting: flash-on-black, laser-fog, UV bloom, strobe freeze, skylight morning haze, outdoor dusk or daylight festival. Colour: full neon-on-black, pastel-chrome gradient print, all-black pole with one accent, sun-bleached daytime neon. Mood: euphoric, trancey, clandestine, playful, exhausted-afterglow. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Boho festival code (flower crowns, crochet, earth tones); disco glamour (mirror balls, gold lamé, satin, champagne); crisp studio-lit editorial with no haze, grain or sweat; pure graffiti-wall scenes with no party, light or costume; head-to-toe cybergoth rig (neon dread falls, permanent gas-mask goggles) as the whole statement; vaporwave/synthwave static pastel grids with no bodies; rock-gig instrumentation (drum kits, guitar stacks); daylight domestic or suburban scenes with no party trace; sterile haze-free CGI stage renders; luxury-fashion perfection in place of handmade excess.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Rave's difference |
|---|---|---|
| Acid Design | Flat graphic system: smileys, 303 swirls, chrome type on print or screen, no party | Rave absorbs those graphics on flyers but is embodied — crowds, costume, fog-filled space |
| Electroclash | Early-2000s posed retro-electro: leather, angular bobs, cold black/white/red, ironic detachment | Rave is sweaty, plural, euphoric, fog-drenched, unposed |
| Graffiti Pop | Subject is the painted wall and spray canon | Rave walls carry pasted flyers only; light, crowd and costume dominate |
| Krocha | Fitted brand sportswear, neon-on-black accents, groomed crew poses, hardstyle jumps | Rave silhouettes are baggy or costumed, accessories handmade (kandi), mood communal |
| Madchester | Shares baggy jeans and bucket hats but earthy paisley/rose palette, band tees, fishtail parkas, daytime street or gig hall | Rave adds lasers, fog walls, pacifiers, kandi, neon synthetics, nocturnal industrial space |

Note on internal poles: the all-black Berlin/New Beat pole can drift toward minimal techno or goth imagery; anchor it with industrial concrete, fog, strobe and crowd density rather than goth fetishwear. Evidence is thinnest for pagan altar decor and the New Beat uniform — text-documented, scarcely imaged — so treat both as optional historical accents, not core requirements.
