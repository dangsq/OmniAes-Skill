# Rococopunk

Rococopunk is an aesthetic and niche costuming community that welds the opulent fashion of the 18th-century Rococo court onto the rebellious attitude of punk. It gained visibility in early-2010s online costuming circles, positioning itself deliberately as a more humorous and more colorful alternative to Steampunk. Its outfits are handmade by their wearers, celebrate decadence and excess, and are never meant to be historically accurate: adherents call themselves "classholes," aristocratic in silhouette and defiant in demeanor. The style lives in group photos, party snapshots, stage performances and ensemble "digital baroque painting" tableaus rather than in commercial product — anti-commercialism is one of its stated values, so a handmade, slightly imperfect finish is a feature, not a flaw.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is a **pastel ground carrying rich, vibrant accents**, never one without the other. Garment bodies sit in powdered pastels — powder pink (#FADADD–#F4C2C2), mint (#BFE3D0–#98D7C2), ivory/cream (#F7EEDF), baby blue (#AEC6CF), lilac (#C9A9D6) — while wigs, feathers, ribbons, fans and bodice panels inject saturated jewels: fuchsia (#D92681), vermilion (#C23B22), turquoise-teal (#1FA9A0–#2E8B8B), marigold (#E8842C), brocade gold (#C9A227). Powder white (#F2EDE4) appears on faces and wigs. Near-black (#0A0A0A) enters only as **void or hardware**: the black backdrop of a flash snapshot, a dark club interior, studs and chokers — never as the dominant garment color. Either pole may lead (a turquoise coat over a pink wig is as valid as a pink gown over a fuchsia crest), but pastel and vivid must coexist; a fully muted or fully monochrome image has left the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Frills and lace** — jabots, sleeve ruffles, tiered skirt flounces, lace trims; the style's most cited element.
2. **18th-century silhouettes** — corseted bodices with visible lacing, pannier-width skirts, waistcoats and frock coats, robe à la française lines; deliberately inexact, parodic versions.
3. **Powdered wigs** — pale powder, tall stacked curls, frequently streaked or dyed pink at the edges.
4. **Mohawks and punk hair** — teased crests, feather pompadours, shaved-or-backcombed hybrids sitting atop otherwise courtly heads.
5. **Punk accessories** — studs, spikes, chokers, harness straps over corsets, clownish makeup (round rouge spots, drawn beauty patches), dyed hair.
6. **Handmade anachronism** — patchwork panels, clashing prints (harlequin diamond, toile, gingham, floral brocade), modern intrusions like denim jeans under a stomacher or boots with striped stockings.
7. **Props and headgear (image-supported)** — painted folding fans, raised wine glasses and goblets, period instruments, feather plumes, tricorn hats, heart motifs.
8. **The ensemble tableau** — multiple costumed figures arranged like a baroque conversation piece, a tradition cemented by large-scale "digital baroque painting" projects; group shots outnumber solo ones.

### Materials and textures

Raised floral brocade and damask, satin ribbon, cotton lace, taffeta, velvet, quilted patchwork, feather plumes, matte powder on skin and wig hair, paper fans — set against small hard notes of metal studs and buckles. Fabric should read as bought-by-the-yard and sewn at home: visible seams, mixed weights, proud slight wrinkling.

### Recurring subjects

Costumed people, any gender, solo to crowded group: toasting, curtseying, winking, pouting, posing with exaggerated aristocratic disdain; performers mid-stage; models on a runway; party guests caught mid-drink. Faces are expressive and camp, never reverent. Object-only still lifes and empty interiors are undocumented in this style — the costume on a body is the subject.

### Composition and lighting

Three documented lighting modes: **direct on-camera flash against black** (harsh highlights, candid party-documentary feel); **warm tungsten or saturated stage wash** (amber interiors, magenta and blue stage light, silhouetted audiences); and **even studio/tableau light** against a textured backdrop (riveted metal, paneled walls). Composition tends maximalist and crowded — overlapping figures, diagonal energy from raised glasses, fans or canes, pyramidal group arrangements — or a centered full-body figure for runway and cover-style shots. Negative space is scarce by principle: excess fills the frame.

## Mood & Values

Humor, rebellion, decadence, excess, anti-commercialism. The mood is a costumed party that takes its fun seriously and its history loosely: camp affection for the aristocratic image paired with a punk sneer at authority and at gatekeeping. Bad taste is worn with total confidence; handmade pride outweighs polish; parody and genuine love for the 18th century coexist in every seam.

## Era & Origins

Early 2010s, born in online costuming communities (the text specifies no single country). It emerged as a reaction against the perceived commercialization and seriousness of Steampunk, swapping that subculture's industrial browns for candy color. Lineage reaches back through Vivienne Westwood's punk-historical fusions; the performer Prince Poppycock (of *X Factor* fame) is its iconic public figure, and Fifth Wall Media's RococoPunk Project formalized its group-tableau tradition. Historical accuracy is explicitly rejected — this is playful reinterpretation, not reenactment.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Fuse, don't costume: every look needs at least one Rococo structural element (silhouette, wig, frill, brocade) *and* at least one punk interruption (crest, stud, dyed streak, clown rouge, modern anachronism).
2. Pastel ground, vivid accent; black only as void or hardware.
3. Handmade over couture: patchwork, mixed prints, visible construction, confident imperfection.
4. Excess is the argument — layer ribbons, feathers, ruffles until the frame is full.
5. Put humor in the face and gesture: toasts, winks, pouts, theatrical disdain.
6. Welcome anachronism: denim, sneakers, a soda cup in a lace-gloved hand.
7. Choose one documented lighting mode (flash-on-black, warm/stage wash, even tableau light).

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo portrait, duo, group tableau, runway walk, stage act, party snapshot, cover-style layout with masthead. Composition: crowded pyramidal group, centered full-body, tight candid crop. Lighting: any of the three modes above. Color handling: pastel-led or jewel-led, so long as both families appear. Mood: camp cheek, tender decadence, riotous party, aristocratic parody. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a rococopunk figure sewing in a kitchen or toasting on a subway platform, lit by flash and dressed by the rules above, is fully in style.

**Avoid**

- All-black leather punk with no Rococo element; grit-only urban realism.
- Steampunk gear: brass goggles, gears, brown leather, Victorian industrial silhouette, sepia earth palette.
- Historically accurate, solemn Rococo recreation with no punk interruption or wink.
- Minimalism, muted neutral palettes, clean lines, generous negative space.
- Couture-level finish, seamless digital polish, luxury-brand gloss (the style is anti-commercial).
- Goth Victorian mourning black, funereal lace.
- Neon cyberfuturism: chrome, LEDs, holographics.
- Reverent, straight-faced historical-reenactment mood.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Rococopunk |
|---|---|
| **Rococo (historical)** | Rococopunk always carries a punk interruption — dyed streak in the powder, feather crest, studs, clown rouge, denim — plus camp self-awareness; pure Rococo is harmonious, period-faithful, serene. |
| **Punk** | Punk is lean, black-leather-tartan, urban, angry or bleak; Rococopunk is maximalist, pastel-and-brocade, celebratory, with spikes only as garnish on opulence. |
| **Steampunk** | Steampunk: brass, copper, goggles, gears, Victorian silhouette, subdued browns. Rococopunk: pre-Victorian court silhouette, pastels and candy jewels, feathers and frills, no goggles — it was founded as a colorful rebuttal to Steampunk. |
| **New Romantic** | New Romantic fuses history with punk too, but through 1980s clubwear: pirate shirts, synth-era hair, eyeliner glamour, cool nightclub sheen, jewel-and-black palette. Rococopunk commits to full 18th-century costume architecture (panniers, corsets, powdered wigs, tricorns), pastel dominance, and handmade party camp rather than pop-star polish. |

**Evidence note:** the style's geography beyond "online communities," and any non-figure subject matter (still lifes, interiors without costumed people), are undocumented; treat them as open rather than characteristic.
