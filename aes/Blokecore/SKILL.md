# Blokecore

Blokecore is the codification of decades of British football-fan dressing into a self-aware fashion aesthetic. "Bloke" — British slang for an ordinary man — names the posture as much as the wardrobe: retro club kits, plain jeans, period Adidas trainers, worn with deadpan pride in a hallway, on a doorstep, under a roller shutter, or on a terrace of plastic seats. The term was coined as a joke by American TikToker Brandon Huntley in late 2021 and went viral in April 2022, but the uniform itself predates the hashtag by forty years, descending from 1970s–80s hooligan culture, the Casuals and Ultras subcultures, and the grime scene. Its emotional core is nostalgia for classic football culture and the reclamation of a working-class style historically treated as tasteless.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette.** The rule is simple: the kit supplies the color. Any combination of team hues is valid — club reds (#B01E24–#D6001C), sky blues (#6CABDD–#9CC3E5), navies and blacks (#101820–#1F2A44), hoop greens (#006747–#2E8B57), shirt-panel whites (#F2F0EA) — usually two or three bold hues plus white, in stripes, hoops, sashes or 90s geometric prints. This is anchored by three constants: denim blue in light-to-mid stone wash (#9FB4CF–#5C7799), white leather trainers with gum soles (#B98D5F–#D2B48C), and a background that is either mundane neutral (white plaster, painted wood, grey concrete) or one saturated urban color-block surface (corrugated shutter yellow #E8C400 or red, stadium-seat red, brick). Flat-lay catalogue variants may quadrant these items on pastel blocks (butter yellow, mint, pink). There is no graded, cinematic or beige palette in this style.

**Signature motifs and objects, ranked by textual prominence.** (1) Old football jerseys from the 1980s–90s, ideally a British Second Division club — the defining item, named twice in the source record. (2) Basic jeans: straight or baggy, light wash, undistressed; knee-length denim shorts are an accepted variant. (3) Old Adidas sneakers, explicitly the Samba; gum-soled Gazelle-family trainers read correctly. (4) Team colors as the governing palette. (5) The British working-class milieu: pub culture, terraced houses, shutters, stairwells. (6) The TikTok vernacular: vertical 9:16 frame, plain sans-serif caption bands ("hottest trend of 2022"), username watermarks. (7) Adjacent cultural props documented but optional: ribbed white socks pulled up, bucket hats and beanies, canvas crossbody bags, references to British music (The Jam, The Stone Roses, The Streets) and football YouTube channels; brand grammar of Adidas, Nike, Kappa, Levi's, with Balenciaga as the high-fashion touchpoint.

**Materials and textures.** Slightly shiny polyester knit of vintage shirts with ribbed collars, button plackets, embroidered crests and flock or screen-printed sponsor logos; stiff cotton denim with honest fade and whiskering; suede toe tabs and gum rubber soles; ribbed cotton tube socks; corrugated metal, pebbledash, brick, tiled porches and painted wood as environmental texture. Nothing looks new, pressed or luxury-finished.

**Recurring subjects.** Ordinary people — young men most often, but the style accepts any age, gender or group; solo full-body figures dominate, with groups on steps or rooftops as a common variant. The subject stands still: arms crossed, thumbs hooked in pockets, or holding a garment up to camera; crouches on a doorstep; or tugs the shirt collar in close-up. The face is deadpan or faintly smirking, never performing joy for its own sake.

**Composition and lighting.** The source text prescribes garments and values but is silent on photographic technique; the image record supplies it. Framing is amateur and phone-native: center-weighted full-body figure with headroom for a caption band, vertical crop typical, horizontal acceptable. Backgrounds are found, not styled: hallways, staircases, bathrooms, driveways, alley walls, shutters, stadium seats, rooftops. Lighting is flat and unshaped — overcast daylight, ordinary domestic interior light, warm tungsten, occasional phone flash. Exposure is snapshot-honest: saturated but ungraded color, slight grain, no studio modifiers.

## Mood & Values

Deadpan sincerity with an ironic wink. Blokecore expresses love for football and nostalgia for classic football culture: passion for the club, identity, self-esteem, and the claim that a historically unappreciated style is in fact beautiful. Ordinariness is the virtue — the hallway, the mug of tea, the mum-in-a-shirt variant all signal that this uniform belongs to real life, not to fashion week. A acknowledged tension runs underneath: the trend has been criticized for gentrifying working-class symbols, so the best executions keep a self-aware, slightly jokey flatness rather than glossy appropriation.

## Era & Origins

Term coined late 2021, viral April 2022 on TikTok, timed with the 2022 FIFA World Cup in Qatar; inspiration drawn from UK fan dress of the 1970s–90s, hooligan culture, Casuals, Ultras and grime. Crucially, where Casuals avoided replica kits to stay discreet, Blokecore embraces the visible shirt — the replica is the point.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Kit first, and retro: collared or geometric 80s/90s cut, embroidered crest, period sponsor typeface, worn untucked and visible.
2. Denim anchor: plain light/mid-wash straight or baggy jeans, or knee-length denim shorts.
3. Period footwear: gum-soled vintage Adidas trainers; ribbed socks allowed above the collar.
4. Deadpan staging: static pose, straight to camera, expression flat or faintly amused.
5. Mundane stage: domestic interior, shuttered street, doorstep, terrace seats, rooftop — nothing curated.
6. Amateur register: phone-camera look, vertical crop welcome, flat light, optional plain caption overlay.
7. Palette rule: kit team colors + denim blue + white/gum + neutral or single saturated background block.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any age, gender, ethnicity, solo or group. Jersey provenance: any club or national team, obscure preferred; stripes, hoops, sashes, splashes all valid. Setting: interior, street, stadium, rooftop, or flat-lay color-block grid. Composition: full-body stand, crouch, collar close-up, garment hold-up, group arrangement, catalogue grid. Lighting: overcast daylight, domestic interior, tungsten, phone flash. Mood: deadpan, warm nostalgia, or lightly playful (a deadpan heart-hands gesture survives in this style). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Modern post-2010 kits (slim fit, heat-applied crests, template graphics); active sport depiction (playing, running, match action); studio editorial polish, seamless backdrops, beauty lighting, retouched skin; feminine coquette styling (skirts, ballet pumps, ribbons, lace); carnival yellow-blue beach or flag festivity; designer terrace wear with no replica shirt; night-out chaos with pints and club neon; bright puffer jackets and logo-belt preppiness; American franchise jerseys and snapbacks; skinny jeans, chunky modern sneakers, luxury logomania, pristine garments, curated minimalist interiors.
