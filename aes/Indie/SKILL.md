# Indie

Indie takes its name from "independent" — independent labels, independent release circuits, independent taste — and its visual language was formed where that independence was first made visible: on record sleeves, xeroxed gig posters, fanzine pages and cheap snapshot film. Born in the United Kingdom in the 1980s around jangling guitar bands and the C86 compilation, the aesthetic defined itself by opposition. Where major-label pop sleeves used bold contrasting colour, expensive fashion and glossy group photos, indie sleeves went the near-opposite way: monochrome portraits of mid-century film or sport stars, past cultural icons, or plainly dressed working-class people, printed matte and muted. That refusal — of gloss, of wealth signalling, of performed cheer — remains the style's core grammar, whether it appears on a 1986 seven-inch sleeve, a bedroom Polaroid with a sad line of type over it, or a faded colour photo of friends in a back garden.

## Visual Identity

### Colour palette

The rule: everything photographic stays desaturated, pale and slightly faded — paler and less intense than a pastel aesthetic, but moodier rather than sweet. Anything highly saturated must read as printed ink on paper (type, a flat colour block, poster ink), never as light inside the scene.

| Role | Hex range | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Paper-and-ink neutrals | #F0EBE3 → #C7C0B2 → #8B857A → #262421 | cream poster paper, greige walls, warm grey shadow, soft photographic black (never pure #000000) |
| Desaturated blues / teals | #2E5F66 → #7FA6A9 | overcast sky, washed denim, duotone tint washes |
| Sage and olive greens | #5C6E4F → #A9B495 | foliage, knitwear, faded park grass |
| Muted reds | #8E3B32 → #C06A57 | brick, rust, faded rose — never fire-engine |
| Sepia / warm fade | #D8C7AE → #B99C7E | aged snapshot warmth, brown ribbed knits |
| Faded duotone pink-magenta | #E3A9C0 → #C77E9C | occasional printed tint over a band photo |
| Flat graphic accents (print only) | cyan #00A3B4, cobalt #1E4FD6, poster yellow #E8C33A | vertical type bars, collaged colour blocks, gig-poster ink |

Monochrome is always available: straight silver-gelatin black and white, or a single-tint duotone (teal-green, cyan, sepia, faded magenta) applied like a print process, not like a digital filter.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how strongly the text documents them)

1. **Records and CDs, and the sleeve logic around them** — the style's central artefact. Sleeves favour found or staged monochrome portraits of 1950s–60s stars and cultural icons, or ordinary working-class subjects; contemporary graphics and glamour group photos are conspicuously absent.
2. **Moody atmosphere** — overcast light, dim rooms, a sense of weather and interiority; the infobox lists it as a key motif and the history ties it to the style's darker tone.
3. **Polaroids and nostalgic photographs** — snapshot imperfection, white Polaroid borders, found-photo subjects; in the blog-era variant, photos often carry sad overlaid text to state the mood outright.
4. **Books and poetry** — the bookish strand: paperbacks, notebooks, handwritten lines. Documented in the infobox but visually quiet; treat as prop-level presence, not set dressing.
5. **Thrifted casual wardrobe** — anoraks, cardigans, striped t-shirts and band t-shirts, jeans, flannel, denim and leather jackets, skullcaps and beanies, floppy fringes; Chuck Taylors, Doc Martens, winklepickers, tennis shoes; Ray-Ban sunglasses; little to no jewellery; natural hair and makeup.
6. **Fanzine and sleeve typography** — xeroxed cut-and-paste lettering, hand-drawn poster type, vertical sans-serif name bars, letterspaced serif capitals, flat geometric colour blocks collaged over photographs.
7. **Ordinary British environments** — terraced brick streets, back gardens, churchyards and park monuments, bedrooms and living rooms, rehearsal rooms, small gig venues; the everyday as subject, not backdrop.

### Materials and textures

Analogue film grain and matte print surface; silver-gelatin tonality with slightly lifted, greyed blacks; xerox and offset ink on cream paper with faint misregistration; faded dye colour of old snapshot prints; plastic Polaroid frames and taped poster corners. Cloth textures matter: ribbed wool knits, brushed flannel, stiff denim, corduroy-ish matte cotton. Nothing glossy, nothing laminated, nothing chrome.

### Recurring subjects

Young people presented as themselves rather than as stars: unsmiling or faintly tender faces, deadpan stares, hands in pockets, mid-gesture candids. Bands photographed like documentation — a plain-background row of friends, a cluster in a garden — instead of hero shots. Solo close-crop portraits, often in profile. Found vintage portraits of strangers or mid-century icons reused as sleeve imagery. Still lifes of the style's props: a stack of sleeves, a paperback, a Polaroid, a beer can or mug on a lived-in table.

### Composition and lighting

Composition follows sleeve and snapshot logic: tight off-centre face crops; full-length figures standing plainly inside an ordinary environment; documentary group arrangements on neutral ground; candid frames with imperfect centring; collaged layouts where flat colour shapes or type bars cut across a photo; fanzine grids of clipped type and line drawings. Negative space is either blank paper/white studio or cluttered domestic wallpaper — both acceptable, neither styled.

Lighting is anti-glamour by rule: diffuse overcast daylight with no directional sun; dim interior tungsten with pools of lamplight; direct on-camera flash throwing hard shadow into a dark room; flat scanner or copier light on paper artefacts. No three-point studio glamour, no golden hour, no softbox sheen.

## Mood & Values

Individualism, independence, philosophy, overcoming adversity — the infobox values — surface visually as understatement and sincerity. The mood is moody, wistful, deadpan; sadness is worn openly but quietly, sometimes literally captioned onto the photo. Thrift is an ethic as much as a look: assembled, personal, unbranded. There is a cult-belonging warmth inside the melancholy — the scene's fans were "cuties," "anoraks," "indie kids" — and a borrowed nostalgia for decades (the 1950s and 60s) predating the scene itself. Authenticity outranks polish at every decision point.

## Era & Origins

United Kingdom, 1980s, from post-punk independent guitar pop; the C86 compilation codified indie as a distinct genre and sensibility, and the music term reaches back to 1970s independent labels in the US and UK. The aesthetic was a rejection of 1980s mainstream pop culture, looking backwards to 1960s inspiration and mid-century imagery. Later currents shift the surface without replacing the core: the late-80s Madchester "baggy" brightening; the early-90s return to muted palette with shoegaze and grunge; the mid-90s Britpop boom as a brash commercial contrast while indiepop persisted on labels like Sarah Records and through bands like Belle and Sebastian; revivals including 2000s–2012 Indie Sleaze, 2010s Twee fashion, and the 2019–2020 Indie Kid iteration. The "vintage indie" core described here is the 80s–90s analog, muted strain.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- **Artefact first.** Imagine the output as a found object — sleeve scan, gig poster, snapshot, Polaroid — with print grain, paper tone and slight fade baked in.
- **Mute the scene, saturate only the ink.** Photographic colour stays faded; strong colour appears only as printed type or flat blocks.
- **Ordinary over iconic.** Cast real-feeling people in thrifted clothes in real-feeling places; if an icon appears, it arrives as a reused vintage monochrome portrait.
- **Deadpan emotion.** Unsmiling, wistful, quietly defiant; grief as a caption, never as a scream.
- **One era anchor.** Wardrobe, props and typography from 80s–90s thrift or mid-century found-photo source; no contemporary devices or styling.
- **Restraint in frame.** Few elements, plain or lived-in backgrounds, no styling-team polish.

Variation axes:
- **Subject:** band portraits, solo portraits, reused vintage portraits, domestic interiors, street/garden/churchyard exteriors, still lifes of records, books, Polaroids and poetry notebooks, gig posters, rehearsal rooms.
- **Composition:** close-cropped profile, full-figure-in-environment, deadpan group row, candid mid-gesture, collage with colour blocks or type bars, fanzine clip-grid.
- **Lighting:** overcast daylight, dim tungsten interior, direct on-camera flash, flat scanner light on paper.
- **Colour handling:** straight monochrome, single-tint duotone (teal, cyan, sepia, faded magenta), faded pale colour, or cream paper with black ink plus one flat accent.
- **Mood:** deadpan, wistful, tender, quietly miserable; never celebratory-glossy.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — muted analog surface, ordinary subject, unassuming mood, print-aware graphics — not any reference image. A still life of paperbacks on a windowsill in overcast light is as valid as a band portrait, and need not resemble any sleeve shown here.

Avoid:
- Glossy saturated pop colour, airbrushed skin, studio glamour lighting, expensive designer fashion, posed smiling group shots — the major-label sleeve logic indie defined itself against.
- Crisp digital rendering: HDR micro-contrast, creamy modern bokeh, lens flare, clean vector gradients.
- Neon club glow, chrome, electro logos, night-party flash glamour (Bloghouse territory).
- Union Jacks, mod parkas, brash primary-colour laddish swagger (Britpop).
- Baggy flared silhouettes, bucket hats, psychedelic swirls, dancing crowd energy (Madchester).
- Candy-pastel sweetness, cute critter illustration, innocent storybook tone (Twee / Pastel); indie is paler and moodier.
- Grunge's distressed uniform: torn denim, bandanas, grime-warm darkness, confrontational angst; indie flannel is cleaner, paler, bookish.
- Heavy jewellery, glam makeup, manicured styling of any kind.
- Cheerful advertising copy or brand-forward commercial framing; text in frame should be sleeve type, fanzine clipping, or a melancholy caption.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Their tell | Indie's tell |
|---|---|---|
| Madchester | Baggy flared jeans, bucket hats, psychedelic swirl prints, neon-bright colour, dance-crowd motion | Slim thrifted layers, muted palette, stillness, overcast or dim-room light |
| Twee | Candy pastels, childlike cute illustration, Peter Pan innocence, sweet hand-lettering | Paler-than-pastel neutrals with a moody undercurrent, melancholy captions, working-class realism; shares striped tees and anoraks but not the sweetness |
| Bloghouse | 2000s compact-flash party photos, neon glow, electro skinny-jean glamour, saturated LCD colour | Analog grain, pre-digital thrift, matte paper, no club lighting |
| Britpop | Union Jack motifs, mod parkas, brash saturated primaries, glossy magazine confidence | Unassuming matte surfaces, anti-brash posture, found-photo and fanzine graphics |
| Grunge | Ripped denim, bandanas, flannel-as-uniform with combat boots, dirty warm darkness, confrontational slouch | Cleaner thrift (cardigans, anoraks, striped tees), bookish props, cooler paler fade, deadpan rather than angsty |

Where evidence is thin, say so: the books-and-poetry motif is firmly listed in the style's key motifs but appears mostly as prop-level detail in surviving artefacts, and the sad-text overlay belongs specifically to the blog-era photographic variant rather than to 80s sleeve design. Everything else above is anchored in the style's documented history and its surviving printed and photographic matter.
