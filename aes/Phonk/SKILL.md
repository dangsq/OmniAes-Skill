# Phonk (OG / Rare Phonk)

Phonk's visual world is a fabricated memory of the 1990s Southern rap underground, rebuilt by early-2010s SoundCloud producers who never lived it. The style treats every image as a recovered artifact: a bootleg VHS tape, a dubbed cassette, a xeroxed mixtape cover found in a crate. Everything is tinted purple or pink — a direct quote of Houston "lean" (codeine syrup) culture — and everything is degraded: grain, tracking errors, dithering, halftone dots, flash glare. Onto that decayed ground the style layers the grammar of "bling"-era hip-hop cover art (the Pen & Pixel school): chrome bevelled lettering with starburst sparkles, collaged skies, sticker badges, and an unapologetic pile-up of type, props and cutout figures. Its subject matter circles a small set of obsessions: skulls and grim reapers, parked or slowly creeping 90s luxury sedans, cassette tapes, styrofoam cups and syrup bottles, money, graveyards, clouds, and anonymous hooded or masked figures. The mood splits into two documented registers — the dark, glitchy, horror-synth "Dirt" side (Doomshop Records) and the jazzy, foggy, psychedelic "Rare" side (DJ Smokey, Soudiere, Purple Posse, Holy Mob) — with a third, cartoon-ironic "Positive Squad" current of neon haunted houses and re-contextualized 90s cartoons.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Purple is atmosphere, not accent: the entire frame sits under a violet-to-magenta cast, with black as the co-key colour. Work inside this range:

- **Grounds:** violet-black `#060309` → `#140A1E`; pure black fields for the "Dirt" register.
- **Body purples:** `#2E1150` → `#5B21B0` / `#6A0DAD`; magenta `#9333EA` → `#C026D3`.
- **Tint and highlight:** hot pink `#E879F9` → `#FF4FD8`; pale lilac paper stock `#CBA6E4` → `#DFC0F0` for flat, printed/xerox-style covers.
- **Metal:** chrome silver `#B8B8C8` → `#FFFFFF` with a white outer glow, reserved for lettering and sparkles.
- **Sparse accents:** slime/acid green `#39FF14`–`#7CFF4F` (drip fonts, cartoon irony), blood red `#7A0C0C`–`#B91C1C`, and dusk amber `#D97706`–`#F59E0B` appearing only as a small sky pocket behind silhouettes.

Rule of thumb: if the dominant cast of an image is not in the violet–magenta family, or if black-plus-purple is not doing the structural work, it is not phonk. Saturation may run from washed-out lilac print to screaming magenta flash photography; both extremes are in-style.

### Typography and graphic fixtures

Type is a subject, not a label. The style mixes: chrome/bevelled metallic lettering with lens sparkles and heavy outer glow (bling-era Pen & Pixel inheritance); blackletter and arched gothic/occult serifs for collective names and titles; dripping slime or blood horror fonts, often acid green or red over purple; neon-tube outline script for the cloudy "Rare" register. Layout fixtures include cassette J-card and tape-spine margins, faux catalog numbers, deliberately wrong vintage years ("1991", "1993"), handwritten marker annotations, collective stamp seals, and the Parental Advisory badge as a near-constant corner sticker. Camcorder on-screen displays — REC timestamps, "SP", play icons, battery glyphs — are burned into frames as authenticity props.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how firmly the text documents them)

1. **Purple/pink lean tint** over everything — the style's unifying act.
2. **VHS / bootleg degradation:** grain, tracking errors, scan lines, low fidelity, "lost media" feel.
3. **Bling-era cover grammar:** chrome type, sparkles, collaged skies, sticker overlays (Pen & Pixel influence).
4. **1990s luxury sedans** — Lexus LS400, Mercedes W140, Cadillac — parked or slowly cruising, never speeding.
5. **Skulls, skeletons, the grim reaper**, frequently holding money or a drink: horror fused with "flexing" culture.
6. **Cassette tapes and bootleg tape emulation**, including mis-dated "1991/1993" Memphis references.
7. **Drug imagery:** styrofoam lean cups, purple syrup bottles, smoke; explicit in the Rare/"Nuke" sub-style.
8. **Re-contextualized 90s/2000s cartoons** (The Simpsons, SpongeBob) edited psychedelically — well documented in text, though in sampled covers the cartoon irony surfaces mostly as cartoonish haunted houses and mascots in the Positive Squad current.
9. **Graveyards, tombstones and crosses at night** — strongly image-corroborated extension of the Memphis horror root rather than a text-led motif.
10. **Religious iconography crossed with street themes** ("Holy Mob", gospel-titled tracks, reaper as pilgrim) — caption-level evidence; use sparingly.
11. **Cosmic horror:** planets, nebulae, starburst lens flares behind skulls — caption-level evidence; a minority register.
12. **Anonymous figures:** hoods up, masks, faces in shadow or turned from camera — the text never names this, but it pervades the imagery and follows directly from the culture's value of obscurity.

### Materials and textures

Bootleg VHS tape (grain, head-switching noise, horizontal tracking tears, colour bleed), dubbed cassette plastic and J-card paper, xerox and halftone print generation-loss on flat covers, on-camera flash glare and hotspots, fog and soft-focus cloud banks for the cloudy register, collage cutout edges with paper borders, vinyl sticker sheen, syrup liquid glow, dithered pixel noise. Surfaces should feel copied, re-copied and handled — never rendered.

### Composition and lighting

Square album-cover format dominates. Common structures: title-as-hero spanning the top or bottom third with the image stacked beneath; a single centered emblem or silhouetted figure against a sky; collage stacking (cutout figure + graveyard or sunset sky + prop pile + type layer + stickers); tape J-card layouts with a spine strip; loose flash-photography snapshots of hooded friends. Lighting options: harsh direct on-camera flash at night; backlit dusk horizons turning figures to silhouette; diffuse foggy overcast for the Rare register; emissive glowing type as the only light source on a black field; cosmic starburst flares. Depth stays shallow and murky, edges vignetting into black.

## Mood & Values

Haunted nostalgia for an era the maker may never have touched; obscurity as currency ("rare"); crate-digging authenticity and DIY competence prized over polish; a slowed, drug-weighted sense of time inherited from chopped-and-screwed vocals. The style holds two temperaments at once: menace (horror synths, reapers, skulls, the "Dirt" side) and cosy, ironic haze (jazz samples, clouds, cartoons, the "Rare" side). Values expressed: nostalgia, obscurity, crate-digging, DIY production, authenticity — loyalty to the underground tape against mainstream gloss.

## Era & Origins

Emerges early 2010s on SoundCloud as a global network with roots in Miami (SpaceGhostPurrp's Raider Klan, 2011: "Pheel Tha Phonk", "Bringin Tha Phonk") and in 1990s Memphis rap (Three 6 Mafia, DJ Squeeky, Tommy Wright III), plus Houston's chopped-and-screwed technique (DJ Screw) and lean culture. The 2014–2019 "Rare Phonk" era (DJ Smokey's Positive Squad/Nuke, Purple Posse, Holy Mob, against Doomshop's darker Dirt) fixes the visual language described here. From 2019 the name is co-opted by Russia-born Drift Phonk and later Brazilian Phonk (Funk Mandelão), forcing the retrospective labels "OG Phonk" / "Rare Phonk". The aesthetic reacts against clean modern production and streaming-era polish by fetishizing decay, and against its own viral successor by staying slow, parked and hazy.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Degrade last, but degrade always: finish the composition, then pass it through bootleg VHS/cassette generation loss (grain, tracking tear, dither, halftone).
2. Tint as atmosphere: the purple/magenta cast covers sky, skin, metal and type alike.
3. Let type act: chrome, blackletter, drip or neon-script lettering occupies real compositional weight, with sparkles and glow.
4. Collage over cohesion: mismatched resolutions, cutout edges, sticker overlays and stamped logos are virtues.
5. Keep the world slow: cars parked or creeping, figures standing, fog drifting — no speed, no motion blur.
6. Anonymize people: hoods, masks, shadow, backs turned, faces blown out by flash.
7. Fake the archive: wrong vintage years, tape labels, catalog numbers, camcorder OSD, Parental Advisory.
8. Pick a register and stay in it: Dirt (black-dominant, glitch, horror), Rare (cloudy lilac, jazz-soft, neon script), or Positive Squad (cartoon irony, acid green).

**Variation axes.** Subject: reaper or masked/hooded figure, boxed 90s sedan, skull pile, graveyard, cassette or tape stack, lean cup/syrup bottle, money stack, cartoon character, haunted house, planet or moon, cloud bank, group flash snapshot — any of these, or none, provided the rules hold. Composition: type-hero square, centered emblem, collage stack, J-card with spine, snapshot, silhouette-against-horizon. Lighting: night flash, dusk backlight, foggy diffuse, glowing-type-on-black, cosmic flare. Colour handling: full magenta duotone, violet-black low-key with one neon accent, pale lilac flat print with black line art, or a small amber sunset pocket inside a purple frame. Mood: menacing, cloudy, or ironic.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A magenta-flash night shot of a laundromat with chrome lettering, a tracking tear and a tape-label date is fully phonk even though no reference shows it.

**Avoid.** JDM drift cars, tire smoke, racing motion blur, highway speed and "sigma" edits (Drift Phonk); post-2005 car bodies, smartphones, modern UI, LED lighting; clean 4K sharpness, smooth digital gradients, vector minimalism, corporate sans-serifs, streaming-cover whitespace; cyan/teal cyberpunk neon and Y2K chrome futurism (Cyber Ghetto); vaporwave marble busts, palm grids and pastel computer UI; modern trap gloss (balloon letters, logo walls, pristine 3D renders, daylight luxury); untinted neutral daylight, warm earth tones, bokeh lifestyle photography; gore shock without haze or Southern signifiers (Horrorcore); medieval dungeon/occult xerox imagery without street-luxury elements (Dungeon Rap); candy-paint slabs with wire-wheel swangas as the hero (Trillwave); festival lasers, strobe crowds, any high-tempo EDM energy.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Dungeon Rap:** shares lo-fi cassette xerox and occult dread, but swaps Southern street signifiers for medieval/fantasy dungeon iconography and monochrome black-metal print; phonk keeps chrome bling type, purple tint, cars and lean cups.
- **Trillwave:** also purple and Houston-derived, but smoother and celebratory — candy-painted slabs, wire wheels, slab culture and clean cloud gradients; phonk adds VHS decay, Memphis skulls and Pen & Pixel collage roughness.
- **Cyber Ghetto:** Y2K futurism — chrome, cyan/magenta neon, digital UI, forward-looking tech gloss; phonk is backward-looking decay (VHS, cassette, halftone), never sleek or futuristic.
- **Drift Phonk:** JDM coupes mid-drift, tire smoke, motion blur, high-contrast black/red or neon, cowbell-meme energy; phonk's cars are parked or creeping boxed sedans under purple haze at 60–85 BPM visual tempo.
- **Horrorcore:** broader rap horror imagery that can be clean, digital and gore-forward; phonk's horror is always filtered through nostalgia haze, purple tint and bling-era collage, with skulls as flex props rather than shock props.
- **Shared-border note:** Cloud Rap's haze and Vaporwave's media-decay nostalgia overlap, but phonk remains anchored in hip-hop street signifiers — tape badges, advisory stickers, chrome type, sedans, syrup cups.

**One-line recognition test:** purple-tinted, VHS-degraded, chrome-lettered Southern hip-hop collage that feels like a lost 1993 tape — slow, hooded, skull-strewn and proudly obsolete.
