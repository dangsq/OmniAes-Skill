# Hipster

Hipster is less a single look than a philosophy of looking: authenticity over polish, the handmade over the mass-produced, the obscure over the popular. Visually it resolves into a broad family of images united by analog mediation (film grain, faded filters, letterpress texture), objects with provenance (thrifted clothes, vinyl, old cameras, bikes), and settings that are either intensely urban — brick, signage, coffee shops, loft windows — or deliberately wild — conifers, lakes, mountains, night sky. Suburbs are absent by ideology: they stand for the conformist culture the style rejects. The register oscillates between deadpan irony and sincere warmth, and both are correct.

## Visual Identity

### Color language

Any hue is admissible, but only through one of four handling modes. Commit to one mode per image; mixing modes without intent reads as generic stock photography.

1. **Faded analog earth tones** (the dominant mode): bone and cream papers, mustard ochre, rust and brick red, olive moss, walnut and saddle leather brown, denim indigo, faded teal. Approximate range: `#F4F1EA`–`#E9E2D4` papers; `#C9973B`–`#D8A94E` ochre; `#9C4A2F`–`#B3543A` rust; `#6C7A4A`–`#8A8F6A` olive; `#6B4A32`–`#8A5A3C` leather; `#46586A`–`#5E7186` denim; `#6F8F92`–`#8FA8A8` washed teal. Blacks are lifted toward charcoal (`#2E2C2A`), never pure.
2. **Dissonant, intentionally unstylish clash** (the Twee/"cutester" branch): dusty pink `#E8B7C4`, mint `#BFDCC8`, pastel lemon `#F2E3A1` colliding with hot magenta `#C43A6B` or buffalo-check red `#A63A2A`. The clash is the point — it signals rejection of coordinated fast fashion.
3. **Gothic/artist minimal**: ink black `#141414`–`#2E2C2A` type and line art on textured off-white or pale gray paper, with generous empty margin.
4. **Night cool versus artificial warm**: deep indigo sky `#10182B`–`#1C2A3F` against a single tungsten or firelight amber source `#F2A93B`–`#E8873A`.

Saturation is either muted by fade (modes 1 and 4) or deliberately garish in clash (mode 2). Glossy, evenly saturated commercial color is never correct.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the culture documents them

1. **Music and analog audio** — vinyl records and crates of them, turntables, colored and translucent pressings, acoustic instruments in small rooms, jam sessions, obscure instruments (singing saw, hurdy-gurdy), small-venue stages. Music is the subculture's fundamental credential.
2. **Vintage and thrift** — film cameras held up to the face (rangefinders, TLRs, instant cameras), fedoras and bowlers, beanies, suspenders, flannel (red buffalo check above all), ripped skinny jeans, knit scarves, round or thick-framed glasses, visible tattoos, large beards and mustaches.
3. **Bicycles** — fixies and upright Dutch-style bikes, ridden or leaned against graffiti walls; cycling as transport, politics, and outfit display.
4. **Intellectual signifiers** — books, glasses, art prints, typographic posters and quotes on textured paper, hand-drawn annotated line-art diagrams of the hipster himself.
5. **Urban fabric** — quirky storefronts and hand-painted signs (coffee shop, barber, hairdresser), brick walls, barber poles, historic avenues, graffiti, downtown skylines seen through loft windows.
6. **Third places** — coffee shops, bookstores, brew pubs, brunch tables, craft beer glasses and cheap lager cans.
7. **Lumbersexual nature** — rough-sawn wood, coniferous forest, mountains, lakes, leather boots and belts, acoustic guitars, isolated cabins and roads.
8. **DIY, travel and causes** — handmade goods, notebooks and field cameras laid out flat, backpacks, hostels, markets, protest signs.
9. **Photography about photography** — stacks of old cameras, film strips, snapshots of people taking snapshots.

### Materials and textures

Patina is mandatory. Raw brick, rough wood with open grain, worn and cracked leather, selvedge denim, wool flannel and knitwear, letterpress paper with visible tooth, record sleeves with ring wear, brass and chrome on old optics, concrete sidewalks, pine needles and bark. Surfaces should show scuffs, fading, fraying, or hand-made irregularity; nothing looks factory-new.

### Composition and framing

Two poles, both authentic: **minimal** — centered distressed typography on empty paper, a close-up of bedsheets, a lone jetty reaching into a still lake, symmetric storefront elevations; and **cluttered** — Twee interiors stacked with records and keyboards, top-down flat-lays of personal objects arranged in loose grids, crowded house-party rooms. Between them sits the candid snapshot: slightly off-center, imperfect horizon, subject caught mid-gesture or deadpan toward the lens. Square and 4:5 crops recall the Instagram era that the culture helped create, but any aspect ratio works if the treatment holds.

### Light and film treatment

Available light first: flat overcast daylight, soft window light in cluttered rooms, warm tungsten interiors. Hard on-camera flash appears in party and snapshot candids, giving hard shadows and slightly blown skin. Night work uses long exposure: star fields and Milky Way over a dark treeline with one warm artificial glow. Regardless of light source, the image is mediated — film grain or sensor noise, lifted faded blacks, washed highlights, a slight green or magenta cast, gentle vignetting, or altered sharpness. The edit is visible but humble; it signals artistic vision, not technical spectacle.

## Mood & Values

The style expresses authenticity, independence, artistry, and education; rejection of corporate and mainstream products; sustainability, veganism, and green politics; and a romanticism hidden under a veneer of irony. The emotional temperature is understated cool with sincere warmth underneath — a smirk, not a grin. Objects are loved for their history and obscurity ("real music isn't on the radio"), places for their localness and smallness. There is nostalgia for analog media and for mid-century eras the subject never lived, plus a documentary impulse: ordinary things — shoes on concrete, a coffee cup, a bike lock — photographed as if anthropologically significant.

## Era & Origins

The term descends from 1940s jazz-hip circles (the Beatnik lineage), but the visual style described here is the repopularization of the 2000s–2010s: codified in Williamsburg, Brooklyn and London's Shoreditch, spread to Berlin, Seattle, Vancouver, Montreal, then Portland and Austin. Indie music's mainstreaming (2004–2008), the Great Recession's disillusionment with corporatism (2008–2011), and Etsy-enabled commodification plus "stomp-clap" indie folk (2011–2014) successively built and dissolved it; the Pacific Northwest contributed the lumbersexual branch of flannel, beards, and conifers. Crucially, "hipster" also named the early Tumblr photo-blog culture of tagging images "aesthetic," which is why faded filter photography and minimal typography belong to the style even when no flannel shirt is in frame.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Pick one color mode from the four above and hold it across the whole frame.
2. Mediate the image: grain, fade, lifted blacks, vignette, or letterpress/paper texture. Nothing may look like a clean digital render.
3. Give every object provenance — thrifted, handmade, analog, worn. Newness breaks the spell.
4. Set the scene in an urban core, a third place (café, bookstore, brew pub, small venue), or wilderness. Never suburbia.
5. Style people in layered thrift: flannel, suspenders, beanies, scarves, ripped skinny jeans, round glasses, tattoos, facial hair. Posture is relaxed or deadpan, never glamour-posed.
6. If type appears, make it distressed stamp-style sans, hand-lettering, or an annotated line-art diagram; the copy may be a quote, a boast, or a dry joke.
7. Let irony and sincerity coexist: a wink in the juxtaposition, warmth in the light.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- *Subject*: anything from the motif list, or any ordinary object treated as artifact (bedsheets, a pastry, a bus ticket, a houseplant on a windowsill).
- *Composition*: minimal negative space ↔ dense Twee clutter; flat-lay knolling ↔ off-kilter candid; symmetric elevation ↔ long-exposure landscape.
- *Lighting*: overcast daylight ↔ window light ↔ tungsten interior ↔ hard flash ↔ starlit long exposure.
- *Color*: faded earth tones ↔ dissonant pastel clash ↔ black-on-paper ↔ night cool/warm.
- *Mood*: deadpan irony ↔ earnest warmth; solitary contemplation ↔ crowded house party.
- *Medium*: 35mm film look, filtered phone snapshot, letterpress print, hand-drawn line illustration.

**Avoid**

- Glossy HDR, airbrushed skin, seamless-studio commercial polish.
- Suburban tract houses, malls, chain-restaurant interiors, global corporate logos as props.
- Pristine current-generation gadgets as hero objects; sleek product-render lighting.
- Neon cyberpunk/synthwave palettes, luxury fashion monograms, yachts, sports cars, stadium crowds.
- Saturated tropical-resort brightness; clean textureless vector flatness.
- Smudged-makeup nightclub hedonism, xerox-zine anarchist squalor, monochrome turtleneck mid-century modernism, EDM blog graphics — these belong to neighboring styles listed below.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Hipster |
|---|---|
| **Hipness Purgatory** | Almost exclusively flat, deadpan hand-drawn line illustration on beige/white with sardonic captions; no photographic texture, no objects-with-patina. Hipster borrows the line-art diagram as one branch but stays rooted in photographed, textured reality. |
| **Indie Sleaze** | Flash-lit party chaos, smeared makeup, sweat, nightclub decay, hedonistic glamour. Hipster's flash candids are warmer, clothed in flannel and knitwear, set in houses and venues rather than clubs; curation and artisanal calm outweigh decadence. |
| **Beatnik** | 1950s–60s monochrome: black turtlenecks, berets, bongos, existential coffeehouse jazz, mid-century modernist framing. Hipster shares the intellectual coffeehouse lineage but adds faded color film emulation, thrift-layering, bikes, craft beer, and Pacific Northwest woods. |
| **Bloghouse** | Late-2000s electro-blog energy: neon and LED color, digital saturation, dance-floor graphics, Myspace-angle gloss. Hipster is acoustic, analog, earth-toned; its nightlife is a jam session, not a rave. |
| **Folk Punk** | Genuine DIY poverty and political rage: patched clothes, cassettes, squats, xerox zine grit, banjo-fueled urgency. Hipster's lo-fi is a chosen filter over comfortable, brunch-funded surroundings; its politics are protest-sign progressive, not anarchist squatter. |

When in doubt, ask: does the image look lovingly mediated by old technology, populated by objects with history, and set against either brick or pines — with a smirk of irony over a sincere heart? If yes, it is Hipster.
