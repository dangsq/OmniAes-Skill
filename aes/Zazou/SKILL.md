# Zazou

Zazou was a French youth movement of the early 1940s, flourishing roughly 1941–1944 under Nazi occupation and the Vichy regime. It was cultural resistance worn on the body: against rationing, curfew morality and official solemnity, Zazous answered with exaggerated tailoring, forbidden American swing jazz, and a public commitment to leisure. Their look borrowed from British dandyism and the American zoot suit (Cab Calloway above all, whose scat lyric "Zaz Zuh Zaz" gave the movement its name, alongside Johnny Hess's "Je suis swing"), then distorted it into something deliberately wasteful and mocking. Cellar clubs in the Latin Quarter (Dupont-Latin, the Capoulade), the terrace of the Pam Pam on the Champs-Élysées, and afternoon bicycle rides in the Bois de Boulogne were their stages. Authorities repaid them with round-ups, head-shavings and the JPF slogan "Scalp the Zazous!" — which only confirmed the style's meaning: elegance as insubordination.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
The field is monochrome plus pattern: ink black and shirt white carry the silhouette, while checks, stripes and tartan supply the "colour" as structure rather than hue. Saturated colour is rationed to small defiant areas — lips, socks, a tie, a pocket square — exactly inverse to wartime austerity logic. Workable ranges:

- Neutrals: wool black `#0e0e0e–#2a2a2a`; paper and shirt cream `#e9e4d6–#f5f2ea`; silver-gelatin mid-greys `#7a7a7a–#a8a8a8` for monochrome renderings.
- Check and tartan field: muted madder `#7d2a2a–#a03c3c`, bottle green `#2e4636–#3f5c46`, navy `#22304a–#2c3a52`, mustard `#a8842c–#c49a3a`, woven over a cream ground.
- Defiance accents: scarlet lipstick `#b3121f–#d92632`; bright sock yellow or red `#e0b32a–#efc53f`; favoured blonde hair `#cfa75e–#e0c084` with deliberate dark roots `#2f2620`.

Rule of thumb: never let saturated colour exceed a few percent of the frame; the check grid is the style's chromatic engine. Period colour photography of the movement effectively does not exist, so any colour handling beyond black, white, checks and named accents is inference — keep it plausible to 1940s dye chemistry, not modern neon.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)
1. **The exaggerated jacket**: extra-large, often knee-length, multiple pockets and half-belts, openly flaunting fabric rationing; for women, extremely wide padded shoulders.
2. **Checks, stripes, tartan**: the jacket and skirt patterns named as key colours, in defiance of austerity.
3. **Contradictory proportions below**: men's trousers fitted, gathered at the waist, cut short to reveal socks; women's skirts short and pleated, often tartan.
4. **Thick-soled shoes**: suede with white or brightly coloured socks for men; thick wooden platform soles for women; striped or net stockings.
5. **The furled "Chamberlain" umbrella**, carried closed and never opened — an Anglophile emblem.
6. **Hair**: long, greased, teased into an untidy quiff (men); shoulder-length curls or braids, blonde with visible dark roots (women).
7. **The face**: heavy 1930s-Hollywood makeup, bright red lipstick, darkened eyes, oversized sunglasses; occasionally a small Clark Gable moustache.
8. **Shirt and tie**: high-collared shirts, sometimes held by a horizontal pin, with narrow ties of cotton or heavy wool.
9. **The swing body**: jive kicks, lindy turns, energetic dancing to banned jazz in cellar clubs.
10. **Jazz-leisure props**: gramophones and records, café terraces, cellar club interiors, bicycles.

Hats (wide brims, feathered trims) appear in the period photographic record but are not text-canonical; treat them as permissible period accessories, not required motifs.

### Materials and textures
Wool tweed and flannel checks with visible weave; heavy wool or cotton tie cloth; suede uppers and stacked leather or wooden soles; rayon net and striped stockings; crisp pleated tartan; greased hair with a wet shine; cotton umbrella canopy and polished wooden ferrule. In image surface terms: silver-gelatin gloss, newspaper halftone dot grain, or clean ink line for caricature mode.

### Recurring subjects
Young men and women as couples or small groups; dancers caught mid-motion; seated or lounging dandies radiating nonchalance; street and terrace clusters; stage performers grinning through a routine; and the style's own print tradition of hand-drawn caricature doubles with a caption band.

### Composition and lighting
The silhouette is the argument, so framing favours full-body or three-quarter views that let the proportion joke land: enormous shoulder-and-jacket mass above a narrow, shortened leg. Dance images tilt into diagonals and frozen kicks; dandy portraits go static, crossed-legged, chin up, sunglasses on. Lighting in the photographic record is hard and direct — on-camera flash flattening a club wall, or a stage spotlight with fast falloff into darkness — producing stark monochrome contrast. Daylight terrace and street snapshots are flatter but still high-contrast black and white. The illustrative strand uses flat even light, contour line, check hatching and halftone texture.

## Mood & Values

Individualism, nonconformity, defiance, hedonism, freedom. The Zazou face is ironic: a grin mid-jive, a shrug, a raised eyebrow behind oversized lenses. Pleasure is performed loudly in public — cafes, dancing, elaborate outfits — as passive contempt for demands of sacrifice. Dark humour and sarcasm run through it (some wore yellow stars inscribed "Zazou" or "Goy" in mockery of the compulsory badge). Nothing about the posture is martial, mournful or earnest; the style's weapon is frivolity taken seriously.

## Era & Origins

Paris, 1940–1944, under occupation and Vichy. A spontaneous reaction to rationing, censorship and imposed moral codes; descendant of British dandyism and the American zoot suit, sibling to Germany's Swingjugend and ancestor-in-spirit to later European style rebellions. It ends as a movement with the Liberation, though some Zazous took up arms in 1944 — the final inversion of their carefully cultivated indifference.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- Build the silhouette first: jacket length and shoulder width inflated, trouser hem raised and narrowed. If the proportion contrast disappears, the style disappears.
- Spend fabric ostentatiously: pockets, half-belts, pleats, padding — waste is the message.
- Keep colour discipline: monochrome field, check as pattern-colour, one or two hot accents at lip or ankle scale.
- Put banned music in the body: syncopated weight, kicked heel, turned torso, clapped hands.
- Set the scene in occupied-Paris leisure: cellar club, café terrace, park ride, narrow street — never battlefield, never office.
- Choose a period surface: high-contrast monochrome photograph with flash or spotlight, grainy halftone print, or ink caricature with a hand-lettered caption.

Variation axes:
- **Subject**: solo dandy, female Zazou, couple, dance pair, group cluster, stage performer, caricature figure, even a still life of the props (furled umbrella, thick-soled shoes, record sleeve). Any subject within this range is correct.
- **Composition**: full-body silhouette, candid mid-dance diagonal, seated portrait, street snapshot group, poster-like illustrative layout with caption band.
- **Lighting**: hard flash, stage spotlight, overcast terrace daylight, flat illustrative light — all acceptable if contrast stays assertive.
- **Colour handling**: pure monochrome; monochrome with hand-tinted accents; restrained period colour centred on checks and scarlet.
- **Mood**: from exuberant jive joy to cool, sarcastic lounging.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid:
- An opened umbrella (the emblem is strictly furled).
- Ballooning, pegged, wide draped trousers — that is the zoot suit / pachuco proportion; Zazou trousers are narrow and short.
- Teddy Boy Edwardianism: velvet collars, brocade waistcoats, bootlace ties, drainpipe trousers.
- Head-to-toe candy saturation in the Stilyagi manner.
- Modern artifacts: sneakers, denim jeans, synthetic sheen, screens, post-1950s vehicles or instruments.
- Military uniforms, martial posture, propaganda gravitas, grief or earnestness.
- Austerity plainness on the principal figures: make-do-and-mend utility dress is the opposite pole the style mocks.
- Soft modern studio lighting, pastel low-contrast grading, neon or digital colour.
- Military short back-and-sides or modern fades; crisp contemporary pompadours instead of the long, greased, untidy quiff.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Swingjugend (German Swing Kids)**: the closest twin — same era, same banned jazz, same long hair and checked Anglophile jackets. Separate them by context and silhouette detail: Zazou images centre the cropped trouser hem with flashing sock, the furled Chamberlain umbrella, and a fully documented female look (blonde curls with dark roots, tartan pleated skirt, wooden platform soles, oversized sunglasses) in Paris cellar clubs and French halftone caricature; Swingjugend imagery skews to male groups in Hamburg dance halls with more conventional trouser lengths and without the tartan-and-umbrella emblem set. Overlap is real; the trouser hem, the women, and the Parisian print culture are the tells.
- **Teddy Boys**: 1950s London, Edwardian revival — long drape jackets with velvet collars, high-waisted drainpipe trousers, brocade waistcoats, bootlace ties, slicked duck's-arse quiffs, rock 'n' roll rather than swing, sober black-grey-white palette, territorial toughness instead of ironic hedonism. No umbrellas, no cropped socks, no tartan skirts.
- **Stilyagi**: Moscow from the late 1940s–50s — post-war, Soviet, and chromatically loud in solid candy blocks (turquoise, orange, yellow) with wide lapels and shouting ties; the mood is aspirational mimicry of Western abundance, not occupation-era defiance. Missing: wartime monochrome press grain, tartan pleats, the furled umbrella, the blonde-with-roots female code.
- **Bonus cue — American zoot suit / pachuco**: inverted proportions (trousers wide and draped, pegged at the ankle). Zazou keeps the leg narrow and short while only the jacket grows; if the trousers balloon, it is not Zazou.
