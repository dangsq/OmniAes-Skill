# Signalwave (Broken Transmission)

Signalwave is an underground subgenre of vaporwave that emerged in the early 2010s on Bandcamp and YouTube, built from short, raw samples of old television and radio broadcasts: commercials, jingles, news bumpers, public service announcements, weather channel background music. Its visual language is a direct extension of that sound. Album art and companion imagery almost exclusively consist of a single, low-quality screencap taken from a VHS recording of an old broadcast — a news anchor, a commercial set, a weather map, a station's sign-off screen — rendered uncanny by media degradation and by detachment from its original context. The image is not designed; it is *found*, a frozen moment from a forgotten tape, simulating the experience of channel surfing late at night and tuning into a transmission nobody has watched in decades. Where parent vaporwave polishes and collages, signalwave leaves the fragment raw: atmosphere and texture over melody, decay over composition.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The wiki text does not prescribe a palette; the rule below is distilled from the corpus of broadcast screencaps. Every hue behaves as if it has passed through composite video and several generations of tape copying: saturation muted one step, blacks lifted to milky indigo rather than true black, highlights blooming with halos, adjacent colors bleeding into each other at high-contrast edges.

| Palette role | Hex range | Typical carrier |
|---|---|---|
| Tape-shadow / lifted black | `#1E1B2E` – `#4A3F63` | void backgrounds, night sky, unlit studio |
| Institutional blue & cyan-teal | `#4E6E9E` – `#8FB6C4` | wireframe globes, map seas, daylight sky, studio backdrops |
| Pale broadcast green | `#9CC4A8` – `#B8D4B8` | weather-map landmasses, banner bars, CRT phosphor tint |
| Chroma-leak magenta / pink | `#C858A8` – `#F078C0` | gradient fills, glitch bands, smeared silhouettes |
| Sodium amber & dusk rust | `#D98E32` – `#E8B44A`, accent `#B4552D` | sun discs, sunset skies, lens-flare beads, horizon glow |
| Cream / off-white graphics | `#E4E0D4` | caption type, wireframe lines, map outlines |

Any hue is permissible inside the style as long as it obeys the mediation rule: faded, bled, bloomed, never crisp. A frame may lean cool (teal-blue night), warm (amber dusk), or chroma-stormed (magenta glitch), and still read as signalwave.

### Signature motifs and subjects, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Weather forecast graphics.** The text names weather channels as a key motif, and the genre's iconography includes a weather-forecast artist alias and a sunflower-titled forecast album. Expect pixel-dithered regional maps, repeating sun-behind-cloud and snow icons, front arrows, and a solid banner bar carrying a weekday or program name in plain white sans-serif.
2. **Station sign-off screens and idents.** The text lists station sign-offs among key motifs: the emblem floating in a void — a rotating wireframe latitude-longitude globe, an orbit ring cradling a small Earth, a starburst flare, a test card, a "goodnight" slide — often with a short slogan set in spaced lowercase type.
3. **News broadcast frames.** News anchors and news bumpers are named source material: the desk, the medium-shot presenter, the lower-third bar, the ticker, the studio's flat blue backdrop.
4. **Commercial sets and jingle graphics.** Television commercials and radio jingles are the genre's raw feed: product on a pastel studio set, slogan text, the smiling presenter portrait of a regional ad.
5. **Tape degradation itself.** Static, hiss-equivalent snow, head-switching noise bands, tracking-error skew at the frame edge, scanlines — the text lists static and VHS distortion as key motifs, so damage is subject matter, not accident.
6. **Channel-surfing residue.** On-screen display text: a `PLAY` indicator, a timestamp, a channel bug, monospaced captions, occasionally a computer-window frame swallowing the broadcast — the visual trace of someone pausing, rewinding, surfing.
7. **Mundane establishing footage.** The text calls the subjects mundane; the corpus specifies the types: dusk city skylines with speckled window lights, a water tower silhouetted against a soft sun disc, snowed-in apartment blocks, a broadcast tower against pale sky — the B-roll a station airs between programs.
8. **Early CGI broadcast graphics.** Wireframe globes with satellites, low-poly objects drifting over checkerboard voids, a wireframe hand meeting a real one: the computer-generated bumper art of 1980s television. The wiki text is thin here, naming only sign-offs and broadcasts; the image corpus documents this strand prominently, so treat it as a subset of the ident/sign-off motif rather than a separate pillar.

### Materials, textures, medium

The surface is always a CRT-and-tape surface. VHS grain and composite-video noise sit over everything; early computer graphics and map fills show dithering and hard pixel edges; interlace combing touches anything that moved; chroma bleed smears reds and magentas sideways; bright sources halo and bloom; focus is uniformly soft, never selectively sharp. Highlight noise can sparkle into bokeh-like beads in dark scenes. Optional datamosh smears and color-band displacement stand in for a damaged head. Nothing in the style is paper, canvas, or glossy digital render — the medium is a recording of a recording.

### Typography and on-screen graphics

Type is diegetic broadcast graphics, never poster design: blocky pixel sans-serifs, chrome or gradient-bevel logo type for station idents, plain white sans-serif inside solid banner bars, monospaced OSD readouts, tickers and lower-thirds. Text content is institutional and incidental — weekday names, slogans like station taglines, channel names, window title bars. Misspellings of decay (dropped lines, torn characters) are welcome; sleek modern kerning is not.

### Composition and framing

The frame behaves like a paused tape: frontal, static, centered. Subjects sit at middle distance with straight horizons; ident emblems float centered in a void; maps fill the frame edge to edge; portraits are medium shots against flat backdrops. Negative space is generous — empty sky, black void, snowfield — because the broadcast is between moments. The 4:3 television ratio is native, often cropped square for album use. Frame edges may carry a noise band, a skew, or a flutter of tracking error. There are no dynamic camera angles, no rule-of-thirds storytelling, no depth-of-field photography look; the composition is whatever the broadcast happened to be when the tape stopped.

### Lighting

Lighting is whatever the source tape had, flattened by video: even, shadowless studio light for sets and presenters; ambient dusk for exteriors, with a large soft sun disc and sodium-orange horizon; night as a blue field punctuated by blooming point lights from windows and traffic; void idents lit only by self-emissive graphics and starburst lens flares from an analog video lens. Every bright edge blooms. There is no chiaroscuro, no motivated cinematic light, no golden-hour photography glow — only video light.

## Mood & Values

Signalwave expresses nostalgia, memory, liminality, media decay and mystery — including nostalgia for media the viewer never personally lived through. The mundane becomes uncanny simply by being frozen and decontextualized: a weather map for a Friday thirty years gone, a slogan addressing an audience that no longer exists. Comfort and eeriness occupy the same frame; the transmission is fading in and out of reception, and that fading is the point. The style values rawness over polish, fragment over composition, distance over clarity. It is an archival tenderness toward dead media: the broadcast has been freed from its selling function and left to drift, hypnotic and looped, like the audio it accompanies.

## Era & Origins

Signalwave emerged in the early 2010s from the experimental side of the vaporwave scene, on Bandcamp and YouTube. Foundational releases include *Midnight Television* (2011), Infinity Frequencies' early work (2012), and Vektroid's *Prism Genesis* (2011, as Fuji Grid TV), whose chaotic collage of cut-up commercials and jingles provided the blueprint for the "broken transmission" approach. The source stratum it excavates is VHS-recorded broadcast television of roughly the 1980s through mid-1990s: the era of regional weather maps, station idents, and analog sign-offs. The style reacts twice over — against vaporwave's melodic sample-flipping by keeping fragments raw and atmospheric, and against designed album art by substituting the found screencap.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Render the tape, not the scene: the mediation layer (grain, bleed, bloom, lifted blacks, scanlines, optional tracking skew) is the style; without it, the subject is just a picture.
2. Choose mundane institutional content: something a television station would air — forecast, ident, news, ad, sign-off, B-roll skyline — never a personal or heroic moment.
3. Freeze one moment: no action in progress, no narrative beat, no before or after. The frame is a still caught mid-broadcast.
4. Degrade with grammar, not chaos: dither on graphics, chroma bleed on saturated edges, bloom on lights, snow in shadows; damage should look like aging equipment, not random filters.
5. Let text be diegetic: banners, OSD, tickers, ident logos. If type appears, it belongs to the broadcast world.
6. Keep it sparse: one subject, generous emptiness, silence made visible. Restraint is the composition.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be any broadcast content type from any region or channel: a forecast map of any country, an anchor of any nationality, a product ad for any object, an ident emblem of any device (globe, orbit, tower, hand, eye), a skyline of any city, a test card, a sign-off slide, a satellite view of any continent, a blurred party-light crowd, a snowbound housing estate, a presenter portrait. Composition may range from centered void ident to full-bleed map to medium portrait to wide skyline, with or without text overlay, with or without a window/UI frame. Lighting may be dusk amber, night blue, flat studio, or void-with-flare. Color handling may lean cool teal, warm amber, or magenta chroma-storm, anywhere in the palette table, as long as saturation stays faded and blacks stay lifted. Mood may slide from sleepy warmth to eerie emptiness. Text may be absent, a single banner, an OSD readout, or a ticker. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A generated frame that shows none of the exact scenes above can still be perfect signalwave if it reads as a paused, decaying broadcast of something mundane.

**Avoid.**
- Crisp modern digital clarity: 4K sharpness, clean gradients, true blacks, lens-corrected perfection, HDR saturation.
- Horror payloads: menace, blood, monsters, threatening or corrupted faces meant to scare, emergency-alert intrusions, occult symbols — these convert the image into analog horror.
- Vaporwave props: Greek busts, marble, palm trees, mall atriums, dense sticker collages, ironic consumerist montage.
- Synthwave/outrun signage: neon grid horizons with chrome suns, sports cars, saturated neon cityscapes.
- Clean vector illustration, flat modern UI, smartphones, contemporary web or app interfaces, post-2000s typefaces and meme fonts.
- Pristine cosmic imagery: crisp nebulae, high-resolution planets, awe-framed space vistas without tape mediation (spacecore).
- Empty architecture photographed clean or flash-lit with no broadcast mediation (liminal space photography).
- Mid-century sepia print, educational filmstrip or public-information-film collage (hauntology).
- Cozy hand-drawn bedroom or lo-fi illustration scenes (lo-fi art).
- Narrative motion: running figures, events unfolding, multiple-panel collages telling a sequence. Signalwave pauses; it never plays.

## Discrimination Cues

**Analog Horror** shares the entire VHS broadcast grammar — scanlines, OSD, alert screens — but inserts dread: a distorted face, an intrusive message, a narrative threat breaking through the signal. Signalwave's degradation is aging, not intrusion; its content stays mundane and its mood wistful, ambient, calm. If the frame wants to frighten you, it is analog horror; if it wants you to half-remember something, it is signalwave.

**Hauntology** also mourns lost media, but its source stratum is mid-twentieth-century print and film: public information films, educational filmstrips, sepia and bleached palettes, modernist print typography, paper collage. Signalwave is electronic and VHS-era — tape noise, broadcast OSD, dithered computer graphics — with cool video color rather than sepia paper tone.

**Liminal Space** trades in the same emptiness and threshold feeling, but delivers it as clean or flash-lit photography of empty transitional architecture: corridors, malls, pools, no mediation layer. Signalwave's emptiness is on-screen — broadcast voids, sign-off slides, snowfields shot as B-roll — and always carries the tape surface; its architecture appears only as television content.

**Lo-fi Art** shares grain and a chill, sleepy mood, but is hand-drawn or illustrated cozy domesticity: bedrooms, rain on windows, warm illustrative palettes. Signalwave is found institutional footage, not illustration, and its comfort is threaded with eeriness and distance rather than domestic warmth.

**Spacecore** shares globes, satellites and orbital views, but frames them as sublime: crisp or dreamily rendered cosmic vistas meant to inspire awe. Signalwave's space is bureaucratic and mediated — a wireframe CGI globe, an ident satellite, a low-res Earth horizon — degraded on tape and framed as a station graphic, never as a window onto the infinite.

**Parent vaporwave**, finally, is the polish test: vaporwave collages, glosses and ironizes; signalwave leaves the screencap raw. If the frame looks designed, it has drifted upstream; if it looks recovered, it is signalwave.
