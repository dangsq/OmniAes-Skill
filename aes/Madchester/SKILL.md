# Madchester

Madchester is a music-driven youth culture that turned a grey industrial city into a colour event. Born in Manchester, England around 1987 from the meeting of indie guitar bands (The Stone Roses, Happy Mondays, Inspiral Carpets, The Charlatans, James, 808 State) with the acid house played at The Haçienda nightclub, it peaked in 1989–90 — the Spike Island gig of May 1990 as its "Woodstock for the E generation" — and dissolved by 1992 into Britpop, rave and Casuals. The term was coined by Tony Wilson of Factory Records, and the scene's visual language carries that double inheritance: post-punk's printed-matter seriousness deliberately drowned in 1960s pop psychedelia, football-casual sportswear blown up to baggy proportions, and a hedonistic, locally proud, working-class confidence. Everything below describes rules, not recipes: the style survives radical changes of subject as long as its colour logic, hand-made print feel and baggy body language hold.

## Visual Identity

### Colour palette
The infobox rule is literal: **all colours, vibrant and saturated**. In practice this resolves into four acceptable colour modes:
1. **Rainbow maximalism** — three or more high-chroma hues clashing in one field: lemon/acid yellow (`#FFDE00`–`#F5E642`), tangerine (`#F26522`), hot magenta (`#E6007E`), turquoise (`#00A9A5`), lime (`#7AC943`), violet (`#7D4F9E`), sky blue (`#29ABE2`). Black keylines or outlines hold the chaos together.
2. **Saturated duotone wash** over photography — purple-blue (`#6C5B9E`/`#5A67C4`), magenta, or yellow-green tints swallowing a whole image, skyline and faces alike.
3. **Painterly abstraction** — thick brush strokes of teal and gold, canvas texture, as on single sleeves of the era.
4. **Graphic limited collage** — torn red/white/black paper fields, a deliberate counterpoint within the same printed matter.
Grounding neutrals are brick red (`#A63A2A`), off-white paper (`#F2EDE4`) and outline black (`#111111`). Absent by rule: earth tones, beige minimalism, matte desaturated grading, and neon-on-black digital rave palettes. Pastel appears only as painted skin inside an otherwise high-chroma field, never as an overall grade.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the source documents them)
- **Baggy silhouette**: oversized T-shirts, loose jumpers, wide-legged jeans, vintage flared trousers; deliberately unisex, built for dancing.
- **Bucket hat** — the scene's single most cited garment.
- **Casual sportswear**: Adidas and Reebok trainers, outdoor jackets, borrowed from football casuals but worn loose.
- **Graphic T-shirts** with Manchester slogans or psychedelic artwork.
- **Psychedelia and 1960s iconography**: sunburst rays, swirls, florals, Summer of Love references, pop-art painted faces.
- **Raving and nightclubs**: The Haçienda, warehouse parties, dancers mid-move, crowds.
- **Manchester civic pride**: skylines, slogan plaques, "God created Manchester"-style wall signage, local place-names in type.
- **Hairstyles**: centre-parted "curtains", longer mod-influenced cuts.
- **The band group shot** as default portrait: five or six lads in a horizontal line, arms slung over shoulders, slouching.

### Typography, materials, textures
The surviving posters, sleeves and magazine covers agree on a type voice consistent with the text's psychedelia: **hand-painted bubble and curvy letterforms**, each letter a different saturated colour, wrapped in a black keyline; cut-out collage letters; warped, melting baselines. Against this, the Factory Records lineage supplies a spare counterpoint — small serif type on a black band, catalogue-number footers on sleeve bottoms. Materials are hand-made and printed: painted boards and hand-lettered signs, paper collage and candy/object piles, screenprint grain and newsprint halftone, mosaic tile plaques on brick walls, raw brick arches and club exteriors, painterly canvas strokes, and 35mm film grain with direct-flash candour.

### Composition and lighting tendencies
Type-hero posters (logotype dominating the upper field, photo strip or skyline below); all-over collage fields with no single focal point; horizontal group line-ups filling the frame width; a single figure with a prop against a flat colour wash; architectural snapshots of brick, shutters and plaques; multi-panel grids mixing painted portrait, object collage, logo and pop-art face. Lighting is flat even studio light, overcast daylight snapshot, direct flash at night, or a coloured gel/wash over everything. Nothing is cinematic, glossy or lens-flared; the image should feel like it was shot on film by a mate or screenprinted onto a wall.

## Mood & Values
Euphoric hedonism and communal dance energy; cheeky, confident Northern pride; youth rebellion expressed as pleasure rather than anger; DIY working-class resourcefulness (warehouse parties, hand-painted signs, second-hand flares). Posture is languid — slouched, leaning, mid-sway — never tense or glam-posed. Irony and play run through it: candy letters, painted faces, joke civic plaques. The MDMA-shaped euphoria of the "Second Summer of Love" shows up as colour saturation and movement, not as literal paraphernalia.

## Era & Origins
Manchester, England, 1987–1992, peak 1989–90. Grew out of the early-80s post-punk city (Joy Division/New Order, The Smiths, Factory Records, The Haçienda opening 1982) and deliberately broke with its monochrome seriousness by adding acid house, colour and dance. Declined as key bands stalled and The Haçienda faltered (temporary closure 1991, permanent 1997); absorbed mid-90s by Britpop, rave/techno and Casuals lad culture.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Colour first: saturated and plural — rainbow clash or one saturated wash; black keyline to organise chaos.
2. Hand-made over machine: painted letters, cut paper, screenprint misregistration, film grain.
3. Baggy body: loose silhouette, slouched or dancing posture; never tailored, never glam.
4. Put the North in the frame: brick, skyline, local slogan, civic plaque as set dressing.
5. Sixties echo through an eighties lens: pop-art portraits, sunbursts, florals — but with trainers, bucket hats and photocopied zine energy.
6. Group over hero: ensembles, crowds, arms over shoulders; even solo portraits feel like a snapshot of a mate.
7. Design as print artefact: poster, sleeve, magazine cover, wall plaque — something stuck to a surface, not a clean digital canvas.

**Variation axes (what may freely change)**
- *Subject*: bands, dancers, crowds, club interiors/exteriors, streets and skylines, painted portraits, letter/object collages, slogan signage, sleeve or poster layouts, casual street style.
- *Composition*: type-hero, all-over collage, group line-up, single figure on a wash, architectural detail, panel grid.
- *Lighting*: flat studio, overcast daylight, flash night, gel wash, duotone tint.
- *Colour handling*: full rainbow, duotone wash, painterly teal-and-gold abstraction, red/white/black torn collage; rare monochrome only as a deliberate quote of the pre-scene post-punk city.
- *Mood*: euphoric, cheeky, languid, earnestly civic.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A still life of painted capsules, a brick arch with a hand-painted slogan, or an invented band on a invented sleeve are all fully Madchester if the rules hold.

**Avoid**
- Skinny fits, mod tailoring, parkas with Union Jacks, grunge flannel.
- Glowsticks, LEDs, lasers, glow paint, pixel glitch, cyber-neon on black (modern rave / New Rave).
- Pure acid design: black field + acid yellow + smiley/fractal squiggles with no indie, baggy or 60s-pop anchor.
- Muted anorak miserabilism, jangle-pastel greyness (C86 indie).
- Terrace-only designer sportswear with no psychedelic colour or music context (Casuals).
- Op-art precision, monochrome-plus-one-pop, neat mod haircuts (Groovival).
- Corporate vector flatness, minimalist sans branding, matte desaturated grades, glossy digital photography.
- Folk-rustic American hippie (tie-dye on bare skin, peace signs); Madchester's 60s is pop art and pop print, not craft.
- Photorealistic drug-use scenes; graphic capsule/confetti shapes are fine, literal paraphernalia as focal subject is not.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Shared DNA | What separates Madchester |
|---|---|---|
| **Acid House** | Rave, Haçienda, MDMA euphoria | Acid House graphics are minimal and synthetic — black ground, acid yellow, smileys, fractal squiggles, no bands. Madchester always keeps an indie-band face, baggy body or 60s pop-art anchor inside the colour chaos. |
| **Casuals** | Trainers, outdoor jackets, football culture | Casuals is label-precise, sharp-fitted, terrace-bound, colour-muted. Madchester loosens the same wardrobe to baggy and dyes it rainbow. |
| **Indie** | Guitar bands, DIY press | Classic indie is understated: anoraks, pastels or grey, anti-flash. Madchester is its loud cousin — saturation, dance movement, type-hero posters. |
| **Groovival** | 1960s revival | Groovival does the 60s tidy: mod suits, op-art monochrome, precise graphics. Madchester's 60s is hand-painted, baggy, rave-soaked, grainy. |
| **New Rave** | Dance-rock fusion, bright colour | New Rave (2000s) is digital: skinny jeans, glowsticks, glitch pixels, neon on black. Madchester is filmic, hand-made, 60s-referencing, baggy. |
| **Britpop** (successor) | Baggy inheritance, Manchester lineage | Britpop swaps psychedelia for mod parkas, Union Jacks and mid-90s gloss; Madchester stays pre-1992, hand-painted and club-centred. |

Quick recognition test: saturated rainbow or saturated wash + baggy silhouette or bucket hat + hand-painted/curvy type or 60s pop-art face + Northern brick/civic anchor + film-grain or screenprint texture = Madchester. Remove the indie/baggy/60s anchor and it collapses into Acid House; remove the colour and it collapses into indie or Casuals.
