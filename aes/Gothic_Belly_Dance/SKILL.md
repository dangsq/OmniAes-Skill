# Gothic Belly Dance

Gothic Belly Dance is a subculture and performance art movement born in the United States in the 1990s, when dancers inside the Goth scene began adapting the movement vocabulary and costuming of American Tribal Style (ATS) and Tribal Fusion belly dance to gothic rock, industrial and dark alternative music. It is first and foremost a *performance costuming* language: the belly dance silhouette — bare midriff, choli or bra top, hip skirt, coin belt — is the foundation, and Goth materials, primitive ornament and dark palette are the lens through which it is rebuilt. Also called Dark Fusion Belly Dance or Tribal Goth, it grew through early-2000s online communities and festivals such as Gothla in the US and UK into an international movement with workshops, star performers and a troupe culture. Imagery in this style is therefore mostly photographic: stage documentation, promotional portraits, troupe processions and gig-poster art.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** Black owns the frame — costume and, usually, background sit in #060606–#1b1b1d. Against it, at most one saturated accent family per image, drawn from: oxblood and blood red (#4d0d17–#8a1226), aubergine and shadowed amethyst (#241a2e–#4b2a5e, with stage-gel magenta around #8e2f68 permitted only as light, not fabric), and dark earth tones (#33291d–#5b4a33). Metal and bone act as neutrals: tarnished silver #6f6a63–#9c9c9c, bone ivory #d8d0bf, dark wood #4a3524. Skin reads pale porcelain (#e6dcd0) or neutral. Backgrounds may drift to desaturated cool gray-teal (#5f7474 and darker). Full monochrome and heavily desaturated cool grades are canonical treatments, collapsing all accents into grays. Bright gold, turquoise, pastel or candy color anywhere in the costume breaks the style.

**Motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them.**
1. *Belly dance costuming foundation* — hip skirts and scarves, coin belts, choli/bra tops; the bare midriff is structural, not optional.
2. *Goth material overlay* — lace, leather and velvet replacing or supplementing traditional belly dance fabrics; lace scallops at parasol edges, chokers and skirt hems.
3. *Modern primitive ornament* — handmade jewelry of bone, wood and stone; bone teeth, claws and tusks fringing belts; wood beads; stone pendants.
4. *Body as ornament* — large tribal-style tattoos, an abundance of piercings, dreadlocks or long messy hair (including dread falls and hairpieces).
5. *Skulls* — listed as a key motif but sparsely visible in surviving imagery; treat as an occasional prop or print, not a required element.
6. *Image-supported extensions, fully permissible* — coin or chainmaille headpieces and face veils, horned or feathered headdresses, feather fans and veils, claw or spike finger extensions, studded arm cuffs and fingerless gloves, gauze hoods, lace parasols, an occasional top hat, striped sleeve accents.

**Materials and textures.** Tarnished coin metal, chains and studwork; scalloped lace; crushed velvet; grained leather corsetry and belts; fringe and gauze; fishnet; bone and horn; feather plumes. A correct silhouette stacks several of these at once — metal over lace over skin over velvet — so the figure reads armored and handmade rather than smooth or sequined.

**Recurring subjects.** Predominantly feminine-presenting dancers (the sampled record is entirely so; the text does not forbid others): a solo dancer frozen mid-movement or in a posed portrait; duos and troupes of three to four in rows or shallow wedges; bust portraits emphasizing headdress, choker and coin bra; stage performers under gels; processional groups walking street or festival ground. Evidence for male performers, props beyond costume, and non-dance contexts is thin.

**Composition and lighting tendencies.** Vertical portrait formats dominate. The figure is centered or near-centered, camera at eye level or slightly below, full-body to bust. Arms carry dance grammar even in stillness: framing the face, snake-armed extensions, one line raised overhead, hips offset. Backgrounds are plain and dark — seamless studio gloom, textured canvas, club blackness, overcast sky, foliage, candle-lit shelves, bare street concrete. Lighting ranges: single-source low-key with rim light; saturated red or magenta stage wash; diffuse overcast cool; harsh direct sun rendered as high-contrast monochrome; candle amber interiors. Grades skew desaturated, cool-shifted, or fully black-and-white; contrast medium to high. Poster variants with blackletter or antique serif typography exist but are optional.

## Mood & Values

The style expresses artistic expression, fusion, dance, primal energy and community. Its images feel ritualistic and trance-adjacent — dark cabaret, occult theater, a procession of priestesses — yet grounded in troupe solidarity and handmade authenticity. Melancholy romance (lace parasol, downcast eyes) and aggressive ritual power (horns, claws, spikes) are both valid poles of the same mood: serious, embodied, deliberately "the darker side of fusion," never playful or glossy.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in the 1990s United States from Goth club nights and themed events; consolidated in the early 2000s via online communities and festivals like Gothla (US and UK). It reacts to and merges two parent lines: the Goth subculture's materials and palette, and ATS/Tribal Fusion belly dance's silhouette and group-improvisation culture, filtered through the "modern primitive" emphasis on bone, wood, stone, piercing and tattoo.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Costume grammar first: bare midriff layered between a choli/bra top and a hip skirt or belt hung with coins, chains or metal fringe; then goth overlay of lace, velvet, leather.
2. Black-dominance rule: black fills most of the costume and usually the background; accents limited to one family of red, purple or earth plus metal/bone neutrals.
3. Tarnished metal and bone over glitter: antiqued silver coins, studs, claws and teeth — never rhinestone sparkle or bright gold sequins.
4. Treat modification as costume: visible tribal tattoo fields, piercings, dread falls or messy long hair complete the silhouette.
5. Pose is dance: even a static portrait keeps dance arms, hip offset or mid-step weight; the frame implies stage, club, ritual circle or procession.
6. Light low and moody: chiaroscuro single source, gel wash, overcast cool, candle amber, or high-contrast monochrome.
7. Stack textures: at least three of lace, fringe, gauze, leather grain, tarnished metal, bone in one figure.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo portrait, duo, troupe procession, stage performance, candle-lit interior session, overcast street, forest edge, festival lawn, poster layout. Composition: bust to full-body; frontal, profile or three-quarter; frozen motion with skirt swirl or held stillness; vertical preferred, horizontal processions acceptable. Lighting: studio low-key, red/magenta gel, diffuse overcast, harsh sun in monochrome, candle amber. Color handling: full monochrome, desaturated cool grade, black plus a single red or purple accent, rarely earth-warm interiors so long as the black costume holds. Mood: melancholic-romantic, aggressive-ritual, celebratory-stage, documentary-candid. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a dancer in a bone-fringed belt under plain daylight monochrome is as valid as a gel-lit stage portrait.

**Avoid.**
- Glittering Egyptian-cabaret bedlah: gold sequins, rhinestones, pastel chiffon, bright turquoise or silver ATS festival wear without the goth overlay.
- Pure Goth street fashion with no dance grammar: fully covered bodies, band tees, trench coats, platform boots, no coin belt or midriff.
- Steampunk hardware: brass gears, goggles, clockwork, sepia-brown dominance, Victorian tailoring coats; a lone top hat is fine, goggles are not.
- Hippie-goth softness: sun-bleached pastels, flower crowns, tie-dye, peace signs, unstructured cotton flow.
- Ethnogoth folk literalism: embroidered folk blouses, woven wool coats, traditional regional headdresses worn as static street fashion.
- Neon rave/EDM wear, UV paint, candy colors, sportswear, sneakers.
- High-key white seamless commercial lighting, clean minimalism, glossy plastic costume shine.
- Modern clutter: smartphones, logos, branding, contemporary signage as focal elements.
- Lingerie-only glamour lacking coin, chain, lace or bone layering.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared surface | Deciding tell |
|---|---|---|
| Ethnogoth | Black plus ethnic ornament, coin jewelry | Ethnogoth is static street/editorial fashion built on folk garments (embroidery, woven wool, folk headpieces), covered and layered; Gothic Belly Dance always shows the bare-midriff dance costume and a performance or processional frame |
| Goth | Palette, lace, leather, makeup | Goth proper is music-scene attire (deathrock teased hair, trenches, fishnet, platforms) with no coin belts, hip scarves or dance posture; the dance silhouette is the divider |
| Hippie Goth | Dark-boho mix, long hair | Hippie Goth is softer, lighter, slouched — flowers, patchouli cotton, peace motifs; Gothic Belly Dance is armored, metallic, disciplined, midriff-baring, posed |
| Steampunk | Corsetry, occasional top hat | Steampunk runs brass/copper, gears, goggles, sepia Victorian tailoring; Gothic Belly Dance runs tarnished silver, bone, coin metal and dance belts with zero machinery motifs |

Internal neighbor: bright Tribal Fusion/ATS in turquoise, silver and white without the goth overlay is the parent dance style, not this one. The fastest recognition test overall: bare midriff + coin/chain/bone belt + black lace or velvet + dance arms + dark or monochrome grade = Gothic Belly Dance.
