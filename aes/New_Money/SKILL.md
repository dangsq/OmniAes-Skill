# New Money

New Money — also nouveau riche, the new rich — is not a period style but a performance: the visual language of wealth that arrived recently and intends to be seen. Where Old Money conceals, New Money publishes. The aesthetic is defined by intentional, often maximalist status display — expensive brand logos worn visibly, current luxury trends, highly visible consumption — rather than by any fixed silhouette or decade. Its surface is fluid and trend-driven, shifting with locale (a bohemian tilt in parts of California, a chic-modernist tilt in Manhattan) and with season, but its grammar is constant: wealth must be legible at a single glance, in metal, in logo, in vehicle, in stone.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Two registers share one rule: **metal or logo must appear somewhere in frame; a fully matte, unbranded palette exits the style.**

- **Loud register (Gold / Jet Set):** metallic gold from dark goldenrod to champagne highlight (roughly #B8860B → #D4AF37 → #F1D98E), silver and chrome (#C0C0C0 → #E8E8E8), bronze and copper (#B87333 → #CD7F32), rose gold (#B76E79); anchored by deep black (#0B0B0B → #202020) and a cream-to-ivory field (#F7F3EA → #FFFDF8); punctured by one loud accent — lipstick red (#B01030 → #D22630) or saffron packaging orange (#E4702A).
- **Quiet register (Minimalist branch):** gray, black, white and tan (#C9B79C → #E3D5C0), with primary color used only as accent, especially red. More masculine, androgynous, simplistic graphics.
- **Airy register (Bourgeois Bohemian branch):** white, natural blonde wood, pale blue (#BFD6DE), light tan, cream, with gold and rose gold as the tell that this is purchased wellness, not rustic simplicity.
- **Logo-bearing neutral:** monogram coated-canvas brown (#4A2F1B → #6B4423) with light leather trim functions across all registers as a readable neutral.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Visible brand names, logos and monograms** — the style's first signal; followed closely by **gold and silver** metal, including gold-plated objects that have no business being gold (toilets, fixtures) and gold leaf on food.
2. **Luxury vehicles and travel infrastructure:** luxury cars (often customized), private jets, yachts; **designer handbags and luxury shopping bags**, usually in multiples.
3. **Huge diamond rings and flashy jewelry;** fur coats; sequins and metallic fabrics.
4. **Marble floors, huge houses, mansions, luxury apartments;** grand staircases and monumental interiors.
5. **Loud patterns** — zebra stripe, leopard print; cash stacks; expensive champagne.
6. **Fashion cuts:** dresses with plunging necklines and high slit skirts, skimpy bikinis, men's shirts worn open, designer sneakers and athleisure, extremely high heels.
7. **Grooming:** long coffin nails, heavy contouring, dark fake tan, brightly colored or metallic lipstick, eyeshadow and nail polish, fake eyelashes; in the Minimalist branch, bold red lip and darkened brows instead.
8. **Variant décor:** Mid-Century Modern furniture, Cubist paintings and abstract sculptures, black-and-white photographs, neutrally colored walls (Minimalist); Feng Shui or Scandinavian layout, succulents, organic cotton, linen, silk, kaftans, espadrilles (Bourgeois Bohemian).

### Materials and textures

Long-guard-hair fur pile against sheared cream pelt; sequin scatter and metallic pleats that catch light point-by-point; quilted lambskin; coated monogram canvas with vegetable-tan trim; polished chrome, gilt ironwork balustrades, mirrored sunglass lenses; veined marble underfoot; lacquered car paint; satin linings; gold leaf flaking on food; the glass-and-metal sheen of cutting-edge technology.

### Recurring subjects

The newly wealthy as self-presenters — athletes, musicians, entrepreneurs, reality-television families — posed coolly behind oversized or mirrored sunglasses, or direct and promotional as a campaign cast. Their assets act as co-protagonists: the car, the jet, the staircase, the pile of boxes. Attendants and staff handling luggage or holding doors appear as ownership cues. The body is a billboard: revealing, trend-cut, accessorized at oversized scale.

### Composition and lighting

Advertising grammar throughout. Recurring structures: accessory-forward cropping with a handbag entering the frame corner at monumental scale; overhead asset-maps where jet stairs meet a waiting car on patterned tarmac; product-pile still lifes of bags, rigid boxes and luggage stacked around an open trunk; full-length figures staged on sweeping staircases or panoramic terraces so architecture dwarfs and validates them; staged group portraits in neutral paneled salons, bodies arranged like a fashion campaign. Negative space is cream interior, open sky or tarmac — never cluttered domesticity. Lighting is commercial gloss: high-key diffuse daylight that blows white paint, white fur and white fuselage toward highlight; warm tungsten glow on gilt, sequins and cut roses indoors; soft window light through sheer curtains for portraits. Specular highlights on every hard surface. No grain, no grit, no overcast melancholy.

## Mood & Values

The stated values are looking rich, consumerism, capitalism, materialism — and the mood delivers them: ambition, thrill-seeking, the celebration of financial ascendancy as public proof of social mobility. Loudness and spending sprees are features, not bugs; the style accepts being looked down upon by Old Money and Preppy observers as attention-seeking, and accepts the suspicion that the money arrived quickly, perhaps illegally. The emotional range runs from triumphant (chin up, hidden behind mirrors) to playful (bags piled like trophies) to cool-detached (the minimalist office) to gaudy-delighted (gold leaf, gold plating). Every image is self-aware: it knows it will be posted.

## Era & Origins

The archetype of the newly wealthy distinct from the established wealthy reaches back to Ancient Greece, and the aesthetic surfaces hardest in times of economic change — booms that mint newcomers, downturns that make spending conspicuous. Its modern visual vocabulary is 2010s social-media-native, codified by the Rich Kids of Instagram trend (2016–2017), layered on decades of televised gloss: Dynasty (1981 and 2017), Dallas, the Real Housewives franchises, and films such as The Wolf of Wall Street (2013), Crazy Rich Asians (2018) and Iron Man (2008). It exists in reaction to Old Money and Preppy restraint: inherited style versus earned-and-displayed style. Because it is trend-driven, its true era is always the current season.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. *Legibility:* wealth must read in one glance — a logo, a metal, a vehicle, a stone.
2. *Accumulation:* status objects appear in multiples and piles, never as a single discreet item.
3. *Currency:* products are current-season and cutting-edge, never heirloom or vintage.
4. *Gloss:* surfaces reflect; lighting is commercial and clean.
5. *Body as billboard:* revealing trend-cut fashion, oversized accessories, groomed tan and nails per branch.
6. *Scale:* accessories, fur volume and architecture oversize the human figure.
7. *Accent discipline:* metallic plus neutral field plus one loud accent (red, saffron) — or full loud gold; choose a register and hold it.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be any of: a fur-clad portrait in a car or on a terrace; a vehicle or jet as protagonist; a tarmac or driveway asset-map; a still life of bags and rigid boxes; a gown on a monumental staircase; a staged group portrait in a salon; a gold-leaf table; a yacht deck; a minimalist office with red accent; an airy wellness retreat with rose-gold touches. Composition may range from tight accessory-forward crops to overhead maps to full-length architectural staging. Lighting may be high-key daylight, warm interior glow, soft window light or flash-gloss night. Color handling may be full loud gold, black-and-cream with red, or the airy boho palette. Mood may be triumphant, playful, cool or gaudy. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Heirloom codes: patina, antiques, faded crests, tweed, a single pearl strand, equestrian trophies, vintage cars as props.
- Quiet luxury: unbranded tonal cashmere with no metal, logo or red accent anywhere.
- Handmade and earthy: macramé, rattan, terracotta, thrifted layers, visible mending, craft textures.
- Grit and modesty: film grain, desaturation, documentary candidness, public transit, fast food, clutter of poverty.
- Wrong-decade retro: 1970s satin lounge (Playboy), 1980s pastel shirts and suspenders (Yuppie).
- Matte, flat, overcast lighting; melancholy or irony as the dominant tone.
- Wilderness nature; New Money outdoors is manicured hedges, resort pools, Monaco harbor, tarmac.
- A single modest object as the only wealth cue.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared surface | New Money tell |
|---|---|---|
| Boujee | Gold, diamonds, champagne, logos | New Money scales up to whole assets — jets, tarmac, mansions, marble staircases — adds advertising polish and trend-cut fashion, and includes Minimalist and BoBo branches Boujee lacks |
| Playboy | Sexualized leisure, mansions | New Money centers wealth display itself: legible logos, monogram canvas, metal; Playboy swaps these for satin loungewear, bunnies, retro 70s gloss |
| Power Dressing | Suits, authority posture | Power Dressing implies wealth through tailoring alone; New Money shows the money — cars, bags, jets — and opens the shirt collar beside gowns and assets |
| Yuppie | 1980s careerism, nice cars | Yuppie is preppy-derived, tasteful, briefcase-and-suspender aspirational; New Money is louder, logo-forward, social-media-native, and includes athlete, musician and influencer wealth |
| Bohemian | The BoBo branch's linen and pale palette | True Bohemian rejects materialism: no logos, no metal shine, no accumulation; New Money's boho variant keeps gold and rose gold, designer provenance, wellness as purchase |

## Record and gaps

The surviving photographic record concentrates on the Gold/Jet-Set branch — fur, monogram canvas, jets and tarmac, metallic gowns on grand staircases, cream paneled salons — with one neutral-salon group portrait bridging toward the Minimalist register. The Minimalist and Bourgeois Bohemian branches rest chiefly on textual description; when generating them, lean on their documented palettes and object lists (Mid-Century Modern furniture, Cubist sculpture, black-and-white photographs; succulents, blonde wood, kaftans, linen) rather than on imagined imagery, and keep one metallic or red accent so the branch still reads as New Money.
