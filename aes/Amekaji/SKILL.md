# Amekaji (American Casual / Cocogyaru)

Amekaji is the Japanese gyaru substyle that worships, and deliberately overdraws, American casual streetwear: surf, skate and hip-hop dress filtered through Shibuya's late-1990s–2000s girl culture. Where the original 1960s Japanese "amekaji" meant muted men's vintage, preppy and workwear, the gyaru version inverts it into something loud, synthetic and cartoonish — an imagined California of brand logos, star prints and candy fleece. It was commercialized by brands like COCOLULU, ALBA ROSA, ROXY and Billabong and disseminated through gyaru magazines (*egg*, *Ranzuki*), so its native media are the glossy magazine scan page and the candid shop-or-street snapshot. Its values are playfulness, energy, brand loyalty, American pop culture and casual comfort; its silhouette is baggy, layered and soft, its grooming unmistakably gyaru (tanned skin, heavy eye makeup, bleached hair).

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is **saturated candy color-blocking anchored by earthy Americana bases**. Two to four high-chroma hues appear simultaneously in one outfit or layout — never a single accent on neutrals — while denim indigo, khaki, tan and white hold the ground underneath.

| Role | Hues | Hex range |
|---|---|---|
| Candy primaries | hot pink, canary yellow, turquoise, orange | #FF4F9A–#FF9EC4, #FFD918–#FFE867, #17C3B2–#4DE3D2, #FF7A1A–#FFA64D |
| Candy secondaries | royal blue, pop red, grape purple | #2456E0–#4D79FF, #E62E3E, #8A4FD3 |
| Earthy anchors | denim indigo, khaki, tan, beige, white | #27408B–#6E85B7, #B98D4F–#EAD9C0, #FFFFFF |
| Print graphics | flag red/blue stars, confetti stars, zigzag chevrons, polka dots, gingham checks | drawn from the candy primaries on white or yellow fields |

Desaturation, pastel washes and monochrome outfits are outside the palette; if a hue appears, it appears loud.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Oversized hoodies and sweatshirts** — the core silhouette, sleeves swallowing hands.
2. **Prominent brand logos and bold graphic tees** — screen-printed logo boxes, rhinestone or glitter lettering on caps and chests, vertical brand lettering running down a pant leg; logos are worn as decoration, not hidden.
3. **Puffer vests** — quilted nylon layered over hoodies or tees.
4. **Baggy bottoms**: flared pants, banner pants, baggy jeans, cargo pants, jorts, overalls.
5. **Mini skirts** worn as part of layering (over pants, leggings or socks), not as glamour wear.
6. **Layering itself** — tee over long-sleeve, vest over hoodie, skirt over pants, socks over socks.
7. **Surf/skate culture signage** — beach-brand names, skate graphics, sporty numbers and varsity letters.
8. **Headwear**: baseball caps (often logo-rhinestoned), beanies.
9. **Chunky plastic jewelry**: brightly colored bead necklaces, bangles, colorful belts.
10. **Gyaru grooming**: tanned skin, dramatic eye makeup (liner, false lashes), bleached honey-blonde to light-brown hair, long straight with blunt bangs or swept into buns/pigtails.
11. **Footwear**: Ugg-style shearling boots, chunky sneakers.
12. **Americana star graphics** — star fields, star bursts and chevron zigzags as prints and as backdrop patterns; the photographic record extends the text's "American pop culture" value into a literal stars-and-stripes confetti vocabulary.

### Materials and textures

Matte cotton fleece and jersey knit; glossy quilted nylon on vests; loose denim twill, sometimes distressed; flannel; terry sweatcloth; suede-and-shearling boots; glossy plastic beads and acrylic bangles; rhinestone and glitter print catching flash; straightened, glassy hair. Expect soft matte cloth against small hard sparkles — fleece bulk plus plastic shine.

### Recurring subjects

Young women in gyaru presentation, most often in pairs or friend groups wearing coordinated ("twin") outfits; solo full-body shop shots also common. Poses are goofy and energetic: peace/V-signs, one-leg kicks, leaning on a friend, pointing at a logo, crouched group huddles, open-mouth laughter. Settings: brand-shop interiors with colored pillars and clothing racks, Shibuya street corners with signage, studio sets with graphic star/chevron backdrops.

### Composition and lighting

Two native frames. **Magazine scan page**: full-body or three-quarter figures arranged asymmetrically around vertical Japanese typography, speech-balloon bursts, star confetti and price tags; saturated print ink with slight dot gain. **Snapshot**: centered waist-up or full-body framing, on-camera flash or flat fluorescent shop light, high-key and slightly overexposed, hard flash shadow behind the subject, busy cluttered background accepted and even prized. Composition is never sparse or cinematic; negative space gets filled with type, stars or friends.

## Mood & Values

Loud optimism and performed childishness: playfulness, energy, casual comfort, and brand loyalty as identity. Amekaji treats American pop culture as a toy box — stars, logos and varsity letters worn ironically-cute rather than cool. Community matters: matching coords, group shots, magazine crowds. Nothing about the style is restrained, ironic-minimal or mature.

## Era & Origins

The term "amekaji" dates to 1960s Japan for men's American vintage/preppy/workwear; the gyaru substyle of that name emerged in Shibuya, Tokyo, popular from the late 1990s through the 2000s (sources place its cocogyaru wave cresting mid-to-late 2000s; treat roughly 1998–2009 as the safe period window). It reacts against both the muted original amekaji and against glamour gyaru by choosing sportswear over dresses, and it rides the same Y2K/McBling/Swag energy as its Western cousins while remaining distinctly Japanese in grooming and magazine culture. Recently revived by non-Japanese gyaru enthusiasts.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Build the outfit as layers: graphic tee or sweat base + oversized hoodie or puffer vest + baggy bottom (flared/banner pants, baggy jeans, cargo, jorts) or mini skirt over socks/leggings.
- Color-block at least two candy hues plus one earthy anchor (denim, khaki, white); keep chroma high everywhere.
- Make branding visible and decorative: logo boxes, vertical pant-leg lettering, rhinestone cap text, varsity numbers, surf-brand names.
- Apply gyaru grooming: tan, heavy eye makeup, bleached light hair with blunt bangs or buns.
- Pose playfully (peace signs, kicks, group leans); never cool-detached.
- Choose a native medium: flash snapshot in a shop or on a signed street, or a magazine scan layout with vertical type, bursts and star confetti.
- Fill the frame; clutter and type are period-correct.

**Variation axes (what may change)**
- *Subject*: solo, pair or group; any model reading as gyaru-styled; settings from brand shop, fitting room, street corner, studio star backdrop, to skate-park or beach-adjacent props.
- *Composition*: full-body catalog stance, waist-up snap, crowded group huddle, or collage magazine page.
- *Lighting*: on-camera flash, fluorescent shop light, flat daylight, bright studio — always high-key and flat, never moody.
- *Color handling*: any combination inside the candy range; pink-yellow-turquoise-orange are typical but red/blue/purple-led coords are valid; anchors may be indigo, khaki or white.
- *Mood*: from silly-grin energy to posed confident cute.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A beachless interior shot, a group of three, or a invented-brand coord all read as amekaji if the layering, palette, logos and grooming rules hold.

**Avoid**
- Muted, minimalist, monochrome or normcore palettes; beige-only outfits; matte modern grading.
- Glamour gyaru signals: cocktail dresses, heels, fur stoles, evening nails-as-focus, himegyaru frills.
- School-uniform kod: sailor collars, blazers, plaid skirts with loose socks and loafers (that is kogal).
- Dark hip-hop / B-Boy streetwear: all-black tracksuits, bandanas, grit, sneakerhead monochrome.
- Contemporary streetwear: slim joggers, techwear, modern luxury logomania, post-2010 sneaker silhouettes.
- Cinematic lighting, teal-orange grading, soft gradient studio beauty light, sparse negative space.
- Anachronisms: smartphones, LED lighting, current-day signage.
- Pastel cassette-fade desaturation (that reads Heisei Retro, not amekaji).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Amekaji counter-tell |
|---|---|---|
| B-Boy | Dark palette (black, white, camo), sportswear tracksuits, bandanas, masculine stance | Candy color-blocking, feminine gyaru grooming, cute brand logos, fleece bulk |
| Gyaru (parent) | Dresses, heels, fur, glamour hair-and-nails focus | Sporty American casual wardrobe: hoodies, vests, baggy pants, Uggs; glamour replaced by tomboy-cute layering |
| Heisei Retro | Broad Heisei nostalgia: pastel decals, character goods, cassette-fade color, any wearer | Specifically gyaru grooming plus surf/skate brand logos; saturated candy over denim, not softened pastel |
| Hip-Hop (US) | Baggy but grounded in black/white/gold, team jerseys, luxury logos, street grit | Japanese feminine exaggeration: polka dots, stars, chunky plastic jewelry, bleached bangs, tan |
| Kogal | Sailor uniform or blazer, plaid skirt, loose socks, loafers, tartan-beige palette | No uniform code; caps, beanies, banner pants, puffer vests, surf-brand signage instead |

Quick check: if you see tanned gyaru makeup plus a baggy logo-laden American-sportswear coord in candy colors, flat bright light, and a playful pose — it is amekaji; swap any one of those three pillars out and you have drifted into a neighbor.
