# Crowcore

Crowcore, also tagged Corvidcore, is a Tumblr aesthetic of the corvid mind: intelligent, free, simple in its wants, and weak for shiny things. It descends from Goblincore and Cottagecore but sits a shade darker than both, swapping pastoral warmth for black plumage, wet stone, and the hoard of a bird that raids both creek beds and city sidewalks. Its subject matter is collecting itself — small, lost, broken, thrown-away, conventionally trivial objects rescued and kept — alongside environmentalism, birdwatching, and the quiet activities of a studious scavenger: sewing, reading, charcoal drawing, wading creeks for frogs and salamanders, organizing treasures into pretty containers. The mood is mischievous rather than macabre: a crow holding a knife in its beak is a joke, not a threat.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The style runs on a triad of **black, white, and green**, held at natural, desaturated values, with metallics and gem-flash as accents rather than fields of color.

- **Black** (crow plumage, wool cloaks, ink, boot leather): `#0A0A0C` to soft charcoal `#26262A`.
- **White** as bone, pearl, frosted glass, and paper (not optic white): `#F4F1E8` down to `#E6E1D6`.
- **Green** as leaf and moss, never lime: `#4C5F45` to `#7E8F6C`, with sage-grey backdrops acceptable.
- **Supporting earth**: bark brown `#5C4632`, corduroy tan `#9A7B5F`, rust `#8A4B2F`.
- **Metals**, always slightly tarnished: dull brass `#A9853F`, old silver `#B9BAC0`, copper `#A9663C`.
- **Gem sparks**, used in tiny doses inside a hoard: cobalt `#23408F`, turquoise `#3F8F8A`, amethyst `#6C5679`, rose garnet `#B05068`.

Rule of thumb: saturation stays low across the frame; the only bright notes are small hard highlights — a rhinestone, a pearl, a blade edge, an opalescent stone — catching diffuse light.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Crows and corvid presence** — live birds perched, foraging on cobblestones, or gathered in foliage; crow illustrations and prints; any object bearing a crow image. The bird is the style's namesake and mascot.
2. **The shiny hoard** — old coins, vintage brooches and lockets, tiny glittery pebbles, seaglass, agates, shown as piled flat-lays or sorted into pretty containers. Mixed eras, mismatched pieces, and tarnish are the point.
3. **Feathers** — loose, matte, black or grey, among stones and metal.
4. **Urban-foraged trash-treasure** — rusty bottle caps, button jars, bent findings: the city equivalent of the creek find.
5. **Bones and small animal skulls**, kept respectfully as natural-history objects (the style borrows this from Vulture Culture).
6. **Shiny sharp things** — swords, knives, daggers; ornate silver blades on wet stone, or a crow cheekily gripping one, emblematic of the style's dry humor.
7. **Tincture bottles and tiny sculptures** of birds or forest friends.
8. **Dark layered fashion** — long black cloaks and coats, corduroy overalls, torn hems over thigh-high or chunky combat boots, coin-and-brooch belts, white collars peeking from black knitwear. Darker than Cottagecore, but with light, mysterious, playful aspects: a dusty-rose sweater or a frayed white skirt can carry the look.
9. **Text graphics** — deadpan meme captions in plain white boxes over bird photos, and woodcut/linocut-style prints with corvid one-liners and bordered ornament.

### Materials and textures

Tarnished filigree and milgrain metal; pearl and rhinestone facet; frosted seaglass; banded agate and opalescent stone; matte feather barb; pitted rust; corduroy wale; wool crepe and heavy cotton; linen or fleece cloth as a flat-lay ground; weathered wood; wet stone and moss; paper grain in prints and meme blocks. Everything reads handled, kept, and found — never new-in-packaging.

### Recurring subjects and scenes

Collection flat-lays filling the frame; crows on branches, lupines, or pavement; full-body outdoor fashion figures on rocks, wet decking, or at the urban edge; creek and woodland exploring; reading, studying, charcoal drawing; feeding birds; thrifting hauls and sewing tables; poster-like graphics centered on a crow portrait.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends casual and documentary: overhead scatters that spill edge-to-edge, loosely grouped sorts on a plate or cloth, full-body or cropped fashion portraits with generous negative space of fog or foliage, eye-level bird snapshots, and stacked-text graphic layouts. Lighting is soft and natural — overcast daylight, misty forest backlight with visible haze, or flat even indoor light for tabletop hoards. Monochrome treatment is acceptable. Phone-camera honesty, slight grain, and imperfect framing belong; hard studio strobe and golden-hour glow do not.

## Mood & Values

Simplicity, intelligence, freedom, and shiny things. Crowcore celebrates the kept rock in the pocket: the conviction that small overlooked objects deserve keeping, that birds deserve watching and feeding, that streets deserve hopping down for no reason. Its humor is dry and self-possessed — entrails read for opinions, screams of greeting for friends — and its darkness is plumage-deep, not horror-deep. Mysterious and playful at once, it values environmentalism, study, craft (sewing, dressmaking, drawing), and time with friends, online and off.

## Era & Origins

Origin decade undocumented; the style was created on Tumblr within the platform's core-suffix taxonomy, circulating in the same late-2010s/early-2020s wave as its relatives. It derives primarily from Goblincore and Cottagecore and positions itself darker than either; it is sometimes adopted as an alternative to Goblincore, sharing that tag's love of collected trifles and nature visuals while centering corvids and shine. Heavy cross-tagging with Gremlincore, Cottagecore, Cottagegore, Cryptidcore, and Naturecore is characteristic. It reacts against Cottagecore's sunlit pastoralism by admitting the city — cobblestones, pigeons, rusty caps — and against polish generally, in favor of the found and the kept.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build the frame on the black/white/green triad at low saturation; let metals and one or two gem flashes supply all the brightness.
2. Include a corvid presence, a hoard of shiny found objects, or both. The hoard must read as found: mixed eras, tarnish, mismatch, rust beside pearl.
3. Keep light diffuse and natural — overcast sky, misty backlight, or flat tabletop light. No strobe, no sunset warmth.
4. Privilege texture over finish: tarnish, frost, fray, pitting, wet stone, matte feather.
5. Allow dry wit: deadpan caption boxes, a bird with a blade, a linocut insult. Humor is corvid-smart, never cute.
6. Dress figures in layered, dark, thrifted silhouettes — long loose black, corduroy, chunky boots, coin or brooch belts — with room for one light, mysterious, playful note.
7. Shoot it like a keeper documenting their treasures: casual framing, collections filling the frame, honest grain.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: wild or urban birds; hoard flat-lays or container-sorted collections; fashion figures; activities (creek wading, reading, sewing, charcoal drawing, bird feeding, thrifting); meme or print graphics.
- *Composition*: overhead scatter, organized grid in containers, full-body portrait, cropped garment or object detail, bordered poster layout.
- *Lighting*: overcast, misty backlight, flat indoor, or monochrome treatment.
- *Color handling*: full muted color, black-dominant, green-dominant, or black-and-white; gem sparks optional but small.
- *Mood*: playful meme, mysterious gothic-soft, or naturalist-documentary.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A misty creek bank with a tincture bottle and sorted agates, or a text poster over a pigeon photo, is as valid as a crow portrait or a brooch pile.

**Avoid**

- Neon or high-saturation synthetic color; pastel kawaii styling.
- Glossy studio product photography, jewelry-advertisement lighting, plastic new objects, visible branding.
- Gore, blood, or occult ritual staging — bones and skulls yes, horror no.
- Pirate narrative costuming: ships, tricorns, nautical stripes, eye patches, treasure-chest staging.
- Dragons, scales, reptilian fantasy creatures or plushies (that is Dragoncore's hoard, not a corvid's).
- Cottagecore's butter-cream pastoral glow, baking tables, and flower-subject prettiness.
- Mud-first grotesquerie (snails, dirt-smeared ugliness as the subject) without the corvid-and-shine focus.
- Minimalist modern interiors, hyper-polished fantasy digital art, clean maximalist glamour.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | How Crowcore differs |
|---|---|
| **Dragoncore** | Dragoncore hoards like a fantasy reptile: scales, plush dragons, jewel-toned fantasy art. Crowcore's hoard is real and found — coins, bottle caps, seaglass — and live birds, not fantasy creatures, anchor it; palette stays black/white/green with muted metal. |
| **Goblincore** | Goblincore leans mud-brown, mossy, and deliberately ugly (snails, frogs, dirt). Crowcore is darker in value (true blacks), insists on shine and metallic glint, and always keeps a corvid in or near the frame; its humor is dry rather than gremlin-sloppy. |
| **Pirate** | Pirate is a costumed seafaring narrative with ships, hats, and stripes. Crowcore shares coins, brooches, and daggers but treats them as foraged keepsakes on cloth or wood, set among birds, creeks, and city pavement — no nautical story. |
| **Vulture Culture** | Vulture Culture is osteology-first: bones, skulls, taxidermy, often clinical and monochrome. Crowcore includes bones only as one motif among many, alongside live birds, fashion, memes, and shiny non-skeletal treasure. |
| **Cottagecore** | Cottagecore is warm, cream-and-butter pastoral: baking, gardens, soft florals. Crowcore is its darker cousin — black wool, urban foraging, rusty caps, corvid sarcasm — and never bathes in golden-hour cream light. |

When in doubt, apply the litmus: black plumage or black cloth, a kept shiny object of no market value, and a tone of intelligent mischief. If all three hold, the image is Crowcore.
