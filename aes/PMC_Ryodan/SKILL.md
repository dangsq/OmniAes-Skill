# PMC Ryodan (ЧВК Рёдан)

PMC Ryodan is the visual language of a loosely organized subculture of teenage anime fans in Russia and Ukraine that surfaced into public view in early 2023. Its name is a double borrow: "Ryodan" from the Genei Ryodan (Phantom Troupe) thief gang of the manga *Hunter x Hunter*, and "PMC" as an ironic nod to the Wagner Group private military contractor — a joke of faux-militarism worn by kids who carry nothing more warlike than a crossbody bag. The aesthetic is a uniform rather than a mood board: a black hoodie with a white spider emblem, checkered trousers, long dyed-black hair falling over the face, and a crew of same-dressed friends around you. It lives in the mundane infrastructure of the post-Soviet city — shopping-mall atriums, paved park alleys, panel-block courtyards, grey waterfronts — and in the flat black-and-white meme graphics of VK and Telegram channels. Media panic framed it as a "fight club"; visually it is closer to a melancholic, deadpan team jersey for anime kids who got detained for wearing it.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The style is functionally monochrome. Black is the field, white is the signal, grey check is the only permitted busy surface.

- Garment black: `#0b0b0b`–`#191919` (hoodies, oversized tees, cropped trousers, bags).
- Emblem and print white: `#ececec`–`#ffffff` (spider logo, number 4, line-art graphics).
- Checkered grey: `#3f3f3f`–`#7d7d7d` woven with off-white grid lines around `#d9d9d9` (plaid trousers, flannel weave).
- Environment: concrete and asphalt `#8d9296`–`#b4b8ba`, overcast sky `#c2c7ca`, drained park green `#6d7a68`, pale skin `#d9d2cb`.
- Tolerated minor accents, never dominant: oxblood or dark maroon knit (`#5e222b`), charcoal navy (`#232733`), a black-and-white striped sweater.
- Reserved antagonist color: saturated tracksuit blue (`#2b3f8c`) with white stripes belongs only to rival gopnik/offnik figures in confrontation narratives, never to Ryodan-coded subjects.

Rule of thumb: roughly nine tenths of any frame should read as black, white or desaturated grey. If a color can be named at a glance and it is not grey, it is probably breaking the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them

1. **Black hoodie with the white twelve-legged spider and the number 4** — the membership marker, derived from the Phantom Troupe emblem and the Hikikomori Kai clothing brand (est. 2020). Typically printed large on the back, sometimes on the chest; screen-print white on black fleece. Variant prints (larger artistic spiders, skeleton-like or red-inked editions) exist, but the legible default is white spider + "4".
2. **Checkered / plaid trousers** — grey-black check, straight or slightly baggy, worn with chunky sneakers; the second half of the uniform.
3. **Long, dyed-black hair covering the face** — curtain bob or shag falling fully over the eyes; facelessness is both mask and meme.
4. **Anime imagery and *Hunter x Hunter* lineage** — the spider itself is the anime citation; further anime references appear in meme graphics rather than as extra costume elements.
5. **Online-gaming and platform references (DotA 2, Telegram, VK)** — contextual rather than costume-level; shows up as channel watermarks, meme formats and shared lore, overlapping with the Dead Inside scene.
6. **The crew in mundane public space** — groups of teenagers gathered at malls, concerts, park paths and waterfronts; the gathering itself is a motif, as is the back-view lineup that displays the emblems.
7. **The Aviapark "battle map"** — schematic mall floor plans circulated as ironic "battle" maps; a rare graphic artifact of the meme layer.
8. **The Adidas-tracksuit rival** — three-stripe blue or black tracksuits signify offniki/gopniki, the opposing side; they appear only as antagonists in confrontation or illustration narratives.

### Materials and textures

Cotton fleece hoodie with a slightly cracked white screen print; soft oversized jersey tees; flannel or brushed suiting weave in the checkered trousers; matte nylon crossbody straps and small black bags; chunky rubber-soled runner sneakers; occasional coarse knit in striped or maroon layers. Photographic texture is smartphone-native: mild JPEG softness, slight motion blur, flat contrast, cool desaturated grade. Graphic texture is vector-flat: pure black ground, clean white line art, handwritten Cyrillic caption blocks.

### Recurring subjects and figure grammar

Subjects are teenagers, roughly 14–16 in the culture's core, alone or in crews of two to five. The face is systematically withheld: hair curtain, downward tilt, turned back, or distance. Bodies are slim, swallowed by oversized layers, standing deadpan — hands in pockets, or holding an ordinary object (a bag, a phone, a market-bought fruit) with solemn straight-faced gravity. Group shots favor the row or loose cluster seen from behind so the spider prints face the camera. Full-body framing matters: the uniform reads head-to-toe, hair to sneakers.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical smartphone crops dominate; camera height is chest-to-eye level, straight-on, unstyled. Back-view lineups, frontal faceless portraits, wide environmental shots with small figures against mall glass or waterfront railings, and tight detail crops of the emblem on fabric are all native compositions. Lighting is diffuse and unglamorous: shadowless overcast daylight, cool mall fluorescent wash, or night phone-flash with black falloff. Grades drain warmth and saturation toward grey. The parallel graphic register abandons photography entirely: centered white spider line-art on absolute black, handwritten Cyrillic slogans ("we are a generation; Ryodan is with me"), stick-figure brawl diagrams pitting a spider-backed figure against a tracksuit-clad one.

## Mood & Values

Deadpan solidarity. The crew stands together in identical black because the uniform is the point: it declares membership before any face is seen. Over that sits an ironic pseudo-militarism — "PMC" as parody of private military contractors, battle maps of shopping malls as mock campaign documents — humor that deflates the very violence the media accused the group of. Underneath the irony runs melancholic anime romanticism inherited from the Dead Inside milieu: long hair, drained color, teenage sorrow worn as posture. And underneath both runs defiance: the subculture formed its identity partly through being confronted and harassed for its clothes, so the uniform doubles as a banner of generational self-assertion against gopnik street norms, moral-panicking officials and sensationalist headlines. Facelessness is therefore triple-coded — anime cool, anonymity under detention-era surveillance, and meme-ready erasure of the individual in favor of the troupe. Notably, the infobox lists Gopnik among the style's key values: Ryodan inherits the gopnik ethics of crew loyalty, street presence and territorial gathering, while replacing every gopnik visual signifier with anime black.

## Era & Origins

2020s, Russia and Ukraine, with copycat cells in Belarus. The clothing lineage begins in 2020 with the Hikikomori Kai brand's spider-emblem garments; the first major VK community appeared in September 2022 and grew to roughly 190,000 members before Roskomnadzor blocked it in February 2023. Public existence dates to February 22, 2023, when a fight at Moscow's Aviapark mall — reportedly initiated after Ryodan teens were confronted about their clothing by right-wing affiliates — went viral, followed by mass detentions (over 500 nationwide, 131 after a Galeria mall brawl in St. Petersburg), coerced apology videos, and parliamentary declarations blaming "Western intelligence" and anime itself. The style reacts on two fronts: against the Adidas-clad gopnik/offnik street culture it clashes with, and against a state and media apparatus that labeled a loose fan community a "transnational terrorist organization." Its aesthetic sources are *Hunter x Hunter*'s Phantom Troupe, the depressive anime register of Dead Inside, DotA 2 fan culture, and the vernacular of VK/Telegram meme channels.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Monochrome uniform first: oversized black hoodie or tee bearing the white twelve-legged spider with the number 4, grey-black checkered trousers, chunky sneakers, black crossbody bag.
2. Emblem legibility: white print on black fleece, usually across the back; twelve legs, numeral on the abdomen; screen-print flatness, not embroidery sheen.
3. Withhold the face: hair curtain, bowed head, turned back, or distance. No glam, no makeup, no eye contact.
4. Crew grammar: two to five same-dressed bodies in a loose row or cluster; back-view lineups are the signature shot.
5. Mundane post-Soviet stage: mall atrium glass, paved park alley with painted curbs, panel-block courtyard, grey waterfront, market stall row. No exotic or luxurious locales.
6. Amateur register: smartphone focal length, slight softness, flat overcast or fluorescent light, desaturated cool grade — or the parallel vector register of pure black ground, white line-art spiders and handwritten Cyrillic captions.
7. Irony over aggression: props stay ordinary or absurd and are held deadpan; conflict is implied through rivalry signifiers, never shown as gore.
8. Grey check is the only busy pattern allowed; everything else is solid black, white or drained grey.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: crew lineup or walk, solitary faceless portrait, mall gathering, waterfront stand, market or flea-market scene, detention-era street snapshot, meme illustration, emblem fabric close-up, mock "battle map" graphic.
- *Composition*: back-view row, frontal hair-curtain portrait, wide environmental shot with small figures, vertical full-body crop, centered flat graphic.
- *Lighting*: shadowless overcast, cool mall fluorescent, night phone-flash with black falloff, flat vector black; always diffuse, never sculpted.
- *Color handling*: strict black/white/grey core; check as sole pattern; at most one muted accent garment (oxblood knit, striped sweater); saturated tracksuit blue only on antagonist figures.
- *Mood*: deadpan, melancholic, ironic-militant, playfully absurd — any blend of these.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A lone teen on a railway platform, a graphic of spiders over a hand-drawn mall plan, or a crew shot from behind in falling snow all qualify without reproducing any documented frame.

**Avoid**

- Saturated or neon color dominating the frame; pastels, gold, kawaii pink.
- Adidas three-stripe tracksuits or any sportswear branding on Ryodan-coded subjects (that is the rival gopnik/offnik signifier).
- Visible styled faces: makeup, colored hair streaks, chains, rings, ring-light selfie glam (E-Boy territory).
- Tokyo Ghoul masks, black sclera contacts, bandages, blood (Dead Inside territory).
- Real military gear: camouflage, tactical vests, plate carriers, realistic weapons, gore — the "PMC" is a joke, and actual warkit destroys it.
- Glossy studio lighting, warm golden-hour grades, heavy bokeh professional portraiture.
- Luxury logos, hype-brand monograms, streetwear flex culture.
- Misdrawn emblem: eight-legged generic spiders, black-on-white inversion, neon spiders, spider without the troupe feel.
- Clean-cut short hair, smiling face-forward group selfies, thumbs-up poses.
- Gothic lolita frills, Victorian elements, cyberpunk neon, vaporwave grids.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Dead Inside / "Ghoul"**: shares long black hair, drained color, DotA 2 and depressive anime mood. Decisive difference: Dead Inside cites *Tokyo Ghoul* (Kaneki mask, kakugan eyes, bandages, solitary bedroom doom-text) and stays individual and self-wounding; Ryodan cites *Hunter x Hunter* via the spider-and-4 emblem, adds checkered trousers, and is fundamentally a crew uniform photographed in public space with ironic militarism.
- **Ofnik**: the rival street faction; the text characterizes offniki (with gopniki) by Adidas tracksuits and antisocial framing. Visually: sportswear brands, caps, no spider emblem, no plaid, no hair curtain. Presence of tracksuit stripes on the subject means offnik, not Ryodan.
- **Gopnik**: classic three-stripe Adidas, squat posture, caps, short hair, hardbass vernacular. Ryodan explicitly defines itself against this silhouette; the two appear together only as opponents in brawl narratives and meme diagrams.
- **E-Boy**: overlaps in black oversized layers and curtain bangs, but E-Boy is a curated online performance — bedroom ring light, silver chains, painted nails, dyed streaks, posed selfie. Ryodan refuses the face entirely, rejects jewelry and glam, and stages itself offline in crews against post-Soviet municipal backdrops.
- **Herki**: neighboring CIS street style sharing black oversized layering and long fringe; the source text documents it only as a related aesthetic without defining features, so evidence here is thin. In practice, disambiguate by Ryodan's hard markers: the spider-and-4 print, checkered trousers, and crew back-lineup. Without the emblem, a black-clad fringe haircut alone is not sufficient to claim Ryodan.
- **Nyashka and soft anime styles**: bright, cute, face-forward; Ryodan's monochrome facelessness is the opposite pole despite the shared anime fandom.

When in doubt, apply the triplet test: white twelve-legged spider with the number 4 on black, grey checkered trousers, face erased by black hair. All three present — Ryodan. Only hair and black — likely Dead Inside or E-Boy. Tracksuit stripes — gopnik or ofnik.
