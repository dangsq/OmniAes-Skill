# Cyberminimalism (Cyberbougie)

Cyberminimalism is what happens when minimalism gets subtracted once more. Born in the late-2010s web and SaaS scene and coined by Alex Edwards, it pushes past Flat Design's reduction into a grammar of essentially three ingredients: sans-serif words, a color gradient, and a design grid — with little to nothing else. The alternate name "Cyberbougie" names its attitude: this is minimalism as boutique luxury, tech optimism dressed in premium restraint. Originally confined to websites and tech branding, it leaked into music, wellness, fashion and event culture by the early 2020s, and its module language was canonized when Apple popularized the bento grid in 2022. Almost all surviving artifacts are screen-native — landing pages, app interfaces, presentation decks, identity systems, device mockups — with posters and business cards as its occasional print outpost.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The ground is binary and the color is borrowed light. Canvases are near-black (roughly `#0A0A0A`–`#161616`, the dominant mode) or paper white / off-white (`#F4F4F2`–`#FFFFFF`); rarely, a single muted pastel field (pale lavender, sky blue, warm grey) stands in as ground. Typography is strictly monochrome — white on dark, black on light, only rarely a colored micro-label. All chroma lives in gradient fields, and a given composition typically commits to one hue family: ember orange/amber (`#FF6A00`–`#FFB25E`), magenta-rose (`#FF2E63`–`#FF7A8A`), violet-ultraviolet (`#7B5CFF`–`#B39DFF`), electric blue (`#2E5BFF`–`#7FB4FF`), or acid teal-green (`#00C48C`–`#30D158`). Light-mode posters may instead carry grainy pastel smears (peach, lilac, mint) blurred across white. The rule: high chroma at the gradient's core, dissolving to black, white, or an adjacent hue at its edges — color as a light source, never as flat fill.

### Signature motifs (ranked as the style documents them)

1. **Bento grids** — rounded-rectangle modules of differing spans tiled edge-to-edge with consistent gutters and a shared corner radius; the style's structural signature, especially post-2022.
2. **Gradients, normal and grainy** — radial glow orbs, blurred blooms bleeding off a canvas edge, linear panel washes, soft streak-smears; fine noise is overlaid to kill banding.
3. **Grotesque / sans-serif type** — neutral neo-grotesques and geometric sans (Inter/Helvetica-family voices), set black or white.
4. **Black backgrounds** — the matte void in which gradients glow.
5. **Simple symmetrical geometry** — squares, circles, centered orbs; a gradient disc behind a floating device is a classic hero.
6. **Slab serifs and high-contrast serif display** — an occasional "bougie" counterpoint: one oversized serif word or glyph against an otherwise sans system.
7. **Flat UI surfaces**, with Neumorphism or Glassmorphism as permitted garnishes rather than structural principles.
8. **Micro-typography apparatus** — tiny all-caps labels, hairline rules, oversized numerals and stat callouts (`6 hours`, `328,443,984`) doing hierarchy by scale jump.

### Materials and textures

Surfaces are screen-smooth: vector gradients with a whisper of grain, matte black voids, paper-white stock in light mode, optional frosted-glass cards. Product renders (earbuds, phones, tablets, smart-home hardware) are softbox-clean with crisp speculars on matte plastic and metal, floating shadowless or haloed by their own glow. There is no environmental texture — no wood, no fabric, no weathering; the only "material" evidence is grain over gradient and the soft-touch sheen of rendered hardware.

### Recurring subjects

Digital artifacts first: landing pages, dashboards, app screens, year-in-review/stat decks, wordmark and identity boards, business cards, phone-and-tablet mockups. Consumer-tech feature grids (audio gear, OS presentations, weather apps) are the style's product photography. The same grammar readily absorbs non-tech subjects — wellbeing posters, music-event placards, club flyers, fashion identities — treated as abstract type-plus-gradient compositions. Photography, when present at all, appears as muted, cropped fragments inside grid cells, never as a hero image.

### Composition and lighting

Composition is module-first: either a full bento tiling, or a single dominant glow anchored top or bottom with type massed in the opposing margin, or split light/dark halves, or an editorial column grid with micro-type at the top and a bloom at the foot. Symmetry and centering are welcome; negative space is treated as luxury. Hierarchy comes from extreme scale contrast — 8-pt caps labels against 200-pt numerals or a single display glyph. Lighting is self-emissive: in dark mode the gradient is the only light source, falling off radially into black; light mode is shadowless and evenly lit. No cast shadows, no atmosphere beyond the bloom itself.

## Mood & Values

Sleekness as moral virtue. Cyberminimalism expresses calm authority and premium restraint — "less, but luminous." Its values are clarity, hierarchy, precision, and whitespace as status; its mood is quiet futurism without dystopia, tech confidence filtered through boutique taste (hence "cyberbougie"). Nothing shouts; one glow hums. The emotional register is a dim, expensive room with a single lit panel on the wall.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s (circa 2017–2019), coined by Alex Edwards as a description of design pushing beyond Flat Design into further reduction. Documented artifacts cluster 2019–2022: landing pages and identity systems of 2019–2021, then the bento-grid wave after Apple's 2022 popularization. It began in web and tech spaces and spread outward into events, wellness, music and editorial. It reacts against both Flat Design's cheerful solid-color plainness and against maximalist, photography-led corporate web design — answering both with darkness, grids, and gradient light.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Subtract to three ingredients — grotesque type, a grid, one gradient — and make everything else justify its existence.
2. Keep the ground binary: near-black or paper-white (a single muted pastel field at most); keep type monochrome and inverse to the ground.
3. Confine all chroma to gradient fields; choose one hue family; saturate the core, dissolve the edges into the ground.
4. Lay fine grain over gradients so they read as light, not vector fill.
5. Build structure from modules: rounded-rectangle bento tiles with uniform gutters and corner radius, or one hero glow with type in the opposing margin.
6. Drive hierarchy by scale jump: micro all-caps labels versus oversized numerals or a single display glyph; hairline rules as joints.
7. Light the frame from within: the glow is the light source; everything else is shadowless and evenly lit.
8. Finish sleek: crisp vector edges, clean product renders, zero ornament.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any digital artifact or poster subject — tech product grid, wellness program, music event, fashion identity, abstract orb composition, dashboard, business card. Ground: black (dominant), white, or rarely one pastel field. Hue family: ember, magenta, violet, blue, teal-green, or pastel multi-smear in light mode. Gradient treatment: smooth radial orb, grainy blurred bloom, linear panel wash, horizontal streak-smear. Layout: full bento tiling, hero-glow-plus-type, split light/dark halves, column editorial with foot bloom, centered symmetric geometry, device mockup floating on a glow disc. Type voice: pure grotesque, or grotesque system with one serif/slab display accent. Density: sparse single-glow poster through fully tiled bento deck. Mode: dark or light. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Multicolor confetti or rainbow palettes; colored body text; photographic heroes or imagery-heavy layouts; skeuomorphism, bevels, embossing, heavy drop shadows; ornate or Victorian serifs, script faces; hand-drawn illustration and organic doodles; grunge or distress texture beyond gradient grain; cyberpunk signifiers — neon tube signage, chrome, glitch, HUD circuitry, scanlines (the name is not the genre); pervasive frosted translucency as the structural idea; unmodulated flat color blocks carrying the composition; dense corporate clutter, stock photography, office iconography; Y2K chrome and 3D glossy blobs; maximalist collage; naturalistic lighting, cast shadows, or atmospheric haze.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Flat Design** uses solid, unmodulated color fields, often polychrome and bright on white, with no gradients, glow, or grain. Cyberminimalism keeps the flat UI discipline but puts luminous gradients on monochrome grounds — usually black — and reads darker, sleeker, more expensive.
- **Glassmorphism** is structured around frosted translucency: blurred backgrounds seen through semi-transparent panels, vivid wallpaper bleeding through glass. Cyberminimalism's grounds are opaque; a glass card may appear as garnish, but the structural signature is grid plus glow, not transparency.
- **Minimalism** at large is medium-agnostic and matte — architecture, objects, neutral palettes, physical space — with no required glow, grain, or module grid. Cyberminimalism is screen-native, luminous, and bento-modular; its emptiness hums with color light rather than resting in neutral tone.
- **Corpcore** is enterprise mundanity: beige and grey decks, stock photography, logo walls, dense tables, office realism. Cyberminimalism is boutique-tech premium — dark mode, gradient orbs, subtraction instead of documentation; it never shows the office.
- **International Typographic Style** shares the grotesque type and strict grid but insists on asymmetry, solid color fields, objective photography and print-era rationalism, and forbids gradient glow, grain and dark mode. Cyberminimalism inherits the grid and the sans, then swaps solid color for luminous gradient, welcomes symmetry and centered orbs, and lives on screens.

**Honest gaps:** Neumorphism is named in the style's documentation but barely surfaces in surviving artifacts; print presence is thin (posters, cards); and the serif-display accent, while documented, is a minority voice against the grotesque norm. Treat both as permitted spice, not core grammar.
