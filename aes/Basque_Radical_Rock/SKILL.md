# Basque Radical Rock (Rock Radikal Vasco / Euskal Rock Erradikala / RRV)

Basque Radical Rock is the visual arm of the punk movement that erupted in the Basque Country and Navarre in the early 1980s around bands like Kortatu, La Polla Records, Eskorbuto, RIP, Cicatriz, Barricada, Hertzainak and Soziedad Alkoholika. Its natural habitats are the 7" or LP sleeve, the concert poster and the fanzine page — never the gallery or the glossy magazine. The style is deliberately poor: xerox degradation, scissors-and-tape collage, rubber-stamp and stencil lettering, newsprint halftone. That poverty is the point; it mirrors a region living through industrial reconversion, mass unemployment, heroin and political violence after the Franco dictatorship, and it refuses the commercial polish of the Madrid music industry. Everything below describes rules for building or recognizing that look, not copies of any particular sleeve.

## Visual Identity

### Palette
The palette is a rule, not a mood: **ink black + paper off-white + one aggressive red**, with **Ikurriña green admitted only as a flag quotation** (a stripe, a saltire fragment, a flag strip along an edge). Any other hue enters as a defect — rust-brown paper decay, a misregistered magenta or cyan pass — never as a designed harmony.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Ink black | `#0a0a0a` – `#1c1c1c` | Dominant field or dominant figure; crushed photocopy black with smudged edges |
| Newsprint off-white | `#efe9dd` – `#f7f4ee` | Paper ground; never pure bright white except as blown highlight inside duotone |
| Flag / poster red | `#c8102e` – `#e03a2f` | The shout: full-bleed fields, duotone ink, star, slogan bars |
| Ikurriña green | `#007a3d` – `#009b48` | Accent only, always read as "Basque flag", never as decoration |
| Decay rust / cardboard | `#7a4a2b` – `#a15c2f` | Optional aged-print variant |
| Misprint magenta / cyan | `#cc0066`, `#0099cc` | Rare, tiny, obviously a printing error |

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the text documents them)
1. **The Ikurriña (Basque flag)** — whole, cropped, waved as a stage backdrop, or reduced to its red-white-green geometry under a figure. The single strongest identity marker.
2. **The red star** — socialist five-pointed star, small and punchy: beside a logotype, on a banner, scattered across a flyer.
3. **Cut-and-paste collage** — torn or scissored photo fragments, taped-down scraps, overlapping layers with visible edges; the page assembled by hand.
4. **DIY typography** — stencil caps, ransom-note cut letters, typewriter blocks, rough handwriting, rubber-stamp slogans; plus the scene's blunt **condensed grotesque logotype slabs** set crooked or boxed. Deliberate **"K" spellings** ("Radikal", "Kortatu", "Kaotiko", "alkoholika") are a linguistic motif made visual.
5. **Protest and confrontation imagery** — raised fists, demonstrations, placards with slogans, police and Guardia Civil encounters, references to state violence and prisoners; grief markers (dates of massacres, "no olvidamos"-type lines).
6. **Crude drawings** — spiky, ugly-deliberate ink line work: skulls, fists, weapons, maps of Euskal Herria, childlike aggressive cartoons; occasionally flat crude colour cartoons on cardboard beige.
7. **Punk iconography fused with local symbols** — mohawks, leather jackets, skulls and crossbones, anarchy signs, but always paired with Basque flags, red stars or Basque/Spanish slogans rather than standing alone.
8. **Thresholded photography of ordinary people** — shouting youths, an elder with a raised fist, a child with a placard: anti-portraits, anonymous and grainy, never glamorous.

### Materials and textures
Photocopier toner with contrast crushed to two or three tones; halftone dot screens from newspaper reproduction (the Egin press and fanzines are the scene's paper trail); newsprint fibre and paper grain; scissor cuts, tape strips, staple holes; screenprint misregistration; stencil spray fuzz; typewriter and lettraset imperfection. Surfaces should feel scanned or mimeographed, not rendered.

### Composition and lighting
Layouts behave like zine pages and gig flyers: type as heavy bars or blocks pinned to top/bottom edges, vertical type runs down a side, circular badge or patch compositions, stacked band-name lists with venue and date lines, slogans dropped into corners. Asymmetry and jarring juxtaposition are structural; there is no grid system and no breathing whitespace as a virtue. Photographic source light is harsh on-camera flash or flat overcast documentary light, then flattened further by thresholding into duotone — no modelling, no gradients, no soft shadow. Grainy colour snapshot photography of stages draped in Ikurriñas belongs to the scene's documentary record and may inform background treatment, but the graphic sleeve language above is the core.

## Mood & Values
Urgent, confrontational, accusatory — and communal. The style carries anger at the state, the church, the military, NATO and the police; grief for the dead of the heroin epidemic and of state violence; defiance and local pride; and a fierce anti-commercial DIY ethic. Satire and nihilism are acceptable registers (La Polla's sneer, Eskorbuto's despair), as is a rough festive energy from the ska-and-reggae wing of the scene — but never cynicism-about-the-cause, never polish, never irony that dissolves the politics.

## Era & Origins
Emerging in the late 1970s from industrial towns like Bilbao and Mondragón, named in 1983 by José Mari Blasco and Marino Goñi after an anti-NATO festival, peaking around 1987 with the Egin newspaper's *Bat, bi, hiru... hamar* compilation, and broken by heroin and AIDS by the turn of the decade. It reacts against the Franco legacy, the perceived failure of the Transition, industrial decline — and aesthetically against the glamorous, art-school Movida Madrileña. Revival and tribute acts keep the sleeve language alive today, so dated-looking materials are acceptable in any decade as long as the medium logic is photocopy-and-scissors.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Build the image as a *printed object*: a xeroxed sleeve, a stapled zine page, a pasted poster. Decide the paper before the picture.
- Limit ink to black + off-white + one red; add green only when quoting the flag.
- Treat type as imagery: stencil, ransom cuts, handwriting, crooked condensed slabs, K-spellings, Basque or Spanish slogans.
- Crush any photography to duotone or halftone; keep grain, blur and flash flare.
- Collage with visible seams: cut edges, tape, overlap, misregistration.
- Anchor meaning locally: Ikurriña, red star, placards, industrial backdrops, Basque-language words.

**Variation axes.** Subject may range across protest crowds, factory gates and closed steelworks, street corners and taverns, concert pits, ordinary elders or children, skulls and punk figures, maps and flags, or pure symbol-and-type compositions. Composition may range from full-bleed duotone photo with slogan bar, to off-white field with a single halftone figure and logotype column, to circular badge, to ransom-note scatter, to flyer list-layout, to crude cartoon panel. Lighting ranges from flash-blitzed night to flat overcast, always post-crushed. Colour handling ranges from pure black-and-white to red-on-black duotone to red field with black/white figures to off-white with red and green accents, with rare rust or misprint tints. Mood ranges from rage to mourning to satire to rough festivity. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**
- Glossy vector gradients, clean corporate branding, balanced minimal grids, generous Swiss whitespace.
- Studio glamour, soft beauty light, airbrush, bokeh, warm nostalgic film grading, HDR or 3D render sheen.
- Movida-style neon pastels, fuchsia-turquoise pop palettes, hedonist pop-art gloss.
- Harmonious multi-hue palettes; green used decoratively rather than as flag citation.
- Cute or manga-inflected cartooning; the crude drawing must stay ugly and hand-violent.
- Generic Anglo-punk clichés (union jacks, safety-pin royalty satire) or pure anarcho/crust apocalyptic war collage with no Basque markers.
- Legible modern sans-serif set perfectly straight as the only type; ornate decorative serif flourishes.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Tell that separates RRV |
|---|---|
| **Movida Madrileña** | Movida is airbrushed, neon, pop-art glossy, apolitical and playful; RRV is xerox-gritty, duotone, flag-and-star political, anti-glamour. |
| **Bravú (Galician)** | Bravú shares DIY collage and regional pride but leans on Galician folk-carnival grotesquerie, rural festivity and warmer earth tones; RRV is starker red/black/green, industrial and protest-first. (Visual evidence for Bravú is thin; treat the flag/star combo as the safer RRV tell.) |
| **Anarcho-Punk** | Anarcho-punk is internationalist: circle-A, peace signs, monochrome stencil, no national flags. RRV flies a specific national flag (Ikurriña), uses the red star and Basque-language slogans tied to local grievances. |
| **Crust Punk** | Crust covers rot: sepia-olive decay, apocalyptic war imagery, illegible scrawl logos. RRV stays flat-graphic, red-and-green accented, with legible slogans and contemporary street politics. |
| **Punk '77 (generic)** | Sex-Pistols-era punk is ransom-note with pop-art swagger, bright clashes and magazine gloss and a sneering humour; RRV is poorer, harsher, more photocopy-degraded, and its anger is programmatic and regional rather than theatrical. |

When in doubt, ask: does this look hand-assembled on a photocopier in Bilbao or Pamplona around 1985, flying a Basque flag and a red star against the state? If yes, it is Basque Radical Rock.
