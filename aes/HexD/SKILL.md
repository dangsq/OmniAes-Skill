# HexD

HexD is simultaneously a music microgenre and a cover-art aesthetic. The sound — existing tracks "hexed" through bitcrushing, compression and pitch-shifting by the collective Hexcastcrew on late-2010s SoundCloud — produced a visual language that behaves like its audio: a faithful object (early-to-mid-2000s internet graphics) run through a destructive process until it emerges murky, noisy and strangely sacred. Nearly all HexD visuals are square cover art for songs, mixes and bootlegs, assembled the way a 2004 teenager would assemble a Blingee page: anime cutouts, glitter stamps, sigils, game sprites and clashing fonts layered with no depth consistency, then saved and resaved until the compression artifacts become the texture of the piece. The result is nostalgic yet melancholic, cute yet occult, sincere yet ironic — a "deep-internet" relic mood.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is a narrow family played at extremes of value: **pure white or pure black grounds** carrying **pinks and purples** (bubblegum around `#FF9ED2`, hot magenta around `#FF00CC`, lavender and lilac around `#C8A2E8`, deep violet near `#6A0DAD`), with **black** reserved for wings, horns, sigils, goth clothing and star stickers, and **holographic/iridescent accents** (pearl chrome, mint-cyan near `#7FFFD4`, oil-slick gradient foil) used sparingly as glow or foil text. Two ground modes are equally valid: the white-ground "sticker sheet" mode (character cutout on blank white with floating sparkles) and the black-ground "neon shrine" mode (magenta and violet glowing out of darkness). Duotone washes — mint-green, magenta, violet tinting an entire image — are within range, as are near-monochrome sketch pages pierced by one saturated accent. Because everything is meant to look lossy-compressed, gradients should band and colors should bleed slightly at edges; a clean, smooth gradient is off-style.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Heavily edited, pixelated female anime characters.** The core subject. Recurring archetypes: succubus/devil girls with bat wings, curved horns and arrow-tipped tails; vampires and gothic-lolita figures in black lace; angels and fallen angels with mismatched or half-black half-white wings; magical girls and chibi mascots. Source material is often recognizable 2000s anime, traded and re-edited until unrecognizable.
2. **Esoteric and mystical symbols as stickers.** Demonic sigils, the six-pointed hexagram / Star of David, pentagrams, all-seeing eyes, candles, crosses and occult seals, usually stamped flat over the composition, often with a neon glow or chrome foil treatment. Vampiric and demonic references (fangs, bat-winged heart lockets, coffins) belong to this layer.
3. **Blingee-style glitter and Old Web graphics.** Four-point lens glints, sparkle bursts, drifting star particles, glitter dust, animated-GIF shimmer, ornate decorative frame borders, and even period watermarks and corner badges (video-platform logos, band URLs). This glitter layer is the style's signature light source.
4. **2000s video game graphics.** RPG and Japanese arcade imagery (racing cabinets, street-race aesthetics), and in the Sextrance offshoot: rhythm-game and racing-game graphics, low-poly 3D models, anime trading cards and merchandise scans. Sprites repeated in marching rows are a known compositional trick.
5. **Cluttered collage layering.** Photo cutouts (flowers, ruins, real faces, jewelry, flip phones), naive MS-Paint-style drawings, chibi stickers and sanrio-cute mascots piled around the central figure with deliberate amateur layout logic.
6. **Text as graphic debris.** Gradient WordArt display titles, cursive script pierced by sparkle glints, pixel and monospace fonts, blackletter, bubble letters — several mismatched fonts per image. Cryptic filenames with underscores and extensions (`.mp3`, `.gif`, `.gp`), tracklist fragments, URLs and handle watermarks count as texture, not information.

### Materials and textures

The medium is *lossy raster*: JPEG blocking, GIF banding, dithering, downscale pixelation, oversharpened halos, blown-out highlights. Underneath the compression you may find airbrushed anime cel shading, naive paint-program line work, low-poly renders, trading-card scan grain, chrome/foil gradient fills and glitter PNG overlays. Nothing in a HexD image should look vector-crisp, photographically lit or rendered at modern resolution.

### Composition and lighting

Square cover-art format is the norm. Composition ranges from a sparse single character cutout centered on blank white, orbited by a few sparkles and corner stickers, to horror-vacui all-over collage where figure, frame, sigils, photo scraps and text compete edge to edge; ornate Blingee-style frames and repeated sprite rows are common structural devices. Layout is intentionally untrained: no grid, no alignment, no hierarchy. Lighting is never simulated photographic light — surfaces are flatly lit like scans, and all illumination comes from overlays: sparkle glints, neon glow halos around sigils, lens flares, overexposed washes. Black-ground images glow; white-ground images float.

## Mood & Values

HexD values **nostalgia** (for early-2000s digital self-expression — Blingee pages, personal shrines, deleted web hosts), **mystery** (cryptic filenames, occult stamps, half-legible text, anonymous handles) and **surrealism** (layers that refuse to cohere, dream-logic juxtapositions). Beneath the glitter sits melancholy: the aesthetic mourns a dead web while resurrecting its corpses as spells. Amateurism is authenticity — the clumsy layout and crushed quality are acts of sincerity and of resistance to streaming-era polish. Cuteness and dread are held together: a smiling chibi next to a demonic seal, glitter over a vampire's fangs.

## Era & Origins

Coined in the late 2010s on SoundCloud by Hexcastcrew, whose method of bitcrushing existing tracks was framed as casting digital spells; the genre codified itself with the June 2019 mix *Rare RCB hexD.mp3* (Stacy Minajj / tomoe_✧theundy1ng) and spread through the Dismiss Yourself label and *Surge Compilation Vol. 1* (2020), with the fast trance offshoot Sextrance emerging around late 2020. Visually, however, the style is a haunted revival of **early-to-mid-2000s internet culture** — Blingee GIFs, Web 1.0/2.0 personal pages, Y2K game graphics — filtered through a darker, distorted lens. It reacts against clean, high-fidelity contemporary design by weaponizing obsolescence.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Degrade last.** Build the collage, then crush it: downscale, JPEG-compress, band the gradients, pixelate edges. Every element must share the same lossy fate.
2. **Layer like a 2004 web editor.** Character cutout, sticker stamps, glitter, sigils and text sit on independent flat layers with no shared perspective, shadow or depth.
3. **Stamp the occult.** Include at least one esoteric symbol layer (hexagram, sigil, pentagram, eye, candle) treated as a flat glowing sticker rather than an integrated object.
4. **Let glitter be the light.** Sparkle bursts, four-point glints and star particles supply all highlights; keep base lighting flat and scan-like.
5. **Misbehave with type.** Mix gradient WordArt, glinting cursive script, pixel font and blackletter; add filename strings, URLs or watermarks as decorative debris.
6. **Stay in the family palette.** White / black ground, pink–purple mid-tones, holographic accents; duotone washes allowed; compression must visibly damage the color.
7. **Frame it as cover art.** Square format, cluttered or sparse-stickered, never a clean poster layout.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any anime-girl archetype (succubus, vampire, angel, magical girl, chibi), game sprite or low-poly model, trading-card scan, naive paint-program drawing, edited photograph of a person or object (phone, pendant, flowers), or even an abstract glitter field with sigils. Composition: from one cutout on white with three sparkles to maximal edge-to-edge collage; centered, off-center or all-over; framed or frameless. Lighting: flat white-ground scan look, black-ground neon glow, duotone tint, overexposed wash. Color handling: pastel-dominant, magenta-dominant, violet-dominant, mint duotone, or monochrome sketch with a single accent. Mood: cute-playful, melancholic, eerie-occult, surreal-absurd. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — degradation, flat sticker layering, occult-glitter stamping, family palette, amateur layout — not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Crisp high-resolution modern digital painting, HD anime key visuals, vector-sharp logos or esports graphics.
- Photorealistic lighting, depth of field, studio photography, realistic shadows between layers.
- Cohesive professional typography, modern flat UI design, aligned grids, intentional negative space as polish.
- Smooth clean gradients free of banding and artifacts.
- Databending, datamoshing, pixel sorting or RGB-split as the dominant effect (that is Glitch Art; HexD decay is uniform compression crush).
- Desaturated grey drain-style photo realism and emo selfie mundanity.
- Sharp chrome tribal linework isolated on empty ground (Cybersigilism).
- Serene, clean new-age crystal moodboards (Xpiritualism).
- Vaporwave grids, marble busts, palm sunsets; corporate cyberpunk neon.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared surface | What separates HexD |

| **Xpiritualism** | Sigils, stars, angels, mysticism | Xpiritualism is clean, serene, cohesive new-age (crystals, zodiac, soft high-res pastels). HexD is compressed, cluttered, anime-centered, glitter-stamped and ironic; JPEG decay plus Blingee glitter plus anime equals HexD. |
| **Animecore** | Anime subjects, maximal edits | Animecore keeps images crisp, saturated and modern-edit sharp. HexD mandates lossy degradation, Old Web graphics, occult stamp layers and period watermarks; hexagrams and Blingee glitter are rare in Animecore. |
| **Cybersigilism** | Sigil imagery | Cybersigilism is sharp vector tribal/chrome linework, monochrome, minimal, sleekly futuristic. HexD sigils are raster stickers glowing on cluttered pastel collage; crisp linework on empty ground means Cybersigilism. |
| **Draincore** | Melancholy, internet sadness | Draincore is photo-based, desaturated to grey, grounded in real-world emo selfie scenes. HexD is saturated pink-purple, collage-based, glittered and anime-led. |
| **Glitch Art** | Distortion | Glitch Art foregrounds structured corruption — scanlines, pixel sorts, RGB splits — as subject. HexD's damage is uniform lossy compression and pixelation that still leaves readable characters, stickers and clutter intact. |

When in doubt, ask: does this look like a beloved 2004 web graphic that was hexed, compressed and re-uploaded from a dead host? If yes — glitter, sigil, anime girl, crushed JPEG and all — it is HexD.
