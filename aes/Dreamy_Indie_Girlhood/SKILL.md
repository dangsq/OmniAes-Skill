# Dreamy Indie Girlhood

A photographic aesthetic of the early-2010s Tumblr era, also circulating under the name *female gaze*. It depicts the world of teenage girls through vintage, ethereal visuals — but deliberately real and intimate rather than polished. Its look was codified by Rookie Magazine (founded 2011) and its resident photographer Petra Collins, and it inherits its haze from 1960s–70s dreamlike cinema (Czech and Soviet fairy-tale films, *Picnic at Hanging Rock*, Sofia Coppola's *The Virgin Suicides*) and its sincerity from the 2010 Hipster/Twee revival's cult of emotional authenticity. Photos were tagged with adjectives — "dreamy," "pastel," "grunge" (a soft pastel grunge, not Seattle) — rather than a community name.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** Every hue of the rainbow is admissible, but only after passing through overexposure and faded film stock: all colors sit at pastel saturation with milky, lifted shadows. Anchor range: milk pink `#F7DDE6`, lilac `#E3D3EC`, mint `#D3E7DC`, powder blue `#CBDAE9`, peach cream `#F6E6D6`, butter yellow `#EFE7C0`, faded leaf green `#A9BFA3`, milky white `#F2EDE6`. Saturated color survives only in tiny doses — a row of nail-polish bottles, a sticker sheet, bunting flags, an orange light leak `#E58A5C` at the frame edge — and dark notes (black hair, black shoes, a knit cap) appear as small punctuations, never as fields. A correct image may lean cool (mint/blue dominant) or warm (peach/pink dominant); both are in range.

**Medium and light.** Analog film is the substrate: visible grain, halation around highlights, blown-out whites, faded blacks, occasional edge light leaks, soft focus, and sometimes the artifacts of a scanned print (white border, dust, stickers adhered to the scan itself). Light is warm and diffuse: backlit sun flare, sunshine sieved through tree leaves, overexposed window light washing a room to white, dappled garden light. A minority variant uses direct flash at night against glitter-scattered darkness. Light itself is a subject — reflected off water, bubbles, colored glass, mirrors, glitter, shiny stickers.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them.**
1. Hazy, vintage-camera photography of teenage girls in moody, inward moments — faces turned away, eyes closed, absorbed in a private gesture.
2. Light and reflection: glitter, shiny stickers, sun through leaves, light on water, bubbles, colored glass.
3. DIY and collage: physical or digital stickers overlaid on photos (butterflies, stars, frames in corners), magazine cut-out collages, taped mementos — concert tickets, birthday cards, childhood toys.
4. The cluttered teenage bedroom as identity shrine: posters, cosmetics, found objects from mixed eras sharing one dresser (a grunge poster beside antique perfume and a 2000s flip phone).
5. Childhood seen through melancholy: toys, playgrounds, childhood photos, girly costumes, sometimes captioned by hand on the print ("I miss this...").
6. Rainbow items and random assortments of objects, pastelized.
7. Suburban teen settings with no markers of wealth or poverty: high school, bedrooms, birthday and slumber parties, parks and fields, occasionally fairs.
8. Fashion: flower crowns; mod dresses with contrasting collars, ruffles, lace; collared shirts under sweaters; tulle and pleated skirts; Converse; prominent white socks against black shoes; Mary Janes.
9. Girly totems in abundance: flowers, stickers, glittery nail polish, the color pink.

**Materials and textures.** Tulle, lace, chiffon, cable knit, satin ribbon; paper (stickers, cut-outs, tickets, handwritten notes); glitter and foil; glass (perfume bottles, colored glass, vanity mirrors); water and soap bubbles; foliage (clover, leaves, garden flowers); worn wallpaper, painted wood, metal bed frames, sheer curtains. Surfaces should feel touchable and slightly worn, never seamless.

**Composition.** Snapshot candor over staging: off-center subjects, headless crops that privilege garment and prop, backs turned, faces hidden by hair or haze; still lifes of vanity tables and dresser clutter; wide tableaux with small figures in fields or gardens; scanned-print and collage-grid formats. Foreground veils — flare, haze, out-of-focus foliage or tulle — are welcome. Eye contact is rare and, when present, soft and unperformative.

## Mood & Values

Interiority above all: subjects seem aware of being seen by a peer, not by an audience — the female gaze. The mood is wistful, tender, sometimes bored, always intimate; childhood is remembered through a melancholy, reflective lens even while it is still happening. Values: emotional authenticity over polish, amateurism as honesty, DIY creativity as self-expression, the ordinary suburban world treated as worthy of myth. The style romanticizes without sexualizing and without commercial gloss.

## Era & Origins

Early 2010s, Tumblr; institutionalized by Rookie Magazine (2011) and Petra Collins, then spread through fashion photography and, in parallel, early Japan's *Larme* magazine, whose pastel, floaty fashion fed the same feeds. It reacts against polished digital commercial femininity and inherits the handmade sincerity of 2010 Hipster and Twee, while its visual grammar quotes 1940s–70s film stills.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Film first: grain, halation, faded blacks, overexposed highlights; optionally a light leak or scanned-print border.
2. Pastelize whatever hue you choose; keep saturation in props, not in fields.
3. Intimacy over spectacle: private gestures, inward faces, small narratives.
4. Accumulate: cluttered surfaces, mixed-era trinkets, layered textures.
5. Make light a subject: flare, sparkle, reflection, bubbles, dapple.
6. Leave a DIY hand: stickers, tape, cut-outs, handwriting on the print.
7. Keep setting suburban and unmarked — neither wealth nor poverty.
8. Keep girlhood innocent: subjects read as adolescents or young women, fully clothed, posed introspectively, never seductively.

**Variation axes.** Subject: a solo girl, a pair, a small group, a people-less still life, an interior, a garden or pond, a prop tableau (paper moons, birdcages, painted stars), a memento scan. Composition: candid snapshot, headless crop, back view, obscured face, dresser still life, bordered scan, collage grid, distant-figure tableau. Lighting: blown noon light, golden backlight, window haze, dusk, rare night flash with glitter; leak placement free. Color: any pastel family, cool or warm dominant, rainbow scatter as props. Mood: wistful, bored, tender, quietly playful, melancholy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Crisp digital sharpness, HDR, saturated primaries, crushed blacks, clean minimalism and empty negative space; studio glamour strobe, airbrushed skin, sexualized poses or framing; designer logos, luxury or urban markers; contemporary tech (smartphones, earbuds) as props; Wes Anderson symmetry and mustard-teal quaintness; rococo gold and deliberate flirtation; celestial fantasy realms; crisp candy-flash party literalism; tidy airy domestic still life; neon, cyber or vaporwave elements; hard Seattle-grunge grit. Absolutely never sexualize the adolescent subject.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | How it differs |
|---|---|
| **Twee** | Illustrative flatness, orderly symmetry, mustard/teal/brown palette, gender-neutral quaint props; Dreamy Indie Girlhood is photographic haze, pastel wash, girl-centered interiority and mess. |
| **Coquette** | Digital crispness, deliberate flirtation, ribbons and pearls as costume, cherry-red accents, clean boudoir staging; this style is amateur film, suburban clutter, melancholy rather than seduction. |
| **Delicate Sweet** | Tidy, airy, smooth-lit pastel still life without grain, leaks or clutter; this style insists on film decay, stickers, teen accumulation and narrative wistfulness. |
| **Ethereal** | Otherworldly realms, cool silver-blue, flowing goddess gauze, no earthly anchors; this style dreams inside ordinary suburbia — bedrooms, high school, back-yard fields. |
| **Girls' Sleepover** | Event-driven communal fun, pajamas and popcorn in saturated candy color under crisp flash; this style borrows the setting but slows it into haze, solitude or quiet pairs, pastel-faded and film-grained. |

Nearby but distinct: Larme Kei shares the pastel floaty fashion yet is polished magazine studio work, and Nymphet overlaps in subject matter where this style explicitly refuses sexualization. Evidence note: the infobox's "adventure" motif surfaces mainly as outdoor wandering and water play; handwritten captions are text-documented but sparse in surviving images, and the night-flash glitter mode is a minority variant — treat all three as optional, not core.
