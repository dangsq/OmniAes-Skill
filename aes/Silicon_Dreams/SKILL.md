# Silicon Dreams

Silicon Dreams — also called Early CGI, Retro CGI, Old CGI or Drum 'n Bass CGI — is the aesthetic of computer-generated imagery from the moment 3D rendering first became visible to the public: roughly the mid-1980s to the early 2000s. It is not a subject matter but a *render condition*: the look of software and hardware (Bryce 3D, Pov-Ray, Terragen, SoftImage on Amiga and SGI workstations) that could imagine infinity but only afford simple polygons, one or two lights, and 8-bit color. The result is a body of imagery that reads as sincere, sterile, and faintly wrong — surreal landscapes "like something that came out of a dream," distributed on demo tapes, TV bumpers, Trapper Keeper binder covers, game CDs, and later jungle/drum-'n'-bass mix covers. Its revival currency comes from Vaporwave and Seapunk nostalgia, but the style itself is the primitive render, not the nostalgia filter around it.

## Visual Identity

### Palette
The color language is a cool, low-saturation gradient family interrupted by pastel warmth. Skies and voids run periwinkle to powder blue to pale cyan (roughly `#7E8FD8` → `#A8C8E8` → `#BFE3F0`), often with lavender and teal variants (`#B9A7E0`, `#1FB8B8`). Horizon bands and cloud strips introduce pastel pink, peach and magenta (`#E8A7C8`, `#F0B890`, `#D868B8`). Terrain and creatures sit in muted earth tones — tan, mauve, gray-brown (`#C8B090`, `#9A8898`). Accents are chrome and glass grays (`#C0C8D0`) plus, sparingly, one saturated signal color: an emissive green horizon line (`#38E838`), orange-painted girders (`#D85828`), or a magenta swirl. Crucially, all gradients arrive *degraded*: horizontal banding, dithering, and low bit-depth stepping are part of the palette, not defects to remove.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)
1. **Surreal extraterrestrial landscapes** — fractal terrain, infinite calm seas, sparse dreamscapes with no human scale.
2. **Big planets, orbs and floating shapes** — spheres, capsules, tori, stacked discs and monoliths suspended in sky or void, singly or in loose depth arrays.
3. **CGI architecture**, including neoclassical postmodern statues, colonnades and columns dropped into impossible settings.
4. **Simple primitives and surfaces** — checkerboard planes, wireframe-overlaid meshes, glass bubbles, chrome mirror spheres; "simple visual effects and objects" as a virtue.
5. **Early CGI characters and creatures** — faceted low-poly dinosaurs, thin articulated humanoids, grotesque caricature heads, blobby primitive figures with tube limbs.
6. **Rendered industrial/sci-fi interiors** — truss ceilings, girder grids, receding rows of glowing discs (the music-video strain).
7. **Music-video and album-cover framing** — jungle/intelligent DnB covers, vaporwave video stills, 4:3 frames with logo bugs and watermarks.

### Materials and textures
Fractal-noise rock and terrain; mirror-chrome and raytraced glass with a single hard specular dot; matte Phong plastic; low-resolution bitmap textures with visible texel repetition and stretching; fog and haze as flat gradient veils; checkerboard and wireframe as surface decoration; optional analog layer of VHS softness, interlace, scanlines and tape grain when the image poses as a video still, or glossy 8-bit dither when it poses as print.

### Composition and lighting
Emptiness is the composition: one centered object or landform in a vast negative space, frequently a mid-frame horizon with a near-perfect mirror reflection in still water below. Alternatives are loose floating arrays receding into haze, and one-point perspectives down beams, columns or light rows. Lighting is primitive by necessity — a single directional source with a hard specular highlight, flat ambient fill, self-luminous emissive discs or horizon glow bands, and no global illumination, no soft shadows, no bounce. Backgrounds are either banded gradient sky or near-black void.

## Mood & Values

Uncanny serenity. The style expresses naive techno-optimism — the belief that the computer opens a door to another world — rendered with tools too primitive to make that world hospitable. The result is liminal, quiet, slightly eerie: dream logic played completely straight, with no irony inside the frame. It carries childhood-memory weight (binders, bumpers, game CDs) and a quasi-spiritual utopian undercurrent (new-age demo reels, orbital webfiction, ambient jungle covers). "Janky" is affectionate: the faceted edges and stiff poses are the proof of sincerity.

## Era & Origins

First 3D models emerge in the 1970s (the 1972 computer-animated hand); the style becomes visible in the 1980s through demos, advertisements, TV bumpers and early shorts (TRON, Luxo Jr., Tin Toy, Knick Knack); it goes mainstream in the 1990s via blockbuster films, low-poly games (Myst, Star Fox, Wipeout, Final Fantasy VII) and TV (ReBoot, Beast Wars). It fades by the mid-2000s as rendering matures into Frutiger Aero gloss, then returns in the 2010s as nostalgia fuel for Vaporwave and Seapunk, and in the late 2010s–2020s on intelligent drum-'n'-bass and jungle mix artwork.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Think renderer, not camera: simple shading model, faceted or crudely smoothed geometry, one light plus ambient, hard specular dot, no bounce light.
- Compose with emptiness: a solitary subject, large negative space, horizon or void as the stage.
- Use reflection as wonder: mirror-calm water or chrome surfaces doing cheap, perfect reflections.
- Let the sky be a banded, dithered gradient; let fog be a flat veil.
- Juxtapose with dream logic: dome over island, totems on water, bubbles above a sea — played straight, never parodied.
- Finish period-correct: 8-bit dither and texel repetition always; scanlines, interlace and small logo bugs only when posing as a video still.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
| Axis | Allowable range |
|---|---|
| Subject | fractal seascape or terrain; floating planet/orb/primitive array; PoMo colonnade or statue; low-poly creature, humanoid or caricature head; rendered truss/industrial interior; abstract gem or disc stacks; cover-style portrait |
| Composition | horizon-centered symmetry with reflection; floating depth array; one-point receding perspective; framed 4:3 video still; vertical portrait cover |
| Lighting | pastel ambient daylight; dark void with emissive discs; single horizon glow band; fogged murk; lone hard specular |
| Color | cool periwinkle/cyan dominant; pink-peach sunset bands; muted mauve/tan terrain; at most one saturated accent |
| Finish | clean dithered render; glossy print look; VHS/interlaced still with logo bug |
| Mood | serene utopian; uncanny liminal; mildly psychedelic at the cyberdelic edge |

**Avoid**
- Modern render fidelity: PBR materials, raytraced global illumination, soft area shadows, subsurface scattering, HDR, bokeh depth of field, high-poly smoothness, photoreal textures.
- Photographic materiality of real buildings or objects (this is rendered, never shot).
- Neon-night laser grids, striped synthwave suns, or vector glow-lines as the dominant structure.
- Cyberpunk noir: rain, signage, green CRT terminals, dystopian street narrative.
- Frutiger Aero gloss: aero glass panels, lens-flare-saturated HD nature, skeuomorphic product sheen, busy optimistic composites.
- Contemporary clean 3D motion design, isometric vector art, pixel-art sprites, voxel blocks.
- Crowds, clutter, organic sculpted detail, hand-drawn or painterly handling.
- Modern typography or UI; period text only as tiny watermarks, credits or logo bugs.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Demoscene** shares primitive hardware but is a real-time effect reel: plasma, tunnels, scrolling credit text, music-synced tricks. Silicon Dreams is offline-rendered contemplation — serene horizons and album-cover stillness, no demo HUD or scrolltext culture.
- **Early Cyber** is noir: wireframe city grids, CRT green, neon signage, human-machine dread. Silicon Dreams is pastel, daylight or soft void, utopian or neutral, surreal-nature rather than urban dystopia.
- **Factory Pomo** photographs real postmodern industrial architecture with bold color cladding. Silicon Dreams interiors are *rendered*: primitive shading, dark voids, emissive discs, no photographic surface truth.
- **Frutiger Aero** is the successor gloss (2004–2013): HD bubbles, glass, lens flares, saturated aqua-green, dense optimistic composites. Silicon Dreams bubbles are simple raytraced glass with one hard highlight over a flat dithered sea — sparse, low-res, uncanny where Aero is polished and friendly.
- **Laser Grid** is flat graphic design: glowing vector grids and striped suns on black. Silicon Dreams checkerboards and planes are shaded 3D surfaces in perspective inside a pastel world, accompanied by low-poly geometry and banded skies that Laser Grid never has.

**One-line test:** if it looks like a dream of infinity rendered by a machine that had never seen one — faceted, dithered, mirror-calm, and utterly sincere — it is Silicon Dreams.
