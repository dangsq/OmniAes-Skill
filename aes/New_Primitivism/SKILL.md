# New Primitivism (Novi primitivizam)

New Primitivism was a satirical, surreal, anti-intellectual subculture founded in Sarajevo in March 1983 as a mock answer to Western New Romanticism ("sugar-sweet"), the cold seriousness of Slovenia's NSK, and the prevailing Yugoslav New Wave and punk scenes. Its visual side is intentionally démodé and unpolished: a "smart yet trashy" mix of subcultural cut-and-paste print matter and the dress code of Baščaršija petty hoodlums, deployed to put the mahala's "small people" on the national stage. Everything looks handmade, slightly misprinted, and openly joking — the movement's own creed, "Tuđe hoćemo, svoje nemammo" ("What's not ours, we want; because ours, we haven't got"), parodies a communist slogan, and that parodic relation to official language is visible in its graphics too: mock manifestos, mock congress banners, fake credits under pseudonyms.

## Visual Identity

**Palette.** Two families coexist and may be combined. The *loud print family*: xerox black grounds (#0A0A0A–#1A1A1A) carrying flat, unmodulated marker colors — felt-pen yellow (#F2D21F–#E8B400), signal red and plaid red-orange (#C81E1E–#D9481C), neon pink cursive (#FF4FA3–#F26BAE), poster kelly green (#009A44–#37B34A) — plus newsprint off-white (#E9E4D6). The *muted video family*: the beige-greys, olive-greys and washed blues of socialist TV studios and videotape (#B9B19E, #7C7F6E, #7FA3C0, cream #D8D2C0), punctured by exactly one loud costume or lettering accent (a red plaid jacket, a pink script title). Rule of thumb: desaturated, slightly color-bleached base plus hand-applied saturated accents; never a harmonized designer palette.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the movement's own documents place them.**
1. The "small person": petty criminals, coalminers, taxi drivers, pensioners, neighborhood alcoholics, local thugs — portrayed in absurd situations with affection, never contempt.
2. Sarajevo neighborhoods: mahala rooftop clusters, Baščaršija old-town streets, aerial or snapshot views of the hillside city used as background or subject.
3. The leisure-suit uniform appropriated from petty hoodlums: plaid suit jackets, waist-tight bell-bottoms, a thin gold necklace worn over the shirt, pointy shoes (špicoke); oversized power suits appear in the TV-sketch variant.
4. Street jargon as visible texture: hand-lettered cursive titles, handwritten and typewriter captions, comic speech balloons carrying Sarajevan slang and Turkish loanwords; lettering is crooked, off-register, proudly amateur.
5. Surrealist absurdity and B-movie plots inherited from the *Top lista nadrealista* sketches: impossible juxtapositions staged deadpan, like a TV gag rather than a dream.
6. Mock-officialdom: manifestos, congress banners, medals, emblems, pseudonymous credits — state paraphernalia rebuilt as a joke.
7. Camp mass portraits: crowded costumed group photos, uniforms and medals worn ironically (the Plavi Orkestar "camp angle" spin-off marks the outer edge).

**Materials and textures.** Photocopier halftone dots, scissors-cut paper edges, glue smears, marker and felt-pen coloring over printed photos, typewriter and ballpoint handwriting, cheap vinyl-sleeve print with misregistered color, videotape grain and soft chroma bleed, on-camera-flash gloss on promo photos, polyester sheen and wool plaid on clothing, gold chain glint. The surviving record is dominated by sleeves, fanzine spreads and videotape stills, so the "finish" is always whatever cheap 1980s print and studio video allowed — flat, grainy, slightly washed.

**Composition.** Crowded collage grids of panels, clippings and balloons with no hierarchy; naive single-scene illustration with wonky perspective and checkerboard floors; packed frontal group portraits in costume; aerial city photograph overlaid with cursive script; flat, head-on TV-studio framing with desks, nameplates and monitors behind. Deliberate amateurism is structural: crooked cuts, tilted lettering, unbalanced pages.

**Lighting.** Flat and even, always: studio flood lighting of a TV set, direct on-camera flash, overcast daylight snapshot, or the shadowless wash of videotape. No chiaroscuro, no golden hour, no modeled drama; shadows are short and hard (flash) or absent (studio).

## Mood & Values

Self-irony, local patriotism, anti-establishment mockery, humor as first resort, ostensible anti-intellectualism, and resistance to imported trends. The mood is warm toward its losers: the thug, the drunk, the pensioner are heroes, not targets. Pride is worn as shame inverted — the "primitive" wasteland becomes identity — and every solemn form (manifesto, congress, medal, slogan) is handled with a wink. Deadpan absurdity and camp spectacle are both legitimate temperatures of the same joke.

## Era & Origins

Sarajevo, SR Bosnia and Herzegovina, SFR Yugoslavia; manifesto published 1982 by Malkolm Muharem, movement unveiled March 1983 at a double bill of Zabranjeno Pušenje and Elvis J. Kurtović & His Meteors, disbanded in a filmed mock "7th Extraordinary Congress" in 1987. Core media: the albums *Das ist Walter* and *Mitovi i legende o Kralju Elvisu* (both 1984) and the radio/TV sketch show *Top lista nadrealista*. It reacts against Western New Romanticism, NSK's cold neo-avant-gardism, and the intellectual posture of Yugoslav New Wave, embracing instead the Bosnian stereotypes of Yugoslav bar-room jokes.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Build the image as assembled print matter: cut paper, halftone photos, hand-lettered text, marker color blocks; let misregistration and crooked edges survive.
- Dress subjects démodé-on-purpose: 1970s leisure-suit silhouettes, plaid, bell-bottoms, špicoke, gold chain over the collar; or oversized early-80s power suits for studio-sketch scenes.
- Cast the small person: miner, taxi driver, pensioner, thug, alcoholic — absurd situation, affectionate tone.
- Use text as texture: cursive hand-lettered titles, slang captions, speech balloons; invented wording is fine if it looks hand-written and locally flavored (Latin-script South Slavic street slang, Turkish loanwords).
- Light flat: studio flood, on-camera flash, overcast snapshot, videotape softness.
- Stage the impossible like a TV sketch punchline, not like a dream.
- Parody officialdom (banners, medals, congress titles) whenever solemnity is needed.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any small-person archetype, mahala or Baščaršija street scene, TV sketch set, band promo shot, album sleeve, fanzine spread, mock ceremony, artifact still-life of plaid jacket and posters. Composition: collage grid, naive single scene, frontal group portrait, aerial photo plus script overlay, flat studio framing. Lighting: any of the flat sources above. Color: loud print family, muted video family, or muted base with one hand-applied accent; accent hue free within yellow/red/pink/green. Medium texture: xerox zine, vinyl sleeve, videotape still, flash photo, naive painting. Mood: deadpan absurd ↔ warm caricature ↔ camp spectacle. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.**
- Sleek digital polish: gradients, drop shadows, vector-clean typography, 4K crispness.
- New Romantic glamour: airbrushed pastels, frills, satin, beauty lighting.
- NSK-style severity: black monochrome monumentality, geometric sans-serif, cold seriousness.
- Punk rage signifiers: leather, studs, safety pins, ransom-note cut letters, spitting nihilism.
- Partisan heroics: red stars, flags, heroic realism, earnest patriotic marching.
- Ethnographic village folklore: embroidered peasant costume, kolo circles, postcard Orientalism of the old town (Baščaršija appears lived-in and démodé, not picturesque).
- Cinematic lighting: chiaroscuro, golden hour, bokeh, lens flare.
- Generic American comic polish: clean inks, superhero anatomy, digital coloring.
- Anachronisms: anything post-1987 in tech, fashion or product design.
- Mean-spirited mockery or poverty porn: the laugh is with the small person, never at them.

## Discrimination Cues

- **New Partisans:** later, militant, pro-Yugoslav; partisan iconography (red stars, flags, heroic groups) used earnestly. New Primitivism borrows official forms only as parody, stays local (mahala, not state), and keeps its palette cluttered print rather than flag-red monumentality.
- **New Wave (Novi val):** modernist cool — minimalist layouts, angular geometry, monochrome plus one neon, intellectual irony, contemporary fashion. New Primitivism is maximalist clutter, warm amateurism, deliberately out-of-date 70s dress, folk humor over urban cool.
- **Punk:** shares xerox DIY, but punk's xerox confronts (leather, studs, snarling negation); New Primitivism's xerox narrates — speech balloons, naive figures, jokes, polyester plaid instead of leather.
- **Surrealism:** oneiric precision and unconscious symbolism, often polished illusionism. New Primitivism's surrealism is sketch-comedy absurdity: naive drawing, mundane mahala props, punchline logic, flat studio light.
- **Camp/Kitsch boundary:** mass costumed portraits with medals (the Plavi Orkestar spin-off) sit at the edge; keep camp in service of mockery — if the kitsch becomes glossy nostalgia without a wink, it has left New Primitivism.
