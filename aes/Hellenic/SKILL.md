# Hellenic

Hellenic (also "Ancient Greek") is the aesthetic of the ancient Greek world and its mythology, revived as a personal, devotional and lifestyle aesthetic by modern internet culture — mythology fandom, retellings, and pagan or occult practice that adopts the gods as patrons. It behaves like Cottagecore or Dark Academia: a whole way of dressing, decorating, cooking and worshipping organised around a theme, rather than a single visual genre. Its double nature is the key to reading it: the material grammar is antique (marble, bronze, gold, ceramic, linen), but the framing eye is contemporary and affectionate — a bust crowned in blush roses, an altar of pomegranates and coins, a moodboard of lyres and temples. It is explicitly *not* Classicism: where Classicism studies Greece and Rome academically, Hellenic loves Greece personally.

Rule: neutrals of stone and metal carry the image; gold is the accent of divinity; black-and-red ceramic and Aegean blue are the secondary chords; blush pink is permitted only as the modern worshipper's offering, never as the antique surface itself. Note the documented fact that ancient buildings and sculpture were *painted*, not bare — polychrome ornament bands are as canonical as museum-white marble, and both extremes sit inside the style.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

First rank (the style's declared keys): pomegranates, gold coins and drachmas, bow and arrows, wine and grapes, laurel wreaths, white marble, Greek god statues, gold itself. Second rank (the documented visual list): the lyre, togas/tunics/robes (chiton, peplos, himation, chlamys), the golden apple, eagles, owls, sun and moon, storms with thunder and lightning, underground caverns, ambrosia and nectar, Greek armour and weapons (crested helmet, round aspis shield, spear), Cupid/Eros, theatre masks. Third rank, carried by the object culture: columns and capitals (Doric, Ionic, Corinthian), pediments, caryatid porches, amphorae, kraters and kylixes with figural friezes, Tanagra figurines, winged Nike, meander/Greek key, egg-and-dart and palmette borders, olive branches, shells and pearls (Aphrodite's kit), and the modern apparatus of devotion — home altars, offerings, moodboard collages, museum halls, myth-themed merchandise.

### Materials and textures

Veined or weathered marble; patinated or gleaming bronze; repoussé and filigree gold; glossy slipped terracotta; finely pleated linen and wool drapery, often in the clinging "wet look"; honey-toned wood and marbled plaster in Greek-revival interiors; mosaic and terrazzo floors; waxed candle brass; soft silk and flower petals in contemporary stagings. Surfaces should read as carved, cast, fired, woven or grown — never moulded plastic.

### Recurring subjects

Statues (Venus, Athena, Nike, maenad, herma types); painted vessels; temples, colonnades and ruins on hills or coastlines; Greek-revival rooms with frieze bands and coffered ceilings; costumed figures in draped garment sets; still-life offerings and altars; landscapes of olive groves, mountains, storm skies and sea; athletes and dancers (the style values athleticism); gallery interiors; and grid collages or plate-like arrangements of figures and ornaments.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Composition favours the frieze: horizontal bands, bordering meander strips, rows of figures or ornaments like an illustrated plate; symmetrical colonnade recession; the statue or offering centred as a devotional axis; flat-lay altars; collage grids. Figures stand in contrapposto with serene faces. Lighting ranges widely and all of it is legitimate: hard Mediterranean noon raking stone; even, hushed museum daylight; warm candle and chandelier glow in paneled interiors; storm chiaroscuro for Zeus and sky gods; single-source gloom for underworld scenes; soft blush window light for romantic modern stagings. The wiki text does not codify lighting — this range is read from the corpus, and it is deliberately broad.

## Mood & Values

Reverence without severity. Hellenic expresses love of myth as lived practice: loyalty, intelligence, hospitality (xenia), creativity, athletic excellence. Its emotional temperature runs from sunlit order and serene contemplation, through playful fandom affection, to melancholy (Persephone's descent) and, in its Dionysian province, ecstasy. Beauty is treated as worship; the antique is approached as a living patron, not a museum case.

## Era & Origins

Subject era: archaic through Hellenistic Greece, roughly 8th–1st century BC. Style era: a 2010s–2020s internet-native revival, fed by modern myth retellings, games and musicals and by pagan/occult practice with patron deities, altars and divination. It reacts against two things: the academic distance of Classicism, and the myth of bare white antiquity — it happily holds both museum marble and reconstructed polychromy in the same frame.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. **Stone-and-metal logic.** Every major surface resolves to marble, bronze, gold, ceramic, linen or wood.
2. **Attribute iconography.** Gods appear through attributes, not captions: owl and helmet (Athena), thunderbolt and eagle (Zeus), trident (Poseidon), pomegranate (Persephone), grapes and thyrsus (Dionysus), lyre and sun (Apollo), bow and moon (Artemis), shells, doves and roses (Aphrodite), winged sandals and caduceus (Hermes), black and riches (Hades).
3. **Drapery is the signature.** Peplos folds, himation over the shoulder, clinging wet-look cloth; bare feet or sandals.
4. **Ornament grammar.** Meander, egg-and-dart, palmette and lotus bands frame compositions like friezes.
5. **Polychromy permission.** Painted ornament and bare marble are both canonical; choose one register consciously.
6. **Devotional framing.** Even a modern scene arranges objects as offering or veneration — flowers around a bust, coins beside a pomegranate.
7. **Light as climate.** Noon clarity, museum hush, candle warmth, storm flash, or underworld gloom.
8. **The body ideal.** Athletic proportion, contrapposto, calm face; movement is dance or sport, never frenzy unless deliberately Dionysian.

Variation axes: subject (statue, vessel, interior, costume study, still-life altar, landscape, collage); composition (frieze horizontal, colonnade recession, centred idol, flat-lay, plate grid); lighting (any of the five climates above); colour handling (monochrome marble, ceramic black-red, full polychromy, Aegean blue, blush-modern); mood (serene, athletic, melancholic, ecstatic, playful). **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A seaside still life of shells, coins and a linen cloth at noon is as Hellenic as a colonnade.

Avoid:
- Roman imperial signifiers as the lead: colosseums, legionary eagles, Latin inscriptions, emperor portrait busts.
- Christian angelic kit: halos, feathered wings on worshippers, crosses, cathedral light. Wings belong only to Nike, Eros and Hermes contexts.
- Medieval, Gothic or Visigothic ornament: interlace, garnet cloisonné, pointed arches, runes.
- Egyptian or Norse signifiers: pyramids, ankhs, runestones, hammers.
- Neon saturation, chrome, plastic gloss, digital UI, cyberpunk hardware.
- Horror decay or jungle-swallowed ruin; Hellenic ruins are noble, sunlit, romantic.
- Ungrounded modern clutter (cars, signage, cables) unless the staging is an intentional contemporary devotional scene that keeps them out of frame.
- Airbrushed high-fantasy VFX: magic sparkles, dragons, glowing eyes. Myth here is material, not特效.
- Empty minimalism: a white room with a generic bust and no Greek attribute, ornament or offering reads as generic décor, not Hellenic.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Angelcore** is Christian-adjacent ether: soft clouds, halos, feathered wings, cherubs, pastel sky-light with no stone, no meander, no pagan attribute. Hellenic wings appear only on Nike, Eros or Hermes, always anchored in marble, gold or ceramic materiality.
- **Classicism** covers Greece *and* Rome as academic heritage: heroic narrative painting, formal proportion, canonical restraint, no modern hands. Hellenic is personal and fandom-native — altars, merch, blush florals, collages — and welcomes affectionate kitsch Classicism would exclude.
- **Neoclassicism** is the 18th–19th century revival movement (Canova, Ingres): severe lines, period interiors, historical distance. Hellenic may borrow its objects but frames them through contemporary devotion and accepts reconstructed polychromy and mythic still-life offerings Neoclassicism never stages.
- **Dionysism** is Hellenic's ecstatic province gone total: wine-soaked frenzy, leopard skins, thyrsus, night revelry, dark purple and gold. If an image shows only intoxication and abandon without the broader Greek grammar of column, meander, marble or attribute, it is Dionysism; Hellenic balances revelry with Apollonian daylight, athletics and domestic hospitality.
- **Visigothic Art** is early medieval Iberia: fibulae and buckle plates, garnet cloisonné, braided interlace, horseshoe arches, votive crowns on chains, abstracted zoomorphs. Hellenic ornament is meander, egg-and-dart and palmette; its figures are naturalistic bodies in drapery; its materials are marble, bronze and gold sheet, never garnet inlay.

Quick test: meander border + laurel or olive + marble or ceramic surface + a god's attribute + devotional calm = Hellenic.
