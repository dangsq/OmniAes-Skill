# Aristocrat (Gothic Aristocrat, EGA)

Aristocrat — also called Gothic Aristocrat, and known in its most famous commercial form as EGA (Elegant Gothic Aristocrat), the neutral-gendered line coined by Mana of Moi-même-Moitié — is a Japanese street fashion of the mid-1990s that dresses its wearers as mourning-age European nobility: 19th-century tailoring, clerical and military echoes, vampiric stillness. It grew out of goth fashion as the mature, androgynous counterweight to Lolita, and was codified by houses such as Moi-même-Moitié, Atelier Boz, Marble and Sheglit. Everything in the style serves three refusals: no cuteness, no childlike volume, no modern casualness. In their place it offers a long, dark, historically structured silhouette worn with aristocratic poise.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
Black is the ground, not an accent: roughly two-thirds to four-fifths of the cloth in frame sits between #0B0B0D and #1C1C22, appearing simultaneously in matte wool, satin sheen and sheer chiffon. Against it, bone-white to ivory (#EDE8DC–#F5F1E6) appears only in small high-contrast patches — a jabot at the throat, ruffled cuffs, a blouse front, the wearer's powdered complexion. One accent colour is admitted at a time, held to about a tenth of the frame: deep crimson to oxblood (#6E0B14–#A3122A) is the default, carried by roses, skirt linings, hat feathers and lips; midnight blue (#131C33–#22304F) is the documented alternative, and an unrelieved all-black mourning reading with no accent at all is equally correct. Metallic notes are antique, never bright: brass and old gold (#8A6D3B–#B08D3E) on button rows and cross pendants, tarnished silver on chains. Backgrounds are either muted neutrals (warm grey #6B6762, parchment #D9D2C0, stone) or a single saturated theatrical field such as a crimson curtain; multicoloured backgrounds do not occur.

### Silhouette and garment grammar (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)
1. **Androgynous tailoring.** Fitted jackets, waistcoats, breeches and trousers cut on 19th-century lines; high-collared, long-sleeved blouses, ruffled or frilled, worn by every gender.
2. **The long line.** Skirts past the calf to ankle or floor; long flowing coats and tailcoats layered over them; dramatic vertical layering instead of horizontal volume. Corsets and high corseted waists cinch a mature, structured figure.
3. **Neckwear.** Jabots, cravats and ascots at the throat, frequently carrying a cross pendant or brooch — the "old-world charmer" signature.
4. **Headwear and hair.** Top hats full-sized or mini, and veils; elaborate curls, updos, or long heavy dark hair. Childlike drilled spirals are explicitly out.
5. **Emblems.** Roses, crosses, fleurs-de-lis and crowns as jewellery, embroidery, hat trim or held props; brass button rows as military-noble punctuation.
6. **Footwear.** Leather high-heeled boots and Victorian button-up or lace-up boots; Mary Janes are avoided as too Lolita.
7. **Materials.** Velvet and lace head the documented list; sheer chiffon/organza blouses, satin and taffeta skirts with ruffled tiers, matte tailored wool and smooth leather complete the texture set observed in practice.

### Recurring subjects and poses
Solo figures or pairs; androgynous or feminine-presenting adults read as ageless nobility rather than as girls or boys. Complexions are pale and matte, eyes and lips defined in dark, clean makeup. Poses are formal and still: seated upright on a chair or stone, standing with one hand at the hip or holding a single rose, a rosary, or a structured handbag; gestures are small and deliberate. Scattered rose petals, a candelabra or wall sconce, an urn or potted palm may dress the set. Groups larger than two are rare in the record.

### Composition, lighting, medium
Vertical, full-length or three-quarter framing that lets the hem and the boots read — the silhouette is the subject. Centred formal symmetry and offset editorial placement both occur; pairs are often staged at unequal heights (one seated, one standing) so two long silhouettes interlock. Lighting ranges from soft, even catalogue light on a plain sweep, to low-key theatrical light against a saturated curtain, to flat overcast daylight outdoors; high-contrast black-and-white treatment is a documented variant. Shadows stay soft; highlights exist to catch satin sheen, brass and lace. The native medium is lookbook and catalogue photography and Japanese fashion-magazine editorial; printed-page artefacts such as Japanese text blocks and brand logos belong to that medium, not to the style, and need not be reproduced.

## Mood & Values
Decadent restraint. The style values androgyny, elegance, darkness, maturity and reverence for 19th-century European aristocracy, and expresses them as funereal poise: still bodies, closed mouths, downcast or level gazes, cloth that falls rather than bounces. Its romance is melancholic and slightly ecclesiastical — roses and rosaries, mourning veils, candlelight — never playful. Dignity outranks novelty; every ornament is justified by period grammar.

## Era & Origins
Mid-1990s Japan, emerging from goth fashion and the Visual Kei milieu as a deliberate mature alternative to the overtly feminine, childlike Lolita silhouette. Brands — Moi-même-Moitié above all, alongside Atelier Boz, Marble and Sheglit — codified it, with Mana's EGA line naming its neutral-gendered branch. Its visual sources are Victorian and Edwardian upper-class dress, mourning attire, clerical vestment and military noble uniform, filtered through Japanese gothic romanticism.

## Generation Guidance
Principles:
- Build the silhouette before the ornament: long, columnar or flowing, structured shoulder, cinched or corseted waist, hem at ankle or floor.
- Let black be the field; spend white in small patches and one accent colour sparingly.
- Keep ruffles disciplined — jabot, cuff, skirt tier — never allover frill.
- Dress any gender in the same grammar; androgyny is a value, not a costume.
- Cut every garment on historical lines: stand collars, princess seams, waistcoat fronts, tailcoat tails.
- Close the look with one emblem (rose, cross, fleur-de-lis, brass button row) and period boots.
- Render in the lookbook/editorial photographic idiom; monochrome is acceptable.

Variation axes:
- **Subject:** solo or paired figures of any gender presentation; seated, standing, or walking slowly; with or without handheld props. Garment-only still lifes are plausible but thinly evidenced.
- **Setting:** plain studio sweep; atmospheric interior (theatre curtain, conservatory, stone hall); overcast exterior (waterside, garden, street).
- **Lighting:** even catalogue light, low-key theatrical, flat daylight, or monochrome contrast.
- **Colour handling:** black + ivory with crimson; with midnight blue; or unrelieved mourning black; brass/silver glints optional.
- **Silhouette branch:** skirt-based (long ruffled skirts, coat-dresses) versus trouser-based (breeches, waistcoats, tailcoats); both are core.
- **Mood register:** from quiet funeral stillness to decadent theatrical vampirism.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A daylight street snap in black tailoring is as valid as a candlelit curtain portrait, provided silhouette, palette and motifs obey the grammar above.

Avoid (style-breakers):
- Lolita cuteness: head bows, sweet or novelty prints, knee-length bell skirts on heavy petticoats, childlike spiral curls, Mary Janes with frilly socks.
- Candy colour: pastels, neons, rainbow brocade, bright multicoloured backgrounds.
- Modern casualwear: denim, sneakers, hoodies, T-shirts, caps, visible contemporary logos, smartphones.
- Fetish/club goth: latex, PVC, fishnet bodysuits, spike studs, strap harnesses as the main event.
- Steampunk hardware: goggles, cog jewellery, brass machinery, aviator leather.
- Cartoon Halloween goth: plastic skeletons, neon green, bat clip-art.
- Rococo pastel excess (Tanbi Kei territory) and pumpkin pants with knee socks (Ouji territory).
- Kawaii makeup (circle lenses, pastel blush, glitter), candid laughing snapshot energy, sunny golden-hour cheer.

If the hem is long, the waist is structured, the palette is black with a single noble accent, and nothing about the figure reads cute or casual, you are looking at Aristocrat.
