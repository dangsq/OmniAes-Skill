# Après-ski

Après-ski ("after ski") is the social ritual and fashion aesthetic of the hours following a day on the slopes: drinks at a mountainside bar, long fondue lunches, firepit gatherings and resort nightlife, together with the dress code that goes straight from ski run to bar terrace with only the boots swapped. It emerged with the commercial skiing boom of the 1950s in the French, Austrian and Swiss Alps and carries that mid-century jet-set memory in its clothing and photography. Visually it is rustic elegance: warm, comfortable, casually luxurious winter wear photographed in high-key snow light or firelit wood interiors, always with a trace of company, conviviality and equipment at rest.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is a **white field punctuated by heritage accents and a dark anchor**. Snow and cream dominate the frame; black or charcoal grounds the outfit (ski pants, knits, gloves); one or two saturated heritage colors appear as punctuations — a fondue pot, a deck chair, a puffer, a knit set. In interiors, honeyed wood replaces snow as the field.

| Role | Range | Typical carriers |
|---|---|---|
| Snow / cream field | `#F5F1E8` – `#FFFDF7` | snow ground, ivory knits, shearling, white ski suits |
| Dark anchor | `#141414` – `#2B2B2B` | flared ski pants, black knits, gloves, hoods, vehicle shells |
| Deep red accent | `#7E1E28` – `#B33A3A` | enamel fondue pots, deck chairs, puffer jackets, knit sets |
| Navy accent | `#1E2A44` – `#2C3A5C` | sweaters, beanies, quilted jackets |
| Forest green accent | `#24422F` – `#3C5A45` | knits, shell jackets, chalet trim |
| Earth / wood tones | `#8A6A48` – `#4A3524` | chalet planks, fur vests, leather boots, camel trousers |
| Editorial pastel extension | `#EFC9CF`, `#CFDDE6` | powder pink or ice blue ski suits in magazine-style shots |

An all-black monochrome outfit variant against snow is legitimate, as is a pastel-forward editorial variant; what must hold is the cream-or-snow field and the absence of neon technical brights.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Chunky knit sweaters** — Fair Isle and cable patterns, wool or cashmere; the single most cited garment.
2. **Turtlenecks / ribbed rollnecks** as the base layer, for women and men alike.
3. **Puffer and down jackets, quilted blazers, shearling vests and bombers** worn over one-piece ski suits.
4. **Ski pants**: sleek stirrup leggings, bib salopettes, flared ski trousers; flannel or wool trousers for men.
5. **Shearling-lined snow boots** (Moon Boot / Sorel type) and fleece-lined leather boots.
6. **Accessories**: pom-pom beanies and fur trapper hats, snow-glare sunglasses, leather gloves, knit headbands.
7. **Chalet setting objects**: ski chalets, fireplaces, fondue and raclette pots, mulled wine, firepits, vintage ski posters on wood walls.
8. **Ski equipment as at-rest prop**: skis leaned or shouldered, poles held loosely, snowboards under arm, lifts and gondolas in the background.
9. **Social ritual props**: terrace tables, champagne and beer steins, hot chocolate mugs, deck chairs on snow, live music and oompah bands.

### Materials and textures

Chunky wool and cashmere cable knits; ribbed turtleneck collars; glossy shell fabric and quilted down; shearling and fur as trim on hoods, cuffs, collars and boot shafts; sleek stretch ski pant fabric; grained leather gloves and boot uppers; fleece lining; rough-sawn chalet planks and stone fireplace surrounds; enamel pots, ceramic mugs, cut-glass tumblers; granular, tracked powder snow. Texture contrast is the point: soft knit against slick shell, fluffy shearling against hard ski edges.

### Recurring subjects

Women and men in post-ski dress, solo or in pairs and small friend groups; couples on the snow; loungers reclining in deck chairs on open snow; people at terrace tables with food and drink; threshold scenes — vehicle tailgates, chalet porches, lodge doorways — where inside meets outside; chalet interiors with firelight; still lifes of fondue, mulled wine and mugs. Both full-figure fashion framing and candid mid-stride walking shots are characteristic.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical, full-body or three-quarter fashion framing dominates; figures sit against a blank snow field, a high horizon, a wood plank wall, or a clustered table of friends. Props are carried or leaned rather than in use: skis on the shoulder, snowboard under the arm, poles dangling from a wrist. Poses are relaxed and slightly performative — mid-stride walks, lounging diagonals, perched on tailgates and chair arms. Lighting is diffuse overcast snow glare: high-key, shadowless, bright white ground with soft cool shadows. Interiors switch to warm tungsten and firelight on wood. The medium reads as 35mm film or film-emulated digital: visible grain, faded contrast, lifted blacks, slight halation on snow highlights. Magazine-scan and vintage-poster layouts with display typography are a legitimate sub-format, given the style's documented love of vintage ski posters.

## Mood & Values

Convivial warmth after physical exertion: the feeling of boots unbuckled, cheeks flushed, a hot drink in gloved hands. The stated values are coziness, socializing, relaxation, comfort and rustic luxury — luxury that never stiffens into formality. The emotional register spans a documented spectrum: subdued champagne-and-caviar glamour at one end, raucous mountainside dance parties at the other, unfussy beer-and-poutine lodge ease in between. Underneath runs nostalgia for the mid-century alpine jet-set and a "see-and-be-seen" pleasure in being well dressed somewhere cold.

## Era & Origins

The term and culture crystallized in the European Alps in the 1950s as commercial skiing boomed in resorts like Chamonix, St. Moritz, St. Anton and Val d'Isère; mountainside party venues such as La Folie Douce (founded 1981) turned the after-ski hour into an institution. The tradition spread to Aspen, the Dolomites, the American Rockies and Whistler, adapting locally while keeping its core of post-ski relaxation and socializing. The current visual aesthetic — film grain, retro editorials, chalet interiors — is a blog- and Instagram-era revival looking back at 1960s–70s resort photography and vintage ski poster design.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Light the scene first: high-key diffuse snow glare outdoors, or warm firelight and tungsten on wood indoors. Never flat studio-neutral light.
2. Dress in heritage layers: knit base (Fair Isle, cable, rollneck), shell or fur mid-layer (puffer, shearling bomber, fur-trimmed hood), hardware at the extremities (moon boots, leather gloves, sunglasses, pom-pom beanie).
3. Hold palette discipline: cream/snow field, dark anchor, one or two heritage accents (deep red, navy, forest green); wood tones carry interiors.
4. Put equipment at rest: skis leaned against a wall or shouldered, poles held loosely, boots swapped — the day's sport is over, its props remain.
5. Leave a social trace: a second glass, a shared pot, a paired deck chair, a friend at the frame edge, or at minimum a venue that implies company (terrace, firepit, lodge table).
6. Grade like film: grain, softened contrast, lifted blacks, gentle halation on snow.
7. Keep luxury casual — cashmere and tailored down, never black-tie stiffness, never athletic-catalog performance wear.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range across solo portraits, duos, friend groups, couples, menswear outfits (quilted blazer, ribbed rollneck, flannel trousers, fleece-lined leather boots), food and drink still lifes, chalet interior corners, terrace crowds, tailgate and porch thresholds. Composition may be standing full-body, mid-stride walk, seated or reclining lounge pose, table cluster, threshold framing, or a detail crop of knit texture and gloved hands around a mug. Lighting may be overcast high-key, blue-hour terrace with firepit and string-bulb glow, interior firelight, editorial studio high-key, or direct-flash snapshot. Color handling may run heritage triad, monochrome black-on-snow, pastel editorial, or wood-toned interior. Mood may slide from elegant champagne restraint to raucous party to unfussy lodge ease. Format may be photograph or poster/magazine layout with display type. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Neon or volt technical brights (electric orange, acid yellow) as dominant color; glossy modern sportswear-catalog sharpness.
- Urban streetwear context: sneakers, baseball caps, asphalt, city storefronts.
- Plaid-flannel-dominated rusticity with no ski equipment or resort dress (that is Cabincore).
- Pink-dominant Y2K club glam with faux-fur everything and no alpine heritage cues (that is Winter Bimbo).
- Empty minimalist snowscapes with no human, fashion or social trace (generic Winter Aesthetic).
- Coastal or summer materials: linen, straw, canvas, sea light.
- Formal tweed-and-pearls tailoring by a fireplace with no ski element whatsoever (that is Old Money).
- HDR-saturated, hyper-clean commercial digital rendering.
- Nightclub neon, chrome and laser light; party scenes stay tungsten, fire and string bulbs.
- Fur as costume rather than wearable outerwear trim; loud modern logo-mania on garments.
