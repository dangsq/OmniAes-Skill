# Hipness Purgatory

Hipness Purgatory is the house style of the late-2000s indie/hipster moment: the look of a talented friend's sketchbook pressed into service as album art, gig poster, book cover and phone wallpaper. It spans roughly 2004–2013, peaks around 2008–2012, and behaves like a purgatory exactly as its name (coined by Elliot Peebles) suggests — too skilled to be naive, too ironic to be sincere, hanging between childlike drawing and adult design awareness. Everything in it mimics the handcrafted scrapbook or notebook: loose drawings, paper textures, common objects, scratchy thin simplistic linework meant to resemble pencilwork. Underneath the doodles sits "millennial optimism" — a belief that a felt-tip pen, a photocopier and a deadpan joke are enough to make art.

## Visual Identity

**Palette.** Two families share one rule: color arrives as flat, unblended fills on a paper ground, never as gradient or modeled light. The sober family is printer white to notebook cream (roughly #FFFFFF–#F6F2E7) carrying graphite-to-soft-black line (#2B2B2B–#111111) with small accents of the style's key colors: sky/cyan blue (#2AA5D7 range), warm yellow (#F6C445), leaf green (#6FBF44–#A8D42E), orange (#EF7B21). The psychedelic family keeps the same flatness but floods the field: hot magenta and pink (#E6308E–#FF2E9A), purple (#7B2FBE), acid lime (#B4E33D), cyan and orange packed edge-to-edge. A third, collateral ground appears in the collage register: dyed or printed paper in oxblood/maroon (#8E2C34 range) or a single saturated poster color (teal, mint green, tomato red) onto which cut-outs and handwriting are pasted. Rule of thumb: pick a ground, pick either the limited key-color set or the full candy-acid set, and keep every fill matte.

**Linework and mark-making.** The signature is the scratchy, thin, simplistic contour — pencil, ballpoint, fineliner or ink pen — with wobble, overdrawn passes, broken strokes and cross-hatched or scribbled shading. In the maximalist register the line thickens into felt-tip/posca outline holding flat color shapes, but it never becomes a clean bezier: outlines stay hand-wobbled, fills overshoot or miss their boundaries, and dense fields are built from thousands of small doodles (eyes, teeth, leaves, blobs, tiny people) rather than from smooth shapes.

**Motifs and objects, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.** (1) Loose drawings themselves — the doodle as subject and method. (2) Common, everyday objects: mugs, teapots, bicycles, cassette tapes, snacks, houseplants, chairs, bottles, phones; nothing heroic. (3) Paper and notebook apparatus: ruled or blank pages, scrapbook layering, tape, stickers, fold creases, scan grain. (4) Hand-lettered text — titles, captions, credits, speech bubbles drawn in childlike capitals or scrawl, functioning as compositional blocks, often delivering the joke. (5) Naive figures: stick-limbed people with oversized heads and googly eyes, cartoon animals and invented monsters. (6) The maximalist psychedelic doodle field and its calmer cousin, the repeating pattern grid (rows of identical little shapes, doodled borders framing a center). (7) Collage: cut-out photographic fragments (a pair of eyes, a mouth, a seated friend) combined with drawn overlays, scribbled "tattoos" on faces, doodled frames around photos. (8) Crowds of tiny figures filling streets, rooms or party scenes. (9) Deadpan ironic captions pairing sincere image with sarcastic text, or vice versa.

**Materials and textures.** Paper first: cream notebook stock, white printer paper, crumpled and folded poster paper with visible crease lines crossing the whole image, xerox/scan degradation, slight misregistration. Markers: pencil graphite, ink pen, posca-style opaque marker fills, crayon waxiness in the twee corner, cut-and-pasted paper edges. When photography enters, it enters as found material — snapshot flash or flat window light, printed and re-scanned, then drawn on top of.

**Composition and lighting.** Frontal, flat, poster-like. The style owns two density poles and the space between them: the sparse pole places one small figure or object in a vast empty paper field with hand-lettered type floating beside it; the maximal pole fills every centimeter with doodle (horror vacui); between them sit pattern repeats, doodled vignette borders around a photographic center, and collage assemblies of cut-out fragments. Symmetry, when present, is wonky hand-symmetry. There is no modeled lighting in drawn work — light is the even glare of a scanner; photographic elements keep casual amateur lighting (on-camera flash, overcast daylight) and are then degraded by print and fold.

## Mood & Values

Irony and sincerity held in suspension: a twee, childlike warmth undercut by a deadpan wink, or a sarcastic surface hiding genuine affection. Values are DIY authenticity, anti-polish, "retroness" (nostia for analogue childhood tools — notebooks, crayons, cassettes, photocopies), and millennial optimism. The sparse register carries the anti-folk melancholy of Elliott Smith and Jeffrey Lewis; the maximal register carries recession-era party and drug-culture escapism, the same hedonistic drift that fed late-'60s psychedelic poster art and underground comix. Nothing in the style aspires to luxury, precision or corporate confidence.

## Era & Origins

Emerges in the early 2000s out of the hipster and twee/anti-folk movements — New York anti-folk cover art (Jeffrey Lewis, Walter Mitty and His Makeshift Orchestra, Wingnut Dishwashers Union, Paul Baribeau) is its founding dialect — and inherits from '80s twee pop and DIY indie, from Robert Crumb's underground comix, from late-'60s psychedelic rock posters, and from outsider/naive art such as Daniel Johnston and the Chicago Imagists. It fuses with the look of campy 2000s coming-of-age media (Juno, Napoleon Dynamite, (500) Days of Summer, Diary of a Wimpy Kid, Wes Anderson's quirk), leaks into animation (Regular Show, Adventure Time, Bob's Burgers, Superjail!, BoJack Horseman) and games (LittleBigPlanet, Life is Strange), and by the early 2010s is absorbed by mainstream youth culture: EDM and dubstep sleeve art on labels like Dim Mak, Mad Decent, OWSLA and Monstercat, swag-era rap crews like Metro Zu, and pop covers such as Maroon 5's Overexposed. It fades as a distinct movement around 2013, leaving its doodle grammar everywhere.

## Generation Guidance

Principles, not templates:

1. **Paper before picture.** Decide the ground first — printer white, notebook cream, or one flat dyed poster color — and treat every mark as sitting on that physical sheet.
2. **Line before form.** Build everything from scratchy, thin, wavering contour; allow overdrawn passes and hatch shading. No smooth vector curves.
3. **Draw childlike, not childish-naive-by-accident:** wobbly symmetry, oversized heads and eyes, stick limbs, impossible perspective, done with confident amateur energy.
4. **Hand-letter all type.** Titles, captions and credits are drawn letters, speech bubbles and scrawled annotations; text is a visual block and usually the joke's payload.
5. **Everyday objects as heroes.** A mug, a bike, a teapot, a friend on a couch — elevated by attention and a deadpan caption, never by glamour.
6. **Choose a density pole deliberately:** sparse single motif in empty paper, repeating pattern grid, or horror-vacui doodle field. Commit fully to one.
7. **Flat matte fills inside loose outlines,** either the limited key-color set (black/white/blue/yellow/green/orange) or the candy-acid psychedelic set; let fills miss their lines.
8. **Collage is permitted:** cut-out photo fragments, doodled overlays on faces, taped paper, fold creases and scan grain — but the drawn hand must remain visible on top.
9. **Set the irony dial:** deadpan caption against sincere image, or absurd image played straight. Sincerity allowed; slickness never.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any common object, person, pet, invented creature, crowd, room, party, portrait or abstract pattern — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. Composition: sparse center-vs-void, pattern repeat, doodled frame around photo, collage assembly, or full-field doodle. Color: monochrome ink on paper ↔ key-color accents ↔ full psychedelic saturation ↔ single dyed ground. Medium look: pencil, ballpoint, fineliner, posca marker, crayon, cut-paper collage, edited photograph with drawn overlay. Format: album sleeve, book cover, gig poster, notebook page, phone wallpaper, framed print on a wall. Mood: melancholy deadpan ↔ twee warmth ↔ party psychedelia. Lighting: scanner-flat for drawn work; casual snapshot flash or window daylight for photographic elements, always degraded by print.

**Avoid.** Clean bezier vector curves, gradients, drop shadows, 3D renders, CGI gloss; set corporate typography (typed text only as found printed matter inside collage); untouched photorealism or studio product polish; digital airbrush, neon glow, lens flare and meme raster effects; mid-century commercial retro chromo printing and diner-motel Americana; photographed real crafts (knit, felt, embroidery) as the artwork itself; painterly brushwork and weathered folk-sign painting in muted earth tones; gothic darkness, grunge distortion, luxury minimalism, precise anatomy, professional symmetric grid layouts; hyper-clean geometric flat design — Hipness Purgatory's flatness is hand-wobbled, never mathematical.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Hipster:** Hipster is the wider lifestyle aesthetic (fixed-gear bikes, thick frames, flannel, clean digital photography and typed irony); Hipness Purgatory is specifically its hand-drawn illustrative dialect — pencil scratch, paper grain, drawn letters. If it looks like a photo of a beard and a bicycle, it's Hipster; if it looks like a sketchbook page, it's Hipness Purgatory.
- **Vs Genericana:** Genericana is mid-20th-century American commercial vernacular — offset-print ads, motel signs, saturated retro packaging with professional period typography. Hipness Purgatory is 2000s DIY notebook doodle with amateur hand-lettering; no chromo print sheen, no '50s commercial layout.
- **Vs Indiecraft:** Indiecraft photographs actual handmade craft objects (stitched felt, knitted toys, embroidered hoops) with earnest warmth. Hipness Purgatory *draws* its craftiness in pen and pencil and layers irony on top; the handmade feel is simulated on paper, not stitched in wool.
- **Vs Internet Awesomesauce:** Internet Awesomesauce is glossy digital meme maximalism — airbrushed neon, rainbow lasers, glowing cats, raster chaos. Hipness Purgatory's maximalism stays materially hand-drawn: ink and marker on paper, matte fills, visible stroke, no digital glow.
- **Vs Mission School:** Mission School is hand-*painted* naive folk pictorialism — brushy strokes, weathered hobo-sign lettering, muted earth tones, rural-folk sincerity. Hipness Purgatory is pen-and-marker *line* work with bright candy or paper-white palettes, urban indie irony, and photocopier texture instead of paint skin.

**One-line recognition test:** if it looks like a witty, slightly melancholic sketchbook page — scratchy line, paper creases, hand-lettered joke, flat marker color — made between 2004 and 2013 for an indie band, a twee novel or a dubstep single, it is Hipness Purgatory.
