# Queercore

Queercore is what happens when punk's DIY grammar is turned toward queer existence as an open confrontation. Born in the early-to-mid 1980s as an offshoot of punk — instigated in Toronto by G. B. Jones and Bruce LaBruce through the fanzine *J.D.s* — it opposes both cishet society and the assimilationist respectability politics of mainstream gay culture. Everything visual in the style follows from that double refusal: it looks cheap on purpose (xerox, flash, grain), it looks explicit on purpose (leather, drag, sex-positive imagery, imagined-violence text), and it looks communal on purpose (crowds, bands, marches, zine pages passed hand to hand). The image set around it is dominated by live documentation and band portraits, but the style equally lives on printed matter: zine spreads, protest photographs, and raw film stills in the vein of LaBruce's pornographic punk films.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The anchor triad from the style's own key colours is **black, white, pink**, and the working rule is that pink never behaves as decoration — it behaves as a political flare against monochrome or drab institutional surroundings.

- **Xerox monochrome mode:** black `#0b0b0b–#242424`, newsprint off-white `#ece7da–#f6f3ec`, photocopy mid-greys `#6b6b6b–#a8a8a8`. Crushed blacks, blown highlights, halftone dots.
- **Flash snapshot mode:** the monochrome spine plus cheap-film saturated accents — hot pink `#ff2d8f–#ff8fc0` (skirts, mohawks, signs), signal red `#c8102e`, club-gel purple/magenta `#8e4fa8–#d13ba0`, institutional hall green `#7da07a`, sparkle drum-wrap gold `#c9a227`.
- **Faded promo mode:** desaturated pastels on pale grainy grounds — muted teal `#7fa9a2`, mustard `#d0a445`, brick `#b0522f` — thrift-store flannel and patterned shirts under flat, washed light.

Any single image may sit anywhere on this range: an all-monochrome xerox page is as correct as a flash-color hall show with one screaming pink garment.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how strongly the text documents them)

1. **Pink triangle and leather** — the infobox key motifs. They surface as patches, prints, banners, harnesses, jackets, cuffs; the triangle functions as signage and print matter rather than as a prop floating in crowd photos.
2. **Zines and photocopy collage** — the movement's origin medium (*J.D.s*, *SCAB*): cut-and-paste photo strips, typewriter and ransom-note type, staple and tape marks, torn paper edges, text blocks that rant and provoke.
3. **Punk clothing** — studs, patches, pins, ripped fishnets, ringer tees with xeroxed faces printed on them, underwear and briefs worn as outerwear, flannel and thrift layering.
4. **LGBTQ+ punk bands and live shows** — drum kits, diagonal mic stands, hand-lettered banners hung on patterned wallpaper, small stages in halls and basements.
5. **Pride protests and historical LGBTQ+ events** — marches as horizontal sweeps of bodies and handmade signs.
6. **Drag queen makeup** — exaggerated, theatrical, or deliberately crude; text-anchored, sparser in surviving snapshots.
7. **Sex-positive and pornographic imagery, Tom of Finland-style line art** — the style treats sex as political content, not innuendo; radical bodies, SM ritual, queer lust drawn or photographed raw.
8. **The body as billboard** — marker slogans and anarchy symbols written directly on skin and chests; bleached, dyed or pink-shaved hair; sweat and pressed flesh.

Honest caveat: the wiki's visual section is a short list and the photographic sample skews toward show documentation, so motifs 5–7 are text-anchored and may appear without photographic corroboration; the pink triangle likewise reads as print/patch/signage rather than as a literal object in crowd shots.

### Materials and textures

Photocopy grain and halftone degradation, newsprint and copy-shop paper, tape and staples, marker ink on skin, leather grain, denim, fishnet, cheap vinyl floors, wood-panelled community halls, painted cinderblock and plaster walls, string bulbs and colored gel lights, film grain, motion blur, flash blowout and red-eye. Nothing is sealed, laminated or retouched.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Crowd-level or on-stage vantage points; bodies pressed into and cropping the frame edges; performers leaning off the stage into the crowd; mic stands cutting diagonals; flat frontal promo portraits against plain or noisy pale backdrops; zine pages as collaged grids; march photographs as wide sweeps. Lighting is direct on-camera flash with hard falloff and black shadows, colored club bulbs puncturing darkness, high-contrast monochrome with crushed blacks, flat even light for promos, plain daylight for protests. Tilted horizons, off-center subjects and "wrong" crops are correctness criteria, not errors.

## Mood & Values

Defiance without polish. Sex-positivity as politics. Tenderness and joy inside chaos — crowds as chosen family, bands as misfit kin. Black humor and ironic menace (the *SCAB* tradition of imagined violence and deadpan provocation). Above all, anti-assimilation: the style refuses to look respectable, refuses corporate warmth, and attacks mainstream gay respectability as sharply as it attacks cishet norms.

## Era & Origins

Early-to-mid 1980s, Toronto, from the fanzine *J.D.s* (Jones and LaBruce), spreading internationally through zines, raw pornographic film, and music spanning hardcore punk, electropunk, indie rock, power pop, No Wave, noise, experimental and industrial. It positions itself against existing gay institutions and respectability events as much as against straight society, and counts punk, Taqwacore and La Movida among its related aesthetics. Visual DNA remains 1980s–90s analog even in later revivals: xerox, flash, film.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Choose the medium first: xerox monochrome page, flash-color snapshot, or faded promo film — then build grain, blowout and degradation before composing.
2. Make queer signage explicit and handmade: pink triangle patches and prints, marker-on-skin slogans, hand-lettered banners, cut-out type. Never vinyl-professional printing.
3. Treat bodies as the billboard and the crowd as the set: leather, harnesses, underwear-as-outerwear, drag makeup, pressed sweating bodies filling the frame.
4. Let amateurism stand: tilt, blur, flash blowout, awkward crops are the style's proof of authenticity.
5. Spend pink politically: one hot-pink flare against monochrome or a drab hall interior outranks a rainbow of balanced accents.
6. Use text as image: ransom-note cut-outs, typewriter rants, xeroxed photo strips integrated into the composition.

**Variation axes.** Subject: shows, protests, zine spreads, promo portraits, intimate domestic or bedroom scenes, film stills, comic/line-art pages. Setting: basements, community halls, clubs, streets, copy shops, bedrooms. Composition: snapshot chaos, flat frontal promo, collage grid, march sweep. Lighting: on-camera flash, gel bulbs, daylight, flat soft. Color handling: pure xerox mono, flash color, faded pastel. Mood: confrontational, jubilant, tender, ironic. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Glossy commercial polish: seamless gradients, HDR, retouched skin, studio glamour lighting, digital crispness.
- Corporate pride rainbow-gradient merchandise and respectability staging (suits, manicured domesticity, professional "love is love" vinyl signage).
- Clean vector typography; all type must look cut, typed, xeroxed or marker-drawn.
- Couture aspiration, runway glamour, voguing pose grammar (ballroom territory).
- Religious syncretic iconography — keffiyeh, prayer beads, Arabic calligraphy (Taqwacore territory).
- Cutesy pastel girlhood scribble without queer content (Riot Grrrl territory).
- Generic punk anonymity: monochrome leather shots with no queer specificity drift into plain punk.
- Pristine venues: proscenium stages, festival main stages, LED walls, lifestyle-blog beige warmth.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared ground | Queercore tell |
|---|---|---|
| Riot Grrrl | DIY flash snapshots, zine scrawl, overlapping band circles (a Riot Grrrl band even circulates in queercore image sets) | Queer signage and gender transgression at center: pink triangle, drag makeup, leather/SM, queer intimacy; Riot Grrrl centers girlhood and feminist scrawl, childlike pastels, girls-to-the-front framing |
| Taqwacore | Punk base, zine culture, mohawks | Taqwacore fuses Muslim iconography (keffiyeh, prayer beads, Arabic type); queercore's transgression is sexual and gendered, with no religious syncretism |
| Ballroom Culture | Queer community, flash photography | Ballroom is aspirational — couture emulation, runway categories, voguing poses, house glamour; queercore is anti-aspirational xerox-and-flash, crowd chaos, punk gear |
| Punk (general) | Leather, studs, monochrome, DIY venues | Explicit queer content and anti-assimilationist signage; general punk revolts against class and state, queercore additionally attacks mainstream gay respectability and centers queer bodies and desire |

When in doubt, ask: is the queer content explicit, handmade, sex-positive and refusing to look respectable? If yes — queercore.
