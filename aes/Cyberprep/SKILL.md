# Cyberprep

Cyberprep imagines a society that developed along the same technological axes as cyberpunk — cybernetics, megacity infrastructure, cyberspace, robotics — but arrived at utopia instead of dystopia: fair law, no world-controlling corporations, and a leisure-driven culture in which body modification is used for sports, pleasure and self-improvement. The name fuses "cyber-" with "preppy," and that preppy cleanliness is the whole visual argument: where cyberpunk is rain, grime and oppression, Cyberprep is daylight, polished surfaces and civic optimism. Its imagery overlaps deliberately with Y2K Futurism and the Frutiger family of aesthetics because it shares their utopian outlook, but it keeps cyberpunk's hardware vocabulary — grids, circuits, chrome, holograms — rather than the Frutiger world of bubbles, water and gloss-only organicism.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The infobox fixes the key colours as **white and light blue**, and the image set confirms this as an area-dominance rule rather than a mere accent:

- **Base field (60–90% of picture area):** porcelain white and ice blue — `#FFFFFF`, `#F2F8FC`, `#E8F1F7`, `#CFE6F2`, `#A9D3E8`. Skies are pale to overexposed; walls and hulls are white; glass reads pale cyan.
- **Structural accent:** electric cyan / azure — `#29C6E8`, `#00A8CC`, `#7FB8DC` — used for glowing step edges, cove lighting, transit lines, hologram UI, circuit traces.
- **Counter-accent (small area):** orchid, magenta, violet — `#D94FBF`, `#A663D8` — carried by fashion (dyed hair, chrome pantsuits), holographic advertising and signage. Optional pastel secondaries (mint `#BFE8D8`, coral pink `#F2A0B4`) appear in interiors, foliage and playful parametric tunnels.
- **Licensed dark mode:** a near-black cyan-grid variant descended from the Tron lineage — deep navy-black `#06121C`, `#0A1A26` fields traversed by luminous linear cyan `#17E1E1` and violet circuit lines. Even here the neon is *orderly*: straight grids, framed panels, symmetric corridors, never chaotic signage soup.

Rule of thumb: saturated colour occupies a minority of the frame and always reads as emitted light (screens, holograms, light strips) or as deliberate fashion choice; the environment itself stays white/pale-blue. A monochrome white-on-white architectural study and a neon-on-black cyberspace corridor are both valid Cyberprep if the cyan linearity and utopian cleanliness hold.

### Signature motifs and subjects (ranked by textual authority)

1. **Cityscapes** — the top-listed motif. Utopian skylines of white spires, curved parametric towers, glass lattice façades, terraced greenery, elevated monorails and flying vehicles gliding between buildings in clear daylight.
2. **Technology as benign infrastructure** — holographic advertisement panels and UI floats, glowing floor and stair edges, cove-lit ceilings, transit hubs, smart interiors; technology is furniture, not threat.
3. **Cyberspace** — grid corridors, circuit-line tunnels, luminous data geometry (the Tron strand of the canon); abstract but navigable space, usually symmetric and one-point-perspective.
4. **Robotics and body modification** — companion robots and machine animals, prosthetic limbs and bionic hands worn casually as everyday fact, chrome-and-white robot chassis with exposed clean mechanics.
5. **Fashion** (text-documented, image-thin): women in leather jackets and mini dresses displaying their own brain patterns, shiny chrome pantsuits; men in Hussar jackets in the Adam Ant manner and coats with circuitry embedded like artistic embroidery. New Wave and Y2K Futurism silhouettes, preppy cleanliness.
6. **Leisure and nightlife** — synth clubs with hologram light shows, synthpop stages; sport and self-improvement scenes; sunlit domestic interiors with pianos, cats, hexagonal windows overlooking the city.

### Materials and textures

Glossy white polymer and lacquer, brushed aluminium and chrome, structural glass and steel lattice, translucent acrylic and holographic film, soft matte plaster on sweeping parametric curves, pale terrazzo or seamless resin floors. Surfaces are clean, reflective and new: reflections are crisp, highlights are soft and broad, there is no rust, no dust, no water staining. Textural interest comes from geometry — mullions, trusses, ribbed ceilings, circuit traces — not from wear.

### Composition and lighting

- **Composition:** wide panoramic establishing shots of skyline or plaza; one-point-perspective corridors, tunnels and transit interiors; airy negative space with architecture occupying clean diagonals or horizontals; single hero figure paired with a machine companion; figures often small against civic scale, or isolated in portrait against luminous interiors. Horizontals and verticals stay true; curves are architectural sweeps, not organic squish.
- **Lighting:** default is high-key diffuse daylight with pale or blown-out sky and soft global bounce; interiors lit by large windows plus emissive strips and cove light; dusk/"blue hour" variants bathe interiors in cool ambient; night and cyberspace variants substitute orderly neon grids and hologram glow for daylight. Shadows are soft or absent; contrast is moderate even in dark mode.
- **Medium and finish:** polished digital painting and clean concept art, glossy 3D renders, bright editorial-style photography. Smooth gradients, crisp edges, minimal noise or grain; the hand of the artist shows in confident clean linework, not in texture overlays.

## Mood & Values

Optimistic Futurism and High Tech/High Life: the feeling is confidence, leisure, civic pride and play. Bodies are athletic and modified by choice; machines are companions and public services; advertising is colourful but friendly; public space is generous, safe and sunlit. There is menace nowhere — no surveillance dread, no corporate looming, no decay. The emotional register is closer to a synthpop chorus or a world's fair pavilion than to a noir thriller: aspirational, sporty, slightly glossy, genuinely cheerful about tomorrow.

## Era & Origins

Core ideas date to the 1960s — Stanisław Lem's *Return from the Stars* (1961) is the founding text, alongside the era's pop futurism of *The Jetsons* (1962) and Star Trek (1965) — while the term and self-aware aesthetic consolidated in the 2000s as "post-cyberpunk," fed by writers like Bruce Sterling and Cory Doctorow and by the visual refresh of the Tron franchise and contemporary parametric architecture. Cyberprep is therefore a reaction formation: it takes cyberpunk's toolkit (cybernetics, megacities, cyberspace) and refuses its dystopia, and it inherits the mid-century space-age optimism that Y2K Futurism and Frutiger Aero later recycled. Its music culture — synthpop, electronic rock, synthwave (Missing Persons, Electric Youth, Perfume, Lazerhawk) — anchors the mood in bright analog-synth nostalgia rather than industrial techno.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Build utopia first: clean air, orderly public space, leisure time, trustworthy technology; then dress it in cyberpunk's hardware vocabulary.
2. Let white and light blue own the frame; spend saturated cyan/magenta only as emitted light or fashion.
3. Keep surfaces new: gloss, chrome, glass, seamless plaster; geometry supplies detail, wear never does.
4. Light generously: high-key daylight or orderly neon; soft shadows, luminous interiors, pale skies.
5. Show technology in service of people: prosthetics worn casually, robots as companions, holograms as public amenity, transit as pleasure.
6. Finish clean: smooth digital paint, crisp render, or bright photography; no grime overlays, no heavy grain.

**Variation axes (what may change).**
- *Subject:* skyline, transit hub, domestic interior, synth club, cyberspace grid, fashion portrait, sport scene with modified bodies, robot-companion vignette, space/orbital vista, holographic advertising wall.
- *Composition:* panorama, one-point corridor, architectural close study of lattice or curve, figure-plus-machine portrait, collage/moodboard of interiors.
- *Lighting:* blown-out daylight, soft blue-hour interior, dark cyberspace neon-grid mode, club hologram glow.
- *Color handling:* strict white/ice monochrome; white-blue with pastel mint/coral interiors; white-blue with orchid fashion accents; licensed dark navy with cyan circuit lines.
- *Mood:* serene civic, sporty playful, club-energy, contemplative domestic.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette dominance, clean materials, optimistic register — not any reference image. A white parametric stair lit by cyan step-glow, a chrome-pantsuit portrait against a pale lattice, and a black cyberspace corridor of cyan grids are all equally Cyberprep despite sharing no subject at all.

**Avoid (style-breakers).**
- Rain-slicked noir streets, fog, grime, rust, puddles, decay, poverty, crowds in misery — the cyberpunk dystopia package.
- Ominous megacorp branding, propaganda screens, surveillance dread, weaponized hardware.
- Wet, fleshy body horror: grafted wetware, exposed organic-mechanical fusion, lab gore (that is Biopunk).
- Chaotic maximalist street kitsch, candy-saturated clutter, sticker-bomb graphics (Cyberpop).
- Twee craftiness: handmade clutter, cozy vintage cute props, scrapbook pastels (Cybertwee).
- Pure non-representational geometry with no lived world, figures or architecture-in-use (Abstract Tech).
- Earthy/sepia/organic palettes, heavy film grain, desaturated dystopian grading, gothic or steampunk ornament, brass and leather Victorianism.
- Frutiger Aero's water bubbles, fish, tropical gloss as primary subject — Cyberprep borrows the optimism but keeps circuits, grids and cities.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Cyberpunk:** same hardware, opposite society. Cyberpunk = night, rain, grime, neon-on-black chaos, high tech/low life, corporate oppression. Cyberprep = daylight or orderly neon, white/pale-blue fields, clean surfaces, high tech/high life, leisure and sport. Check the ground and the sky: wet asphalt and smog say cyberpunk; pale sky and seamless resin say Cyberprep.
- **Vs Abstract Tech:** Abstract Tech is non-objective — pure geometric, circuit or interface abstraction with no narrative world. Cyberprep always implies inhabitation: cities, rooms, bodies, robots in use.
- **Vs Biopunk:** Biopunk centres biological engineering — flesh, wet labs, genetic modification, organic grotesquerie, green-brown-flesh palettes. Cyberprep's modifications are clean prosthetics and chrome robotics; its palette is white/blue; its tone is consent and sport, not bodily dread.
- **Vs Cyberpop:** Cyberpop keeps cyberpunk's street-level chaos but recolors it in saturated candy pop with loud graphics and kitsch maximalism. Cyberprep is preppy restraint: fewer hues, more white, orderly composition, architectural calm.
- **Vs Cybertwee:** Cybertwee fuses tech with twee cozy-cute — soft handmade clutter, whimsical illustration, domestic craftiness. Cyberprep's softness is architectural (sweeps of plaster, pale light), its figures athletic and stylish, its finish polished digital paint or render, never craft-scrapbook cute.
- **Border notes:** overlap with Y2K Futurism, Frutiger Aero/Eco and Solarpunk is real and intentional; distinguish by Cyberprep's retained cyberpunk hardware — grids, cyberspace, prosthetics, chrome robotics — which those greener or glossier utopians downplay.
