# Roaring Twenties (Jazz Age, Années Folles)

The Roaring Twenties is the visual culture of the 1920s Western world — above all the USA, France and Germany — in the decade between the armistice and the crash: a period that dressed its rejection of Victorian restraint in beads, chrome and brass. Its images fall into two great registers that share one grammar: grainy silver-gelatin and sepia photographs of actual social life (ballrooms, cabarets, streets, chorus lines, motor cars), and flat Art Deco graphic art (magazine covers, posters, hand-tinted postcards) in vermilion, midnight blue and gold. Both registers celebrate the same values — modernity, excess, liberation, optimism, faith in machines — and both lean on the same silhouettes: the loose, knee-hemmed, drop-waisted woman in a cloche, and the stiff-collared, hatted man.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Two coherent registers plus a bridge, governed by the style's key colours: black, gold, silver and deep jewel tones.

| Register | Ground | Accents | Hex range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photographic monochrome | silver-gelatin black-and-white; warm sepia toning | none, or faint hand-tint blush | ink #0A0A0C–#232326; gelatin grey #B9B9B9; paper white #EDEAE4; sepia #6B4A2F–#A8845C |
| Deco graphic | midnight blue or vermilion field | gold line, cream flesh, black silhouette | midnight #101A3A–#1B2A52; vermilion/coral #D8462B–#E0533F; gold #C9A227–#E3C567; ivory #F1E6CE |
| Jewel interior (incl. modern cinematic readings) | black lacquer, dark wood, cream plaster | metal plus one or two jewels | gold #C9A227; silver #C4C7C9; emerald #0B6E4F; sapphire #0F3D7C; ruby #8E1B2E |

Rule: monochrome is not an absence of palette here, it is the period's photographic truth; whenever colour appears it arrives either as metal-plus-jewel on black, or as flat poster pigment. True period colour photography is essentially absent from the record — colour enters through graphics, hand-tinting, and later retrospective cinematography (champagne, cream and rose interiors with jewel accents), which is a legitimate modern register of the style. Pastel sweetness, earthy boho tones and saturated modern party colours sit outside the range.

### Signature motifs (ranked as the text documents them)

1. **The flapper silhouette.** The era's keynote: loose, uncorseted dresses with dropped waists and knee-length hemlines baring arms and legs; cloche hats, bobbed or finger-waved hair, bandeau headbands, long pearl and bead strands, feather boas, fur-trimmed coats, T-strap heels. Poses are kinetic — arms raised, knees kicking, mid-Charleston — never the stiff frontality of earlier decades.
2. **Art Deco surfaces and graphics.** Strong geometric form: sunbursts, zigzags, chevrons, stepped setbacks, stylized florals; bold colour and exoticizing ornament on posters, covers, club interiors and architecture.
3. **Jazz and dance.** Brass bands, drum kits, banjos, saxophones; chorus lines and kick rows; couples mid-dance, sometimes motion-blurred; cabaret stage energy.
4. **Machine-age emblems: automobile and radio.** Mass-produced cars and cabinet radios are the decade's household totems — early motor cars with wood-spoke or wire wheels, brass lamps, upright radiators; furniture-sized radio cabinets with fabric grilles.
5. **Party-excess props.** Balloons overhead, paper streamers and garlands strung from ceilings, confetti, champagne buckets and coupes, small lamps on cloth-covered supper tables.
6. **Nightlife interiors.** Speakeasies, cabarets and ballrooms: crowded small tables, tuxedoed waiters, proscenium arches, candelabra, hanging decoration — the Prohibition undercurrent of illicit pleasure.
7. **The city canyon.** Tall loft blocks and vertical blade signs, street-level crowds in hats, lit theatre streets at night.
8. **Formal menswear and the gangster edge.** Three-piece suits, detachable club collars, bow ties, bowlers, fedoras, boaters, top hats, overcoats and canes; occasionally the crime-boss overlay (pinstripe, chauffeured car) that Prohibition bred — present in the record but secondary.

### Materials and textures
Beadwork and sequins catching flash as scattered glints; silk chiffon and velvet; ostrich feather and marabou softness; fur collars; pearl strands; lacquered wood and brass of radio cabinets and car fittings; chrome and nickel plate; paper streamers and balloon rubber; gelatin-silver grain and print speckle; lithographic dot and flat gouache in graphics.

### Composition and lighting tendencies
- **Ensemble tableau:** a dense crowd arranged across the frame with one elevated or central figure (on a table, a stage, a car seat).
- **Horizontal repetition:** rows of kicking legs, seated chorus girls, lined dancers — rhythm as composition.
- **Vertical urbanism:** street canyons receding to a vanishing point; vertical signage.
- **Flat poster space:** unmodulated colour planes, elongated elegant silhouettes, star-pricked night skies, minimal depth.
- **Lighting:** bright, even, slightly glaring studio or flash light in photographs; open daylight on streets; warm lamplight and footlight glow in clubs; no modelled chiaroscuro in graphics, where colour carries form.
- **Motion as truth:** permitted blur on dancers and spinning limbs; stillness reserved for fashion plates and machine portraits.

## Mood & Values
Exuberance with a spine of defiance. The style broadcasts optimism, prosperity, liberation and social rebellion — youth dancing on the grave of Victorian propriety — plus sincere worship of technological innovation. Under the balloons runs a faint illicit current: speakeasy doors, drawn curtains, knowing glances. And behind everything an unspoken expiry date: this is brilliance lived fast before the 1929 crash, so even its wistfulness wears sequins.

## Era & Origins
The 1920s in the Western world — USA, France and Germany above all, with emblematic scenes in Berlin, Chicago, London, Los Angeles, New York, Paris, Shanghai and Sydney; the *années folles* in France. It emerges directly from post-WWI prosperity and mass production, and defines itself against Victorian and Edwardian restraint: shorter hemlines, looser cuts, bared arms and legs, bobbed hair. Jazz is its soundtrack, Art Deco its design language, the flapper its poster child. It ends abruptly with the Wall Street Crash of 1929.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Anchor the decade first: every object, hemline, hat and machine must be plausibly 1920s Western.
2. Silhouette before scene: drop waist, knee hem, cloche and bob; stiff collar, bow tie, brimmed hat. A wrong silhouette cannot be rescued by props.
3. Choose one colour register and stay in it: silver-gelatin/sepia photograph; flat Deco graphic in vermilion/midnight/gold; jewel-on-black interior; or a modern cinematic reading in champagne, cream and rose with jewel accents.
4. Populate with period machines and props: radio cabinet, early automobile, telephone; balloons, streamers, confetti, champagne.
5. Put energy in the body: kicks, raised arms, blurred turns, crowd surge. Reserve static symmetry for fashion plates and machine portraits.
6. Let geometry breathe in the background: sunbursts, zigzags, stepped forms, arched club interiors, vertical signs.
7. Light per register: flash glare or daylight for photographic looks; lamplight and footlight for clubs; flat pigment for graphics.
8. Excess is a value: crowd, clutter, glitter. Empty minimal rooms are off-style unless the image is a Deco poster plane.

Variation axes (allowed range):
- **Subject:** ballroom party, cabaret or speakeasy supper, chorus rehearsal, jazz band, street crowd, fashion portrait, couple portrait, middle-class parlour with radio, automobile showcase, city night scene, magazine cover, liner or train departure, seaside crowd.
- **Composition:** dense ensemble tableau; single full-length figure; repeated-row rhythm; vertical canyon recession; flat poster plane with two or three silhouettes.
- **Lighting:** flash glare, overcast daylight, club lamplight, stage footlight, flat unmodulated poster colour.
- **Colour handling:** pure monochrome, sepia, restrained hand-tint, full Deco poster colour, jewel-tone interior, modern cinematic champagne warmth.
- **Mood:** euphoric, intimate, rebellious, glamorous, faintly wistful pre-crash.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — silhouette, palette register, geometry, energy — not any reference image.

AVOID (style-breakers):
- Post-1929 Depression iconography: breadlines, dust, patched clothing, hobo camps.
- Victorian/Edwardian bodies: corseted S-bend, floor-length bustled trains, high lace collars, parasols, Gibson-girl updos.
- Retrofuturism: chrome robots, ray guns, alternate-history airships (that is Decopunk).
- Diesel-age grit: 1930s–40s streamlined locomotives, military hardware, soot and smog, utilitarian workwear, hardboiled noir chiaroscuro (Dieselpunk).
- Swing-era styling: zoot suits, victory rolls, smooth big-band ballroom polish.
- Neon as dominant signage light; plastics, LEDs, digital screens, modern vehicles, contemporary makeup or loose modern hairstyles.
- Boho misreadings: suede fringe, turquoise jewellery, flower crowns in place of beaded fringe, pearls and bandeau headbands.
- Clean minimalist modern interiors and saturated electric pink or teal party colour.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Separating tell |
|---|---|
| Art Deco | Deco is the design language alone — geometry, chrome, lacquer, sunbursts — applicable to empty interiors and objects of any decade from the '20s into the '30s. Roaring Twenties always adds lived social content: flappers, jazz, parties, cars. Deco without people is still Deco; the Roaring Twenties frame is Deco plus the crowd. |
| Decopunk | Speculative retro-future with anachronistic robots, ray guns and gleaming alternate technology. Roaring Twenties keeps strictly period-plausible machines: radios, early cars, telephones. |
| Dieselpunk | 1930s–40s diesel grit, war machinery, noir smog, desaturated brown utility. Roaring Twenties is pre-crash sparkle: formalwear, glitter, celebration, brighter contrast. |
| Edwardian | Pre-WWI restraint: corseted waists, floor-length hems, high collars, parasols, muted tea-gown pastels, stiff poses. Roaring Twenties inverts each: dropped waist, knee hem, bare arms, bob, kinetic pose. |
| Flapper | A subset focused on the feminine figure and her wardrobe alone. A lone beaded, bobbed girl reads Flapper first; add the ensemble — stiff-collared men, brass band, cabaret tables, motor car — and the frame becomes Roaring Twenties. |

Adjacent currents the style may absorb in small doses: Weimar cabaret's darker theatricality, garçonne's tailored androgyny, the mafia aesthetic's pinstripe menace. The core remains the bright, beaded, machine-loving party of the Jazz Age.
