# Furry

Furry is the visual culture of the furry fandom: a subculture organized around anthropomorphic animals — animals with human posture, gesture and expression — experienced mainly through personal characters called fursonas, rendered in art or worn as custom-built costumes called fursuits. Because fursonas descend indirectly from cartoon animation, the style's center of gravity is bright, happy color, often pastel, with a tolerated legacy of deliberately kitschy clashing combinations. The style is craft-centered and community-centered: a fursuit is a handmade object (foam, faux fur, sewn pattern pieces, mesh eyes), and much of the style's visual documentation is casual photography of these objects at conventions, at home, and outdoors, posed with friendly hand gestures. While photographic suit documentation dominates the surviving visual record, the style equally includes 2D toony digital artwork, and niche historical branches such as early-2000s glamfur and the multi-colored sparklefur character-design genre.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

Build color in two layers. A **base layer** of plush white or cream (`#F5F5F5`–`#FFF8E7`), grey (`#8A8A8A`), black (`#1A1A1A`), or a naturalistic tan-to-brown animal coat (`#D2B48C`–`#8B5A2B`). Over it, an **accent layer** of one to three hues drawn from either a pastel band — pink `#FFD9E6`, mint `#B8F2D8`, lavender `#CDB4F6`, butter yellow `#FFF3B0`, baby blue `#AEC6CF` — or a neon band — hot pink `#FF1493`, electric blue `#00BFFF`, lime `#BFFF00`, purple `#9D4EDD`, orange `#FF8C00`. Accents land on muzzle, inner ears, hair, paw pads, belly markings and stripes. Two sanctioned extremes: the **kitsch legacy** of pre-2010 fursonas (neutral grey body, neon blue snout and eyes, bright pink stripes — clashing on purpose), and **sparklefur**, which stacks three or more saturated unnatural hues plus complex markings, gems and star accessories on one character. Colors unnatural for the species are the default, not the exception; fully naturalistic coats appear mainly on realistic-register suits.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **The anthropomorphic animal character** itself: upright, gesticulating, expressing emotion through an animal head.
2. **The fursuit head**, in one of the documented registers: toony (large exaggerated features, plastic mesh eyes, fabric teeth and tongue, plush nose, rounded padded silhouette), semi-realistic, realistic (small painted glass cabochon eyes, airbrushed color blending), kemono (detailed anime irises printed on perforated material or tint film, chibi-leaning proportions), furkig (smooth 3D-printed mask base, zentai body, synthetic-hair wig, elaborate clothing), or synth/protogen (hard-shell head with glossy visor and glowing LED elements).
3. **Paw gloves and paw shoes** with contrasting minky or embroidered paw pads.
4. **Tails, ear headbands and partial suits** worn over ordinary human clothing (hoodies, shorts, skirts) — the "poodling" look.
5. **Markings**: pieced fur sewing patterns, airbrushed gradients, and appliquéd stars, hearts, gems, and paint-drip shapes.
6. **Convention material culture**: lanyards, badges, pins, buttons, character-print shirts and backpacks; dense group photos of dozens of suiters.
7. **Kigurumi** animal onesies as the accessible entry tier.
8. **Digitigrade sculpted legs** and plush stuffed-toy body padding.
9. **Electronics and mechanics**: lights, speakers, magnetized eyebrows and eyelids.
10. **Soft props**: flower crowns, bouquets, wings, string lights in the background.

### Materials and textures

Short- and long-pile faux fur; foam-sculpted, fleece-covered head bases; plastic mesh eye windows; fabric teeth and tongues; plush noses; minky paw pads; 3D-printed resin masks; synthetic wig fiber; zentai spandex; airbrushed dye on fur; embroidered felt appliqués; glossy acrylic visors. Surfaces should read sewn, sculpted and padded — handmade, not molded by a studio.

### Recurring subjects and composition

Canids (wolves, foxes, dogs), felids, rabbits, deer and antlered creatures, reptiles and dragons with frills and wings, birds, and fictional synth species; occasionally anthropomorphized objects. Human bystanders in casual clothes frequently share the frame, giving scale and everyday context. Composition is snapshot vernacular: arm's-length selfies, friend-taken full-body portraits, bust crops, and densely packed convention group shots. Lighting is flat and honest — ambient hotel and convention-hall light, on-camera flash, overcast daylight, bedroom string lights — against carpets, draped hall walls, poster-covered bedrooms, porches, lawns and park paths. Poses are warm and performative: paw waves, peace signs, heart hands, head tilts, group hugs.

## Mood & Values

Positivity, unwavering acceptance, sexual diversity, cheerfulness, frivolity. The style celebrates the fursona as a joyful self-portrait and the suit as a pride object of DIY craft. Warmth toward the camera is constant; even dark-based suits pose playfully. Community presence — crowds, matching gestures, shared backdrops — expresses the fandom's collectivist, online-native, convention-built identity.

## Era & Origins

Roots in the 1970s underground comix movement and the 1976 Vootie cartoonists' association; the term itself emerged from a 1980 science-fiction convention discussion, and the subculture consolidated through the 1980s. Cartoon film lineage (Robin Hood 1973 through Zootopia 2016) keeps the palette toony. The early-2000s glamfur trend on DeviantART, FurAffinity and LiveJournal added a goth/emo-inflected niche, while netlabels and EDM microgenres (breakcore, speedcore, happy hardcore) supplied its sound. Japanese kemono runs parallel with anime-derived eye work.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** (1) Character first: an anthropomorphic animal with human posture and readable expression is the subject; setting stays casual and secondary. (2) Pick one suit register — toony, semi-realistic, realistic, kemono, furkig, or synth/visor — and keep its eye type, proportions and surface logic internally consistent. (3) Keep the craft read: sewn fur seams, foam contours, plush padding; never seamless creature-FX. (4) Apply the two-layer palette rule; unnatural species color is welcome, and high cheerful values are the default outside the realistic register. (5) Use the friendly gesture vocabulary. (6) In photographic mode, keep snapshot honesty: flat light, real cluttered backgrounds, amateur warmth. In illustrative mode, keep the cartoon lineage.

**Variation axes.** Species (any animal, fictional species, or anthropomorphized object); register (the six above); coverage (head-only partial with human clothes, 3/4 suit, fullsuit, plush padded suit, quadsuit, kigurumi); setting (convention hall, hotel room, bedroom, backyard, park, street, plain studio backdrop); palette point (pastel, neon, kitsch clash, sparklefur multi-hue, naturalistic, black base with neon drips); medium (suit photography, 2D toony digital art, paper-craft head); mood (cheerful default, serene pastoral, rave-adjacent energy). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Erotic posing or sexualized framing. Corporate mascot cues: sponsor logos, brand lettering, stadium-mascot styling — fursuits declare no affiliation. Non-anthropomorphic wildlife in natural posture (reads as nature photography). A human face with only cat-ear headbands as the sole signal (that is Neko cosplay; furry's center of gravity is the animal head or mask). Dominant blood, wounds or medical imagery (that is Pastel Gore; glamfur's gore is a historical niche, not the default). Pup hoods, harnesses, leashes and latex (Puppygirl). Toy-clutter still lifes with no anthro character (Kidcore). Human scene/emo fashion — side-swept fringe, band tees, skinny jeans, raccoon-tail keychains — with no animal head (Scene). Desaturated cinematic grades, volumetric mood lighting, prestige film grain. Hyperreal CGI creatures with wet noses and individually groomed hairs.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Puppygirl:** human body in kink-adjacent gear — neoprene or leather pup hood, harness, leash; skin and clothing remain visible and the palette is black/red hardware. Furry covers the head with a sculpted, fur-covered animal head and builds the body in plush textile, in cheerful pastels or neons.
- **Kidcore:** candy palette and toy clutter without an anthropomorphic animal protagonist. The fursona character is the divider; overlap exists only in shared color brightness.
- **Neko:** human face and skin retained, with cat ears and tail added over anime cosplay fashion. Furry replaces the face with a muzzle, mesh or printed eyes, and a non-human head silhouette.
- **Pastel Gore:** pastel palette weaponized with blood, gore and medical motifs. Furry's pastel serves cheerfulness; injury imagery as the dominant read exits the style.
- **Scene:** human emo/scene fashion — black skinny jeans, band tees, neon-streaked side-swept hair, raccoon-tail accessories. Furry's neon-on-black appears on fur and foam with an animal head; the presence of a sculpted animal head versus a human face with styled hair is the decisive cue.
