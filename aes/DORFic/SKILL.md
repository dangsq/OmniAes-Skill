# DORFic

DORFic — the name is an acronym for **D**aylight, **O**range, **R**ed, **F**uturism and **Graphic** ("fic"), coined in 2023 to label a style that flourished from the mid-to-late 2000s to the mid-2010s, also circulating under the name PolySunk — is the industrial, minimalist wing of the Frutiger Aero family. Where Frutiger Aero puts grass, water and sky inside the glossy computer, DORFic puts offices, factories, atriums and dashboards there: stark white cityscapes and interiors accented with bright orange and grey, rendered with a sterile, corporate confidence. It lives in video games, console and office-software branding, feature-phone advertising, corporate interior design and 3D motion graphics. Its surface mood is clean optimism; its stated value, quietly, is rebellion against corrupt corporations — the sanitized shell is the stage on which runners, test subjects and whistleblowers act.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette rule is a white field charged by warm structure: orange is not decoration, it is architecture.

| Role | Hues | Working hex range |
|---|---|---|
| Primary field | clinical white, off-white | `#FFFFFF` – `#F2F2F2` |
| Primary structure | bright saturated orange | `#FF6A00` – `#F7941E` |
| Primary warm | yellow, amber, gold | `#FFC20E` – `#FFB000` |
| Accent | deep red, crimson, scarlet | `#C1121F` – `#E63329` |
| Neutral | grey, silver | `#8C8C8C` – `#D9D9D9` |
| Accent (small dose) | cyan-blue, steel blue | `#00AEEF`, `#3E6FA3` |
| Dark-mode field | near-black | `#0A0A0A` – `#141414` |

Allowed handling: yellow may warm toward gold in sunlit hall interiors; red may carry whole low-poly shard compositions; blue appears only in small cool doses (a visor glow, a badge, a shirt under an orange graphic panel). In the **dark alternate version**, black replaces white as the field and orange survives as emissive wall panels, strips and screen glows — still clean, still geometric, never rainy or neon-cluttered.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Glass and daylight**: curtain walls, glazed panels, sun flare bleeding through architecture, glossy mirror-reflective floors, emissive ceiling discs.
2. **Tech patterns**: halftone dot fields, hexagon clusters, circuit-like stripe runs, arrow glyphs, layered vector swooshes and glossy orbs used as overlays or as standalone graphic backgrounds.
3. **Office and corporate space**: reception desks, conference rooms, cafeterias, congress-centre halls, bank offices — sterile, sparse, sharply dressed.
4. **Geometry**: parallel lines, symmetric one-point perspectives, low-polygon facets, explosive shard bursts communicating motion.
5. **Industrialism and robotics**: factory iconography, sterile scientific gear, small robots and product-rendered devices.
6. **Dress**: near-monochrome techwear and business casual — jumpsuits, goggles or visors, gloves, protective gear, ties and shirts rendered crisp and casual-sharp.
7. **White cityscapes**: minimalist skylines and atriums seen through glass or in flight.
8. **Occasional**: military iconography balanced against scientific sterility; the dark black-field variant.

### Materials and textures

Glossy polished floor slabs, frosted and clear glass, molded matte plastic, brushed aluminium and silver metal, lacquered wall panels, translucent synthetic fabric. Graphic layers add vector gradients, halftone dots and skeuomorphic glossy buttons. Everything is immaculate: no dust, no scratches, no wear, no fingerprints. Surfaces reflect; they never absorb grime.

### Recurring subjects

Office and congress interiors; corporate UI dashboards, banners, stickers and business cards; product shots of phones, consoles, chairs and devices on neutral gradients; athletes and runners mid-leap in white atriums; stylized robots and streamlined vehicles; pure abstract vector fields of orange waves; fashion-catalog figures in translucent white and orange gear.

### Composition and lighting

Two compositional poles. **Static-symmetric**: one-point perspective corridors and atriums, parallel repeating panels, centered product or interior views, flat graphic layouts with a white negative-space corner. **Kinetic-diagonal**: off-balance figures in motion, shard and fragment bursts around a subject, low-poly shattered silhouettes, isometric panel collages. Lighting is high-key and even: daylight bloom, sun flare, shadowless studio illumination, ceiling-light arrays; reflections double every orange form in the floor. The dark variant swaps daylight for self-luminous orange panels in black rooms.

## Mood & Values

Cleanliness as virtue, efficiency as beauty, corporate confidence as atmosphere. The mood is crisp, kinetic and optimistic — "sharp, clean, casual" — with an undercurrent of dissent: DORFic's declared value is rebellion against corrupt corporations, so its sterile labs and offices are always potentially the environment someone sprints through, escapes, or exposes. Sterile sublime with a pulse.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in the 2000s, prominent from the mid/late 2000s until the mid-2010s (2016 being its last year of significant usage). Predecessors include 1960s Retrofuturism and Atompunk plus late-1990s mobile-technology branding and street-art graphics. Its natural habitats were video games and corporate design: office suites, console live services, feature-phone Walkman lines, antivirus logos, fitness branding, bank offices and congress-centre interiors. It reacts against the nature-romance of its Frutiger Aero parent by insisting on industry, and against cyberpunk's grime by insisting on sanitation.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. White first. The field is clinical white (or black in dark mode); orange organizes space as panels, lattices, furniture fronts, UI blocks — never as an afterthought sprinkle.
2. Build with geometry: parallel repetition, symmetry, hexagons, low-poly facets; use shard bursts and diagonals only to speak motion.
3. Light it with daylight: bloom, flare, glossy floor reflections, shadowless even fill. Dark mode means emissive orange in black, not neon signage.
4. Dress figures near-monochrome in techwear or business casual; add visors, gloves, protective gear; pose them athletically or with sterile poise.
5. Allow a graphic layer: halftone dots, vector swooshes, glossy orbs, arrow glyphs — as overlay or as the whole image.
6. Keep it immaculate and sparse. Fewer objects, cleaner surfaces, more air.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- *Subject*: interiors, cityscapes, UI/graphic backgrounds, product renders, robots, vehicles, fashion figures, abstract vector fields, posters.
- *Composition*: symmetric one-point, diagonal kinetic, flat graphic layout, isometric collage, centered product view.
- *Lighting*: high-key daylight, shadowless studio, golden sunlit hall, dark emissive-panel mode.
- *Color handling*: orange-dominant on white; yellow-gold warm mode; red-heavy low-poly mode; grey-silver structural mode; black-field dark mode.
- *Mood*: corporate optimism, kinetic play, cool professionalism, quiet dissent.

**Avoid**

- Nature romance: grass, trees, water droplets, bubbles, auroras, fish, blue-sky meadows (that is Frutiger Aero).
- Grime, rain, rust, wear, clutter, dense neon signage, night-street chaos (Cyberpunk).
- Liquid chrome blobs, iridescent metal, chrome typography, rave gear (Y2K Futurism).
- Blue or green as dominant hue; blue is a small accent only.
- Black-dominant imagery without the orange emissive-panel logic (drifts into Dark Aero).
- Handmade texture: paper grain, brushstrokes, wood, craft ornament, vintage film grain.
- Pastel palettes, magenta-teal cinematic grading, holographic iridescence, maximalist clutter, historical costume.

## Discrimination Cues

- **vs Abstract Tech**: Abstract Tech is pure glossy vector graphics — swooshes, orbs, waves — with no built space. DORFic absorbs those graphics but grounds them in architecture, daylight, offices and bodies; if there is a white corridor, a runner or a dashboard, it is DORFic.
- **vs Y2K Futurism**: Y2K leans liquid chrome, iridescence, blobby organic metal and club darkness. DORFic is matte-white, hard-edged, geometric, daylight-lit; its curves are vector waves, not molten metal.
- **vs Cyberpunk**: Cyberpunk is night, rain, grime, neon advertising density and street multiculture. DORFic is day, dry, sanitized, sparse and corporate; even its dark variant glows with orderly orange panels rather than neon clutter.
- **vs Dark Aero**: Dark Aero lives permanently in black glossy shard-space with ominous mood. DORFic's black mode is a variant, not the default; the style's center of gravity is white daylight with orange, and its dark rooms keep industrial architecture and orange emission.
- **vs Frutiger Aero**: Frutiger Aero fuses gloss with nature — sky, water, plants, bubbles — and favors blue-green. DORFic deletes nature entirely: orange-on-white industry, offices and geometry replace meadows and lagoons.
