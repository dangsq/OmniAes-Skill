# Tomboy

Tomboy names a girl or woman whose dress and pastimes are coded masculine: borrowed-fit clothes, broken-in sneakers, knees ripped out of jeans, a ball under one arm or a controller in hand. It is less a period look than an identity look — the wardrobe of doing things rather than being looked at doing them. The term is old (1533 for boisterous boys, applied to girls within half a century, fixed as a group label by the 1890s), but its visual language today runs through streetwear, sportswear, workwear and skater/grunge hand-me-downs, and it carries an explicit politics: feminism, rejection of gender norms, masculinity and androgyny as values, with a strong butch/stud lesbian lineage though never exclusive to it. Sampled imagery skews urban streetwear and sport; the written record widens the style considerably into rural and geek registers (cowboy boots, banjos, farming, taxidermy, comic shops, model kits), and both registers are legitimate.

## Visual Identity

### Palette
The rule is neutral dominance with at most one or two saturated accents, never a fully saturated scheme. Base: black `#101010–#1C1C1C`, heather and mid grey `#7D7D7D–#B9B9B9`, off-white `#EDEDEA–#F7F7F7`. Denim carries the mid-tones: light-wash `#A9BFD3–#C6D4E2`, mid indigo `#4A6076–#5E7A94`. Utility earth tones appear as camo or workwear olive `#4B5320`, `#6B705C`, `#8A7F5C`. Accents — red `#A6192E–#C8102E`, blue `#2E5AAC` and jersey light-blue `#7FA8D9`, purple `#5B3E8C`, orange `#D96A1F` — arrive as a single garment or graphic: a jersey, a cap, a band-tee print, a beanie. An outfit reading 70–90% neutral/denim with one loud piece is canonical; an outfit reading loud all over is not.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual documentation)
1. **Masculine-cut wardrobe on a feminine body** — the most heavily documented register: oversized shirts, boyfriend jeans, distressed and activity-torn denim, sneakers (Nike, Converse, Adidas), ankle/combat/cowboy boots, caps and father's hats, flannel, camo, tie-dye, leather and cowboy jackets, rock/metal band tees, skull belt buckles, fingerless gloves.
2. **Sport and physical play** — basketball, soccer, boxing, martial arts, weightlifting, skateboarding, surfing, rollerskating, gym sessions, climbing, hiking.
3. **Vehicles and outdoor work** — cars, trucks, jeeps, motorcycles, bicycles; fishing, rafting, yardwork, gardening, farming, barbecue, vehicle repair.
4. **Geek indoors** — video games (action, fighting, sports, RPG, battle-royale genres), comic books, action figures, plastic model kits, Lego, drawing, collecting.
5. **Instrument in hand** — electric or bass guitar, drums, keys; or country side: banjo, fiddle, accordion, harmonica.
6. **Hair and face** — messy buns, short cuts, undercuts, ponytails, natural texture; makeup from none at all to light (tinted balm, bronzer, mascara, defined brows).
7. **The feminine counter-accent** — golden hoop earrings, a thin chain: a deliberate spark of contrast inside the masculine grammar.
8. **LGBTQ+ cultural coding** — butch/stud presentation as lived identity, never as costume.

### Materials and textures
Brushed fleece and loopback cotton jersey; rigid denim broken soft with frayed threads at knee rips; wool flannel; matte leather; duck canvas in camo print; rubber cupsoles scuffed at the toe; metal hardware (belt buckles, hoops, zipper teeth); griptape grit, grass stains, sawdust, chain grease. Everything should look owned, not bought yesterday.

### Subjects, composition, lighting
Subjects are girls, women and androgynous people from childhood to adulthood, at ease or in motion. Recurring framings: full-body environmental candid on a rooftop stair, sidewalk or court; seated slouch with one knee drawn up; phone-in-hand mirror selfie; torso crop that documents an outfit; mid-action sport frame (dribble, ollie, swing); flat catalog-style shot of a single garment on a plain ground. Gazes run off-camera or level and unsmiling-at-the-lens; posture is sprawl, perch, lean — never contrapposto glamour. Light is available and honest: cool overcast diffuse daylight with shadowless whites; hard sun rendered with faded film emulation (lifted blacks, slight pink cast); gym fluorescent; window light; phone flash in a doorway mirror. Grain optional, gloss never.

## Mood & Values

Free-spirited, tough, independent, sometimes reserved. The style asserts competence and autonomy: clothes are tools for play, work and motion. Androgyny is the point of tension and the point of pride — a hoop earring in an undercut ear, a cropped fleece over a flat chest, lipstick none or matte and muted. Humor and play sit underneath (yo-yos, go-karts, treasure hunting); so does defiance of the expectation that girls perform prettiness.

## Era & Origins

Term coined 1533 for rude, boisterous boys; transferred to "bold or immodest" girls within fifty years; by the 1890s a group label entangled with nineteenth-century anxieties about women's health, marriage and motherhood. The twentieth and twenty-first centuries recast it through third-wave feminism and butch lesbian visibility. Visually it borrows from 90s skater and grunge streetwear, sportswear branding and American workwear (Carhartt, Dick's, Boot Barn sit in its supply chain; TomboyX, Wildfang, Kirrin Finch are its own vendors), but it is not locked to any decade.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- Dress in borrowed-masculine grammar: one size too big, layered (hoodie under jacket, flannel over band tee), hem frayed.
- Allow exactly one feminine accent — hoop earring, thin necklace, tinted lip — or none; never full glam.
- Show wear: fades, creases, scuffs, stains, pilled fleece.
- Prefer activity or unposed rest over posing: mid-dribble, crouched over an engine, sprawled with a controller, perched on a tailgate, slouched on metal stairs.
- Keep the palette neutral-dominant; spend saturation on at most one or two items.
- Cut the hair short, undercut, bunned or ponytailed; keep the face bare or lightly made.
- Set scenes in everyday terrain: court, skatepark, rooftop, garage, driveway, trailhead, bedroom with a console and comic stack, hardware-store aisle.
- Light with what exists: overcast sky, sun with film fade, practicals, phone flash.

Variation axes: **subject** — any documented activity, from boxing to banjo to bird-collecting; **composition** — environmental full-body, seated candid, mirror selfie, torso crop, action diptych, close portrait; **lighting** — cool overcast, faded hard sun, fluorescent, window, flash; **color handling** — monochrome black/grey, denim-plus-white, olive/camo-led, or accent-led over a neutral base; **mood** — quiet and reserved to grinning mid-sprint; **presentation** — child, teen or adult, softly feminine to fully androgynous body. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — borrowed-masculine wardrobe, visible wear, activity, neutral palette, unposed ease — not any reference image. A rural girl in cowboy boots fixing a fence and an urban teen in a cropped fleece on a rooftop are equally tomboy.

Avoid:
- Full glam makeup, lash sets, blowout curls, manicured nails as the focal point.
- Hyper-feminine garments: ruffles, sequins, bodycon dresses, stilettos, pastel soft-girl knit sets.
- Sexualized posing, lingerie framing, male-gaze camera angles.
- Crisp, tag-fresh clothing; luxury monogram logos; couture tailoring.
- Studio beauty lighting, seamless-backdrop glamour, heavy rim light (a flat catalog ground for a single garment is fine; glamour lighting is not).
- Decade-costume signals as the primary read: chokers, bucket hats, color-block windbreakers (that is 90s Cool).
- Rural party props: daisy dukes, beer cans, flag bikinis (Bro Hoe).
- Feminine clothing on a masculine-presenting body (the Femboy inversion).
- Pristine expedition gear and landscape-dominant frames (Adventurecore).
- Sleek editorial polish, tailored power silhouettes (Girl Crush).
- Concert-pit darkness, cigarettes, nihilist decay as the governing mood (Grunge).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Separating tell |
|---|---|
| Bro Hoe | Costumed, sexualized country-party femininity (daisy dukes, truck-as-prop, beer); tomboy wears workwear functionally, activity-first, unsexualized. |
| Femboy | Opposite gender direction: masculine body in feminine clothes, pastel and soft; tomboy is feminine/androgynous body in masculine clothes, neutral and worn. |
| Girl Crush | Polished feminine power: tailored blazers, sleek hair, makeup, editorial gloss; tomboy rejects polish outright — oversized, distressed, no-glam. |
| 90s Cool | Era-locked snapshot nostalgia: chokers, bucket hats, bright color-block, lo-fi film party frames; tomboy is identity and activity, era-transcendent, palette restrained. |
| Adventurecore | Landscape-first aspiration with pristine expedition gear and national-park vistas; tomboy is person-and-activity-first, often urban or suburban, gear scuffed and everyday. |

Adjacent siblings: Grunge shares flannel and distress but adds musical nihilism and darker decay; Skater shares boards and sneakers but centers board-shoe brand culture. Tomboy subsumes both as wardrobe sources while keeping the subject's lived, gendered self-possession at the center of the frame.
