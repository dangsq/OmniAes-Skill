# Cubism

Cubism is the early-twentieth-century Parisian revolution that stopped painting the world as the eye sees it from one chair and started painting it as the mind knows it from many positions at once. Picasso and Braque deconstructed objects and figures into interlocking geometric planes — cubes, cylinders, cones, spheres, inherited from Cézanne's late analysis of nature and reinforced by the conceptual, non-naturalistic forms of African and ancient Iberian art — and reassembled them so that front, profile, top and side coexist in a single shallow field. The movement runs in two great registers, Analytic (c. 1908–1912), a rigorous dissection of form in subdued monochrome, and Synthetic (c. 1912–1919), a building-up of images from simplified, brighter shapes and real pasted materials. Around this core orbit documented offshoots: Orphism's radiant color discs, Léger's Tubism of machined cylinders and cones, Crystal Cubism's large overlapping flat planes, Czech Cubism's crystalline slicing points, and Rondocubism's round forms (mostly architectural). Its conceptual premise — representation as knowledge rather than perception — made it one of the most consequential visual languages of the century, leaking into architecture and into literature's multiple perspectives and stream of consciousness.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette: registers, not a single hue

Color is not what unifies Cubism; faceting is. A correct image commits to one of the documented registers:

| Register | Hex range | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Analytic grisaille | #3E3226 umber, #6E5C43 sienna-gray, #8C7B5F greige, #A89A7C pale ochre, #B9B2A4 bone | Near-monochrome browns, grays, ochres; value does the work |
| Synthetic flat | #141210 contour black, #D8CDB8 paper cream, #C9A227 ochre, #A63A2E oxide red, #1E4D40 bottle green, #7A4E3A chestnut | Brighter, bolder, flatter patches; earth ground plus flat accents |
| Tubism metal | #5C6672 gunmetal, #9AA3AD steel, #C9CFD6 silver, #4E6E8E blue undertone | Léger's cylinders shaded like turned metal |
| Orphic spectral | #F2C14E yellow, #E4572E vermilion, #2A9D8F teal, #26509B cobalt, #E779C1 magenta, #F4A6A0 rose | Luminous concentric and overlapping discs |
| Czech crystal | #6B6A4F olive-gray, #9C8B57 ochre, #4F5B46 moss, #B7AE93 stone | Muted earth cut into sharp crystalline shards |
| Pop neo-Cubist echo | Any saturated flat plus heavy black contour | Late derivative line (Britto, Mariscal, Krasnyansky); optional register |

Rule: within Analytic and Czech registers, hue stays inside a narrow earth band and modulation is by value; within Synthetic and pop registers, color arrives as unmodulated flat patches; within Orphism, color itself is the light source. Mixing an earthy ground with a few flat Synthetic accents is canonical; rainbow gradients across a naturalistic form are not.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Fragmented objects**: subjects broken into interlocking geometric planes, facets meeting facet like a shattered mirror re-glued in new order.
2. **Simultaneous multiple viewpoints**: a face carrying frontal eyes on a profile skull; a glass drawn from above and from the side in the same object; several moments fused in one image.
3. **Geometric solids as grammar**: cubes, cylinders, cones, spheres; in the Tubist line, whole bodies built from tubes and cone-stumps.
4. **Muted Analytic palette**: the deliberate brown-gray-ochre monochrome that forces attention onto structure.
5. **Papier collé**: pasted newspaper clippings, wallpaper, faux-bois wood-grain paper, stenciled letters and numerals entering the picture as real matter.
6. **Synthetic flatness**: simplified large shapes, bold contour, poster-like plane interlock.
7. Offshoot marks: crystalline sharp points and slicing planes (Czech), concentric color discs (Orphism), ovals and rounds (Rondocubism, chiefly on buildings).

### Materials and textures

Matte oil glazes dragged thin over a grainy ground; soft charcoal-and-grisaille smudging where planes dissolve; in Synthetic work, the literal texture of pasted paper — torn or cut edges, printed type, wallpaper pattern, simulated wood grain — sitting against painted passages. The pop derivative register reads as flat screen-printed paint with crisp black contour. Absent: glossy varnish glare, thick impasto ridges, smooth airbrushed blending.

### Recurring subjects

Still life is the movement's laboratory: guitar, mandolin, violin, bottle, wine glass, pipe, bowl of fruit, folded newspaper, café table with cards. Figures follow: portraits, seated women, card players, dancers, nudes, costumed entertainers. Secondary documented subjects: faceted trees and landscape planes (Czech line), architectural facades and interiors, and — in Orphism — no object at all, only color discs. The subject list is a tendency, not a boundary; the syntax will cubize anything.

### Composition and space

Depth is compressed into a shallow box: no horizon line, no vanishing point, no receding floor. Planes overlap and interpenetrate, edges bleeding into neighbors (passage). Analytic compositions are all-over shard grids whose legibility peaks near the center and frays toward the edges; Synthetic compositions interlock a few large flat patches like a poster; collage fragments offset the grid with real-world rectangles. Vertical and horizontal formats both occur; the picture surface always asserts itself over the window illusion.

### Light and modeling

There is no single key light and no consistent cast shadow. Each facet owns its own value, so illumination seems to come from everywhere and nowhere; Analytic centers often glow faintly from within, like lamplight behind frosted glass. Tubist cylinders take a machined gradient highlight running along the tube; Orphic light is radiating color itself; Synthetic work is shadowless, objects reading as signs rather than solids.

## Mood & Values

Cerebral, contemplative, quietly radical: the pleasure of a puzzle solved by looking longer. Cubism values conceptual truth over retinal snapshot — analysis of form, rejection of single-point perspective, representation as knowledge assembled from many moments — and openly questions what reality a picture can hold. Synthetic Cubism adds café conviviality: newsprint, playing cards, wine glass, the tabletop life of Parisian studios. Orphism adds musical rhythm and luminous optimism; Czech Cubism adds tense, splintered inner energy, planes split to release what its practitioners called the soul's needs.

## Era & Origins

Paris, 1900s–1910s; created by Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque, the term coined by critic Louis Vauxcelles. Seeds: Cézanne's reduction of nature to cylinder, sphere and cone, and the conceptual forms of African and ancient Iberian art, fused in Picasso's 1907 *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*. Analytic phase c. 1908–1912; Synthetic phase c. 1912–1919, introducing papier collé. Offshoots: Orphism (named by Apollinaire, 1912), Tubism (Vauxcelles, 1911, for Léger), Crystal Cubism, Czech Cubism or Cubo-Expressionism, and 1920s Czechoslovak Rondocubism. Influence radiated into architecture's faceting, transparency and spatial ambiguity, and into literature's fragmentation of the individual (Joyce, Stein, Faulkner).

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Choose a concrete referent — instrument, bottle, glass, fruit, newspaper, seated figure, facade — then dissect it into planes; never paint it from one station point.
2. Fuse viewpoints in the same object: frontal and profile features together, top view and side view of one vessel.
3. Compress space: overlap planes, let edges interpenetrate, delete horizon and vanishing point, keep depth to a shallow box.
4. Commit to one register: Analytic earth monochrome with dense small facets; Synthetic flat bright patches with collage inserts and stenciled type; Tubist tubes; Czech crystalline shards; Orphic discs; or the late pop echo with black contour.
5. Light from within or from many directions: value per facet, no unified shadow; machined gradients only on tubes; radiant color only in Orphic mode.
6. Finish matte and painterly, or papery and pasted; keep plane edges crisp enough to read as cut surfaces.
7. Let legibility peak where you want the eye to enter, then fray or flatten outward.

Variation axes:

| Axis | Allowable range |
|---|---|
| Subject | Still life (instruments, bottles, glasses, pipes, fruit, newsprint, cards) → portraits and figures (card players, dancers, nudes) → trees, landscapes, facades → pure color discs |
| Composition | Dense all-over shard grid ↔ few large interlocking flat planes ↔ tubular cluster ↔ crystalline burst ↔ concentric disc field |
| Color | Earth monochrome ↔ earth ground with flat accents ↔ fully saturated flats ↔ spectral luminosity |
| Lighting | Internal diffuse glow ↔ shadowless sign-light ↔ machined tube gradients ↔ radiating color |
| Mood | Contemplative analysis ↔ café play ↔ musical radiance ↔ splintered revolt ↔ pop cheer |
| Era flavor | Historical 1908–1919 painterliness ↔ contemporary graphic neo-Cubism |

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A cubized bicycle, telephone, cat or skyline is fully in style if the syntax holds.

Avoid: single-point perspective or a consistent horizon; one key light with matching cast shadows; smooth naturalistic or photoreal modeling; motion blur, speed lines and repeated kinetic streaks; decorative symmetry, zigzags, chevrons and sunburst ornament; pure non-objective hard-edge geometry with diagrammatic industrial clarity; gestural emotional impasto and mood-driven color distortion; a single-viewpoint mosaic of visible brushstrokes that keeps coherent depth; airbrushed digital gradients and 3D-render gloss; unfragmented cartoon cuteness; consistent naturalistic anatomy and scale throughout.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Futurism** borrows Cubist faceting but spends it on motion: diagonal thrusts, repeated contours, blur and lines of force racing through space. Cubism is static analysis — facets anchor a still object known from many angles, never a body in transit.
- **Art Deco** smooths Cubist geometry into ornament: symmetrical zigzags, sunbursts, stepped sleekness, luxury finish. Cubism keeps space ambiguous and intellectual, decorative never being the point.
- **Constructivism** abandons the observed object for hard-edge abstraction, industrial materials and diagrammatic clarity in service of utility. Cubism always retains a referent — guitar, bottle, face — dissected with painterly or papery texture.
- **Expressionism** distorts to feel: visceral brushwork, strident mood-color, the subjective scream. Cubism distorts to know: controlled planes, cool analysis. Czech Cubo-Expressionism is the documented hybrid edge where crystalline facets carry expressive tension.
- **Post-Impressionism** (Cézanne above all) builds form with modulated constructive strokes but keeps one viewpoint and coherent depth. Cubism's break is the viewpoint itself: planes interpenetrate, space collapses, the object is conceptually reassembled rather than optically recorded.
