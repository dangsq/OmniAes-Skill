# Ishoku Hada (異色肌)

Ishoku Hada is a Japanese fashion subculture and avant-garde makeup practice that treats the skin itself as the costume: face, neck, chest and hands are covered in opaque, unnatural face paint — pink, bright blue, purple, green, orange — to produce an anime- or alien-inspired being. It crystallized in Tokyo's Harajuku district around 2016 out of the Gyaru community's love of clubwear, mixed patterns and bold excess, pushing the gyaru/manba logic of decorated skin past tan into pure fantasy chroma. The result is a weird-cute, maximalist portrait culture: painted faces crowned with antennae and horns, arms wrapped in leopard-print fleece, a stitched-eye plushie hugged to the chest, everything shot in a saturated flash snapshot.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is oversaturation as a worldview: every surface — skin, hair, garments, props, often the backdrop — sits near the top of the chroma scale, and at least three candy hues collide in one frame. Skin hue, wig hue and garment ground hue should each come from different families so they clash rather than match.

| Role | Hex anchor range |
|---|---|
| Candy pink (skin, wigs, plush) | #FF4FA8 – #FF9AD5 |
| Cyan / sky blue (skin, plush, print ground) | #2FC6E8 – #7FE3F2 |
| Violet / purple (skin, wig tips) | #7A5BD8 – #B07BE8 |
| Lime / neon green (fleece, print ground) | #A8E820 – #D8F56E |
| Tangerine orange (accents, hair streaks) | #FF8A26 – #FFB35C |
| Accent white (crosses, nose stripe, stickers) | #FFFFFF |
| Print black (leopard spots, check lines) | #141414 |

Muted, earthy or pastel-desaturated versions of these hues break the style; the paint must read as deliberately artificial candy color, not skin undertone.

### Skin paint craft (the defining core)

The face is a flat, matte-to-slight-sheen field of water-activated body paint (the style's brand list — TAG Body Art, Athena Face Paint, Sunset Makeup — is face-paint craft, not cosmetics). On top of that field sit makeup constructions: heavy false lashes and dark liner, colored contacts, a contrast stripe or highlight down the nose bridge, bright eyeshadow zones, rhinestone teardrops and heart stickers under the eyes or on the cheeks, defined lips in a clashing hue, sometimes lip piercings. The paint covers contiguous skin — face into neck, chest, hands — so no natural skin tone leaks in. It must read as *makeup on a person*, not as CGI creature skin: lash lines, lip edges and slight paint texture stay visible.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Oversaturated unnatural skin paint** — the namesake element; nothing else matters if this is absent.
2. **Cartoon-fantasy headwear** — antennae headbands, horns on clips, white cross hair clips, ribbons, bows, heart-shaped sunglasses pushed onto the head, eyepatches, novelty eyeglasses.
3. **Alien-inspired outfit signals** — big gloves with claws, horns, antennae, otherworldly persona props.
4. **Mixed loud prints** — animal print (leopard above all), stripes, polka dots, checkered patterns, worn simultaneously.
5. **Weird-cute plushies** — stitched-eye, big-eyed soft toys held or hugged.
6. **Texture pieces** — fuzzy leg warmers and fleece sleeves, fishnets, decorated nails, high heels, holographic bags.
7. **Garment silhouettes from Harajuku clubwear** — maid dresses, tutu dresses, kimono sleeves or parasols mixed into club outfits.
8. **Manba makeup motifs** — the white nose stripe, under-eye sticker clusters, exaggerated eye whites.

### Materials and textures

Synthetic wig fiber with a glossy sheen; fleece and faux-fur pile (leg warmers, leopard sleeves); plush toy pile; satin or printed cotton leopard; fishnet knit; vinyl and holographic bag surfaces; plastic novelty sunglasses; faceted rhinestone and paper stickers on painted skin; matte face paint against all of it.

### Recurring subjects, composition and lighting

Subjects are young people (any gender presentation) alone, in pairs, or in crowding groups of three or four, photographed chest-up or face-close. Poses are playful and direct: peace signs, tongue-out bleh faces, claw hands, plushies squeezed to the chest, heads tilted together. Composition is casual and maximalist — cluttered club booths, stickered walls, rainbow or smiley backdrops, parasols overhead; props press into the foreground; slight tilt and cropped limbs are normal. Lighting is frontal and flat: on-camera flash or bright club light that makes the paint read opaque, with saturation pushed in post; neon or rainbow glow behind is welcome. The medium energy is smartphone snapshot / social upload, not studio editorial. (The written source describes motifs and brands but not photography; these composition and lighting rules are distilled from the style's snapshot corpus.)

## Mood & Values

Self-expression is the stated core value: the painted face is a declaration of invented identity, anime-alien self over natural beauty norms. The mood is euphoric play, weird-cute (kimo-kawaii) tenderness — hugging a stitched-eye plushie with a clawed glove — and defiant joy. Group frames emphasize community: this is a scene photographed together, tongues out, shoulders pressed.

## Era & Origins

Japan, early 2010s per the style's own dating, crystallizing in Harajuku around 2016. It descends from Gyaru and its Manba extreme — inheriting leopard print, heart sunglasses, sticker makeup and cheeky poses — and reacts by replacing the tan with impossible color, fused with Harajuku clubwear and alien/anime fantasy. Retail lineage spans gyaru-adjacent labels (COCOLULU, Anap, Takuya Angel, Me Jane) and professional face-paint brands, confirming its dual nature as fashion plus makeup craft.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Paint first: one opaque candy hue over all visible skin per person; keep makeup architecture (lashes, liner, nose stripe, stickers, lips) on top of the paint.
- Clash, never coordinate: skin hue ≠ wig hue ≠ garment ground; stack at least two loud prints (leopard plus stripe, dot or check).
- Crown the head: at least one cartoon-fantasy headwear element — antennae, horns, cross clips, bow, heart sunglasses, eyepatch.
- Add one weird-cute texture prop: plushie, fuzzy warmer, fishnet, claw glove, holographic bag.
- Shoot it like a friend did: flat frontal flash or bright club light, cluttered frame, direct eye contact, playful gesture, saturated post-processing.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo, duo or group portrait; club booth, karaoke room, stickered bedroom, arcade, Harajuku street, stage with parasol or rainbow cloth. Skin hue: any single opaque candy hue in the palette range, including yellow and green beyond the core pink/blue/purple; groups may assign a different hue per person. Hair: twintails, bob, gradient dip-dye, split bicolor wigs in clashing hues. Composition: face-close selfie to three-quarter figure; centered or tilted; props foregrounded. Lighting: on-camera flash, flat ring light, mixed neon club light, or bright daylight snapshot so long as chroma stays maxed. Mood: euphoric, tender-weird, cheeky-defiant. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**
- Natural, tanned or nude skin; small jewel accents on unpainted faces (festival makeup, not ishoku hada).
- CGI-smooth alien skin, scales, prosthetic ridges or SFX wounds — this is face paint, not creature effects.
- Muted or monochrome wardrobes: beige, olive, charcoal, quiet luxury, minimalism, negative-space composition, seamless tidy backdrops.
- Black-based cyber techwear: vinyl trenches, LED strips, circuit prints, goggles over untreated skin.
- Exact cosplay of a named anime character; the persona is original, not canonical.
- Studio softbox glamour, golden hour, teal-orange cinematic grades, desaturated film looks.
- Gothic lolita or punk leather austerity; pure traditional kimono kitsuke without clubwear clash.

## Discrimination Cues

| Axis | Ishoku Hada | Cyberpop | Gyaru | Manba |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skin | Opaque candy paint, full coverage | Natural/pale with neon accents | Bronzed tan | Deep tan with white contour blocks |
| Palette base | Candy hues everywhere, light or dark ground | Neon on black | Warm glam, golds and browns | Neon streaks over dark tan |
| Head/signature | Antennae, horns, cross clips, plushies | Cyber goggles, LEDs, vinyl | Bleached hair, glam lashes | White panda-eye makeup, neon falls |
| Lineage feel | Alien/anime persona, weird-cute | Futurist techwear | Luxury-casual glamour | Rave-extreme gyaru |

Fast tells: if the skin is a flat painted candy hue with sticker makeup and a leopard-print sleeve, it is ishoku hada; if the skin is untreated and the neon lives in clothing and LEDs, it is Cyberpop; if the skin is bronzed and the look is glam-causal, Gyaru; if the skin is dark tan with white eye-blocks and no fantasy hue, Manba. Ishoku Hada borrows from all three but is the only one where the skin itself is the impossible color.
