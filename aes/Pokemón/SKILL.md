# Pokemón

Pokemón (also *HxCitos* or *Hardcoritos*) was a Chilean youth subculture that crystallized around 2006 and faded under media demonization and street violence by roughly 2012. It is a **fusion aesthetic**: Otaku hobbies and worldview, Emo hairstyles, Hip-Hop clothing silhouettes, Punk pacifist politics, and local Chilean inputs — Flaite musical taste (reggaeton, perreo) and hardcore-punk scene habits. Nothing in it is pure; every look is a layered remix of four or five parent scenes, held together by black dyed hair and a compact digital camera pointed at a group of friends. Its native habitat is the early social web: Fotolog pages and MSN Messenger windows, which is why the style's surviving imagery is low-resolution, watermarked, flash-blitzed, and often re-edited into memes.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
A **black-and-white ground punctured by candy accents**. Hair, pants and most tops sit in near-black (`#0a0a0a`–`#222222`) or plain white/off-white (`#eeeeee`–`#f7f7f7`); against that base, one to three saturated accents appear: red (`#c0182b`–`#e0403a`), fuchsia/magenta (`#d81b7a`–`#ff2e9a`), lime/grass green (`#7ed321`–`#a4e044`), purple (`#6b4fd0`), blue (`#2f6fd0`), orange (`#f07818`). Accents arrive as small doses — a bracelet stack, a hair clip, a streak in the bangs, a striped hoodie band, a graffiti wall behind the subject — never as an all-over saturated field. Black-and-white horizontal striping counts as a full participant in the palette, not a neutral.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the text documents them)
1. **Piñas hair** — the anchor of the style: dyed-black, Emo- and anime-inspired cuts; long straight bangs swept across the forehead and often over one or both eyes; layered, spiky shag volumes (the text names Goku from *Dragon Ball* as a reference); occasional small candy-colored streaks or bow/barrette clips.
2. **Patterned clothing** — horizontal stripes and polka dots on long polo shirts; wide white or black t-shirts; tank tops; mini-skirts with belts; leather jackets; hip-hop-inflected pieces; skinny, "decayed" (distressed) pants; Converse sneakers.
3. **Face work** — black eyeliner, colorful makeup, makeup with drawn symbols or drawings, glitter, piercings. (Text-documented but rarely resolved in surviving low-res photos; treat as fine-grain detail.)
4. **Decora-inspired bracelet stacks** — many plastic/silicone bracelets layered on wrists, plus belts as accent hardware.
5. **The group tableau** — packed clusters of teenagers: hugging, leaning, hand-signs, fingers over a friend's eyes, dance poses from *pokemoneo* and *perreo*, the flirtatious crowding of *ponceo*. Affection is physical and public but should stay at hug/cheek-kiss/hand-hold level.
6. **The internet layer** — Fotolog/MSN artifacts: crew-tag watermarks in pixel or rounded display fonts, JPEG compression, meme edits borrowing pixel RPG battle UI (text boxes, PP counters, type labels) pasted over photos. This layer is period-accurate satire-adjacent; use sparingly.

### Materials and textures
Cotton jersey tees, hoodie fleece, thin denim, canvas sneaker rubber, plastic bracelets, glitter particles, synthetic hair shine. Backgrounds are street materials: painted plaster and graffiti walls, bare brick, concrete plazas, glass storefronts, club interiors with laminate tables. Over everything sits the **medium texture of a mid-2000s compact digital camera**: 3–5 MP softness, chroma noise in shadows, flash halation on skin, mild JPEG blocking, slight color cast.

### Composition and lighting tendencies
Casual snapshot grammar: eye-level or slightly high angle, subjects crowded to the frame edges, heads cropped by the border, tilted horizons tolerated, poses half-caught (mid-laugh, mid-dance, mid-gesture). Two lighting regimes dominate: **night = harsh direct on-camera flash** against a black falloff background (street corners, club doors), and **day = flat, overexposed daylight** that blows highlights on white tees and pale walls (plazas, graffiti walls, brick). Indoor fluorescent/mixed-light snapshots are a valid third case. Depth of field is deep and cheap; nothing is artfully blurred.

## Mood & Values

Projected melancholy, actual extroversion: posed sad-boy/sad-girl faces coexist with grinning group piles. The values are pacifism, open-mindedness, queer inclusivity (a significant LGBTQ+, bisexual and polyamorous membership), fantasy and fictional-character identification, and self-expression through appearance — plus a willingness to be childish on purpose. There is a self-aware layer of being mocked: the style circulated inside memes that ridiculed it, yet its members kept documenting themselves joyfully anyway. Aim for **defiant cuteness in a rough street frame**, not gloom.

## Era & Origins

Santiago de Chile, from 2006; decline accelerated after the 2008 state anti-violence campaign and online/physical harassment, largely gone from the mainstream by 2012. It reacts against nothing stylistically — it *absorbs* everything (Emo, Punk, Hip-Hop, Otaku, Flaite) — which is exactly why gatekeeping Punks, Skinheads, Emos, Metalheads and Hip-Hop fans attacked it. Related aesthetics: Alternative, Emo, Flaite, Flogger, Hip-Hop, Jejemon, Punk, Reggaetonero, Scene, Shamate. Native media: Fotolog, MSN Messenger.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Build every look from the base-plus-candy rule: black/white ground first, then 1–3 saturated accents in small doses.
- Hair is the load-bearing element: black, shaggy, long bangs over the brow/eyes. Without piñas hair it is not Pokemón.
- Keep the camera humble: consumer compact digital, 2006–2009. Flash blitz at night, blown highlights by day, noise everywhere.
- Pose socially and physically: clusters, hugs, leaning, hand-signs, dance crouches. Solo portraits should still feel like a Fotolog avatar, not a studio sitting.
- Let the period internet leak in optionally: pixel-font crew watermarks, MSN-era crop ratios, pixel RPG-battle meme overlays.
- Keep settings street-level and Chilean-urban in feel: plazas, graffiti and brick walls, school gates, club corners, storefront glass.

**Variation axes (what may change)**
- *Subject*: solo avatar portrait, duo, packed group of 5–15, dance candid, flirting/ponceo moment, meme edit, screenshot-style collage with pixel UI.
- *Composition*: centered flash snapshot, tilted frame, edge-cropped heads, seated row against a wall, crouched pile on pavement.
- *Lighting*: night on-camera flash, flat overcast daylight, hard sun with blown whites, indoor fluorescent.
- *Color handling*: accent emphasis may shift (fuchsia-heavy, lime-heavy, red-heavy) or collapse to pure black/white striping; the black ground must remain.
- *Mood*: brooding posed melancholy through to laughing extrovert; both are canonical.
- *Medium*: straight snapshot, watermarked Fotolog upload, meme-edited composite.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A lone kid on a bicycle, a dance floor, a bedroom wall of printed photos — all valid if the hair, palette, wardrobe grammar and camera humility hold.

**Avoid**
- Professional studio lighting, seamless backdrops, HDR or modern computational-photo sharpness, bokeh-heavy lenses.
- Post-2012 fashion: undercut/man-bun hair, joggers, dad sneakers, hypebeast minimal logos, normcore, smartphones and selfie sticks, vapes, LED strips.
- Muted earth-tone, beige, scandi-minimal or monochrome-luxury palettes.
- Goth/metal signifiers: corpsepaint, stud-only austerity, Victorian lace, band-logo blackness without candy accents.
- Shamate/visual-kei hair: enormous backcombed neon-teased volumes (Pokemón hair is shaggy, mostly black, street-cut).
- Flogger hair: floppy straight neon-highlighted curtains with electro-cumbia clubwear.
- Pure Flaite/Reggaetonero sportswear looks with no Emo/Otaku face-and-hair layer (no eyeliner, no piñas, no glitter = not Pokemón).
- Clean vector flat design, 4K render gloss, cyberpunk neon grids.
- Any sexualization of subjects; keep affection at hugs, cheek kisses, held hands.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Pokemón difference |
|---|---|---|
| **Flaite** | Chilean street sportswear: football jerseys, caps, flannel, brand tracksuits, macho stance, cumbia/reggaeton | Shares reggaeton taste and some hip-hop pieces, but always adds the Emo/Otaku layer: piñas hair, eyeliner, glitter, candy accents, androgyny |
| **Reggaetonero** | Latin-urban club polish: bling chains, branded sets, groomed perreo-club look | DIY skinny jeans, Converse, bracelet stacks; plaza and Fotolog, not VIP club gloss |
| **Shamate** | Chinese visual-kei descendant: huge backcombed magenta/blond hair, heavy glam makeup | Hair stays black or near-black with small streaks; shaggy not teased; wardrobe is casual streetwear, not glam costume |
| **Emo** | Sincere melancholy: band tees, studded belts, black+red only, isolated poses | Extroverted group joy, polychrome candy accents, hip-hop silhouettes, reggaeton soundtrack, playful fantasy |
| **Flogger** | Argentine electro-cumbia: floppy straight hair with neon highlights, tight bright tees, photo-edit obsession | Black-based piñas, stripes/polka dots, punk/otaku debris, Chilean plaza context, flash-snapshot rawness over glossy edits |

**Honest evidence notes:** glitter and symbol-drawn makeup are text-documented but seldom resolvable in surviving low-res photos — keep them as optional micro-detail. Pixel RPG-battle meme overlays come from mocking meme culture around the subculture; they are period-authentic internet furniture, not a core fashion motif, and should never dominate a generation.
