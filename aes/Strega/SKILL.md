# Strega

Strega takes its name from the Italian word for witch and behaves like a costume cabinet opened inside a fogged forest. Coined on Tumblr in 2014 by Mai Magi (then shortcuttothestars), it crystallized as a distinct offshoot of Dark Mori, itself the shadowed transitional phase of Japanese Mori Kei. It is a fashion style first — loose, layered, medieval-practical clothing with occult-jewelry signifiers — but its visual world extends well beyond the outfit: misty woods, candlelit tables, overgrown huts, moths and swans, blurred dancing figures. Crucially, the wiki stresses that Strega is *not* the working attire of practicing witches; it is a romantic, literary, folkloric dress-up language descended from the fey side of Goth (Siouxsie and the Banshees, Bauhaus's "Hollow Hills," 4AD's This Mortal Coil covers, Danielle Dax in *The Company of Wolves*, Brian Froud's *Labyrinth*, Terri Windling's Borderland anthologies, Emma Bull's *War for the Oaks*). Where its descendants Hippie Goth and Romantic Goth lean boho, Strega keeps a Pre-Raphaelite color sense and a distinctive reliance on brocade, velvet and glitter.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule: build from forest-floor earth tones, then push them toward black; never start from black and add neon. Chroma stays veiled, as if seen through smoke or mist — even reds and greens read muted. Highlights are ash, bone or candle-smoke, never pure white.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **The witch-wardrobe signifiers** (infobox key motifs): pentacles; layered, loose clothing; long flowing dresses; high boots; moon, sun and feather motifs on accessories; gender-neutral garments. A practical, medieval-inspired silhouette: hems and sleeves overlapping, cloaks and scarves stacked, boots worn for walking in mud.
2. **Forest and night atmosphere** (visuals list, headlined): misty forests; bare trees and twigs; thorns; moon and stars; candles and old lamps.
3. **Fauna and curiosities**: moths; unusual insects; swans and wings; black cats.
4. **Domestic ritual and craft**: tea things; stained glass windows; glitter and shimmer on fabric.
5. **Movement and photographic registers**: dancing figures; 1920s theatre/ballet photographs; underwater scenes; deliberately blurry images.

The image record corroborates this ranking: costumed figures in woods, taper candles and tin lanterns, thorned canes and bare trunks recur constantly, while mossy logs with bracket fungi, overgrown plank huts with climbing roses and clay pots, and fogged conifer aisles supply the unpeopled scene-setters. Text-only motifs (underwater, stained glass, 1920s ballet prints, swans, tea things) are fully authorized even though the sampled pictures concentrate on forest and portrait work — treat their absence in any given picture as normal, not as a rule.

### Materials and textures

Brocade, velvet, lace sleeves, felt hat brims, wool layering, slouched leather boots, tarnished silver pendants; glitter or shimmer caught as sparse specks. In the environment: weathered gray plank wood, corrugated roofing, moss and lichen, dry leaf litter, twigs and thorns, climbing rose canes, terracotta pots, wax drips, moth-wing powder, fog droplets. Surfaces should look handled, grown-over or aged — nothing sealed, glossy or new.

### Recurring subjects

Solitary or paired figures (any gender) standing among roots, on fallen logs or in trunk colonnades; duets in matching black robes; unpeopled forest paths and fogged aisles; vernacular overgrown architecture (huts, sheds, cabins half-eaten by roses); macro studies of moss, fungi, thorns; candle-and-tea still lifes; black cats; moths and pinned or perched unusual insects; dancing or mid-twirl figures; underwater swans and submerged fabric where the dreamier register is wanted.

### Composition and light

Vertical tree trunks act as a colonnade; figures sit small-to-medium inside the environment rather than cropped like studio fashion. Foreground occlusion — grass blades, twigs, smoke trails, an out-of-focus candle flame — builds depth. Light is low-key and biased underexposed: diffused overcast daylight, blue-hour dusk, fog-scattered shafts through canopy, or night lit by a single practical flame whose warm glow falls off into black. Soft focus, film grain, gentle vignetting and slight bloom are characteristic; a blurred, dream-like finish is a feature, not a fault. Painterly Pre-Raphaelite density (crowded detail, jewel-dark color) is an acceptable alternate finish.

## Mood & Values

Darkly dream-like, reverent, melancholy-romantic. Strega treats the forest as a cathedral and the wardrobe as a grimoire: layering is ritual, mending and handcraft are virtues, and folklore literacy (fairy-tale retellings, urban fantasy, fae art) is the cultural currency. Its uncanny is gentle — a moth, a thorn, a black cat — never horrific. Gender-neutral clothing is a stated value, so pairs and portraits need not conform to binary costuming. The overall feeling is escapism with a library card: mystical, quiet, a little sorrowful, always elegant.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s, born on Tumblr (2014, Mai Magi), as the named endpoint of a lineage: Japanese Mori Kei → Dark Mori → Strega. Behind that stands the fey strand of Goth from the 1980s onward — Siouxsie and the Banshees' mystical turn, Bauhaus referencing Brian Froud and Alan Lee's *Faeries*, 4AD's ethereal forested album art, Angela Carter's *The Bloody Chamber* via *The Company of Wolves*, Froud's *Labyrinth*, Windling's and Bull's fae-urban fantasy — a "movement-within-a-movement" that Strega finally labeled for social media. It deliberately separates itself from Hippie Goth (boho clothes in dark colors) by insisting on Pre-Raphaelite color schemes and on brocade, velvet and glitter.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Darken the earth, don't brighten the black: derive every hue from soil, moss, bark, dried blood-red, then desaturate toward shadow.
2. One flame, many shadows: at night, a single practical light (taper, lantern, old lamp) with heavy falloff; in daylight, overcast or fog-diffused light, never direct sun.
3. Layer the silhouette: overlapping hems, sleeves, cloaks, belts, boots; no clean single-line outfit.
4. Mist is structure: use haze or fog to separate foreground, figure and background planes.
5. Wear the symbols: pentacle, moon, sun, feather as pendants, pins, embroidery, hat feathers — integrated into costume, not floating occult set-dressing.
6. Nature as architecture: trunks as columns, root masses as thrones, thorn canes as doorways, moss as carpet.
7. Age the surface: film grain, soft bloom, slight blur or painterly density; suppress clinical sharpness and pure-white highlights.

**Variation axes.** Subject: fashion portrait of any gender; duet; unpeopled forest; overgrown hut or exterior; still life of tea things, candles, moth specimens; stained-glass interior; underwater figure; dancing figure; macro of moss, fungi, thorns; black cat or swan. Composition: figure-in-environment, environment-dominant with tiny figure, tight portrait behind foreground occlusion, centered altar-like still life, macro. Lighting: overcast day, blue-hour dusk, candlelit night, fog shafts, dim lamplit interior. Color handling: near-monochrome charcoal; earth-forward green-brown; oxblood-accented; violet-tinged dusk — all inside the desaturated range above. Mood/register: dreamy-blur ethereal, crisp melancholy, ritual-solemn, aged-photograph (1920s theatre/ballet grain). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a stained-glass interior with tea things and a feather pin is as Strega as a fogged forest portrait.

**Avoid**

- High-key sunlight, saturated or pastel candy color, teal-orange grading, neon.
- Clean minimalism, studio white backdrops, glossy commercial sharpness, chrome, plastic, visible logos or sneakers.
- Cottagecore's gingham, picnic baskets, buttery creams, tidy cheerful productivity.
- Horror gore, blood splatter, skeletons-as-shock (that belongs to Cottagegore/horror, not Strega).
- Literal practicing-witchcraft ritual staging or shock-occult iconography; Strega is fashion and folklore, pentacle jewelry aside.
- Dark Fantasy's epic kit: armor, swords, castles, dragons, CGI spell-glow, heroic battle scale.
- Gothic Lolita's doll architecture: crinoline petticoats, oversized bows, porcelain-sweet makeup.
- Fairy Grunge's pastel glitter dusting, distressed crop tees, Y2K flash-snapshot look.
- Manicured gardens, tropical foliage, pristine alpine snow; snow, if present at all, is patchy melt under leaf litter.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Shared ground | Strega's tell |
|---|---|---|
| Mori Kei | Layered natural-fiber forest fashion | Mori Kei stays in cream, oatmeal, sage, mustard daylight with a cozy childlike air; Strega blacks the palette, adds pentacle/moon/feather jewelry, candles, mist and thorns |
| Cottagecore | Rural nature, huts, roses, tea | Cottagecore is sunlit, warm-cream, tidy and optimistic; Strega's hut is overgrown and lantern-lit, its roses thorned, its light twilight or flame |
| Cottagegoth | Black clothing plus cottage objects | Cottagegoth parks goth attire inside bright, cozy cottagecore domesticity for playful contrast; Strega never borrows that light — its tea things and lamps sit in dim, fae, misted registers, with medieval-layered silhouettes |
| Dark Fantasy | Darkness and magic | Dark Fantasy is narrative-epic: armor, weapons, castles, creatures, polished high-contrast render; Strega is intimate and sartorial, its magic a candle flame and a silver pendant in a walkable temperate wood |
| Fairy Grunge | Fae motifs over grunge darkness | Fairy Grunge keeps pastel dusting, glitter wings, distressed denim and flash-lit Y2K snapshot energy; Strega's dark is deep and velvety, silhouettes long and flowing, finish filmic or painterly, palette earthy not candy |

One further boundary: against Hippie Goth and Romantic Goth, Strega's fingerprint is the Pre-Raphaelite color scheme and the brocade-velvet-glitter material trio over plain boho-in-black — if an image reads as sundress-plus-dark-makeup with no medieval layering or fae folklore, it has drifted out of Strega.
