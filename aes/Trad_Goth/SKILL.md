# Trad Goth

Trad Goth — Traditional Goth, "Batcaver" — is the founding look of the goth subculture: the dramatic, androgynous fashion that grew out of British post-punk and gothic rock in the late 1970s and early 1980s, crystallised in London's Batcave club and inseparable from the image of Bauhaus, Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Cure and The Sisters of Mercy. "Trad" is a retrospective label: it exists to distinguish this original silhouette from every goth substyle that came after. Its visual DNA is a punk body rebuilt as theatre — a huge backcombed black hair mass, a face painted into a graphic mask of pale foundation and drawn black liner, and a wardrobe of leather, fishnet, band cloth and studs assembled by hand rather than bought finished.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

Black is the field, not an accent: dyed hair, jacket, tee, skirt, tights and boots merge into one dark mass that typically owns the majority of the frame. Against it, the only light pole is matte pale skin and, occasionally, a bone-white shirt; colour enters as a sparse event, never a scheme.

| Role | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant blacks | `#050505`–`#1c1c1c` | hair, leather, wool, fishnet shadow |
| Textural charcoals | `#2a2a2a`–`#3a3a3a` | where cloth and hair catch light |

| Deep purple accent | `#2e1a33`–`#4a2b52` | documented accent, rarely dominant |
| Film greys | `#6e6e6e`–`#a8a8a8` | silver-gelatin midtones in B&W work |

Black-and-white treatment is fully native to the style and collapses the palette to high-contrast greyscale; colour work tends toward faded warm snapshot casts or flash-saturated reds. The wiki is explicit that 80s goth was never restricted to black, so non-black garments and dyed hair (deep red, purple) are legitimate — but black must still read as the gravitational centre.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the text documents them)

1. **Big backcombed, teased, spiky or puff-ball hair**, usually black-dyed — the single most iconic element; a frizzed, crimped mass that widens the head silhouette far beyond the skull and breaks the top of the frame.
2. **Pale foundation with heavy stylized black eyeliner** — Egyptian / bat-wing liner drawn out past the eye, sometimes into graphic shapes above the crease; thin plucked or redrawn brows; **dark red or black lipstick** and **dramatic blush/contour** carving the cheekbones.
3. **Leather jackets and band t-shirts** — biker cuts, often worn open over layered black.
4. **Fishnet**, especially ripped tights and mesh sleeves.
5. **DIY hardware**: studs, spikes, safety pins, pins and patches of favourite bands, tattered edges, hand-ripped holes — the style's core value made visible.
6. **Footwear**: winklepicker boots and platform boots.
7. **Occult-and-mourning jewellery**: cross and ankh necklaces, black bead rosaries, chokers, spiked cuffs, hoop earrings, piercings; **mesh or lace fingerless gloves**.
8. **Silhouettes**: short low-waisted or mid-length skirts, felted coats, flat black hats.
9. **Props**: a clove cigarette held or smoking; cassettes, vinyl, gig flyers as scene shorthand.
10. Lower-frequency text items, thin in the visual record but permitted: Western-style touches and body paint.

### Materials and textures

Matte everything: waxed leather, felted and boiled wool, dry backcombed hair, cotton band jersey, open fishnet mesh, coarse lace, oxidised silver and blackened beadwork. Texture contrast comes from surface, not colour — the shine of a leather lapel against the fuzz of teased hair, the grid of fishnet over pale leg, torn paper or peeling club walls behind.

### Subjects, composition, lighting

Recurring subjects: solo head-and-shoulders portraits; seated or standing couples; horizontal band-and-friend group rows; club, domestic and transit candids; formal school-photo-style studio sittings. Composition treats the hair as the event: it occupies the upper third, spills past frame edges, and dwarfs the face. Poses are aloof, confrontational or camp — direct unsmiling gaze, sideways glance, mid-shout or mid-laugh in candids, cigarette smoke drifting. Androgyny is constant across genders. Lighting is never beauty lighting: harsh on-camera flash with dark falloff into club or room backgrounds, flat frontal studio light over mottled grey backdrops, or available light pushed to grain. The surface is film — silver-gelatin black and white, faded warm snapshot colour, Polaroid softness, flash halation, visible grain.

## Mood & Values

Aloof theatricality with a punk sneer: morbid, dramatic, deliberately artificial, yet handmade and unpolished. The style expresses a **DIY ethos** above all — the look is assembled, damaged and customised by its wearer — plus devotion to goth as a *music* scene rather than a costume shelf, reverence for elder goths, and comfort with androgyny and gender play. Modern trad goths carry subcultural memory: they are mindful about the religious and cultural iconography they wear, and they gather at goth nights, record shops, bookstores and coffee shops. The mood is belonging worn as armour — intimate group shots matter as much as lone portraits.

## Era & Origins

United Kingdom, late 1970s–1980s. Seeded by pre-Batcave progenitors (Bauhaus's "dark glam", Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Damned, The Cure) and consolidated in the early-80s London club The Batcave, which refused pop and bred The Specimen, Alien Sex Fiend, The Virgin Prunes and Danielle Dax; Nico's *The Marble Index* is honoured as the scene's godmother record. Bass-driven post-punk, morbid lyrics and almost-costume fashion fused into what was then called positive punk, then goth. Period touchstones: *The Hunger* (1983), *The Company of Wolves* (1984), *Vamp* (1986), *The Crow*, *Sandman*.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build the silhouette before the face: the backcombed hair mass is the style's fingerprint. Paint the face as graphic design — pale matte ground, liner as drawn architecture, dark lips — not as naturalistic makeup. Let black own the frame and admit at most one or two accent colours. Damage and handwork everything: rips, pins, patches, tatters, studs. Finish on a film surface: grain, flash falloff, fade, or strong B&W contrast. Pose aloof, theatrical, androgynous. Anchor with scene props — band cloth, ankh beads, a clove cigarette — only where they serve the image.

**Variation axes.** Subject: portrait, couple, group row, club or domestic candid, transit snapshot, gig shot, formal studio sitting, or still life of gear (patched jacket, boots, cassettes). Composition: head-and-shoulders through full-body seated, centred or off-centre, tight cluster or linear row. Lighting: direct flash, flat studio front-light, grainy available light, single bare club bulb. Colour handling: pure B&W, desaturated colour with one saturated accent, faded warm film, flash-hot colour. Mood: confrontational, camp-playful, tender-domestic, funereal. Hair may be black, deep red or purple; garments may occasionally depart from black, as the 80s scene did. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image** — a kitchen snapshot, a record-shop queue or an invented band promo can all be perfect trad goth without reproducing any exemplar.

**Avoid.** Pastel or nu-goth palettes and sleek minimal lines; cybergoth neon, synthetic dreads and goggles; steampunk brass; Victorian corsetry, lace gowns and mourning veils as the primary read (that is the romantic/ethereal strand); Deathrock's wedding-dress and horror-kitsch costuming as the centre; glam glitter, satin and sequins; folk or bohemian layering as the base; clean glossy editorial beauty lighting, smooth blow-dried hair, or neutral "smokey eye" makeup without drawn liner; crisp HDR digital sharpness with no film character; gore props; mallgoth keychain belts.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Their tell | Trad Goth's tell |
|---|---|---|
| Glam Rock | glitter, satin, metallics, feather boas, sequined platforms, 70s stage costume, natural or tanned skin | matte black, pale painted skin, fishnet and leather, DIY damage, club floor not stage |
| Post-Punk | austere ordinary dress: grey macs, plain knitwear, short neat hair, little or no makeup | the same lineage pushed into exaggeration: huge teased hair, graphic liner, fetish-punk hardware |
| Anglo Gothic | heritage and literary gothic: Victorian mourning dress, candlelit stone, moorlands, period costume | a living modern music subculture: punk hardware, band cloth, snapshot film grain, no period staging |
| Deathrock | US punkier cousin: bright hair dye, veils, wedding dresses, opera gloves, metal grommets, military fatigues, horror kitsch, vibrant red/gold | UK monochrome dominance, backcombed mass over coloured spikes, band tee–leather–fishnet core |
| Ethnogoth | folk and ethnic ornament: embroidered shawls, coin and tribal silver, kaftans, worldly bohemian layering (an extension of the ethereal/woodland strand's kimonos and scarves) | subcultural hardware — studs, patches, ankh, fishnet — on a punk-derived silhouette |

Where the record is thin, say so: purple as an accent and the Western-style and body-paint items are documented in the text but barely surface in surviving images, and the wiki itself stresses that 80s goth dress was far more varied than the later stereotype — so treat black dominance as a strong tendency of the trad core, not an absolute law.
