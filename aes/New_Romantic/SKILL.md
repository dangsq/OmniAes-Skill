# New Romantic (Blitz Kids)

New Romantic was a fashion-led youth subculture and music scene born in London nightclubs (Billy's, then The Blitz) in the late 1970s, peaking in the early 1980s alongside synth-pop and the first music-video era. It was a flamboyant, escapist revolt against British austerity and against the drab uniform of punk: a philosophy of extravagant self-expression rather than one fixed look. Everything about it is assembled artifice — makeup as paint, costume as quotation, the club floor as stage — and its two costume strains (romantic-historical and sleek-futuristic) share a single grammar: black-and-white ground, saturated color events, metallic shine, and a posed, mannequin-cool or clown-playful address to the camera.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** Build on a ground of deep black and stark cosmetic white, then puncture it with unmuted primaries and metallics. Vermilion red, electric blue/teal and yellow appear as isolated high-saturation events (a lip, a hat, an eyeshadow block, a jacket), never as blended harmonies. Hot magenta/pink is the most frequent accent outside the strict primaries. Metallic gold (lamé, braid, brocade) and silver supply shine. A secondary pastel register — powder pink, cream, pale peach — belongs to the fashion-editorial strand, and a monochrome black-and-white register belongs to period press photography; both are legitimate variants, but the saturated primaries-on-black core is the style's center of gravity.

- Black ground: `#000000–#1c1c1c`
- Clown white / cream base: `#f2ece0–#fdfaf4`
- Vermilion / lipstick red: `#c8102e–#e60023`
- Hot magenta / pink: `#d6006e–#ff2e88`
- Electric blue / teal: `#008080–#2b4bd7`
- Yellow / gold accent, lamé: `#f0c400–#d4af37`
- Silver / pewter metallic: `#a8a8a8–#d9d9d9`
- Pastel editorial register: `#f6c9cf`, `#efd9c4`

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the text documents them.** (1) *Pirate / Romantic-era historicism*: frilly poet shirts, sashes, breeches, tricorn-and-wide-brim hats, layered robes — the Vivienne Westwood "Pirate" lineage. (2) *Rococo dandyism*: velvet, brocade, military hussar jackets with gold braid and rosettes, top hats, canes, pearls and chains. (3) *Theatrical makeup on all genders*: clown-white or pale foundation, heavy eyeliner, lipstick, graphic color-block eyeshadow (pink, teal, yellow, blue), drawn or shaved brows, blush swept up to the temples. (4) *Heavily stylized hair*: teased spikes, asymmetric geometric cuts, feathered plumes, braids, dyed red/pink/platinum/orange. (5) *Futuristic glam strain*: sharp suits in unconventional colors or gold lamé, cool detached elegance, film-noir decadence. (6) *Club and music-video context*: posed clusters of band members, door-policy peacocks, poster graphics with lightning bolts and airbrushed glow.

**Materials and textures.** Crushed velvet, brocade, satin and silk poet-shirt sheen; glittering gold lamé; leather with cut-outs and lattice lacing; fishnet mesh; lace frills; feather plumes; pearl drops and chunky metallic earrings; chain drapes. Surfaces should read as costume-shop richness, not mall fabric.

**Recurring subjects.** Androgynous young adults of any gender, made up and costumed, alone or in duos; band groups of four to six posed tightly; club-night pairs caught against graffitied walls; runway and editorial fashion figures; headshot beauty portraits where the makeup is the subject.

**Composition and lighting.** Four documented modes: hard on-camera flash snapshots (flat bright faces, black shadow thrown on a wall or graffiti behind); flat studio light against seamless grey or saturated-color backdrops; magazine editorial layouts with cropped profiles, negative space and typography; and airbrushed poster graphics with neon lightning bolts and halo glows. High-contrast black-and-white press photography is a period-valid variant. Medium character: grainy 35mm film, slightly drifted print saturation, polaroid-ish color cast — analog, never digitally clean. Poses are deliberate: chin lifted, sidelong glance, limp-wristed gesture, mannequin stillness or clownish tilt.

## Mood & Values

Escapism as defiance. The style values artifice over authenticity, invention over taste: dressing up is identity-making, and the club door is a jury. It celebrates gender ambiguity — lipstick and eyeliner on men, military剪裁 on women — with playfulness rather than protest solemnity. Alongside the clown-playful strain runs a cool, detached, film-noir decadence in the futuristic register. Both share vanity in the old sense: the pose as a made artwork, glamour assembled by hand from vintage rails and costume shops.

## Era & Origins

United Kingdom, London, 1978 (Bowie night at Billy's, Steve Strange and Rusty Egan) through the Blitz club from 1979, mainstream breakthrough via Bowie's "Ashes to Ashes" video (1980) and MTV (1981–83), dissipated by 1983. It reacts against punk's drab uniform and against economic austerity; it descends from David Bowie and Roxy Music, absorbs Kraftwerk and Giorgio Moroder's electronics, and crosses with Vivienne Westwood's Pirate collection. Synth-pop is its sound; the music video is its natural medium.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Artifice first: every face carries makeup; no naturalism anywhere in frame.
2. Hybrid costume logic: combine at least one historical quotation (frill, sash, hussar braid, tricorn) with one futuristic or punk element (lamé, cut-out leather, geometric crop) — or commit fully to one strain, but keep it extreme.
3. Palette discipline: black/white ground plus saturated primaries, magenta, metallics; color arrives in blocks, not gradients.
4. Light hard or flat: direct flash with wall shadow, or even studio light on seamless paper; never soft golden-hour glow.
5. Analog surface: film grain, print saturation drift, or airbrushed poster flatness.
6. Pose as performance: theatrical, self-aware, cool or clownish — never candid warmth.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo headshot, duo, band group, runway figure, club snapshot, editorial page, poster graphic. Composition: tight face crop to full-body, centered or asymmetric, group cluster or single figure with negative space. Lighting: hard flash, flat studio, graphic poster glow, or high-contrast monochrome. Color handling: full saturated primaries, pastel powder editorial register, or black-and-white press register. Mood: playful clown extravagance ↔ detached noir elegance. Era point: pirate-frill early strain ↔ sleek lamé late strain. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a never-seen composition with these rules still reads New Romantic.

**Avoid.** Bare-faced or "no-makeup" naturalism; earth tones, beige boho, olive and rust palettes; grunge flannel, ripped jeans, athleisure, modern streetwear; punk anarchy without glamour (safety pins and mohawks alone, no makeup, no costume); goth mourning (all-black, crucifixes, sorrow) unless color and metallics interrupt it; contour-heavy modern drag or influencer makeup instead of graphic blocks and clown white; softbox beauty lighting, HDR clarity, smooth digital skin; sepia or faded-vintage filters (the period look is saturated flash, not desaturation); minimalist interiors and contemporary props; cowboy, hippie floral, or Victorian-mourning costume played straight without irony or club context.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Electroclash:** a 2000s revivalist cousin — digital-sharp, minimal neon-on-black, retro sunglasses, leather bobs, ironic distance. New Romantic keeps film grain, clown-white makeup, frills and historical costume; Electroclash has none of the rococo.
- **Glam Rock:** earlier (1971–75), glitter and sequins on bare skin, platform boots, jumpsuits, shag mullets, stadium swagger. New Romantic's makeup is graphic paint blocks on a pale base, its hair teased or geometric, its silhouette costumed rather than spangled.
- **Goth:** same postcode and years, opposite values — monochrome black, silver occult jewelry, backcombed black hair, Victorian grief. New Romantic insists on primaries, metallics, pink and play; absence of color events means Goth, not NR.
- **New Wave:** the broader, tamer music category — mod-ish slim suits, skinny ties, casual neon accents. No pirate frills, no clown makeup, no costume-shop excess.
- **Rococopunk:** shares the Westwood Pirate lineage but vandalizes it: torn brocade, safety pins, mohawks, confrontational snarl. New Romantic keeps the glamour intact, polished and club-posh; the rupture between silk and skin here is elegance, not damage.

**Quick recognition check:** pale painted face + graphic eyeshadow blocks + teased or spiked hair + one historical costume quotation + one metallic or lamé element + black/primary palette + hard flash or flat studio light = New Romantic.
