# Ganguro

Ganguro (ガングロ) is the most iconoclastic substyle of Japanese gyaru fashion, born in Shibuya, Tokyo in the mid-1990s as a deliberate, theatrical rejection of traditional Japanese beauty ideals of pale skin and understatement. Its center of gravity is the face and skin: an extremely deep, dark artificial tan built with tanning beds and dark foundation, set against bleached hair and white concealer. Everything else — neon separates, surf-beach accessories, platform boots — orbits that single contrast. The style lived and died in print media (egg, Popteen, Ego System), so its native surface is the glossy, halftone-grained magazine scan and the hard-flash street snapshot: saturated, slightly overexposed, candid, loud.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule: three value tiers in forced contrast
Every ganguro image is organized around three tiers, and the tension between them *is* the style:
- **Dark tier:** the artificial tan, a uniform matte bronze-to-chocolate skin (#4A2C1A–#7A4A28), plus heavy black eyeliner (#141414–#2B2B2B). The tan must read applied and even, never a natural sun-kissed gradient.
- **Light tier:** white concealer used as lipstick and eyeshadow (#F4EFE6–#FFFFFF) and bleached hair from platinum blonde (#E9DCA8–#F0E3B0) to ash silver (#B9B4A6–#D8D4C8).
- **Chroma tier:** candy neons at full saturation sitting against the dark skin — hot pink (#FF3F9E–#FF7AC0), tangerine (#FF6A13–#FFA040), lemon (#FFD84D–#FFE98A), turquoise (#19C3B1–#7FE8DC), violet (#7A3FF2–#B085F5), cherry red (#E8262D). Pale candy tones (baby pink, sky blue) may appear only as secondary notes in leis and beadwork; the dominant accents stay neon.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them
1. **Deep artificial tan** — the defining feature; without it the image is not ganguro.
2. **Bleached hair**, blonde or silver, often teased, crimped, or grown-out at the root.
3. **Panda makeup:** thick black eyeliner plus white concealer as an eyeshadow ring and as full pale lips; white dots, hearts, or star stickers on the cheeks extend the same graphic logic.
4. **Bright, revealing clothing:** miniskirts and vivid tops in the neon tier.
5. **Platform boots** and chunky thick-soled shoes.
6. **Hibiscus flowers and leis**, the Californian surf-culture import.
7. **Magazine apparatus:** egg-style page layouts with Japanese display type, callout arrows, and color swatches on flat candy backgrounds.

Photographic record adds a consistent layer of period props not named in the defining text: decorated flip phones with charm straps held toward the camera, plastic bead necklaces, metallic snap hair clips, glitter and sticker face decals, pigtailed synthetic extensions. Treat these as authentic optional props, not requirements.

### Materials and textures
Matte, cakey dark foundation; dry bleached and backcombed hair strands; vinyl and patent platform soles; nylon and chiffon lei petals; silk-like artificial hibiscus; glossy plastic beads, charms and watch straps; ABS phone shells; and, in print mode, glossy paper with visible CMYK halftone dot grain.

### Subjects, composition, lighting
Subjects are young Japanese women, solo or in tight packs of friends, in casual late-90s youth settings: street corners, beachside boardwalks, club and arcade interiors, or the magazine page itself. Three composition modes recur: the crammed close-up of several faces filling the frame, heads tilted and laughing; the vertical full-body street snap reading the silhouette from platform sole to bleached root; and the diagrammatic magazine page with the figure isolated on a pink or white ground surrounded by annotations. Lighting is hard and bright — on-camera flash or noon sun — high-key and contrasty, with print reproduction adding saturation lift and grain. Snapshot tilt and candid energy outrank posed elegance.

## Mood & Values

Rebellion, non-conformity, and the rejection of traditional beauty standards, performed as fun. The look is deliberately "too much": maximal contrast, maximal chroma, maximal expression — open-mouth laughs, peace signs, tongues out. Club culture and communal girl-friendship are its social engine; the defiance is playful rather than aggressive, a joy taken in being outrageous in public.

## Era & Origins

Shibuya, Tokyo, mid-1990s, grown out of kogal culture by girls who kept darkening their tans until the tan became the statement. Popularized by the model Buriteri and spread through egg magazine; the even darker "gonguro" sat beside it as a hardcore intensity dial. By 2000–2001 ganguro was largely extinct, displaced by shiro gyaru (white-skinned gals) in the wake of celebrities like Ayumi Hamasaki, but it had already spawned Manba, Yamanba, Banba, Kigurumi, B-kei gyaru and Tsuyome. Correct imagery sits roughly in the 1995–2001 window.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Tan first: establish the deep, matte, uniform artificial tan before anything else; it anchors every other choice.
- Hold the value triangle: dark tan + black liner against white concealer + platinum hair, with neons vibrating between.
- White concealer is graphic, not corrective: pale full lips and a ring or patch around the eyes, never blended contour or highlighter.
- Keep the silhouette short and thick-soled: miniskirt hem, platform boot, tanned legs.
- Accessorize in plastic and tropical: hibiscus, leis, bead necklaces, clips, charms.
- Pose loud: laughter, peace signs, tongue, friends crowding the frame.
- Remember the medium: render with the saturation, flash glare, and optional halftone grain of a late-90s snap or magazine scan; Japanese display type is a legitimate layer.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range across solo portraits, duos, groups of three to five, street, beach, club, karaoke, or printed page layouts and outfit diagrams. Composition may range from face-filling close-ups to full-body snaps to annotated page designs, with snapshot tilts allowed. Lighting may be noon sun, direct flash, club flash, or arcade fluorescent — always hard and bright. Color handling may lean pink-dominant, turquoise-dominant, violet-dominant, or any mix of the neon tier while the tan tier stays constant. Mood may slide from playful-maximal to defiant-loud. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Pale, natural, or lightly bronzed skin; nude, rosy, or gradient lips; subtle "no-makeup" makeup.
- Natural black or brunette hair; sleek unbleached salon gloss.
- Muted, earth-toned, monochrome, or minimalist wardrobes; quiet luxury, normcore, desaturated palettes.
- Modern beauty conventions: blended contour, Instagram highlighter, soft-glam as the only eye statement.
- Ladylike, office, or elegant silhouettes; long modest hemlines as the dominant read.
- Soft diffused studio light, moody low-key scenes, faded film emulation.
- Manba extremes when targeting ganguro: neon dreadlock extensions, white face paint in stripes or blocks across nose and cheeks, rave whistle and glow-stack clutter.
- Kogal schoolgirl coding as the dominant read: uniform plus loose socks plus light tan.
- Anachronisms: smartphones, selfie sticks, 2020s athleisure or minimal streetwear.
- Framing the tan as racial caricature: ganguro is a Japanese youth fashion documented within its own magazine culture, and images should keep their subjects and context inside that culture.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from ganguro |
|---|---|
| **Manba** (2000s descendant) | Concealer becomes geometric face paint (stripes over the nose, blocks on cheeks); hair becomes neon synthetic dreads or massive crimped extensions; accessories stack into rave gear. Ganguro keeps concealer at lips and eye rings, bleached blonde/silver hair, surf-candy props. |
| **Gyaru** (umbrella / later mainstream) | Light or no tan (shiro gyaru), honey-blonde salon curls, glam lashes, polished designer-adjacent outfits; no white concealer, no chocolate skin. Ganguro is the extreme tanned node of this family. |
| **Kogal** (precursor) | School-uniform coding, plaid, loose socks, brown hair, moderate tan. Ganguro abandons the uniform for neon separates, hibiscus and leis, and a far darker tan. |
| **Gonguro** (overlap) | Same visual language at higher tan intensity; an intensity dial, not a separate style. |

Where evidence is thin: flip phones, face stickers, and bead necklaces are attested by surviving photography rather than by the defining text, and should stay optional; the magazine-layout mode is supported both by the style's documented media and by period scans.
