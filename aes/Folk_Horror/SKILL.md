# Folk Horror

Folk Horror (also "Rural Horror") is a subgenre of horror organized around folklore, urban legends and the survival of old belief in lone rural places. Its stories put a protagonist in isolated countryside where they encounter malevolent entities, cults and rites whose logic predates Christianity and modernity alike. The term crystallized around 1970s British cinema — Piers Haggard is credited with coining it — and the canon runs from *Viy* (1967) and *Witchfinder General* (1968) through *The Blood on Satan's Claw* (1971) and *The Wicker Man* (1973) into the modern revival: *The Witch* (2015), *Haguzussa* (2017), *Midsommar* (2019), *Men* (2022), television like *The Third Day*, games like *Darkwood* and *The Excavation of Hob's Barrow*, and fiction from Shirley Jackson's *The Lottery* to Adam Nevill's *The Ritual*. Visually the style is dark, unsettling and mysterious: remote areas close to nature — rural towns, houses in the woods, clearings, moors — where the landscape itself is complicit. The documented written history of the term is thin (the wiki's history section is unwritten), so treat the 1970s media origin and the film canon as the firm ground and the visual language below as distilled from the surviving image culture around it.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is **muted earthly tones anchored by black and white**. Chroma stays low everywhere; nothing is candy-bright, neon, or cleanly saturated. Work inside this range:

| Role | Range |
|---|---|
| Peat / robe black | `#0E0D0B` – `#1E1B17` |
| Bark, wet earth, peat brown | `#3B2F23` – `#5A4632` |
| Dead-leaf russet, sepia amber | `#6E4A2F` – `#8A5A33` |
| Straw, wheat, candle ochre | `#B99B62` – `#D6C08A` |
| Bone, parchment, faded print white | `#E4DCC8` – `#F0EAD8` |
| Moss and lichen green | `#4C5A3A` – `#6E7A4F` |
| Deep forest green-grey | `#2F3A31` |
| Fog grey-green | `#8C968E` – `#A9B2A8` |
| Overcast pale sky | `#C9CDC4` – `#D8DBD2` |
| Cold dusk / night blue-grey | `#55606A` – `#6E7A83` |

Acceptable handling spans near-monochrome black-and-white (moonlit silhouettes over water), sepia/amber faded prints, green-grey fog fields, and pale washed daylight in which straw-yellow and cream dominate. Even the daylight variants read faded, flat and exposed rather than sunny. Warmth, when present, is straw, wheat or flame ochre against black; cold is fog teal-grey, never electric blue.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how the text documents them)

1. **Isolated rural land** — the defining condition: woods, clearings, moors, dirt tracks, a lone cottage or ruined stone shed at the tree line, standing stones and overgrown ruin walls, temperate deciduous or mixed forest, reed beds and fallow fields.
2. **Cults and rituals** — communal rite as subject: circles of robed dancers, processions across a field at dusk, offerings, kneeling supplicants before a costumed figure, wicker or hoop frameworks around a person, effigy burning or carrying.
3. **Animal masks and skins** — antler and stag masks, bird skulls worn on the face, horned hides, bark- and straw-covered bodies, pale cloth or papier-mâché faces on staffs; the masked human passing as creature or god.
4. **Darkness and shadows** — night woods, fog that swallows figures at three paces, vignette blackness at frame edges, silhouettes against a moon, things only half-lit.
5. **Paganism and old belief** — straw "green man" effigies, corn dollies and wheat sheaves, scythes, flower and paper wreaths and crowns, carved or inked sigils, symmetrical folk-art occult drawings on parchment paper, collaged snapshot "evidence" of masked figures.
6. **Ghosts, demons, goblins, urban legends** — the unseen or glimpsed entity: a distant line of watchers in a misted field, a looming shape between trunks, a horned figure in cream fog, birds scattering over an empty hillside.
7. **Superstition vs modernity** — the anachronistic friction: telephone poles and wires over bare trees, a photographed collage held in a modern hand, contemporary clothing intruding on ancient rite.

### Materials and textures

Organic matter dominates: moss and lichen on gnarled, twisted bark; leaf litter and mud; dry grass, straw, reeds; ivy creeping over rough stone ruin masonry. Cloth is undyed and hand-made — linen, wool, burlap, heavy black hooded robes, stitched hoods and wraps. Mask materials are bone, antler, woven wicker, bark, cloth. The image surface itself is a material: visible film grain, soft focus, haze and fog as texture, faded print color, vignetting, flash-lit night exposure, or — in the illustrative strand — dense black ink on cream paper and collaged photographic prints.

### Recurring subjects

Masked or robed ritual participants; a lone wreath-crowned woman in dark dress; horned or antlered humanoids; straw-covered effigy figures standing in meadows; gnarled moss-laden trees; ruined chapels, sheds and stone arches; cottages half-hidden in trunks; crows and small birds in pale sky; fog fields with distant standing figures; folk-art ink compositions of trees, faces and sigils.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Composition favors the communal or the swallowed: rings and processions of figures mid-rite; tiny humans dwarfed by landscape or fog; framing through trunks and branches; a central looming masked portrait; low horizons under heavy sky; strict symmetry in the folk-illustration strand; snapshot collage as an archival format. Lighting is diffuse and mediated: overcast scatter, fog glow, dusk, moonlit monochrome, or a single warm source (torch, candle, flash) carving subject out of black. Flat pale daylight is permitted but must feel exposed and wrong — brightness as vulnerability, not cheer. Depth comes in layers: fog bands, receding trunks, darkness closing the frame edges.

## Mood & Values

Dread of the unknown held at a slow boil. The mood is solemn, hushed, communal-secretive: ritual performed with conviction, nature regarded with awe and terror, the past refusing to stay past. The style's stated values are religion and belief, fear of the unknown, and the conflict of superstition with modernity — so images should feel like evidence of a belief system, not of a monster attack. Restraint over gore: the threat is masked, distant, implied, watched from the tree line. Melancholy emptiness is as valid as ritual fervor; a deserted track under bare trees carries the same values as a coven circle.

## Era & Origins

Emergent as a named media mode in 1970s Britain (Piers Haggard's coinage), drawing on older strata: collected folklore, pagan survival narratives, village urban legends, witch-trial history. It reacts against modernity's disconnection from land and against rationalist confidence — the countryside keeps what the city forgot. The 2010s–2020s revival (film, TV, games) restated the same grammar with analog-film nostalgia: grain, fade, haze, hand-made costume.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Land first: every image is anchored in rural or wild nature — wood, moor, field, track, ruin — even when the subject is a portrait or an object.
2. Keep the palette inside the muted-earth range with black and bone-white anchors; desaturate, fade, let fog or grain mediate everything.
3. Include a trace of old belief: a rite, a mask, an effigy, a wreath, a sigil, an offering — even a subtle one keeps the image inside the style.
4. Imply threat, never demonstrate it: masked, distant, half-seen; solemnity instead of violence.
5. Let the surface show its medium: film grain, print fade, flash-lit night, ink on paper, collaged snapshots.
6. Optionally stage the superstition/modernity friction: wires over bare trees, a modern hand holding old photographs, anachronistic dress.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: empty landscape with no figures; a single watcher; a full coven circle; object still-lifes (mask, dolly, scythe, sigil drawing); architecture (ruin, cottage, standing stones); animals (crows, stag); illustrative or collaged artifacts.
- *Composition*: wide landscape with tiny figures; centered mask portrait; symmetrical folk drawing; framing through branches; snapshot collage; low horizon under heavy sky.
- *Lighting*: overcast diffuse; pale flat daylight; dusk; night with one warm source; moonlit monochrome; flash-lit moss and bark.
- *Color handling*: near-monochrome, sepia amber, green-grey fog, faded straw-and-cream daylight.
- *Mood*: solemn ritual, quiet dread, melancholy emptiness, trance ecstasy, archival unease.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette, material culture, mediated light, belief-under-the-land — not any reference image. A sunlit meadow rite, a black-and-white moonlit silhouette, and an ink folk illustration can all be perfectly Folk Horror without resembling one another.

**Avoid**

- Saturated neon, candy color, teal-orange grading, HDR gloss, clean digital sharpness with no grain or haze.
- Urban or interior modern settings (subways, offices, apartments) as the primary frame.
- Churches, crucifixes, rosaries, priests, incense, Latin liturgy — that is Catholic Horror's vocabulary, not this style's pagan one.
- Chinese ritual idiom: joss paper, paper effigies, red lanterns, talismans, shrine red-and-gold.
- Gore, blood splatter, jump-scare monsters, body-horror close-ups; the terror here is implied.
- Cozy cottagecore warmth, whimsical fairy-glow, romantic pastoral picnics; likewise Halloween costume shorthand (pointy witch hats, plastic skeletons).
- Modern fashion styling, sportswear, glossy editorial posing.
- Tropical or alpine-exotic geography as default; the idiom is temperate forest, moor and fallow field (regional folk traditions may vary, but the earth palette and rural isolation must hold).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cottagegoth** shares rural darkness and black cloth but is domestic, personal and romantic-gothic: candlelit cottage interiors, dried herbs, gardening in mourning dress, melancholy décor. Folk Horror leaves the interior for the clearing — communal cult, masks, fog, ritual terror; it lacks Cottagegoth's coziness and interiority.
- **Haunted Mound** fixes on funerary earth: barrows, burial mounds, mossy gravestones, skeletal hands from soil, corpse-creatures. Overlap in gnarled mossy trees, but Folk Horror centers on *living belief* — rites, masks, effigies, wide rural geography — rather than the grave and its undead.
- **Horror** (generic) may be urban, interior, glossy, high-contrast, blood-driven and jump-cut. Folk Horror is rural, slow, earth-paletted, fog- and grain-mediated, with folk material culture (straw, antler, wicker, stone) doing the work of the monster.
- **Catholic Horror** overlaps on demons and cults but speaks through the Church: gothic architecture, clergy, icons, rosaries, possessed convents. Folk Horror is pre-Christian and pagan: standing stones, antler gods, straw effigies, meadow rites; no crucifix anywhere.
- **Chinese Folk Horror** runs on a different ritual kit — paper effigies, joss paper, red lanterns, talismans, opera masks, ancestor shrines, ritual red and gold in rural Chinese settings. Folk Horror's kit is northern-European pagan: moss, bark, antler, straw, wreaths, fog; if red lanterns or burning spirit money appear, you have left this style.
