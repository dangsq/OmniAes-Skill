# Gloomcore

Gloomcore is an internet aesthetic built almost entirely out of images: liminal, dreamlike photographs of forests, beaches, moors and water, shrouded in fog and kept deliberately quiet. It is a visual-first style — its fashion and music guidelines exist but are loose secondary branches — and its core act is finding beauty in silence: skies, coastlines and forests emptied of people and softened by mist. Two rules govern every Gloomcore image: the scene must be a natural or semi-natural landscape, and it must retain a dreamlike or liminal quality, the sense of a threshold between the ordinary woods and somewhere stranger. The resulting emotion is tranquil but slightly on edge — peace kept in places that are not truly peaceful. The style sits in a family of dark-nature and liminal internet aesthetics (Changelingcore, Cottagegore, Crowcore, Cryptidcore, Dark Naturalism, Dreamcore, Goblincore, Liminal Space, Vulture Culture), and distinguishes itself by being the foggiest, quietest and most landscape-obsessed member of that family.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The colour language is a muted triad — green, brown, grey — with fog white as the highlight and storm-black as the floor. Saturation is suppressed across the entire frame; no vivid hue ever occupies more than a pinpoint accent (a patch of dull yellow wildflowers, a rust stain, a pale blue garment). Value contrast is normally compressed by mist into a narrow mid-grey band; the exception is the "storm mode," where a near-black cloud mass sits over dim, desaturated land. Two filter families are canonical: grey casts and brown casts, plus a cool teal-green cast for deep-forest interiors.

Rule of thumb: desaturate first, then let fog decide the contrast. A correct Gloomcore image can sit anywhere between near-monochrome fog-white and storm-black, as long as it stays inside this muted register.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Fog, mist and rain** — the defining veil. Dull, clouded backgrounds; distance dissolving; drizzle, thunderstorms, wet surfaces.
2. **Forests, especially evergreen** — conifer trunks, layered canopy tiers, mossy understory, ferns; deciduous and rainforest woods also qualify.
3. **Seashores and endless water** — empty beaches, foam lines on dark sand, horizonless stretches of sea, lake, pond, river, stream.
4. **Dilapidated and overgrown structures** — crumbling bridges, plant-encroached trails, abandoned roads, weathered headstones and monuments, forest hideaways; nature slowly reclaiming manufactured things.
5. **Mosses, lichens, fungus** and unusual plant growth — the macro scale of the same world: a single fern frond on wet rock, a dandelion seed head against darkness, shelf fungus, odd sprouting forms.
6. **Shadowy beings, cryptids, strange insects** — optional presences at the edge of legibility: a silhouette in mist, a blurred figure, an animal shape in a fog field.
7. **Surreal intrusions** — a manufactured item placed inexplicably in nature (a computer in the forest), light or shadow falling in wrong places.
8. **Moors, caves, heathland** — open bleak ground under heavy sky, cave mouths as thresholds.

### Materials and textures

Wet moss and lichen crust, fern fronds, fungus shelves, bare and dripping bark, wet shale and stream boulders, dead grass, needle litter, foam on black sand, mirror-still standing water, fog-softened leaf mass. On top of the subject matter sits the texture of the medium itself: consumer snapshot surface — slight motion blur or fuzz, soft focus, visible grain, flat scan-like tonality, mild vignetting, occasionally an orange point-and-shoot date stamp in a corner. Nothing looks medium-format crisp. Where clothing appears, the documented wardrobe is matte natural fibre: sheep's wool knitwear, corduroy, cotton flannel, real leather, canvas satchels, tarnished silver rings and necklaces — oversized, boxy, androgynous, layered for cold damp climate, in the same muted greens, browns, greys and blacks, with at most one small colour pop.

### Subjects and figures

The landscape is the protagonist. People are absent by default; when present they are indirect traces (a path, a road, a grave, a leftover object) or degraded presences: a tiny back-turned figure in fog, a blurred childlike shape, a silhouetted deer or roe standing in a mist field. Animals never perform for the camera; they are silhouettes and shapes. Manufactured objects appear either as decaying relics being digested by vegetation or as surreal, unexplained intrusions.

### Composition

Recession into the veil is the signature move: a road, trail, stream corridor or shoreline running straight away from the camera until fog eats it. Other recurring structures: layered horizontal planes of canopy dissolving tier by tier into white; vertical rhythms of bare or mossy trunks; a single small specimen (seed head, frond, insect) centred against a dark void; horizonless water with no sky line; still water reflecting fogged trees; threshold framing where a dark foreground opens onto a bright fogged beyond. Crops are snapshot-like — vertical portraits and casual horizontals alike, seldom perfectly balanced. Empty centres are welcome; the void is the subject.

### Lighting

Diffuse overcast is the default: fog acts as a giant softbox, erasing shadows and direction. Variants include storm-dark skies dimming the land beneath, under-canopy gloom with faint shafts of greenish light, white-night or dusk greyness, and the documented "off" lighting — flat illumination where shadow should fall, or patches of light and dark in implausible places, giving an ordinary field an unnatural stillness. Direct sunlight, golden hour and hard cast shadows do not exist in this style.

## Mood & Values

Gloomcore values peace, liminality, the enjoyment of solitude, and a love for all things mysterious and dark. Its emotional contract is comfort inside melancholy: silence as luxury, fog as privacy, decay as tenderness. The mood is tranquil first and uneasy second — an ambient, low-grade strangeness rather than fear. Practitioners frame it as being at peace in places that are not truly peaceful: hiking, caving, gardening, reading, photographing, sketching gloomy landscapes, cryptid hunting, collecting trinkets, bones and feathers. Nothing in the image should shout; the style prizes restraint, stillness and the sense that something is quietly watching from just beyond the visible range.

## Era & Origins

Gloomcore is a creature of the internet aesthetic-community milieu (Tumblr-descendent, wiki-catalogued, flourishing through the 2010s into the 2020s). Its photographic body language descends from consumer point-and-shoot and early digital snapshot culture — soft lenses, date stamps, fogged lenses — and from Pacific Northwest fog photography, the misted conifer hills and stream valleys of places like Washington State's Mountain Loop Highway. Its narrative ancestors are eerie small-town and wilderness media: *Twin Peaks*, *The Ring*, *Silent Hill 2*, *Alan Wake*, *Night in the Woods*, *What Remains of Edith Finch*, *From* — works where fogged nature conceals something wrong. The style reacts against saturated, hyper-sharp, optimised feed imagery by insisting on quiet, decay, blur and absence. The wiki documents origins thinly beyond its media lists, so treat the precise founding moment as undocumented; the lineage above is the reliable part.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Nature first, liminality always: every frame is a natural or semi-natural landscape carrying a threshold or dreamlike quality.
2. Keep a veil: fog, mist, rain, storm dimness or photographic blur must soften or swallow distance. The veil, not any specific subject, is the constant.
3. Muted triad only: green, brown, grey, with fog-white highlights and optional storm-black; saturation suppressed everywhere; at most one dulled pinpoint accent.
4. Snapshot medium: soft focus, slight fuzz or motion blur, grain, flat tonality, casual crop; optionally a date stamp. Never crisp commercial sharpness.
5. Silence of subjects: no crowds, no posed people; human presence as trace, silhouette, blur or absence.
6. One degree of "off": a single surreal stitch — flat light in the wrong place, a manufactured object in the woods, a shadowy figure at the tree line, an unusual growth — inside an otherwise ordinary landscape.
7. Tranquil-on-edge: calm is the substrate, unease the seasoning; never horror spectacle.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject matter*: evergreen, deciduous or rainforest woods; moors and heath; empty beaches of black or pale sand; lakes, ponds, rivers, rocky streams; horizonless sea; cave mouths; overgrown paths, crumbling bridges, fogged roads; forest hideaways; macro specimens of moss, lichen, fungus, fern, seed heads, strange insects; silhouetted animals; blurred or back-turned figures; graveyards and monuments in nature; surreal manufactured intrusions. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
- *Composition*: one-point recession into fog; layered canopy planes; vertical trunk rhythms; centred solitary specimen against dark void; horizonless water; high canopy views; low stream-bed views; vertical or horizontal snapshot crops; subject near or far, centred or offset.
- *Lighting*: white fog diffuse; grey overcast; storm-black sky over dim land; under-canopy gloom with faint shafts; deliberately flat or misplaced light; dusk and white-night greyness.
- *Colour handling*: grey filter, brown filter or teal-green cast; near-monochrome fog-white; dark understory with one pale subject; rare dulled accent colour.
- *Mood*: from serene silence to quiet dread, always over a tranquil base.

**Avoid**

- Saturated or vivid colour, golden-hour warmth, blue-sky clarity, hard direct sunlight and cast shadows.
- Crisp hyper-detailed rendering, HDR gloss, studio lighting, polished commercial photography.
- Crowds, smiling posed people, fashion-editorial staging, urban interiors, cityscapes, pure architecture.
- Gore, jump-scare monsters, Halloween props, high-contrast horror staging.
- Neon, candy pastel haze, airbrushed surrealism, vaporwave or dreamcore brightness.
- Cosy cluttered cottages, warm rustic interiors, manicured parks and tidy gardens.
- Sunlit sparkling snow or bright high-key minimalism; bleak overcast snow may pass only if fully muted and fogged.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Dark Naturalism** shares the muted dark palette and stormy nature but keeps documentary crispness: sharp detail, dramatic natural light, sublime clarity. Gloomcore adds the veil — fog, blur, snapshot fuzz, liminal emptiness — and permits surreal intrusions that Dark Naturalism would reject as false.
- **Changelingcore** runs on fae folklore and uncanny childhood: storybook staging, changeling narratives, whimsy mixed with dread. Gloomcore has no fae story; its strangeness is atmospheric, its scale landscape-sized, its palette strictly green-grey-brown.
- **Crowcore** fixates on corvids, bones, feathers, taxidermy and occult still-life arrangements. Gloomcore's fauna is incidental silhouettes and strange insects; bones and feathers belong to its Vulture Culture neighbour, not to its fogged landscapes.
- **Cryptidcore** puts the creature centre-frame as grainy evidence photography. In Gloomcore a cryptid or shadowy being is an optional garnish at the edge of legibility; the fogged landscape remains the subject.
- **Dreamcore** is surreal but synthetic and often architectural: pastel or oversaturated haze, airbrushed pools, malls, plazas, nostalgic uncanny interiors. Gloomcore's haze is meteorological grey outdoors, its uncanny rooted in weather and decay rather than in bright artificial nostalgia. Against plain **Liminal Space**, Gloomcore is simply the natural-world branch: thresholds made of fog and tree lines instead of empty corridors.
