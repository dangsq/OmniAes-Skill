# Blokette

Blokette is a microtrend coined in late 2022 by the fashion-commentary podcast Nymphet Alumni and popularized on TikTok. Its entire premise is a binary fusion: take Blokecore's masculine-leaning sportswear — oversized football jerseys, vintage sports jackets, Adidas Sambas, drawn from a simplified read of English pub and terrace culture — and force it into the same outfit as Coquette's hyper-feminine vocabulary of mini skirts, knee-high socks and hair ribbons. The result is not a blend but a *contrast*: bold sportswear colour, logos and stripes sitting against the soft tones and textures of lace, silk and chiffon. The style is playful and self-aware, an experiment in gender presentation where the mismatch is the message. Because it lives on TikTok, its native image format is the casual outfit snapshot rather than the editorial photograph.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The colour language is a two-register system, and a correct image keeps both registers legible:

- **Team-colour block (saturated, sportswear):** navy `#16234A–#2E4372`, signal red `#C8102E–#D64541`, Brazil yellow `#F2C200–#FFD100` with grass-green trim `#009739`, maroon, sky-blue club tones.
- **Ground (neutral):** kit white and ivory `#F7F5F0–#FFFFFF`, cream `#F1E9DA–#FAF6EE`.
- **Coquette counterweight (pastel):** blush and powder pink `#F4C9D7–#FBE3EA`, baby blue `#A9C6E8–#CFE0F2`, pale lilac, butter yellow.
- **Supporting denim:** light-wash blue `#9FB8D0–#C4D3E3`, and slate/steel blue `#5C6E8E–#7A8AA6` for track tops.

**Rule:** one saturated team colour plus a white/cream ground, softened by exactly one pastel accent (a ribbon, a lace sock, a knit). All-pastel drifts into Coquette; all-saturated head-to-toe drifts into Blokecore or Ultras. The palette may swap team colourways freely (navy/white, red/white, yellow/green, maroon/sky) as long as the saturated-versus-delicate tension survives.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual authority

**Tier 1 — named in the style's motif list and fashion prose (core, must-read):**
- **Football shirts:** numbered kits with crests, sponsor logos, collar trims; worn oversized or shrunken/cropped.
- **Athletic sweaters and jackets:** track jackets with contrast piping and three-stripe sleeves, zip fronts, national or club crests.
- **Pleated wool skirts:** mini length; the corpus widens this slot to ruffled, tiered and lace minis as the Coquette inflection, but the skirt always reads girlish against the kit.
- **Ballet flats:** often in a pop colour (red, pink) with white socks.
- **Leg warmers:** stacked knit wool, usually white or cream.
- **Hair ribbons:** satin bows in the hair, ribbons tying braids or pigtails.
- **Adidas Sambas** (and football trainers generally): the default shoe; gum soles, leather uppers, stripe cages.

**Tier 2 — named in the introduction's fusion description:** mini skirts broadly, knee-high socks, lace / silk / chiffon textures, and the graphic language of sportswear: numbers, stripes, logos, crests.

**Tier 3 — corpus-observed inflections consistent with the text:** satin ribbon re-lacing replacing sneaker laces (a signature Coquette intervention on a Blokecore object); braided pigtails finished with bows; back-view shots that show jersey number and hair ribbon in one frame; shrunken zip track jackets over low-rise denim with a waistband peeking (a Waif-adjacent 2000s note); pointelle and cable knits as the soft layer; stripe-detailed knee boots; headscarves as an occasional accessory. Tier 3 elements are permitted flavour, never required.

### Materials and textures

The style is a texture argument as much as a colour one. Masculine side: polyester mesh kit knit, shiny nylon track cloth, terry towelling, wool pleats, leather sneaker uppers, gum rubber soles. Feminine side: lace, silk, chiffon, satin ribbon, pointelle cotton, cable-knit sock wool, ruffle tiers. **Rule:** every look contains at least one technical/athletic material and at least one delicate textile in direct contact or adjacency — ribbon against leather, lace against mesh, satin against nylon.

### Recurring subjects

The outfit is the protagonist; the wearer is its mannequin and often partly hidden. The corpus skews to young, feminine-presenting subjects, but the style's stated value is playful gender expression, so any body and gender presentation can carry it. Faces are optional: back views, torso crops and leg-and-foot studies are first-class compositions. Settings are stubbornly mundane — bedrooms, doorways, streets, train platforms, lawns, parking lots, walls — never sets.

### Composition, lighting, medium

Blokette inherits TikTok's snapshot grammar (the text prescribes no photography style; these conventions are read from the corpus): on-camera direct flash at night with grain, vignetting and hot highlights; flat overcast or bright blown-out daylight outdoors; plain indoor ambient light against white walls and quilted beds. Framings repeat: full-body outfit checks, rear views centring the jersey number, top-down or raised-leg foot studies of socks-and-shoe pairings, cropped torso details. Poses are unposed — leaning on a doorframe, one leg popped, a shoe held up to camera, standing slack at a platform. Slight softness, grain and imperfect exposure are features, not flaws.

## Mood & Values

Playful contrast and visual friction: lad meets doll, terrace meets boudoir. The mood is self-aware and a little ironic, but affectionate toward both registers — this is fandom tenderness (the kit is loved, not mocked) combined with girlish softness. Values: combining masculine and feminine elements, playful gender expression, visual contrast, and an anti-polish stance that prefers the imperfect snapshot to the glossy campaign. There is a nostalgic undercurrent: vintage kits and 2000s waif silhouettes filtered through present-day gender play.

## Era & Origins

2020s, online-first. Coined late 2022 by Nymphet Alumni, spread on TikTok as Blokecore's post-World-Cup football-kit revival met the Coquette/Balletcore ribbon wave. Commercial adjacency runs through Adidas, Miu Miu, Nike, Diesel, Balenciaga, Free People and Baum und Pferdgarten; iconic wearers include Bella Hadid and Ruby Lyn. Blokette reacts against single-register dressing — it refuses to choose between the pub and the powder room, and wears the refusal literally.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Pair at least one masculine-coded sportswear item with at least one feminine-coded delicate item; the contrast, not either half, is the subject.
2. Give one garment sportswear graphics: a number, crest, sponsor logo or stripe run.
3. Let footwear carry the fusion: Sambas or football trainers with knee socks/leg warmers, ballet flats with white socks, or trainers re-laced with satin ribbon.
4. Obey the palette rule: saturated team colour + white/cream ground + one pastel accent.
5. Obey the texture rule: mesh/nylon/wool/leather against lace/satin/chiffon.
6. Shoot it like a phone snapshot: mundane location, flash or flat daylight, grain and blown highlights welcome, casual posture.
7. Play proportion: oversized kit over tiny skirt, or shrunken zip jacket over low-rise bottoms.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any gender presentation; any everyday location; object-only still lifes of shoes, socks and ribbons are valid; back views and cropped details are valid. Composition: full-body, half-body, rear view, top-down feet, torso crop. Lighting: night flash, overcast daylight, sunlit lawn, indoor lamp light — always casual, never studio. Colour: any team colourway plus any single pastel accent; a monochrome kit with one pink ribbon intervention is fully valid. Mood: deadpan, dreamy, or grinning play. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**
- Head-to-toe sportswear with no delicate counterweight (that is Blokecore).
- Head-to-toe lace, ribbon and knit with no sportswear piece (that is Coquette/Balletcore).
- Tennis coding as the sport register: polos, visors, rackets, country-club whites.
- Terrace-hooligan props: held scarves, parkas, flares, pyro, crowd aggression.
- Modern gym athleisure: leggings, sports bras, performance fleece, chunky running shoes.
- Studio editorial polish: seamless backdrops, softbox glamour, heavy retouching, posed model stances.
- Stilettos, evening gowns, luxury logomania; blokette footwear is flat or sneaker.
- Murky gothic palettes or maximalist clutter; keep the two-register contrast readable.
- Copying any reference image's exact subject, framing or composition.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Its tell | How Blokette differs |
|---|---|---|
| Blokecore | Kit with jeans and trainers, pub/terrace context, zero feminine items | Blokette always inserts lace, ribbon, skirt, knee sock or ballet flat; no delicate textile means Blokecore |
| Tenniscore | Pleated tennis whites, polos, visors, cable knits, rackets, preppy polish | Blokette's sportswear is football-coded (kits, crests, numbers, Sambas) and collides with coquette delicacy, not country-club neatness |
| Ultras | Scarves, parkas, flares, stands and pyro, tribal aggression, dark palette | Blokette borrows the kit but softens it with pastels and bows; mood is playful, never tribal |
| Athleisure | Leggings, sports bras, hoodies, modern performance shoes, functional neutrals | Blokette uses vintage sportswear codes and deliberate gender contrast, never gym functionality |
| Balletcore | Wrap tops, tights, buns, flats and leg warmers with no sportswear graphics at all | Blokette shares flats and leg warmers but always anchors them with a jersey, track jacket or football trainer |

Quick recognition test: count registers. One masculine sportswear signal **and** one feminine delicate signal in the same frame, in team colour plus pastel, shot like a snapshot — that is Blokette. Only one register present, and it belongs to a neighbouring style.
