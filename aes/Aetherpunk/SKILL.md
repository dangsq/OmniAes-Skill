# Aetherpunk

Aetherpunk, also called Arcanepunk or Magicpunk, is one of the most diverse of the "punk" family of aesthetics. Its premise is simple and generative: high fantasy magic does the work that science fiction gives to engineering. Aether — a luminous, semi-material energy — powers sky-ships, weapons, vehicles, automatons and whole cities. Where most punk genres borrow their "punk" from dystopian critique, aetherpunk borrows solarpunk's logic instead: the punk is utopian elevation, technology in service of people who are suffering, a world presented as inherently good. Its conflicts are not man-versus-machine or citizen-versus-smog; they are a singular dark evil (a Dark Lord, a Lich-like antagonist) and the political question of who is allowed to access aether at all. Think of the gilded, sunlit grandeur of Asgard, the arcane civic machinery of Eberron and Kaladesh, the floating wonder of Laputa — marble, gold, stained glass, and magic running the grid.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is a two-layer system, and this is the rule rather than any single picture's scheme:

- **Structural base — stone and gold.** Limestone cream, sandstone, marble white and weathered grey stone carry the architecture; brass, bronze and emphatic gold carry ornament, machine housings, filigree and artifacts. Gold is not an accent here, it is a building material. Approximate anchors: golds `#B8860B`–`#E9D083`, brass/bronze `#A9793F`–`#C99754`, stone `#D9CDB4`–`#8B8378`, pale sky `#BFE2E9`.
- **One saturated aether accent hue**, chosen for its narrative meaning. The exact color of aether varies by world and is deliberately used to signal attitude toward it: neon blue-cyan (`#2FB9E4`–`#8FF2EC`) for scientifically harnessed, civic, upper-city aether; green tinges (`#55915C`–`#A8D08D`) for the undercity and for naturally occurring, organic aether; violet-purple (`#8E5CF0`–`#C9A1FF`) for rune-craft and arcane manipulation; gold-white radiance for divine or mythic registers. Regional base tints may shift (desert ochre `#D8A648`, for instance) but the stone-plus-gold-plus-one-glow structure holds.

Keep the base warm and the glow cool-or-vivid: the contrast between matte stone, warm metal and translucent saturated energy is the style's chromatic signature. Fully desaturated, monochrome, or soot-darkened palettes break it.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Gold everywhere.** Gilded roofs, gold-leaf trim, gilded machine casings, golden artifacts (cubes, vials, orbs, medallions). The infobox lists it first and the visual description repeats the emphasis.
2. **Pagan imagery and nature.** Runes, sigils, knotwork, standing-stone forms, sun discs, leaf and vine ornament carved into or inlaid in technology; organic, curvilinear (near-Art-Nouveau) framing around arcane devices.
3. **Magic-powered technology.** Sky-ships and floating vessels, elemental-bound engines and vehicles crackling with arcane discharge, automatons/constructs/golems as workers and guardians, magically powered weapons.
4. **Enchanted lighting and stained glass.** Flameless lanterns, glowing crystal cores, colored glass casting tinted light into interiors and plazas.
5. **Visible aether itself.** Energy rendered as luminous fluid, lightning-like arcs, rune circles orbiting a caster or machine, glowing conduits running through stone and metal.
6. **Monumental stone architecture.** Domes, spires, tiered towers, great bridges, layered cityscapes.
7. **Civic utopian life.** Crowds in public squares, banners and festivals, infrastructure plainly benefiting ordinary people — the visual proof of the genre's utopian value.
8. **The optional dark antagonist.** A singular sorcerous evil — cold darkness, necrotic shadow — never an industrial one.

### Materials and textures

Polished and filigreed gold and brass with warm specular highlights; carved, slightly weathered stone and marble; leaded stained glass; woven robes, cloaks and hanging banners; cut crystal and gemstone conduits; parchment and rune-etched metal plates. Energy surfaces are translucent, luminous, slightly electric — filaments, arcs, halos. The prevailing finish is painterly digital fantasy illustration: soft blended edges, visible brush shaping in clouds and stone, atmospheric haze stacking depth in cityscapes, concept-art and trading-card-art lineage rather than photorealism or flat vector graphics.

### Recurring subjects

Artificer-mages operating or channeling devices; constructs and golems towering over human figures; sky-ships in transit above tiered cities; a single luminous artifact as the heart of a scene; shrines and gates where pagan iconography merges with machinery; crowds of ordinary citizens witnessing or using magical infrastructure; robed orders and knightly guards as civic, not grim, presences.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Monumental scale contrast:** colossal machine, construct or vessel above small human figures, communicating benevolent power rather than threat.
- **Central glowing anchor:** the aether source — crystal, core, rune ring, arc discharge — sits as the compositional focal point and acts as a local key light on nearby faces and metal.
- **Layered aerial depth:** cityscapes built in receding tiers of spires, bridges and haze, with flying vessels silhouetted at multiple distances.
- **Light:** a base of diffuse daylight or bright open sky, punctured by saturated magical accent light; alternatively dusk and interior scenes lit wholly by enchanted lighting and stained-glass color. Shadows stay soft; the world is legible, not noir.

## Mood & Values

Wonder, generosity, civic pride. Aetherpunk expresses utopianism: technology-as-charity, magic in service of the suffering, a world designed to be inherently good. Adventure and conflict exist — a Dark Lord or Lich-like evil looms, and the politics of aether access (who is in tune with it naturally, who harnesses and exploits it, who is forbidden it) breeds rebellious factions — but the tone never curdles into cynicism. Even its undercity variant keeps luminous energy and ornate craft rather than despair. The emotional register is "the machine is a miracle, and the miracle is public."

## Era & Origins

Aetherpunk is a young genre, named in the internet-aesthetic taxonomy of the 2010s–2020s by analogy to solarpunk, and it crystallized around existing settings rather than a single founding work: Eberron and the Kaladesh plane in tabletop and card games, Piltover in *Arcane*, Asgard in the Marvel films, the Dwemer of *The Elder Scrolls*, *Atlantis: The Lost Empire*, *Castle in the Sky*, *Treasure Planet*, *Final Fantasy*, *Xenoblade*, *Genshin Impact*, *Fairy Tail*. It reacts against the default equation of "punk" with dystopia: where cyberpunk and steampunk interrogate oppressive systems, aetherpunk's rebellion is constructive — elevation, healing, access. Its visual ancestry fuses high fantasy's gold and stone with science fiction's vehicles, robots and infrastructure.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**

1. Magic is the power grid. Every machine must show its arcane source: glowing conduits, bound elemental discharge, orbiting rune rings, crystal cores. Power is visibly spellcraft, never combustion.
2. Gold is structural. Gild the architecture and the machine housings; stone and gold carry the frame, one saturated aether hue carries the energy.
3. Carve the pagan into the technical: runes, knotwork, sun discs, vine ornament on engines, gates and artifacts.
4. Let the aether hue mean something. Blue-cyan reads as harnessed civic utopia; green as natural or undercity aether; violet as rune-craft; gold-white as divine. Commit to one hue per image so the color functions as story.
5. Keep the world inherently good. Open sky, daylight, functioning public wonders, people benefiting. If an antagonist appears, it is a singular dark sorcerous force — cold shadow, necrotic purple-black — not smog, not factories, not police state.
6. Finish painterly: atmospheric haze, soft brush shaping, warm metal speculars, translucent glowing energy, fantasy card-art or concept-art surface.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be an artificer portrait, an artifact still-life, a crowd scene, a panoramic cityscape, a shrine, a vehicle in motion, or a construct at labor. Setting may be a sunlit upper city, a green-tinged undercity, a desert canyon metropolis, or a mythic mountain plateau. Cultural flavor may run Norse-mythic, Art-Nouveau civic, Mesopotamian, East-Asian pagoda-spire, or elsewhere — the genre is explicitly diverse. Composition may be monumental low-angle, intimate close study, or layered vista; lighting may be noon diffuse, dusk enchanted glow, or stained-glass interior color. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — magic-as-technology, gold-and-stone construction, pagan ornament, one meaningful aether hue, utopian tone, painterly finish — not any reference image. An image that never resembles a given exemplar can still be perfectly aetherpunk.

**Avoid.**

- Steam and coal as power source: boilers, smokestacks as the prime mover, soot grime, coal dust; Victorian costume shorthand (top hats, tailcoats, corseted engineer gear) that imports steampunk.
- Dystopian oppression kit: barbed wire, propaganda posters, ruin-porn decay, neon corporate signage, rain-slick chrome, cyberpunk wires and screens.
- Grimdark subterranea as the primary setting: torchlit dungeon corridors, mercenary grit, cramped underground gloom — unless deliberately working the documented green undercity variant, which still keeps luminous aether and ornate stone, not torchlit squalor.
- Generic medieval stasis: castles, knights and wizards with no industrial-scale magic technology layer.
- Hard science fiction: circuit boards, monitors, cables, firearms, chrome spacecraft.
- Industrial evil iconography: belching factories or machines-as-villain; the antagonist here is a dark lord, not industry.
- Desaturated, monochrome, or uniformly muted palettes; the style needs warm stone and gold against a vivid glow.

## Discrimination Cues

**Against Steampunk.** Both field airships and clockwork constructs, so decide by energy and politics: aetherpunk's machines are driven by visible magic — glowing aether, runes, bound elementals — and built of gold, stone and stained glass in a utopian key; steampunk's machines are driven by steam and coal, built of brass, leather and soot in a Victorian, often imperial or dystopian key. Gears alone prove nothing; ask what the gears obey. If the smoke is steam and the costume is Victorian, it is steampunk. If the discharge is arcane and the cornice is gilded, it is aetherpunk.

**Against Dungeonpunk.** The two share tabletop lineage, but dungeonpunk lives below: subterranean corridors, dungeon economies, torchlight and bioluminescent gloom, adventuring-as-grindy-industry. Aetherpunk looks up: open sky, sunlit civic plazas, monumental vertical cities, enchanted light. Verticality and light direction are the fastest tells.

**Against generic Fantasy.** High fantasy keeps magic rare, personal and medieval-stasis: a wizard's spell, a castle, a quest. Aetherpunk industrializes magic: sky-ships as transit, arcane engines as public infrastructure, constructs as labor, aether as a resource with access politics and rebellious factions. If the magic powers a society rather than a hero, it is aetherpunk.

**Quick recognition check.** Three questions: is magic visibly powering technology? is the built world gold, stone and pagan ornament? does the scene feel like a world designed to be good? Three yeses — aetherpunk.
