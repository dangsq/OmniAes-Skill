# Green Academia

Green Academia — also called Botanic, Botanical, Earthy, Nature or Natural Academia — is the splinter of the Academia family that specializes in the biology and earth sciences: botany, ecology, geology, mycology, zoology, anatomy, natural history. Where other academias stage the study of texts, Green Academia stages the study of *the living and the buried*: a pressed thistle taped into a journal, a drawer of small bones laid out in rows, mushrooms fruiting from a mossy log, a library aisle colonized by trailing vines. It values curiosity, education, nature and environmentalism, and treats collecting (rocks, gemstones, skulls, books, feathers) as a form of research rather than hoarding. The mood is quiet, attentive, slightly melancholic fieldwork: knowledge gathered slowly, by hand, outdoors as often as at a desk.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The color language is a desaturated natural register built on two anchors — green and brown — with paper and stone as neutrals:

- **Greens (dominant):** deep forest and viridian shadows (#1F3326–#24402B), moss and olive midtones (#4A5D3A–#6B7A4F), sage and lichen highlights (#8A9A7B–#A3B18A).
- **Browns (co-anchor):** bark and leather (#4B3A2F–#5C4633), rust, tan and dried-leaf ochre (#8A6F4D–#A98467).
- **Neutrals:** parchment and aged paper cream (#E8E0CE–#D9CDB4), bone off-white (#EDE8DC), weathered stone grey (#8C8F87).
- **Sparse accents:** muted gilt on cloth book spines (#B08D3E–#C9A227), dusty rose-tan of fungus caps and clay stones (#C9A08E–#D8B4A0), off-white blossom.

Rule: no hue may appear saturated or synthetic. Everything reads as if filmed on expired analog stock — matte, slightly faded, with soft contrast and a gentle vignette. A green-dominant frame is the default; brown-dominant (autumn leaf litter, rusted metal, leather) and parchment-dominant (journal spreads, specimen flat lays) are equally valid as long as the green/brown family governs the whole.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual authority)

The canon lists, in order of documentation: **nature journals** with handwritten ink annotations and botanical sketches; **plant pressings** and flattened flowers taped or laid between pages; **books**, especially old cloth-bound volumes with gilt spines; **bottles and jars** for specimens; **fungi**; **ink and quill**; **lanterns**; **satchels**; **feathers**; **trees and plants** (ferns, ivy, moss, blossom branches); **bones**; **insects**. The infobox elevates four key motifs above all: **books, skulls, fossils, ink and quill**. Locations canon: libraries, greenhouses and conservatories, ruins, archaeological sites, dense forests, quaint wooded neighborhoods, old buildings covered in vines, natural history museums, botanical gardens. Activities that generate imagery: hiking, collecting flowers/rocks/bones, drawing, reading, writing letters and poetry, calligraphy, journaling, pressing flowers between books, organizing curiosity collections, gardening, photography.

Honest note on evidence: lanterns, insects, satchels and skulls are canon by text but under-represented in surviving image samples (which favor journals, pressings, fungi, bones-as-specimen, stones and books). Treat them as fully valid motifs without assuming one canonical depiction.

### Materials and textures

Paper (toothed journal pages, deckled edges, tape hinges), pressed and dried vegetation, moss and lichen, bark, raw stone and cut agate cross-sections, bone and shell, glass jars and specimen bottles, leather satchels and book bindings, cloth book covers with stamped gilt, wool and corduroy clothing, weathered wood shelves and window frames, rusted metal, ivy and trailing vine. Surfaces should look handled, aged and slightly damp — never glossy, never new.

### Recurring subjects

Specimen documentation (a bone lay-out, a pressed fern, a labeled sketch); the working hand (writing, pressing, arranging, holding a branch); the collection (stones in a box, jars on a shelf, stacked green volumes); the overgrown interior (greenhouse ribs filled with foliage, library shelves draped in vine, statue swallowed by ivy); the fieldwalk (boots and wool socks on grass, a coated figure among trees); the study window (open book and teacup on a sill facing a rain-green garden); the fungus close-up.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

- **Flat lay / specimen grid:** objects arranged in orderly rows or clusters on paper, cloth or a painted ground, shot top-down.
- **Macro with shallow depth of field:** fungi, leaves, stone rings isolated against dark dissolved foliage.
- **Vertical interior vistas:** one-point perspectives down shelf-lined aisles or beneath greenhouse glass, often with a ladder or rib structure giving rhythm.
- **Frame-within-frame:** windows looking onto overgrowth; journal pages framing a pressed specimen.
- **First-person intimacy:** own feet on grass, own hands on the page.
- **Lighting:** diffuse overcast daylight, north-window light, dappled forest light, hazy skylight glow through glass roofs; occasionally warm lamplight on a page. Exposure tends slightly under, shadows soft and vignetted, highlights bloomed and milky. A moody "Dark Green Academia" register exists — rain, dusk, deep shade — but it stays green-led and never turns gothic.

## Mood & Values

Quiet curiosity, reverence for living things, patience, and an ethical streak of environmentalism. Knowledge is tactile and slow: handwritten Latin names, tape-hinged specimens, collections sorted by hand. There is companionship in it — plants and small animals as study partners, two pairs of boots side by side on the grass — and a gentle melancholy in dried leaves, ruined statues and faded film grain. The style romanticizes the *scientist-as-naturalist*: field notes over laboratory chrome.

## Era & Origins

A 2020s internet aesthetic, born from the Academia family on Tumblr- and Pinterest-adjacent platforms as its biology/earth-science branch. It reacts against both the purely literary focus of Dark/Light Academia (by putting specimens and fieldwork at center) and against purely decorative nature aesthetics (by insisting on study, labeling and science). It overlaps Goblincore in loving trinkets and "ugly" nature, but subordinates collecting to knowledge. Media touchstones range from nature documentaries (Planet Earth, All That Breathes, Fire of Love) to novels of scientific life (Frankenstein, The Signature of All Things, The Overstory, Ammonite), plus a folk-tinged musical register (Björk's Biophilia, Lord Huron, Florence + the Machine, AURORA).

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Fuse scholarship with nature in one frame: every image should imply either *studying nature* or *nature invading the study*.
2. Keep the palette inside the green/brown/parchment/stone family, desaturated and filmic; add gilt, bone-white or dusty rose only as small accents.
3. Light with diffuse natural sources — overcast sky, window, dappled canopy, greenhouse haze; soft shadows, mild vignette, analog grain.
4. Privilege hand-made evidence of knowledge: handwriting, sketches, tape, labels, pressed things, sorted collections.
5. Age everything: cloth bindings, weathered wood, rust, moss, ivy, worn boots, rumpled wool.

**Variation axes (a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image).**
- *Subject:* anything across botany, mycology, geology, zoology, anatomy, natural history, plus the scholarly apparatus and fashion around them — a fungus macro, a bone drawer, an agate cross-section, a vine-choked library, a greenhouse, an ivy-ruin, a journal spread, a curiosity box, a portrait in corduroy and knee-high socks, a train-window journal beside passing forest.
- *Composition:* flat lay, macro, vertical interior vista, window frame, first-person POV, candid portrait, specimen grid — all acceptable.
- *Lighting:* bright overcast → warm window glow → dappled sun → rainy low-key → dusk "Dark Green Academia"; all valid if shadows stay soft and green leads.
- *Color handling:* green-dominant, brown-dominant (autumn, rust, leather) or parchment-dominant (paper and bone) spreads, within the desaturated family.
- *Mood:* wonder, tenderness, melancholy, studious calm, quiet adventure.
- *Season:* blossom spring, deep summer foliage, autumn leaf litter, evergreen-and-stone winter.

**Avoid (style-breakers).**
- Saturated, neon or synthetic color; glossy plastic; modern tech (smartphones, laptops, LEDs); clean minimal Scandinavian or corporate interiors.
- Gothic candlelit chiaroscuro, black-dominated tweed tragedy, marble busts and Latin classics without nature (Dark Academia territory).
- Cream-and-gold neoclassical sunlit cafés, latte art, beige minimalism (Light Academia).
- Tents, campfires, enamel mugs, national-park signage, recreational camping gear (Campcore).
- Cottage-garden coziness, harvest baskets and sun hats with no scholarly/scientific layer (Gardencore).
- Muddy chaotic hoarding, trash-core clutter, ugly-cute maximalist grime (Goblincore).
- Tropical palm vibrancy, desert, beach, urban skylines.
- Harsh direct flash, HDR oversharpening, airbrushed perfection, studio seamless backdrops.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Green Academia |
|---|---|
| **Light Academia** | Light Academia is cream, beige, gold, neoclassical marble, sunlit cafés and Greek classics; Green Academia swaps marble for moss, cream for deep green, and literature for field science — its light is overcast forest light, not Mediterranean sun. |
| **Dark Academia** | Dark Academia is black/charcoal, candlelight, gothic architecture, moral obsession with classics; Green Academia keeps old books and ruins but floods them with living plants, daylight and scientific curiosity — melancholy without menace. |
| **Campcore** | Campcore is recreation: tents, fires, trails, enamelware, summer-camp nostalgia in brighter earth tones; Green Academia is *study* of the outdoors — journals, specimens, labels — with no camping hardware. |
| **Gardencore** | Gardencore cultivates: flower beds, watering cans, harvests, domestic cottage warmth; Green Academia documents and collects: pressed specimens, Latin names, bones and fossils, greenhouses as laboratories, wilder locations (forest, ruin, archaeological site). |
| **Goblincore** | Goblincore hoards chaotically and celebrates mud, grime and ugly-cute critters; Green Academia collects *curatorially* — sorted stones, labeled journals, specimen rows — and dresses its walker in wool, corduroy and oxfords rather than rags. |

When in doubt, apply the litmus: *does this frame look like a naturalist's desk, drawer or fieldwalk, rendered in faded green-and-brown film?* If yes — Green Academia.
