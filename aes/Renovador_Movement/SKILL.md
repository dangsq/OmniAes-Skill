# Renovador Movement (Os Renovadores)

The Renovador Movement is the Galician synthesis of European avant-garde form with stubbornly local content. Working from the 1920s through the 1930s — and, after 1936, from exile in the Americas — artists, writers, sculptors and printmakers around the Xeración de 1925 and the Xeración Nós circle took Cubist plane-building, Expressionist texture and Surrealist imagery and pointed all of it at Galicia: its Atlantic coast, its granite villages, its working people, its saints and its ironies. The governing conviction, articulated by Castelao, is that art reaches the universal *through* the regional, not by escaping it. The result is an eclectic but recognizable register: figurative always, modernized always, Galician always, and frequently funny in a deadpan, sideways way (*retranca*).

## Visual Identity

**Palette.** The style operates in distinct colour bands; a given work typically draws from one or two, never all at once.
- *Paper-and-ink core* (satire, mastheads, posters, book covers): parchment and newsprint whites `#F2EBDD–#E4D8C2` against ink blacks `#141414–#2B2B2B`, occasionally one flat accent.
- *Land band* (villages, fields, portraits): granite greys `#6E6A63–#8C877D`, umber and ochre earths `#7A5C3E–#A98A5B`, field greens `#4C6B3C–#6E8B4E`.
- *Sea band* (coasts, rías, marine surrealism): verdigris and teal `#2E6E6A–#4E9C90`, deep Atlantic blue `#1F4E5F`, foam white.
- *Mural pastels* (allegorical ceilings, monumental decoration): powder blue `#A9C3D6`, dusty rose `#D9A6A0`, cream `#EFE3D0`, gold-ochre architectural framing.
- *Civic accents*: vermilion red `#B33A2B–#C6402E` (regional dress, poster stars, shields); in geometric applied-work and murals, restrained Bauhaus-style primaries (red `#C0392B`, blue `#2C4E9E`, yellow `#E0B32A`) on neutral ground.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the movement documents them.**
1. *Galician life as subject*: working peasants and fisherfolk, the gaiteiro (bagpiper), romarías and communal meals, ox-carts, granite hórreos and cruceiros, white village lanes, sail-triangled coastlines.
2. *Graphic satire and caricature with retranca*: economical ink line on white paper, deadpan absurd juxtapositions, social and political critique in newspaper and magazine pages (the Nó s, A Nosa Terra, El Pueblo Gallego milieu), Simplicissimus-descended wit.
3. *Portraits*: frontal, dignified heads of writers, intellectuals and working people; solemn eyes, heavy regional cloth, minimal backdrop.
4. *Avant-garde formal borrowing*: simplified constructive planes (Cézanne-descended), flattened poster shapes, occasional faceting, dream-logic marine imagery.
5. *"Gallaecia" typography*: stone-carved, Celtic-tinged capitals with interior dots, spirals and flourishes (Gallaecia Castelo, Vila Morena families), set in modernist, Bauhaus/Ulm-influenced layouts on covers, mastheads and posters.
6. *Religious iconography redirected*: Virgins, Euchristic hosts and chalices, crosses and saints placed in secular, civic or protest contexts.
7. *Theatre, masks and puppetry*: stage sets with celestial geometry (moon phases, circles), painted masks, Barriga Verde puppet humour.
8. *Still lifes*: named by the movement's own accounts but thinly evidenced visually; treat as a permitted subject under the same palette rules.
9. *Murals, polychrome sculpture and applied arts*: figurative polychrome sculpture with expressionistic surface texture, ceramics, murals fused into architecture.

**Materials and textures.** Printed paper (newsprint grain, litho poster ink), ink pen line with hand wobble, oil paint with constructive visible brushwork or expressionistic impasto, carved stone letterforms, painted wood and polychrome sculpture, fresco/mural matte surfaces, ceramic glaze. Surfaces always read hand-made and printed, never glossy-digital.

**Composition and lighting.** Figures are monumental and frontal even when humble; coastlines sit under high horizons with sails as flat triangles; prints use generous white paper, decorative rule-border frames and typographic header bands; murals organize allegorical figures across vaults and lunettes with architectural coffering. Lighting ranges from soft, overcast Atlantic diffuse light in plein-air scenes, to completely flat even light in graphic work, to gentle modelled light in murals, to low chiaroscuro for evening gatherings, to stage light in theatre settings.

## Mood & Values

Dignity without sentimentality; intellect without obscurity; irony without cynicism. The style carries Galician regionalism as pride, not costume — labour is honoured, the sacred is recruited for civic argument, and humour (*retranca*) arrives sideways, with a straight face. Beneath the modernizing optimism runs an exile's melancholy: much of the mature work was made far from home, which deepens its tenderness toward Galician faces and shores.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in Galicia, Spain, in the 1920s–1930s (Generation of 1925), building on the 19th-century Rexurdimento literary revival and on the post-impressionist, Cézanne-filtered landscape painting of the 1880s–1900s predecessors. It reacts against two things at once: provincial academic folklore-painting, and a rootless cosmopolitan avant-garde that demands regions erase themselves. After the Spanish Civil War the style persists in exile (Argentina, Mexico, the United States) into the 1940s and beyond, and its typography lives on in Galician commercial and cultural design.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Local content, universal syntax: avant-garde form (planes, flattening, dream-logic) in service of legible Galician subject matter.
2. Ink-and-paper citizenship: even painted ideas should feel printable — bold contour, economical line, typographic confidence.
3. Lettering as identity: carved, dotted, spiral-flourished capitals in modernist layouts whenever text appears.
4. Dignity, not pity: working subjects frontal, weighted, monumental.
5. Retranca: irony delivered deadpan — an absurd detail treated completely seriously.
6. Sacred in secular: hosts, chalices, crosses and Virgins carrying civic or political meaning.
7. Hand-made surface: brush, pen, carve, print — never airbrush or CGI sheen.

**Variation axes.** Subject may span coast, village, portrait, still life, satirical scene, mask/theatre set, emblem, mural allegory. Composition may span plein-air realism, poster flatness, framed print page, or vault allegory. Light may span overcast diffuse, flat print light, stage light, evening chiaroscuro. Colour may span ink duotone, earth-plus-teal, mural pastel, or geometric primaries on neutral. Mood may span solemn dignity, deadpan satire, and marine dreaminess. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Full abstraction or non-objective geometry (the movement refused total rupture); generic Spanish folklore (flamenco, bullfighting) in place of Galician markers; Art Nouveau whiplash luxury ornament; sentimental picturesque costumbrismo lacking modernist simplification or ironic edge; analytic-Cubist fragmentation that dissolves the subject; angst-driven Expressionist distortion that destroys dignity; Surrealist automatism with no regional anchor; neon palettes, smooth digital gradients, photographic CGI gloss; contemporary corporate sans-serif typography.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Art Nouveau** shares decorative line and early poster DNA, but its whiplash organicism serves belle-époque luxury; Renovador line is print-satire economy and carved stone lettering, its subjects rural labour and civic argument, its layout modernist.
- **Caricaturism** is one branch here, not the whole: Renovador satire sits beside serious portraiture, typography and mural work inside a regionalist program, and its humour is retranca — dry, social, understated — rather than gag-driven exaggeration for its own sake.
- **Costumbrismo** (the 19th-century predecessor generation) is anecdotal and picturesque with pre-avant-garde brushwork; Renovador keeps the folk subject but adds plane simplification, flat poster shapes, modern type and ironic distance.
- **Cubism** dissolves objects into multi-viewpoint facets and monochrome ochres; Renovador borrows faceting only as simplification, keeping narrative legibility, regional colour (teal sea, green field) and print context.
- **Expressionism** privileges subjective distortion and acidic colour; Renovador's expressionistic texture (as in its polychrome sculpture) stays yoked to figurative dignity, moderated palette and social content, with irony replacing angst.

**Recognition checklist:** Galician subject + avant-garde simplification + printed/carved surface + dignified or deadpan tone + stone-carved Celtic-modernist lettering where text appears.
