# Corporate Grunge

Corporate Grunge is what happened when the anti-consumerist DIY look of Seattle grunge — photocopied zines, thrifted flannel, hand-scrawled flyers — was scanned, digitized and sold back to Generation X by Nike, Coca-Cola, Pepsi, PlayStation, JCPenney and every mall retailer between roughly 1993 and 2005. It is a *print-media* aesthetic first: magazine covers, CD jewel-case inserts, movie one-sheets, catalog pages, TV promo stills and VHS frames, all carrying the grain, halftone dot and copy-generation decay of physical reproduction. Its central irony is its subject: rebellion rendered as a standardized, parent-approved product. The look keeps grunge's grime but files off its danger, producing "controlled rebellion" — edgy enough to signal authenticity to skeptical Gen X eyes, clean enough to sit in a Sears catalog.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The base is a muddy, nicotine-stained earth range: soot black (`#12100E–#221E1A`), paper beige and parchment off-white (`#EDE6D6–#F5F1E6`), sepia brown (`#6E4B33–#93704D`), rust and ochre (`#9C4A1F–#C87F35`), olive drab (`#4E5240–#6C6F55`), charcoal and concrete grey (`#3B3A36–#8F8B82`). Photography is almost never full-spectrum: it is pushed into duotone or heavy desaturation, most often warm amber/sepia/rust, but cold duotones are equally valid — olive-green fog, cold steel blue, dusty mauve-rose — always muddy, never clean. Saturated color appears only as *stained print ink*: oxblood red (`#7E2A1D`), teal ink (`#2F6B62`), or a single acid accent bleeding through distress, never as flat virgin color fields. Rule of thumb: every hue should look one photocopier generation older than it was born.

### Typography (signature motif, most documented)

Type is the style's heartbeat: digital two-color vector imitations of analogue imperfection — typewriter faces (FF Trixie lineage), stencil caps, label-maker tape strips, stamp gothic, distressed confidential/rough faces. Typesetting is deliberately rebellious: inconsistent font sizes, unusual casing, shifted and rotated letterforms, broken baselines, letters overlapping faces or photo edges. Layouts pair one massive corrupted logotype or display word against clusters of tiny technical micro-text — credits columns, track lists, catalog numbers, spec captions — often with hairline registration grids, crosshairs and barcodes as paratext. Hand-scrawled scratchy logos and frayed handwritten notes form the informal end of the same system.

### Texture & Material

Rough texture is non-negotiable: photocopier grain, halftone dot, dust and scratch overlays, rust and corrosion fields, mould and water stains, nicotine yellowing, torn paper edges, ink splatters, tape marks, crumpled and re-flattened paper, VHS glitch and scan artifacts. Surfaces read as rusted metal doors, stained concrete, locker-room paint, old newsprint, jewel-case paper. Glitch/VHS artifacts are documented but should season rather than dominate the print grain.

### Motifs & Objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. Distressed/typewriter/stencil/label-maker typography and rebellious typesetting.
2. Grainy overlays, photocopy degradation, worn-out textures.
3. Collage imagery — cut-and-paste photo fragments, repeated faces, overlapping blocks, the digitized Xerox-flyer look.
4. Industrial decay — rust, mould, peeling paint, lockers, stairwells, warehouses, corroded signage.
5. Horror-grunge overlays: distorted or motion-blurred faces, torn edges, ink splatter, scratches, eerie fog.
6. Glitch and VHS artifacts.
7. Working-class grunge fashion as subject: oversized flannel, pre-distressed denim, combat boots, fishnets, bandannas, tinted oval sunglasses.
8. "Xtreme Kewl" skate crossover: scuffed boards, falling skaters, X-branding shorthand, high-speed action shots.
9. Print paratext: barcodes, catalog numbers, credits columns, registration crosshairs (observed convention of the medium; use sparingly).

### Subjects

Recurring subject matter is mundane Gen X life and marketed rebellion: teenagers in stairwells, swing sets, locker rows, mall interiors, band practice rooms; catalog models in flannel and thermal layers; skaters mid-fall; distorted or blurred portraits for horror-adjacent product; rusted doors and decaying industrial interiors as pure texture fields; product shots (sneakers, consoles, rollerblades, soda) dragged into the collage system rather than glorified.

### Composition & Lighting

Recurring structural tendencies (not templates): an asymmetric paper field — large off-white negative space holding one oversized logotype, one duotone photo block and scattered micro-text; a full-bleed decay field with a stamped or embossed emblem and scrawled edge type; cut-and-paste collage grids with vertical type strips and fragmented faces; duotone portrait plus narrow credits column; grainy flash snapshot with a hand-scrawled logo; black-ground ad banners of distressed color type. Lighting is print-native: flat scanned light, hard direct-flash snapshot, foggy diffusion, or high-contrast duotone with blown highlights and crushed copy blacks. Never glossy three-point studio light.

## Mood & Values

Controlled rebellion, manufactured authenticity, jaded sincerity. The style sells "raw" and "unfiltered" to a demographic that distrusts polish, so it must look effortless and slightly unwilling — never eager, never cheerful. A horror-tinged undercurrent (dread, blur, decay) coexists with a cosy community variant: grainy black-and-white warmth, parchment air, frayed handwriting that promises belonging. Irony hangs over everything: anti-consumerism as consumer strategy.

## Era & Origins

Emerges c. 1993 in US mass marketing and fashion catalogs after Seattle grunge breaks mainstream; peaks mid-to-late '90s across film posters, alt/nu-metal covers, console advertising and TV promo type; tails off by 2005 as Gen X Soft Club minimalism, Y2K Futurism and Frutiger Aero make grime look "low-resolution." It reacts against 1980s slickness — neon, airbrush, safe serifs — by digitizing punk zine DIY. The 2010s Tumblr "Soft Grunge" revival is a later, paler descendant, not this style.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Degrade one generation: treat every image as a scan or photocopy of a print artifact — grain, halftone, dust, scratches, stained paper.
2. Run a two-tier type system: one oversized distressed display voice (typewriter/stencil/label-maker/scrawl) plus clusters of tiny technical micro-text; break baselines, mix casing, shift letterforms.
3. Keep palette discipline: muddy earth duotone or paper field; saturated color only as stained, distressed ink.
4. Build by collage logic: tear, cut, overlap, misregister; no clean vector edges.
5. Choose subjects from mundane Gen X terrain or horror-tinged portraiture; products appear embedded in grime, not spotlighted.
6. Light for print: flash snapshot, fog, flat scan, crushed duotone contrast.
7. Optional print paratext (barcode, credits column, crosshair grid) strengthens the read.

**Variation axes.** Subject: album cover, movie poster, magazine cover, catalog page, sneaker/soda/console ad, skate-flyer, horror promo, fashion lookbook, locker-room snapshot. Composition: paper-field asymmetry, full-bleed texture, collage grid, portrait-plus-credits, snapshot. Lighting: flash, fog, scan-flat, high-contrast duotone. Color: warm sepia/rust, olive, cold blue-green, mauve-rose duotones, or cosy parchment-with-sepia. Mood: dread, jaded cool, cosy community, xtreme kinetic. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Clean vector flatness, smooth gradients, chrome or glass gloss (Y2K/Frutiger Aero); neon or pastel Memphis geometry and confetti squiggles; glossy softbox product photography; beveled chrome metal logos and heroic skull-and-lightning iconography (Metalheart); bare machinery/warehouse documentary with no print degradation (Industrial); cozy warm-neutral minimalism with clean type and zero grain (Gen X Soft Club); airbrushed bling gloss and candy saturation (UrBling/McBling); modern smartphone crispness, bokeh lifestyle shots, UI elements; symmetrical centered layouts with a single clean sans headline; pristine unblemished surfaces; 2010s pastel-faded soft grunge.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Gen X Soft Club** shares 90s warmth and comfort but is clean, minimal and grain-free; Corporate Grunge always keeps distress, sludge and corrupted type.
- **Industrial** shares decay and grey tones but celebrates machines, metal plate and functional signage; Corporate Grunge's decay is *print* decay — photocopy, rust-stained paper, typewriter type — in a consumer-marketing frame.
- **Memphis Lite** is playful pastel geometry on clean grounds; Corporate Grunge is earthy, dirty and analogue-degraded, with collage instead of pattern.
- **Metalheart** is high-gloss dark fantasy with chrome beveled lettering; Corporate Grunge's aggression is low-fi, matte and photocopy-born, its type typewriter/stencil, never polished metal.
- **UrBling** is glossy, airbrushed, saturated streetwear glamour; Corporate Grunge is matte, desaturated, grain-choked and deliberately unkempt.

Where the record is thin — notably the frequency of glitch/VHS artifacts versus photocopy grain in surviving artifacts — treat glitch as an accent, not a foundation.
