# Neo-Tokyo

Neo-Tokyo (ネオ東京, also "Neon Tokyo") is a Japanese subgenre of cyberpunk that imagines a future Tokyo of staggering technological achievement and simultaneous social collapse. It was codified by Katsuhiro Otomo's *Akira* (manga 1982, film 1988) and carried forward through *Bubblegum Crisis*, *Ghost in the Shell*, *Cyber City Oedo 808* and games such as *VA-11 Hall-A*, *Katana Zero*, *Stray* and *Shin Megami Tensei II*. Where Western cyberpunk often viewed Japan-dominated futures from outside, Neo-Tokyo critiques from within: bubble-economy overcrowding, corporate power, social conformity, and cultural identity dissolving into the machine. Visually this means one thing above all — a dense, vertical, sign-plastered city at night, glowing in the rain.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The color language is a **neon triad over a void**: at least two emissive families (magenta and cyan as the core pair, violet as the ambient wash) burning out of a ground of near-black and cool concrete gray, with red reserved as punctuation.

| Family | Hex range | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Electric magenta / hot pink | #FF2FA0 – #FF6EC7 | Dominant sign glow, poster and wall wash |
| Cyan / electric blue | #00E5FF – #4DD9FF | Counter-glow, tube lights, holograms |
| Violet / purple | #7A2FFF – #B026FF | Ambient haze, sky tint, fog color |
| Signal red | #FF1E3C – #FF2A2A | Punctuation: taillight bars, logos, jackets |
| Near-black void | #050508 – #0A0A12 | Shadow mass, silhouettes, polluted sky |
| Cool concrete gray | #4A5560 – #8A939C | Unlit facades, asphalt, overcast-day variant |
| Sodium amber (minority) | #FF9E45 – #FFB347 | Occasional warm counterpoint in alleys and interiors |

Rule of thumb: neon occupies a minority of the frame's area but the majority of its luminance; everything not lit falls to black or gray. A single-hue monochrome scene (all-cyan, all-magenta) breaks the triad logic; so does a sunlit warm palette. The cool-gray family licenses a minority variant of overcast daylight decay views where neon survives only in pockets.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **The vertical megacity** — megastructures and neon-lit skyscrapers stacking endlessly into a dark, polluted or fogged sky; the defining image of the aesthetic.
2. **Japanese signage** — kanji and katakana on vertical sign stacks, overlapping lightboxes and vinyl banners; the text names this the key element grounding the style culturally. Signage behaves as architecture-scale texture, not decoration.
3. **Holographic advertisements** — massive luminous projections and glowing script suspended between towers.
4. **Rain-slicked streets** — wet asphalt and puddles mirroring the neon overhead; the reflection doubles the light budget of every scene.
5. **Street-level sprawl and decay** — narrow alleyways, decaying infrastructure, tangled overhead cable bundles, pipes, scaffolds, patched concrete beneath the gloss.
6. **Flying vehicles and multi-layered highways** — traffic threading elevated stacks far above the street.
7. **Biker gangs and youth rebellion** — motorcycles (the red sport bike is the genre's heirloom), capsule jackets, goggles; text-canonical from *Akira*, though surviving imagery often reduces this to a parked bike or a speeding streak.
8. **The lone figure** — a small silhouetted pedestrian, coat collar up, umbrella, or crouching worker, used to measure the megastructure and state the alienation thesis.

### Materials and textures

Wet asphalt and puddled concrete as mirror; acrylic lightbox faces and gloss-painted sign cans; rain-streaked glass curtain walls; raw concrete, rust-streaked steel, exposed cable looms, paper posters plastered over plaster; fog and haze as volumetric material; emissive plastic tubing. Rendering mediums range widely and all are legitimate: 1980s hand-drawn cel anime (flat chroma fields, hard shadow shapes, gouache-feel backgrounds), painterly digital concept art with loose brushwork and atmospheric perspective, pixel art with dithered glow, and stylized low-poly 3D with emissive materials. The constant across mediums is emissive high-chroma light against dark mass, glossy wet ground, and grainy haze.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical emphasis and one-point perspective down a wet street or alley; low angles watching towers converge into fog; high angles looking onto a carpet of signs; foreground silhouette layers (eaves, railings, wire tangles) framing the depth; signage covering vertical surfaces edge to edge; scale contrast keeping humans to a small fraction of the frame; reflections repeating every light source on the ground plane; haze desaturating distance so foreground reads near-black, midground saturated, background dissolved. Lighting is practical and emissive: neon and holograms are the key light, with bloom around every tube and volumetric fog catching color. No visible sun. Night or perpetual twilight is the default; overcast cool-gray daylight is the accepted minority variant; interior scenes (bar counter, phone booth, shopfront, arcade) are lit by screen and sign spill.

## Mood & Values

Dark, melancholic, oppressive — yet seductive. The style holds beauty and alienation in the same frame: gorgeous light belonging to advertisements nobody watches, falling on empty wet streets. Its values are urban decay, technological overreach, corporate power, social alienation, and youth rebellion; the biker gang supplies the defiant energy counterpoint to the lone pedestrian's insignificance. Awe at scale and loneliness in the crowd are the two emotional poles.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in 1980s Japan during the economic bubble, a period of rapid urbanization and technological acceleration. *Akira* established the core visual and thematic language: a rebuilt Tokyo beset by corporate corruption, government conspiracy and violent youth gangs. *Blade Runner* is the key Western influence, but Neo-Tokyo inverted its gaze — Japanese creators using cyberpunk tropes to critique their own society's overcrowding, conformity and dehumanizing growth. The lineage runs through late-80s/90s OVA anime into contemporary games and *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners*, keeping the cel-anime color script alive.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Neon is the key light; everything else is shadow, silhouette or haze.
- Wet ground repeats every light source; unless deliberately dry, the street mirrors the sky's signage.
- Crowd the frame vertically; the sky is dark, polluted or fogged — never clear.
- Signage is language: kanji and katakana glyphs in vertical stacks at overlapping scales; add at least one holographic element in wide city views.
- Measure the megastructure: include a human-scale anchor (pedestrian, parked bike, car, phone booth).
- Layer decay under gloss: cables, pipes, rust streaks, patched facades, paper posters.
- Enforce stark contrast between gleaming emissive tech and gritty infrastructure in the same frame.
- Build depth with fog: sharp dark foreground, saturated midground, desaturated dissolved background.

**Variation axes.** Subject: street canyons, alley clutter, after-hours interiors (bar, arcade, phone booth, shopfront), rooftops and flyover vantages, cruising or parked vehicles, coat-clad pedestrians, biker gangs, graphic title cards with katakana logos. Composition: portrait canyon, wide horizontal sprawl, close-in alley clutter, interior looking out, high-angle down onto signs, low-angle up into fog, symmetric one-point or oblique. Lighting: night rain, twilight drizzle, foggy dry night, overcast gray day, interior sign-spill. Color handling: full triad saturation; cool-gray-dominant decay with neon pockets; near-monochrome magenta or violet washes kept alive by a cyan or red counter-note; flat cel chroma versus painterly atmospheric chroma. Mood: melancholic loneliness, tense rebellion, quiet after-hours calm, oppressive awe. Medium: cel anime, painterly concept art, pixel art, stylized 3D.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A dry foggy rooftop with no rain, or a gray daylight alley with three pink signs, still reads Neo-Tokyo if the density, signage language and light logic hold.

**Avoid**

- Sunny daylight, clear blue sky, golden-hour sun as key light.
- Vaporwave pastels and cream tones, classical busts, palm trees, flat mall interiors, glitch-art irony.
- Synthwave openness: horizon grid planes, striped setting suns, chrome wireframes, sparse lone highways, clean gradient skies.
- Biopunk organics: flesh-machines, sinewy tubing, gene-lab greens and browns as the dominant material.
- Clean utopian futurism: white curvaceous towers, tidy plazas, grime-free glass.
- Blank facades or Latin-only signage where kanji/katakana should anchor the scene.
- Wilderness, gardens, nature-dominated frames.
- Single-hue monochrome minimalism or chrome-and-black sparse geometry.
- Shadowless flat vector brightness with no emissive light logic, no haze, no reflections.
- Festive pastel daytime pop-city without decay or night lighting.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Cyberpunk (parent genre):** Neo-Tokyo is the Japanese-inflected branch. Tell: kanji/katakana signage as structural element, Tokyo-style alley density and overhead wire tangles, 1980s cel-anime color lineage, biker-gang youth culture, bubble-economy critique. Generic cyberpunk tolerates any megacity, English-only signage, heavier chrome and implant focus, noir detective framing; if the city reads interchangeable with a US or European sprawl, it is cyberpunk at large, not Neo-Tokyo.
- **Vs Biopunk:** biopunk's decay is biological — wet flesh, grafted tissue, vat greens and ambers; Neo-Tokyo's decay is infrastructural — concrete, rust, cable, paper — and its technology is hard-surface and emissive, not organic.
- **Vs Synthwave:** synthwave is sparse, horizontal and nostalgic — open horizon, grid plane, stripe sun, chrome, magenta-orange gradient skies, clean darkness. Neo-Tokyo is dense, vertical, cluttered, rain-wet and fogged; its darkness is polluted depth, not a smooth gradient.
- **Vs Vaporwave:** vaporwave is ironic, pastel, daylight or studio-flat, filled with consumer relics and classical sculpture. Neo-Tokyo is earnest dystopia: night, saturated neon on black, vertical density, and melancholy rather than irony.
