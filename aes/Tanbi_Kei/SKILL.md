# Tanbi Kei (耽美系)

Tanbi Kei — literally "aestheticism style" — is the branch of Japanese Visual Kei that dresses rock musicians as European nobility. Its costumes pull from Baroque, Victorian and Rococo fashion and carry the exaggerated completeness of theatre wardrobe: every sleeve, jabot and hem is worked, layered and ornamented. The style treats beauty as a constructed artifact: porcelain makeup, bright lipstick, ribbons and bonnets are applied generously, and male-to-female crossdressing is a celebrated core practice, framed by its wearers as magnified, overemphasized artistic expression rather than imitation. Because the look is so legible — a court painting walked onto a stage — it is one of the most cosplayed of all Visual Kei styles, and its canonical images are band promotional photographs: group tableaus arranged like aristocratic family portraits, solo performers posed with roses and fans, all finished with the soft polish of record-label photography.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is jewel-tone depth plus porcelain light plus metallic warmth. Core range: wine and bordeaux (`#5E1224`–`#8E2437`), crimson and scarlet (`#A61C32`–`#C03A2B`), funeral black (`#0C0A0D`–`#1A1519`), ivory and candle-cream (`#F5EDDC`–`#FBF7EE`), and antique gold (`#B08D3E`–`#D4AF37`) appearing as braid, embroidery and frame gilding. Accent range, used in smaller doses: royal and sapphire blue (`#23398F`), violet (`#5B3A8E`), rose pink (`#D98BA3`), and silver-ash or platinum hair tones (`#C9C6CE`). A given image may lean ivory-dominant (white fur, cream gowns), black-dominant (gothic leather-and-velvet edge), or red-dominant (crimson washes and rose brocade); the constants that bind the variants are gold threadwork, pale skin, and at least one deep jewel tone. Saturation is rich but aged — dyed fabric, not neon light.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the text documents them

1. **Elaborate European court costume** (Baroque / Victorian / Rococo): brocade frock coats and justaucorps-style jackets, military hussar braid, corseted gowns with trains, capes and mantles with embroidered edges, tricorn and feathered hats.
2. **Beauty-maximizing makeup**: porcelain white base, heavily lined and shadowed eyes, drawn brows, bright red or rose lipstick.
3. **Head ornament**: hair ribbons, bonnets, lace headdresses, veils, miniature top hats, crowns and tiaras, plumed hats.
4. **Theatrical royalty**: the props and poses of court portraiture — roses held or worn, folding fans, cane-sceptres, gilded settees and thrones, candelabra, chandeliers.
5. **Androgyny and crossdressing**: male performers in gowns, bonnets and hime-cut wigs; gender ambiguity as deliberate artistic overstatement.

Image-corroborated secondary motifs: roses as bouquets, corsages and textile prints; white fur stoles and feather boas; jabots and ruffled shirt fronts; pearl strands, cameos and cross pendants; damask wallpaper and painted canvas backdrops; ornate script logotypes with scrollwork and fleur ornament set into the promotional frame.

### Materials and textures

Floral brocade and jacquard with visible weave relief, velvet pile, satin sheen, chantilly and chemical lace, chiffon frills, metallic gold braid and bullion embroidery, fur and feather trim, pearl and glass-bead jewelry. Against these heavy textiles sits the style's other signature surface: skin retouched to matte porcelain smoothness, and wig fiber drawn into glossy, gravity-defying curls. Texture contrast is the point — stiff goldwork next to soft fur next to flat white face.

### Recurring subjects

The dominant subject is the band: a group of four to six costumed performers arranged in a single formal tableau. Close behind is the solo performer portrait, full-body or three-quarter, and the duo or paired portrait. Performers may be male, female, or deliberately unreadable between the two; cosplayers recreating band looks belong to the same visual family. At the dark edge of the range sit gothic-tinged solos — black leather, top hat, a skull prop on a plinth — still wearing the style's makeup and ornament.

### Composition and lighting

Composition is staged, frontal and hierarchical, borrowing from court family portraiture: a central figure seated on a velvet sofa or throne while others stand, lean or kneel around them in a balanced cluster; or a lone figure centered against a seamless or painted ground. Frames run from horizontal promotional banners to vertical poster crops. Lighting varies across four accepted modes: soft diffused high-key on white or cream seamless; low-key painterly chiaroscuro against mottled dark canvas; warm chandelier or candlelight glow inside antique interiors; and occasional saturated monochrome washes, most often crimson. The finish is promo-photographic: soft focus, even flattering light on faces, slightly aged or richly graded color, no snapshot accident.

## Mood & Values

Tanbi Kei expresses aristocratic decadence and beauty-as-artifice: the feeling of a costume from a play, elegance pushed to royalty, grandeur with a melancholic, decaying undertone. Its values are devotion to beauty ("tanbi" itself), craft and detail density over utility, gender as performance and artistic exaggeration, and a romantic homage to European antiquity filtered through Japanese stage culture. Nothing in the style is casual; everything is intended, arranged and adorned.

## Era & Origins

The style originates with the musicians who founded it inside Japan's Visual Kei scene; the wiki gives no precise dates, but its band roster — Malice Mizer and Mana through Moi Dix Mois, Kaya, Versailles, Megaromania, Phantasmagoria and later acts — places its crystallization in the 1990s–2000s Japanese rock world, with Mana as the iconic crossdressing figure. It reacts against Visual Kei's punk and metal roots by turning toward European antique fashion, and it shares influences with the rise of Lolita fashion. Its recognizability made it a staple of Visual Kei cosplay, which keeps the style circulating in promotional and convention photography alike.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Costume first: the silhouette must read as 17th–19th century European court or theatre wardrobe — frock coat, corseted gown, mantle, tricorn — never modern clothing.
2. Enforce a detail-density floor: lace, brocade, ruffles, braid and jewelry in layers; an unornamented garment means the style has failed.
3. Keep palette discipline: wine, black, ivory and gold as the spine, jewel-tone accents allowed, neon forbidden.
4. Paint the face: porcelain base, defined lined eyes, bright lips; the face is a mask of constructed beauty.
5. Build hair as architecture: teased volume, long curls, wigs in blonde, ash, brown, red, pink or violet, crowned with ribbons, bonnets, veils or hats.
6. Stage, don't snapshot: arrange subjects as a conscious tableau before a damask interior, gilded furniture, painted canvas or clean seamless ground.
7. Finish like a record-label promo: soft focus, polished skin, rich but aged color.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- *Subject*: group band tableau, solo portrait, duo; male, female or androgynous performers; cosplayers; optionally a prop-led still figure with roses, fan, sceptre or skull.
- *Composition*: symmetrical court tableau, diagonal cluster, centered solo full-body, bust-length portrait dominated by hat and hair; horizontal banner or vertical poster crop.
- *Lighting*: high-key white seamless, dark painterly chiaroscuro, warm chandelier candlelight, or a saturated single-color wash.
- *Color handling*: ivory-dominant, black-dominant gothic, wine-dominant, or gold-warm; accent blue, violet or pink in trims and hair.
- *Mood*: regal serenity, melancholic decadence, coquettish playfulness (the pinker, bonnet-heavy end), or martial aristocracy (the braided military-jacket end).
- *Medium*: promotional-style photography by default; painterly digital rendering acceptable if texture density and finish hold.

**Avoid**

- Modern casual wear: denim, sneakers, t-shirts, plain suits; punk studs and spikes as the dominant outfit.
- Neon, cyber or UV-glow color; club lighting.
- Candid snapshot aesthetics: harsh on-camera flash, street daylight, unplanned poses.
- Period-accurate historical reenactment without Visual Kei makeup and wigs — that slides into Rococo costume, not Tanbi Kei.
- Sweet Lolita coordinates as the main read: knee-length bell skirts, cotton novelty prints, plush props.
- Minimalist restraint: single-color unornamented garments, no jabot or jewelry — that is Aristocrat.
- Bare skin and sexualized framing; Tanbi Kei covers and layers the body.
- Grunge, distress, mud, battle damage.
- Flat vector cartoon or low-detail CGI that cannot carry brocade, lace and goldwork.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Its tell | Tanbi Kei's counter-tell |
|---|---|---|
| Aristocrat | Mature, restrained elegance; long slim monochrome silhouettes in black, white, wine; hats and canes with little ornament | Maximal gold brocade, jabots, ruffles, crowns; androgynous crossdressing; theatrical excess |
| Lolita | Street fashion: knee-length bell skirt on a petticoat, coordinated bonnet or headbow, modest covered styling, no stage context | Stage costume with trains, frock coats and hussar braid; heavy Visual Kei makeup; band-promo framing; male performers in gowns |
| Rococo | Historical 18th-century fashion or painting: pastel powders, panniers, period-correct coiffures | Modern Japanese stage reinterpretation: dyed teased wigs, white-face makeup, platform boots, jewel-tone depth, photographic promo finish |
| Visual Kei (umbrella) | Punk leather, studs, modern streetwear, cyber or oshare-kei color pop | Specifically the European-antique-court subset: if the outfit reads as punk or clubwear without court costume, it is Visual Kei but not Tanbi Kei |

When in doubt, apply the two-part test: does the outfit read as European court theatre wardrobe rather than any modern or street fashion, and is the beauty deliberately artificial — porcelain skin, bright lips, architectural hair? Both yes, with dense ornament throughout, and the image is Tanbi Kei.
