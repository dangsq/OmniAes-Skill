# Grime Art (Zombie Effect)

Grime Art is a digital parasitism: a normal photograph — usually a portrait — stays exactly itself, except that someone has drawn cartoon goo all over the face. The style emerged in 2015 with Richie Velasquez (online: deladeso), went viral through Getter's dubstep videos "Head Splitter" and "Rip N Dip," and spread as a mobile-app folk practice on Instagram and Tumblr, later cemented by a 2018 PicsArt challenge. Its whole statement is anti-beauty: a deliberate, playful defacement of the curated selfie economy, turning flattering portraits into melting, zombie-like grotesques that are simultaneously horrifying and funny. The defining act is therefore *layered*: photographic reality underneath, flat crude digital doodle on top, and the seam between the two is the aesthetic.

## Visual Identity

### Substrate and overlay logic
The base is a pre-existing-looking image: a snapshot portrait, a group party photo, a full-body street shot, a classical painting, a found cartoon character, or printed matter such as a magazine cover. The photograph keeps its own grain, focus, color cast and lighting — including black-and-white conversion, harsh flash, club dimness or overcast daylight. Onto this, opaque flat digital paint is applied only to flesh: face, neck, hands, sometimes exposed arms. Clothing, props and surroundings stay photographic. The overlay reads as finger- or stylus-drawn in a mobile editing app: crisp blobby silhouettes, opaque matte fills, little or no shading, at most a single highlight dot or a darker contour line. The lighting mismatch between photo and paint is diagnostic and intentional.

### Palette
The goo never takes natural skin tones. One vibrant, slightly sickly hue usually dominates the melt per image, chosen to clash with the photo's own palette:

| Role | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Slime pink | `#FF8FC0`–`#FFB3D1` | the most characteristic goo color |
| Acid green | `#6BE32A`–`#9CFF57` | sickly, toxic variant |
| Mint / teal | `#3FE0C5`–`#7FF0DC` | cool counterpart, often on B&W photos |
| Electric blue | `#2E9BFF`–`#6FC4FF` | secondary goo or eye/lip accent |
| Bright purple | `#8A2BE2`–`#B46BFF` | patches, eyebags, sockets |
| Sickly yellow | `#FFD21F`–`#FFE86B` | cheese/pizza melt variant |
| Glow white | `#FFFFFF` | solid luminous eyes, no iris |
| Iridescent rainbow | full pastel spectrum | known variant: drips run through a holographic gradient |
| Backdrop flats | deep green `#2F6B3F`, mustard `#D9A62E`, or pastel washes `#FFE86B → #FF9EC4 → #9AD7FF` | only when the background is replaced |

Accent colors (red pepperoni dots, pink lips, black outlines) sit inside the goo mass. When the background is swapped out, it becomes either a flat poster color with Memphis-style geometry (circle, wedge, triangle), a soft pastel gradient, or a wallpaper of squiggle lines — but an untouched original photo background is equally correct.

### Signature motifs, in order of definitional weight
1. **Dripping, melting slime over the face** — the core. Sagging goo masses obscure parts of the face, run downward in stalactite trails with rounded teardrop terminals, and may pool or splat on surfaces below (bench, floor, chin, collar). Gravity is always true for drips.
2. **Cartoonishly exaggerated features** — large droopy eyebags, multiple sets of eyes, wide unsettling grins with drawn teeth.
3. **Solid glowing white eyes** — irises replaced by blank luminous orbs, or eye sockets hollowed into dripping cavities.
4. **Gross-out skin textures** — the goo surface patterned like pizza (dots of pepperoni), vomit (lumpy pustules and blobs), cracked plaster, or filled with squiggle scribble hatching.
5. **Vibrant, sickly color** — the unnatural palette above, applied flat.
6. **Cartoonish gore and zombie-like details** — exposed "flesh," dangling strands, star-shaped splats, but always drawn, never photoreal.
7. **Dripping typography** (secondary, corpus-documented) — hand-lettered captions or mastheads that themselves melt ("IM FINE"-type ironic one-liners under a destroyed face).
8. **Decorative squiggle ribbons and poster geometry** (secondary) — goo-colored ribbons winding around the body, or flat shapes framing the portrait in poster layouts.

### Recurring subjects
Stranger selfies and street portraits; celebrity and meme likenesses; party and group snapshots; seated or standing full-body figures; classical paintings paraphrased into melts; cartoon characters zombified; magazine covers and printed matter defaced and then re-photographed as objects. The subject is almost always frontal and aware of the camera, which makes the defacement feel personal.

### Composition and lighting
Portrait orientation and centered framing dominate: head-and-shoulders or waist-up, subject facing the viewer. Full-body and still-life-of-printed-matter compositions exist as wider variants. The photograph's lighting is whatever the snapshot had — flash flattening, dim club color, soft daylight, or monochrome studio tone — while the painted layer ignores all of it. Drips define the vertical axis of the composition; pools and splats anchor the bottom edge. Depth stays photographic; the goo sits on the picture plane like a sticker.

## Mood & Values

Grotesque transformation, anti-beauty, surrealism and digital manipulation are the stated values. The emotional register is horror-comedy: repulsion delivered as a joke, often underscored by deadpan ironic captions. There is a self-deprecating, party-sketchbook humor — the melting face as a portrait of feeling not fine while saying "IM FINE." The style also carries a democratizing ethos: it was made with phone apps, by non-professionals, in public challenges, and its crude line quality is a feature, not a flaw. Above all it is a rejection of idealized, filtered, curated online imagery: instead of smoothing the face, Grime Art liquefies it.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s internet culture, originated 2015 by Richie Velasquez (deladeso). Viral exposure came from Getter's "Head Splitter" music video (and "Rip N Dip"), tying the style early to dubstep and hip-hop visual scenes — album covers, crew photos, festival snapshots. It then proliferated on Instagram and Tumblr as a community practice and was formalized by PicsArt's 2018 Grime Art challenge, expanding from music scenes to celebrity portraits, memes and everyday selfies. It reacts against two things simultaneously: glossy influencer self-presentation and polished professional digital art.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Photo first.** Build or imply a believable photograph as substrate — snapshot grain, casual framing, ordinary clothing and settings — and keep it legible beneath the paint.
2. **Selective zombification.** Paint goo only on flesh (face, hands, exposed skin). Let clothing, hair edges, props and background remain photographic so the overlay reads as an invasion.
3. **Gravity-true melt.** Every slime mass runs downward: stalactite drips with rounded terminals, strands, pools and splats on the nearest surface below.
4. **Flat paint over real light.** The goo is opaque, matte, near-unshaded cartoon color that disregards the photo's lighting; at most one highlight dot or contour line per mass.
5. **Sabotage the face by recipe.** Choose among: solid glowing white eyes; hollow dripping sockets; heavy droopy eyebags; extra pairs of eyes; a wide unsettling grin; nose or mouth erased by goo.
6. **One dominant goo hue.** Pick a single vibrant sickly color for the melt (pink, acid green, mint, blue, purple, yellow), or the iridescent rainbow variant; make it clash with the photo palette.
7. **Add a gross texture pass.** Pepperoni dots, pustule bumps, crack lines, or squiggle scribble fill inside the goo mass — never a smooth plastic surface.
8. **Caption optionally, ironically.** If text appears, it drips or is hand-scrawled, and undercuts the image with deadpan humor.
9. **Background is free.** Keep the original scene, or replace it with a flat poster color plus Memphis geometry, a pastel gradient, or a squiggle wallpaper.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject:* stranger selfie, celebrity likeness, meme figure, group party shot, seated or standing full body, classical painting, cartoon character, magazine/printed matter, plain object.
- *Photo mode:* color, black-and-white, grainy flash, club dim, daylight, studio.
- *Goo color and texture:* any single hue in the palette table or rainbow; smooth blob, pepperoni, pustules, cracked, scribble-hatched.
- *Face edit:* white eyes, eyebags, multi-eye, grin, fully masked socket melt.
- *Composition:* tight headshot, waist-up, full body, object still life, poster layout with shapes and caption.
- *Mood dial:* goofy ↔ genuinely unsettling; ironic caption present or absent.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A portrait of an unknown elder, a dog, a statue or a Renaissance madonna are all valid subjects if the substrate-overlay logic, palette and melt grammar hold.

**Avoid**

- Glitch artifacts as the distortion language: pixel sorting, RGB channel split, scanlines, datamoshing, compression mush.
- A wholly homogeneous illustration where the goo is native to the character design; the tension of paint *over* a pre-existing image must survive.
- Photoreal gore: real blood, wounds, viscera. Gore here is always cartoon goo.
- Airbrushed beauty retouching, smooth gradient skin, flattering filters.
- Muted, earthy or desaturated goo; the melt must be vibrantly unnatural.
- Drips rising upward or floating blobs detached from gravity (decorative winding ribbons in the background are the one exception).
- Goo spread uniformly across the whole frame; selectivity onto flesh is what makes it read.
- Intact elegant typography; any lettering must melt or look hand-scrawled.
- 3D-render gloss, bevels, subsurface scattering or realistic wetness on the slime.
- Kaleidoscopic all-over pattern or fractal swirls replacing the portrait; pattern belongs to backgrounds only.
- Rendered dreamlike scenes with impossible architecture or illusionistic depth in the goo.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Glitch Art:** Glitch corruption is machine-born and systemic — horizontal tearing, channel offsets, pixel sorts across the entire frame, including background. Grime Art's corruption is hand-drawn, organic and localized to flesh: rounded drips, flat opaque paint, no digital noise. If the distortion looks like a broken file, it is Glitch; if it looks like a drawn melting, it is Grime.
- **Vs Caricaturism:** Caricature redraws the whole head with exaggerated proportions in one consistent drawn style, with no photograph underneath. Grime Art keeps the photographic substrate visibly intact and adds exaggeration as a separate goo layer — extra eyes, eyebags, grins, melt — rather than reconstructed anatomy.
- **Vs Psychedelia:** Psychedelia is all-over: swirling fractal fields, kaleidoscopic color, transcendental glow, 1960s poster lettering, often non-representational. Grime borrows the acid palette but stays anchored to a snapshot portrait and a gross-out body narrative; any pattern is background wallpaper, never the substance of the subject.
- **Vs Surrealism:** Fine-art Surrealism renders impossible juxtapositions illusionistically, with coherent dream-space depth and painterly finish. Grime Art is anti-skill mobile doodle: flat paint stuck onto a photo, humor of the melting body rather than of the scene, zero rendered depth in the goo. Where Surrealism dreams, Grime Art pranks.

**One-line recognition test:** a real photograph of a person, still real everywhere except the face — and the face is flat, bright, dripping cartoon goo with wrong eyes. That seam is Grime Art.
