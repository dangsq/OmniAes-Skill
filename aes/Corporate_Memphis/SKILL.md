# Corporate Memphis

Corporate Memphis is the flat, geometric vector illustration style that became the house look of major technology companies in the late 2010s and the COVID-lockup era of the early 2020s. It is a specialized subgenre of Flat Design optimized for mass production: circles, rectangles and arcs assembled in Adobe Illustrator, small in file size, infinitely scalable, trivially animatable. Its human figures are deliberately de-individualized — huge torsos, pinheads, rubber-hose limbs, faces reduced to a mouth or nothing — so that they function as universal placeholders rather than persons. The name is a pejorative nod to the 1980s Memphis Design group, and the style carries a double life: earnestly deployed by Facebook, Google, YouTube, Slack, Lyft, Airbnb, Discord, Duolingo, Headspace and Kroger to seem friendly and inclusive, and simultaneously mocked online as "Globohomo" — soulless, sanitized, homogenized corporate cheer. Both readings are part of its identity: an image in this style should look frictionlessly joyful, and slightly, suspiciously perfect.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is a *limited* palette of solid, high-contrast fills: either a primary-leaning set (red, blue, green, yellow, pink) or a soft pastel set, with blue, purple and yellow as the style's gravitational center. Fills are flat and matte; color contrast between adjacent shapes replaces line work and shading. Backgrounds are single solid fields — off-white, pale blue, blush, or one saturated color carrying the whole canvas. Skin is a color choice, not a biological fact: blue, purple and green sit alongside tan, brown and peach tones, often mixed within a single ensemble to signal diversity without assigning identity.

Typical ranges (distilled guidance, not a fixed swatch):

- Blues: cobalt to periwinkle, roughly `#4D68F0` – `#7FA8FF`; deep field blues near `#2B4BD8`
- Purples: violet to lavender, `#6B4FA0` – `#B39DF5`
- Yellows: marigold to cream, `#F5C044` – `#FFE9A8`
- Pinks/corals: `#F26D85` – `#FFB3C1`, coral accents near `#E8604C`
- Greens/teals: `#2FA37C` – `#8FE0C8`
- Neutral fields: off-white `#F7F5F2`, pale sky `#EAF2FB`, blush `#FBEDE6`
- Non-representational skin: blue `#5A8DFF`, purple `#8F6FD8`, green `#6FC29A`, beside natural tan/brown `#C98850`, `#7A4A2B`

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Disproportionate anatomy** — the defining motif: massive torsos, diminutive heads (roughly one-sixth to one-tenth of body height), elongated "bendy" limbs with no elbows, knees or skeletal logic; limbs taper like tubes and curve through impossible arcs.
2. **Flat vector geometry** — circles, rectangles,半 arcs and simple polygons as the entire construction kit; no strokes, shapes defined purely by color edges.
3. **Non-representational skin tones** — blue, purple, green people as the style's most famous signal.
4. **Minimal or absent facial features** — ranging from fully blank visages to a single mouth or dot eyes; inconsistency here is normal.
5. **Abstract geometric scaffolding** — oversized diamonds, blobs, arcs, arrows, bars, dots and squiggle confetti arranged behind or between figures as pure composition, not scenery.
6. **Joyful generic activity** — dancing, jumping, high-fiving, stretching, collaborating around laptops and oversized smartphones; bodies in mid-motion, weightless.
7. **Supporting props (image-evidenced, secondary)** — potted houseplants and monstera-like leaves, delivery bags and shopping bags, clouds and coins as pictograms, patterned clothing accents (polka dots, tiny squiggles) as the only permitted surface pattern.

### Materials, textures, surface

The surface is vector-matte: uniform fills, clean edges, no brushwork, no paper fiber, no photographic grain in the core idiom. Print- and packaging-oriented work sometimes lays a subtle screen-print-style noise or grain over the flat shapes (visible in poster and packaging examples); this is an acceptable finish, never a license for shading. There is no volumetric material at all in 2D work — no plastic, no metal, no fabric weave. The style's **3D subgenre** (Grubhub "Delivery Dance", Chips Ahoy-style spots) keeps the same bendy anatomy and bright palette but renders it as soft matte clay or plastic with gentle diffuse studio lighting and mild ambient occlusion — puffy, toylike, never glossy or specular.

### Recurring subjects

Universally applicable placeholders doing universally approved things: teams collaborating at desks, customers tapping phones, shoppers with bags, meditators on sofas, dancers mid-twirl, couriers and commuters, families and friends in ensemble clusters. Objects are simplified pictograms — phones, laptops, carts, coins, clouds, plants — never branded, never specific. The subject is always *people as a friendly system*, never an individual portrait.

### Composition and lighting

Composition favors generous negative space and figure-first arrangements: a single monumental figure on a solid field; an overlapping ensemble cluster floating without a ground plane; a wide horizontal hero banner for web headers; a centered poster stack with wordmark above; or a pose-grid of separated figures. When ground exists it is implied — a flat ellipse, a curved line, a slab of contrasting color — rather than drawn perspective. Abstract shape scaffolding (a large diamond, blob or arc) often anchors the figures. Depth is achieved by overlap and scale only. **Lighting in the 2D core is absent**: no light source, no cast shadows, no gradients; at most a flat darker solid tone or hard flat shape stands in for shadow. An edge variant exists in e-commerce marketing: isometric vector dioramas (phones as platforms, bags and coins as icons) set on deep blue radial-gradient fields with soft glow — treat this as the outer rim of the style, not its center. The 3D subgenre uses soft, even, shadowless-feeling studio light.

## Mood & Values

The style projects inclusivity, minimalism, scalability, friendliness and mass appeal: a frictionless, conflict-free world where every interaction is joyful and every person is welcome because no person is specific. Its emotional register is upbeat, weightless, sanitized optimism — "toxic positivity," say its critics, a dystopian sameness dressed in pastels. Both the earnest and the satirical readings belong to the style: an image may play it straight (corporate warmth) or lean into its eerie perfection (parody), but it must always read as *cheerful by design*, never grim, gritty, intimate or personal.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s, United States tech industry. The visual language crystallizes in 2017 with the "Alegria" design system created by the agency Buck for Facebook — flat shapes depicting joyful interaction, built to scale across products without stock photography. The term "Corporate Memphis" was coined by Mike Merrill in 2018 as a pejorative reference to the 1980s Memphis Design group's anti-functional playfulness. Mainstream dominance ran 2018–2021, accelerated by stock libraries like UnDraw and Humaaans that made the look free and ubiquitous; backlash and decline began around 2022, with brands pivoting toward Claymorphism and Glassmorphism, while a 3D clay/plastic iteration kept the anatomy alive in animated commercials.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build everything from strokeless flat vector shapes. Give every human impossible proportions: pinhead, huge torso, tube limbs bending without joints. Keep faces blank or nearly blank. Choose skin as color, including blue/purple/green. Limit the palette to a coherent pastel set or a high-contrast primary set on one solid background field. Replace shading and light with color contrast and overlap. Populate the scene with generic joyful activity and abstract geometric confetti. Keep the whole image matte, clean and scalable-looking.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter: any human activity fits — work, wellness, shopping, dancing, parenting, commuting, gaming, eating, exercising; solo figure or crowd of a dozen. Composition: centered poster, wide hero banner, floating ensemble cluster, single figure on a field, isometric diorama, pose grid; ground plane optional and always implied. Color handling: pastel-dominant or primary-dominant; light field or saturated field; figures may carry the only saturated colors on a neutral ground, or sit as pastel notes on one loud color. Lighting: none in 2D core; soft diffuse studio light only in the 3D clay/plastic subgenre. Texture: pure flat fills, or a subtle print-grain finish. Mood: earnest corporate friendliness through deadpan satirical perfection. Line work: normally zero outlines; a single flat curved line may serve as ground or prop.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — flat fills, bendy disproportion, faceless universality, geometric confetti, frictionless cheer — not any reference image. A meditation scene, a football match, a kitchen, a space station crew: all valid if the rules hold.

**Avoid.**

- Gradients, airbrushing, volumetric shading, cast shadows, glow or rim light in 2D work (these belong only to the isometric/3D edge variants).
- Realistic proportions, articulated joints, detailed faces, individualizing markers (realistic hair strands, logos, culturally specific dress, age or body realism).
- Outlined cartoon line work, thick black strokes, comic inking.
- Photographic texture, grime, distress, paper collage, hand-drawn wobble.
- Pattern-maximal backgrounds: terrazzo, clashing zigzag fields, all-over squiggle prints (that is Memphis Design, not Corporate Memphis).
- Neubrutalist devices: heavy borders, hard offset drop shadows, raw default typography, deliberately ugly clashes.
- Puffy inflated 3D UI blobs and glass panels (Claymorphism/Glassmorphism) unless deliberately working in the 3D Corporate Memphis subgenre, which still keeps bendy figurative anatomy.
- Empty non-figurative compositions: this style is people-first; pure abstraction without figures reads as generic Flat Design or Minimalism.
- Dark, moody, cinematic or naturalistic lighting and complex perspective interiors (isometric projection is the only permitted depth system).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs. Claymorphism:** Claymorphism is puffy, rounded, soft-shadowed 3D volume — inflated UI cards, pills and blobs with inner highlights. Corporate Memphis 2D has zero volume; even its 3D subgenre is figure-led with bendy humans, whereas Claymorphism is mostly abstract interface geometry.
- **Vs. Memphis Design:** The 1980s original is pattern-maximal — terrazzo, squiggle lines, clashing prints, black outlines, postmodern grid games. Corporate Memphis borrows the playfulness but keeps flat solid fills, outline-free shapes, harmonized corporate palettes and human figures as the subject.
- **Vs. Minimalism:** Minimalism reduces toward essential, often non-figurative form with muted neutrals and empty space. Corporate Memphis is minimal in *rendering* but busy in *content*: multiple bright colors, active characters, geometric confetti.
- **Vs. Neubrutalism:** Neubrutalism is deliberately raw — thick black strokes, hard offset shadows, default typography, abrasive clashes. Corporate Memphis is smooth, strokeless, polished and friendly; its edges are color boundaries, never lines.
- **Vs. Vector Minimalism / generic Flat Design:** The parent Flat Design idiom includes icons, UI and proportionate figures, often monoline or subtly shaded. Corporate Memphis is the specific figurative branch: disproportionate bendy anatomy, non-representational skin, blank faces, and abstract shape scaffolding around joyful generic people.

**One-line recognition test:** flat matte vector shapes, no outlines and no shading, a blue- or purple-skinned person with a pinhead and rubber limbs dancing weightlessly on a solid pastel field among blobs and dots — friendly, scalable, and faintly soulless.
