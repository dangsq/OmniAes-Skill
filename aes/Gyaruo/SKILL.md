# Gyaruo

Gyaruo (ギャル男, "gyaru-o") is the male counterpart of the Gyaru subculture: a Japanese street-fashion tribe of young men who emerged in late-1990s Shibuya, peaked through the 2000s, and built an identity out of trend-obsessed, brand-focused clothing, meticulously sculpted high-volume hair, and — in many but not all substyles — a deep artificial tan. It is a peacocking, party-centric style: club nights, Para Para and Eurobeat, materialism and glamour worn as open rebellion against conventional Japanese restraint and subtlety. Its natural habitats are the Shibuya streetscape and the 109-② / 109-mens building (home of flagship stores like Vanquish, Jack Rose and Varosh), Shinjuku, and Osaka's Amerikamura; its natural media are the pages of *Men's Egg* (the "Gyaruo bible"), *Men's Knuckle* and *Men's Egg Bitter*, plus the compact-camera snapshot culture those magazines printed.

## Visual Identity

**Hair is the first and most defining signal.** Bleached or dyed brown, copper, orange-blond or platinum, built into a high-volume, teased, sculpted mass with distinct streaks or bundles — the "sujimori" (スジ盛り) look. Color streaks (pink, red, gold) inside a bleached base are common; the extreme Sentaa Guy end pushes to rainbow-bright hair paired with very deep tan and white facial makeup. Hair volume and visible streak separation should read even in silhouette. Flat, sleek, natural-black or neatly trimmed hair contradicts the style (the mature Onii Kei end is the only softened exception, and even there hair is styled with lift).

**Skin:** a bronze-to-deep-brown artificial tan is a core characteristic, mirroring ganguro Gyaru, but it is a dial, not a switch — several substyles (Salon, Onii Kei, some Military looks) keep lighter skin. When the tan is deep, contrast it deliberately: bleached hair and white or neon clothing pop against it.

**Silhouette:** predominantly tight-fitting — skinny jeans, slim leather jackets, fitted tees and cardigans — with extensive layering (long sleeves under graphic tees, hoodies under jackets, shirts over parkas, ties under open hoodies). The Surfer and American Casual ends legitimately loosen up: baggy light-wash denim, cargo pants, board-short-ish khakis, oversized striped salopettes. Either pole is valid; what stays constant is deliberate, visible *styling* — nothing accidental or minimalist.

**Accessory overload:** belts (often studded, often worn low), wallet chains, stacked bracelets and wristbands, multiple necklaces and pendants, rings, sunglasses (worn at night and indoors without irony), bandanas and hats. Accessories are layered in multiples, never a single discreet item.

**Brand consciousness:** logos and label identity matter — Vanquish, Jack Rose, Varosh, Diavlo, Roi Franc, Gennaro — plus graphic clothing carrying English words and American pop-culture references (eagle-and-flag prints, college-style rugby shirts, slogan tees). Logos are worn proudly and visibly, not hidden.

### Palette rule

The palette is substyle-dependent, but every valid palette is *saturated and committed*, never muted-by-default. Three clusters cover the range:

| Cluster | Hex range | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Dark glam | black `#0a0a0a`–`#1c1c1c`, white `#f2f2f2`, gold/brass `#c9a227`–`#d4af37`, silver hardware `#b8b8b8`, leopard pile `#b98a4a` with `#5e4025` spots | Rock, Biker, Onii Kei, key-colour core |
| Earth/utility | olive `#6b705c`, khaki `#c3b091`, tan `#8a6f4d`, black accents | Military, Biker denim end |
| Bright/pop | mint `#a8e6c3`, sky `#7fd4e8`, pink `#f7a8c4`, coral red `#e8452c`, sunshine yellow `#f5d033`, neon magenta `#ff4fa0`, neon cyan `#35d0e0`, lime `#7fe04a`, over bleached-hair blond `#d9a05b`–`#f0d08a` and copper `#b06a3a` | Surfer, American Casual, Sentaa Guy, club/Kanji neons |

Bleached hair tones and tanned skin sit on top of whichever cluster is chosen; animal print (leopard fur coats, trim, bags) may interrupt any cluster as an accent.

**Materials and textures:** glossy leather and vinyl, faux-fur and leopard pile, distressed and light-wash denim versus coated black skinny denim, jersey cotton graphic tees, fleece hoodies, camo cotton twill, chambray and plaid flannel, metallic studs/chains/buckles, plastic beads and leis at the extreme club end. Texture contrast (shiny leather against matte cotton, fur against denim) is typical.

**Recurring subjects and settings:** young men (late teens to late twenties) solo, in duos, or in loose groups of friends; Shibuya sidewalks and crossings, rooftop or waterfront hangouts, convenience-store aisles, game centers and candy-bright shop interiors, club queues and trance nights, catalog studios, and the magazine page itself. Poses are performative and friendly-aggressive: peace signs, thumbs-up, pointing at the camera, arms slung over shoulders, exaggerated open-mouth expressions; the Rock/Onii end substitutes cool posed smolders with sunglasses.

**Composition and lighting tendencies:** vertical framing dominates. Three media modes coexist and all are style-correct: (1) the candid snapshot — compact-camera look, slight overexposure in daylight or harsh direct on-camera flash at night against dark store interiors and neon streets, occasional orange date stamp; (2) the catalog/coordinate page — even studio light, full-body standing figures, grids of numbered accessory cutouts (necklaces, hats, glasses) beside the model; (3) the magazine editorial spread — saturated CMYK print, Japanese headline typography, starburst callouts and yen price tags overlaid around grouped models. Grain, flash blowout and print halftone saturation are authentic texture, not flaws.

## Mood & Values

Exuberant, glamorously loud, deliberately excessive. Gyaruo communicates youth rebellion, partying, materialism and fashion devotion — pride in being seen, spent-on and styled. The mood ranges from playful goofiness (props, gestures, laughing group candids) to cocky glamour (leather, sunglasses, smolder), but never to austerity, irony-free minimalism, or quiet good taste. Excess is sincerity here.

## Era & Origins

Late 1990s, Shibuya, Tokyo, as the masculine answer to Gyaru; most prominent across the 2000s, now a documented chapter of Japanese street-fashion history. It reacts against traditional Japanese norms of subtlety and conformity, and is tied to trance, Eurobeat and synchronized Para Para dancing, to club events like "Men's Egg Night," and to print magazines as trend engines. Substyles span Rock, Biker, Military, American Casual (アメカジ系), Surfer, Sentaa Guy (センター街, the male Manba equivalent), Kanji / UraShibu-kei (漢字, trance-club style), Derika (hippie/bohemian/onii fusion), Salon (beauty-industry trendsetters), the obscure Kyoba Bo-i (キョバホイ, male Banba equivalent — thin historical and visual evidence), and the mature Onii Kei end.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Build the hair first: bleached/dyed, high-volume, teased, with readable streak bundles (sujimori). Everything else hangs off this.
2. Choose one substyle palette cluster and commit to it fully; mix clusters only through accents (a leopard bag on a black Rock look, neon beads on a club look).
3. Layer accessories in multiples — belts, chains, bracelets, necklaces, sunglasses — and let at least one brand logo or English-word graphic be visible.
4. Set the tan dial consciously: deep bronze for Sentaa Guy / classic looks, light or none for Salon / Onii Kei ends.
5. Pick one authentic media mode (daylight snapshot, night flash candid, studio catalog, magazine spread with Japanese typography) and keep its lighting and texture consistent throughout the image.
6. Pose with performative energy: hand gestures, group clustering, open expressions — or deliberate cool at the Rock/Onii end.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any male or male-group subject within youth street culture — friends on a rooftop, a club queue, a store aisle, a beach day, a catalog model, a magazine cover. Composition: full-body street snap, three-quarter portrait, tight face close-up with hand gesture, dense group candid, editorial page with overlaid type, coordinate grid with accessory cutouts. Lighting: overexposed daylight, harsh night flash, even studio flash, saturated print reproduction. Color: any point on the dark-glam → earth → bright/pop spectrum above. Mood: goofy play to cool glamour. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A rooftop group in pastel surf layering and a lone night-flash figure in black leather with leopard fur are equally valid Gyaruo.

**Avoid.**
- Feminine Gyaru coding: skirts, dresses, female gyaru makeup grammar, nail-art-centric framing.
- Modern 2010s–2020s streetwear: boxy oversized hoodies, drop shoulders, minimalist sneakers, normcore or techwear.
- Default-muted, Scandinavian-minimal palettes and quiet luxury restraint.
- Flat, sleek, natural-black or buzzed hair (volume and bleach are the signature).
- Authentic Western punk/biker grit: worn patina, gang regalia, tokko-fuku long coats with kanji embroidery, pompadours, motorcycles as identity props (that is Bosozoku).
- All-over rainbow decora/Fruittari candy overload without masculine silhouette, brand logos and bleached-base hair.
- Pristine contemporary digital color grading, HDR clarity, or clean modern studio beauty lighting that erases the snapshot/print era texture.
- European suburban contexts or costume-like uniform repetition (that reads as Partille-Johnny imitation, not Shibuya-origin style).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Gyaru:** the female counterpart. Shared tan and bleach, but Gyaru carries skirts, dresses, female makeup (white eyeliner, heavy lashes) and feminine branding. Gyaruo keeps menswear grammar: skinny or baggy denim, leather jackets, rugby shirts, camo, men's brand logos, sujimori rather than gyaru curls.
- **Fruittari:** fruit-neon saturation everywhere, rainbow hair head-to-toe, candy accessory overload, often without tan or masculine brand hardware. Gyaruo's bright end (Surfer, American Casual, Sentaa Guy) still anchors color in menswear silhouettes, visible logos, and usually a bleached base with color streaks rather than full rainbow dye.
- **Partille-Johnny:** the Swedish gyaru-imitation phenomenon; reads as costume-like repetition (uniform tan-plus-blonde-wig formulas) in Scandinavian suburban settings. Gyaruo shows genuine Japanese brand garments, varied substyles, Shibuya/Shinjuku/Amerikamura context and magazine editorial grammar.
- **Bosozoku:** biker-gang aesthetics — embroidered tokko-fuku long coats, kanji slogans, pompadour/regent hair, masks, motorcycles at night. Gyaruo's Biker substyle is fashion leather and denim without gang coats or bikes as identity.
- **Onii Kei:** the mature, subdued host-club end of the family (*Men's Knuckle* territory): slim black suits and host coats, sleek styled hair, restrained jewelry, little or no tan. If an image is entirely restrained host elegance with no hair volume, tan or logo play, it is Onii Kei rather than Gyaruo proper — though the two share a lineage and a blended middle exists.
