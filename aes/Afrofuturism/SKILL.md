# Afrofuturism

Afrofuturism is a cultural and philosophical aesthetic that examines the relationship between the African diaspora and scientific, technological and speculative themes. It is not a single look but a method: envisioning future possibilities through a Black cultural lens, converging imagination, technology, future perspective and liberation. It speaks in science fiction, fantasy, alternate history and magic realism, and it moves freely between media — album mythologies, fashion editorial, photomontage, airbrushed paperback illustration, collage, sculpture and film. Because it addresses a difficult past and present while insisting on future prosperity, its images always carry two timestamps at once: ancestral memory and prophecy.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (the rule, not a swatch)
Build every image in three layers:
- **Ground layer — earth tones and deep blues:** umber and bark browns (`#6B4A2F`–`#8B5A2B`), ochre and terracotta (`#C8892E`, `#B4531F`), sand and clay (`#E4C9A0`); or midnight and indigo blues (`#0A1030`–`#232E7A`) with teal-cyan water tones (`#0E7C8C`).
- **Elevator layer — metallics:** brass to gold (`#B08D24`–`#E9C46A`), chrome and silver (`#A8B2BD`–`#D8DEE6`), cast bronze (`#7A4A21`). At least one metallic surface or cosmic light source should touch the subject; this is the style's signature lift.
- **Optional pop layer — saturated collage accents:** magenta (`#D6218F`), mint green (`#7FD1AE`), sunset peach (`#F2A65A`), electric cyan (`#19A7CE`), used as flat backdrop fields, animal-stripe patterns or printed fabric, never as ambient neon glow.

Never all-neon (that is cyberpunk/synthwave) and never all-grey grit (that is Cyber Ghetto). Muted, misty greens and raw wood/bronze are a legitimate fourth register belonging to the organic sculpture and collage strand.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the culture documents them
1. **Cosmology and outer space** — the deepest root (Sun Ra, *Space is the Place*, the claim that Black people "can go into space"): starfields, a single moon or planet in an otherwise empty sky, orbital rings around heads and hats, sun discs and halos aligned behind the head.
2. **Technology as liberation instrument** — Wakanda's vibranium science, Uhura's communications console, Cyborg: visors, blindfold-masks, helmets and chrome prosthetics, antenna-like headdresses, analog-future consoles and antennae; technology reads as regal equipment, not consumer gadgetry.
3. **Diasporic heritage regalia and textiles** — the fashion lineage: Ankara/kente-type prints, gold bead collars and chains, towering headwraps, crowns, fringed capes and layered wraps adapted to forward-cut, fashion-line silhouettes.
4. **Reclamation and royalty** — liberation and heritage made visible: enthroned or processional poses, dignified frontal or meditative downcast gazes, mirrored and doubled figures suggesting lineage and replication, scarab and talisman pendants.
5. **The hybrid, androgynous body** — the 1970s funk-glam metallic androgyny and the collage tradition of grafted bodies: organic matter (feathers, grass, fungus, wood) fused with machine parts, eyes concealed or opened as a third eye at the crown.
6. **Vernacular future architecture** — a secondary register, thin in the written record but present in the visual culture: stilt-built waterfront settlements, barrel towers, improvised megastructures assembled from scrap with obvious agency and pride.

### Materials and textures
Metallic lamé and chrome; gold leaf, glitter and foil; glass beads and cowrie; woven raffia, straw and basketry; carved wood and cast bronze; printed cotton; cut-paper collage edges with visible scale jumps; airbrushed matte gradients; glossy photographic skin. Surfaces should feel *handled* — sewn, cast, woven, glued — even when the subject is cosmic.

### Recurring subjects
Black figures as explorers, queens and kings, priests, engineers, dancers and ancestors; hybrid creature-beings; sculptural figures in landscape; floating or stilted settlements; portraits where the eyes are covered by a visor, mask or blindfold, or multiplied by a third eye.

### Composition and lighting tendencies
Centered bust or full-figure against a flat, mottled studio backdrop or a cosmic void; halo or disc precisely centered behind the head; profile busts crossed by orbital rings; symmetrical doubling and mirroring; or the figure small within dunes, misty forest or open road. Collage work layers cutout planes with deliberate edge visibility. Lighting ranges from dusk gradients (lavender fading to peach at the horizon) and radiant beams emanating from crown or third eye, to flat even studio light for fashion, to specular glitter on metal, to misty diffuse daylight around organic and sculptural work.

## Mood & Values

Liberation, innovation, heritage, future vision. The emotional register is self-possession: calm royalty, sacred ceremony, prophetic melancholy turned into announcement, or celebratory funk joy. Even the grotesque hybrids keep elegance and intent — "listen to your spirit" is the style's own design advice, so intuition and ritual sit inside the technology rather than opposing it.

## Era & Origins

Literary ancestors reach back to W.E.B. Du Bois, Charles W. Chesnutt, George S. Schuyler and Ralph Ellison's *Invisible Man* (1952); the musical and cosmic root is Sun Ra from the late 1950s; the 1970s funk era (George Clinton, Sly and the Family Stone, James Brown) supplied metallic, androgynous stage futurism, extended in the 1980s by Grace Jones and Afrika Bambaataa. Mark Dery coined the term in 1993, Alondra Nelson's late-1990s conversations expanded it, and *Black Panther* (2018) carried it into mainstream discourse. The style reacts to the exclusion of Black people from mainstream imagined futures and to the rupture of the slave trade, answering both with reclamation: invented, approximated or recovered heritage projected forward.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Fuse at least two registers in every image: diasporic heritage (textile, beadwork, regalia, sculpture) **and** speculative technology or cosmology (starfield, visor, chrome, alternate infrastructure). One alone drifts into a neighboring style.
2. Give the subject agency and rank: explorer, engineer, deity, ancestor — never victim, never prop.
3. Collapse time: analog retro-future hardware beside traditional dress; a centuries-old weaving technique on an orbital structure.
4. Obey the palette rule: earth or indigo ground, metallic elevator, optional saturated pop field.
5. Treat the eyes as a semantic device: concealed by visor or blindfold, multiplied as a third eye, or held in a direct, unflinching gaze.
6. Let the medium show: cut-paper edges, airbrush grain, glitter sparkle, cast-metal weight, photographic gloss.

**Variation axes (all of these may move independently)**
- *Subject:* portrait bust, fashion figure, ceremonial procession, hybrid creature, sculpture in landscape, floating architecture, street scene with regalia, cosmic tableau.
- *Composition:* centered symmetry, profile against void, mirrored doubling, collage scatter, figure dwarfed by landscape.
- *Lighting:* dusk gradient, radiant crown-beams, flat studio, misty daylight, starfield black with one luminous body.
- *Color handling:* muted earth-dominant, indigo-dominant nocturne, saturated collage pop, or misty green-bronze organic.
- *Mood:* meditative, regal, celebratory-funk, eerie-hybrid, prophetic.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A misty forest sculpture, a roadside figure beside a sedan, and a blue-skinned deity on a magenta stripe field are all valid outputs of the same rule set.

**Avoid**
- Generic cyberpunk neon cities, synthwave grids, palm sunsets or chrome type with no diasporic content.
- Ethnographic pastiche: traditional dress staged as historical costume with no speculative or technological layer.
- Dystopian grime, ruin-porn or precarity as the message; improvisation here signals ingenuity, not despair.
- Non-Black default subjects carrying the signifiers (the style is culturally situated; parallel futurisms cover other communities).
- Steampunk brass gears, dieselpunk, anime mecha, smooth white corporate sci-fi minimalism.
- Wax-print fabric as the *only* signifier, or gold jewelry as mere decoration without cosmic or regal framing.
- Gratuitous gore or abjection in hybrid bodies; critique yes, horror-makeup no.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Afrofuturism's tell |
|---|---|---|
| Cyber Ghetto | Concrete precarity, bootleg cassette tech, grey-and-rust decay, survivalist street improv | Regal dignity, cosmology, gold and indigo; improv reads as invention, not scarcity |
| Funk | Stage costumes, platform boots, cartoon hedonism, sweat and groove | Adds space mythos, reclamation narrative and philosophical weight; calm ceremony beside the party |
| Indigenous Futurisms | Indigenous regalia, land- and water-based cosmologies of Native communities | African diasporic textiles, beadwork patterns and Black subjects; methods shared, signifiers distinct |
| Solarpunk | Green tech, art-nouveau curves, community gardens, racially unspecified optimism | Black cultural specificity and cosmic myth; greenery appears as misty organic collage, not utopian gardening |
| 80s–90s Afrocentrism | Kente, medallions, Egypt-centered iconography anchored in historical pride | Requires the future vector: visors, starfields, alternate history, technology |

Overlap is real and honest: Parliament-Funkadelic and Drexciya sit between Afrofuturism, Funk and Cyber Ghetto, and retrofuturist chrome appears inside Afrofuturist work — but only ever welded to diasporic regalia, cosmology and the insistence on a liberated future.
