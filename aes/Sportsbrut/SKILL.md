# Sportsbrut (Sports Brutalism / Corporate Neu-Grunge)

Sportsbrut is the look of sports branding run through a photocopier and a bootleg merch table: broadcast-package confidence collided with Neubrutalist unpolish. It emerged as the graphic language of hype drops, fixture announcements, tour merch and social templates in the mid-late 2010s, occupying a deliberate middle ground — more maximalist and grittier than glossy corporate Y2K neu-grunge, but more dynamic and textured than sterile digital Neubrutalism. Everything in it serves one goal: making a flat canvas feel like it is moving fast.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette: monochrome skeleton, one accent blast

The base scheme is stark and limited: ink black (`#0A0A0A`–`#161616`), paper white (`#F2F2F0`–`#FFFFFF`), and monochrome grays carried as halftone density rather than smooth tone (`#6E6E6E`–`#B4B4B4` dot fields). Into this, a single intense accent detonates: electric magenta/hot pink (`#FF1E6E`–`#FF2E93`), acid yellow (`#E8FF00`–`#F5D90A`), mint/teal (`#6EE7D0`–`#7FE0C3`), signal orange-red (`#E8401C`–`#FF3B1F`), cobalt/royal blue (`#1F4FD8`–`#0057B8`), or violet (`#6C2BD9`–`#7A3CFF`). A second accent may appear only in small doses (a quote line, a badge, a pixel icon). Three permitted palette modes: (a) black ground with white type and one accent blast; (b) white/paper ground with black type and one accent; (c) full-bleed accent field with type knocked out in white or repeated as outline rows. Duotone and threshold photo treatments tint photography into the accent color so photos read as ink, not light.

### Typography: the loudest object on the canvas

Type is the primary image. Ultra-condensed, blocky slab serifs and heavy grotesque sans-serifs set at imposing weight; letterforms compressed, vertically stretched, sheared or otherwise deformed for impact. Signature type behaviors: giant display words stacked in repeated rows (often the same word repeated five to seven times, one row solid and the rest outline); type running vertically down sleeves, spines and canvas edges; extreme scale jumps from poster-height display type down to 6–8pt technical caption type (dates, venues, credits, specs); outline and stroke-only echoes of solid words; occasional pixel or bitmap letterforms as accents. Legibility at jersey-and-hoarding distance is the standard.

### Layout and composition: rigid grid, then breakout

The canvas is dissected into harsh, bare-bones chunks — boxes, bars, hairline rules, column divisions — a visible industrial grid. Then the rule is broken on purpose: the ball, the athlete, the sneaker, the mascot or a letterform bursts out of its frame boundary, overlapping edges and neighboring panels to create forward depth and explosive motion. Common structures: stacked date/city list columns (tour-date blocks); sidebar spines of vertical type; corner-anchored logo badges and shield stamps; asymmetric weight with one heavy corner and one breathing corner; diagonal thrust implied by overlapping cutouts rather than by tilted cameras. Composition is collage-plus-grid, not freeform scatter.

### Motifs and ephemera (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Large bold deformed typography** — the defining motif.
2. **Sports imagery** — athletes mid-action, balls, hoops, nets, jerseys, sneakers, mascots and cartoon collabs.
3. **Credential ephemera** — barcodes, ticket stubs and perforations, boarding-pass cards, event lanyard data, post-stamp repeats, repeating lines, technical data overlays (scores, coordinates, serial numbers, "08.27"-style date stamps).
4. **Merch logic** — longsleeve and tee prints, sleeve runs of type, back-print tour lists, woven-label blocks.
5. **Logo badges and shield stamps** — league-style silhouettes, club crests, pixel icons placed as official seals.
6. **Repeat patterns** — smiley-or-letter tile grids, outline-word wallpaper rows.

### Materials, textures and photo treatment

Low-fidelity, raw print texture is mandatory in some dose: rough halftone dot screens, photocopy grain and toner blotches, subtle paper distress and crease, threshold/bitmap filters that crush photos to two tones, torn-paper collage edges, pixel dither, occasional mesh or chain-link overlays. Photography is never left naturalistic: it arrives duotoned, thresholded, halftoned or cut into offset strips. Rendered or 3D objects appear only occasionally and are treated as flat collage cutouts, not glossy hero renders — the style's core surface is print-flat ink on stock.

### Lighting

Lighting follows print logic, not photographic logic: flat scanner light, hard direct flash, or high-contrast studio key that survives thresholding. Backgrounds may carry a grayscale gradient sweep or a dot-screen fade, but never atmospheric haze, lens flare, golden hour or softbox lifestyle glow. Contrast is pushed so shadows clip to ink black and highlights clip to paper white.

## Mood & Values

Action, excitement, energy — the infobox values are the whole brief. Sportsbrut feels like the last minute before tip-off: urgent, loud, slightly dirty, officially sanctioned but fan-made in spirit. It values speed over refinement, impact over elegance, legibility at distance over subtlety, and the tactile authority of printed credentials (tickets, passes, stamps) over digital smoothness. It celebrates drop culture, fixture-day hype and bootleg affection for athletes and clubs.

## Era & Origins

Coined by Froyo Tam; rose to prevalence in the mid-late 2010s. It is a reaction formation on two fronts: against the glossy, airbrushed corporate neu-grunge of Y2K-era sports marketing, and against the sterile, flat, off-white minimalism of early digital Neubrutalism. Its lineage is sports broadcast packages, streetwear drop merch, bootleg tour tees, gig posters and ticket design — all recomposed in vector tools with deliberate lo-fi degradation.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build a rigid box grid first (bars, panels, hairlines, columns), then let one or two hero elements — ball, athlete, sneaker, mascot, giant letter — break out of its frame and overlap neighbors.
2. Make type the hero: ultra-condensed heavy slab or grotesque, stretched or compressed, with at least one repeated row stack (solid + outline echoes) and one vertical spine run.
3. Add a credential layer: barcode, ticket stub, date/city list column, serial numbers, small technical captions, logo badge or shield stamp.
4. Degrade deliberately: halftone screens, photocopy grain, threshold-treated photos, torn edges, paper distress. At least one texture family must be present.
5. Hold the palette to monochrome base + one blasting accent (plus at most a whisper of a second). Tint photos into the accent via duotone or threshold.
6. Light for print: flat scan, hard flash, clipped contrast. No atmospheric photography.
7. Imply motion through overlap, diagonal cutouts and stretched letterforms — not through motion blur or camera tilt.
8. Keep scale contrast extreme: poster-height display type against 6–8pt data type.

**Variation axes (what may change)**

- *Subject*: any sport or athlete; but equally music promos, product launches, cocktails, sneakers, type specimens, club fixtures, social-media template panels, event boarding passes, merch apparel prints, pattern tiles. Sports imagery is typical, not compulsory — the sports-*package* logic is what binds.
- *Composition*: poster, apparel print, ticket/card, collage sheet, grid-of-panels template, full-bleed repeat pattern, cover art.
- *Color handling*: black-base, white-base, or accent-flooded field; any single neon accent from the range above; duotone intensity from clean vector flatness to heavy photocopy crush.
- *Lighting*: flat scan to hard flash; grayscale gradient sweeps allowed as background only.
- *Mood*: from cold technical fixture-sheet to maximalist fan-zine scream.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A cocktail card, a chess poster or a software launch can be perfectly Sportsbrut if it obeys the grid-breakout, type-deformation, credential-ephemera, lo-fi-texture and monochrome-plus-one-accent rules.

**Avoid (style-breakers)**

- Soft gradients, glassmorphism, glossy 3D hero renders, smooth chrome as the main subject.
- Pastel, earth-tone or multi-color rainbow palettes; more than one loud accent competing.
- Naturalistic photography with shallow depth of field, golden hour, soft lifestyle light.
- Elegant serifs, scripts, hand-lettering or organic hand-drawn illustration as the display face.
- Centered, symmetric hero compositions with generous calm negative space.
- Rounded-corner cards, soft drop shadows, neumorphism.
- Databent RGB-split corruption or scanline destruction as the subject (that is Glitch Art, not texture).
- Liquid chrome, psychedelic swirls, Y2K rave goo (that is Acid Design).
- Sterile off-white minimal layouts with clean flat boxes and no grain (that is Neubrutalism).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tell | Sportsbrut's difference |
|---|---|---|
| Neubrutalism | Off-white ground, clean flat vector boxes, primary or pastel fills, no grain, no sports content | Adds photocopy/halftone grit, athletic imagery, ticket-barcode ephemera, deformed condensed type, darker ink base |
| Acid Design | Liquid chrome, psychedelic gradients, 3D goo, rave Y2K futurism | Grid-bound, print-flat, ink-textured; distortion lives in type stretching, not melting surfaces |
| Cyberminimalism | Sparse HUD lines, thin mono type, vast negative space, single neon on near-black | Maximalist chunk density, stacked repeat type, heavy slab weight, noisy ephemera layers |
| Glitch Art | Corruption is the subject: databending, pixel sorting, RGB split, scanlines | Distortion is a seasoning on legible grids; the layout stays readable and print-logical |

Quick check: if you can imagine the image silk-screened on a tour longsleeve and stapled to a stadium concourse wall — grid intact, one accent screaming, grain in the blacks, a barcode somewhere — it is Sportsbrut.
