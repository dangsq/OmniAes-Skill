# Mochette

Mochette — from *moche*, French for "ugly," plus the diminutive *-ette*, "little" — is a niche internet aesthetic built on a single structural rule: **the cute and the disgusting must occupy the same frame, or the same blog, at the same time.** Where adjacent styles make prettiness dark or creepy, Mochette makes it *unsanitary*. Its subjects are dolls with grubby faces, lace yellowed and stained by age, snails with pink-striped shells, ballet slippers on a floor of peeling paint, abandoned bedrooms whose wallpaper is still blush pink while the plaster rots behind it. The intended feeling is not fear but revulsion softened into affection — nausea remembered fondly, beauty located precisely in decay, dirt and death. It is a Tumblr-native aesthetic, descended from the faded pastel photography of the early-2010s "Pale and Delicate Sweet" movement and later named (2022, by user @frowup) after a decade of circulating under tags like forgottencore, lostcore, usedcore, grosscore and vomitcore.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette is a two-register system: a tender base of pale pinks and creams, contaminated by sickly greens, browns and grays. Neither register may appear alone for long; a wholly candy-pastel image or a wholly earthy-rot image has left the style.

| Register | Range | Typical hexes |
|---|---|---|
| Blush / dusty pink (the "cute" base) | pale rose to faded fleshy pink | #F6E3E6, #EBC9D2, #D9A7B0 |
| Cream / yellowed white (lace, paper, Polaroid frames) | clean off-white to age-stained ivory | #F4EFE6, #E9E0CD, #E3D6B4 |
| Sickly green (mold, sage, olive, leaf) | ghostly mint to damp moss | #C9D8C6, #A9B49E, #7E8A6A, #6C7A57 |
| Brown / rust (rot, wood, peeling plaster, raw matter) | tan wallpaper to deep rust | #C9B299, #8A6F5B, #6B4F3F, #7C4A38 |
| Gray / pale blue-gray (dust, vinyl, overcast light) | warm dust to cold institutional pale blue | #BDB8B0, #8C8781, #A9B6C0 |
| Sparse accent: dried blood / lipstick red | muted oxblood, never bright scarlet | #8E3B3B, used in tiny doses |

Rule of thumb: desaturate everything one step further than feels comfortable, then let pink and green sit side by side (a pink sweet in a green leaf; green fern fronds on pink cloth; a pink shell on a pale slug). Brown and gray do the work of age; red appears only as a wound-sized accent.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the text documents them

1. **Rot and its agents** — bugs, insects, worms, larvae, slugs and snails (especially pink ones, or ones placed against pink); mold and fungi, ideally growing on or near something pretty; decaying buildings, food, corpses, anything. This is the style's first subject.
2. **Dolls and their parts** — broken, disassembled, dirty dolls; cloth doll torsos; ball-jointed dolls, particularly fairy- or elf-like ones; dolls' hands, heads, limbs arranged as objects; creepy handmade art assembled from broken toys.
3. **Tainted feminine textiles** — lace (clothing, tablecloths, curtains) stained or yellowed; floral quilts and quilted fabrics with dark stains; dirty, torn clothing; white or cream lace above all.
4. **Abandoned and burned interiors, especially pastel ones** — rooms with pink or pastel walls in decay, peeling wallpaper, mint skirting under ruined plaster, childhood bedrooms after the childhood, haunted places.
5. **Anatomy, injury and bodily trace** — bones, hands, eyeballs; bruises, bug bites, spider veins, stitches, scars, bite marks; vomit and other bodily fluids in the style's rawest source material. In generated work this register is carried by marks, stains and specimen-like objects rather than explicit content.
6. **Age-worn domestic objects** — furniture and antiques worn with age, rusty or tarnished vintage jewelry, old weapons and tools, dried flowers or vintage fake flowers, dingy food made unappealing by its lighting and surroundings, curio arrangements in old tins and boxes.
7. **Lower-frequency but canonical** — rodents and vermin, dangerous or rabid animals, wild animals, raw meat, saints and church iconography old, dirty, faded or abandoned, wet specimens and other Vulture Culture objects. The image record samples these less often than dolls, lace, mollusks and pastel decay rooms; treat them as legitimate but rarer entries.
8. **Surreal whimsy devices** — small wings, flower crowns, doll-like posing, a light tone handling disturbing content, inherited from surrealist film (*Daisies*, *Valerie and Her Week of Wonders*, Švankmajer's *Alice*, *Little Otik*).

### Materials and textures

Porcelain and ball-joint plastic; cloth doll bodies gone gray; lace and eyelet cotton; satin ribbons and ballet slipper canvas; floral chintz and quilted bedding; snail flesh and shell, fungal fingers, mold bloom; peeling paint, flaking plaster, water-stained wallpaper; rust, tarnish, patinated metal; dried petals; dust, hair, grime. Surfaces should read *handled, aged, or alive with decay* — never new, never sealed in gloss.

### Recurring subjects and figures

Doll-like feminine figures: pale subjects in milkmaid or babydoll dresses, satin or lacy lingerie, pajamas or hospital gowns; striped stockings with scuffed combat boots; messy hair and smeared makeup; figures posed small and quiet inside ruined rooms, or lying among debris with flat, unbothered expressions. Ball-jointed dolls stand in for humans often enough that doll-and-human readings blur deliberately.

### Composition and lighting

- **Snapshot casualness**: off-kilter handheld angles, on-camera flash flattening a dim room, subjects caught mid-gesture or sprawled; the composition feels archived rather than staged.
- **Analogue surface**: film grain, faded contrast, slight green or magenta cast, overexposed washouts; Polaroid/instax white frames are a common but optional container. Blog-era 3×3 grid collages of mixed motifs are a native format.
- **Still-life and curio framing**: small objects arranged like shrines or specimens — rows of doll hands, charms in a tin, a snail centered on bare wood — shot close with shallow depth of field.
- **Detail close-ups**: a lace collar with a mint ribbon, a stained quilt square, a curtain gone translucent with age.
- **Figure-in-ruin wide shots**: a pale figure dwarfed by a peeling, debris-floored room.
- Light is always compromised: flat flash, overcast window light, dingy tungsten, washed high-key fade. Never clean studio light, never golden-hour glow used sincerely.

## Mood & Values

Mochette values **finding beauty in decay and death, and in the "ugly" itself**; it carries a soft nihilism and an acceptance of death rather than a fight against it. The emotional target is revulsion and nausea held at arm's length by tenderness — queasy affection, not dread. There is nostalgia in it ("Nauseous Nostalgia"): for forgotten homes, previously loved objects, childhood items past their salvage. Its whimsy is real but off-key — dark concepts handled with a light, almost playful tone, the way a surrealist film dresses cruelty in lace. Silence, dust and abandonment give it a hushed, private atmosphere, as if the viewer has opened someone's sealed bedroom or a jar kept too long.

## Era & Origins

- **2010–2012, Tumblr**: an offshoot of the Pale and Delicate Sweet photo movements — same faded filters, light colors, pink femininity — pushed toward grotesque content: bugs, worms, injured and sick bodies. Nicole Dollanganger's early blogs (with usernames balancing the two registers) are the canonical example.
- **Split from Morute**: as that circle drifted toward gothic morbidity, the bloggers who stayed with disgust, rot and rawness became Mochette's lineage; late-2010s tags forgottencore, lostcore, usedcore, grosscore and vomitcore collected abandoned rural homes, burned childhood bedrooms, battered dolls, and the most graphic material.
- **Earlier roots**: Katie Jane Garside's Kinderwhore-adjacent practice — blood, dirt, torn clothing, mutilated dolls, baby bottles, the 2007 exhibition *Darling, They've Found the Body* — and 20th-century European surrealist cinema.
- **2022**: the term Mochette coined and defined on Tumblr, spreading to Pinterest. Music taste stays folk/singer-songwriter, grunge, metal, early industrial, outsider and experimental (Dollanganger, Garside/Ruby Throat, Hole, Swans, Current 93).

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Two registers, one frame.** Every image must contain both the tender-feminine-cute and the gross-decayed, either literally (a ribbon on a worm; a stain on a christening gown) or tonally (dingy light and grubby surroundings tainting a cute object). One register alone is not Mochette.
2. **Contaminate the pastel.** Start from blush pink, cream and white; pull sickly green, rust brown and gray into the same palette. Desaturate, fade, add grain.
3. **Age or taint every surface.** Lace yellowed, quilts stained, wallpaper peeling, metal rusted, dolls chipped, food gone dingy. Nothing pristine enters the frame.
4. **Shoot like an archive, not a campaign.** Analogue snapshot logic: flat flash or overcast window light, off-kilter framing, faded contrast, optional instant-film frame or collage grid. The image should feel found.
5. **Aim for nausea-tenderness.** The grotesque is handled softly, whimsically, even lovingly. If the image reads as horror meant to frighten, or as wholesome cuteness meant to charm, it has missed.
6. **Keep the grotesque non-sexual.** Source blogs occasionally included explicit material; generated work expresses taint through stain, tear, dirt, decay, specimen-like objects and marks on clothed skin — never through sexual content.

**Variation axes (what may change freely)**

- *Subject*: anything in the motif ranks or analogous inventions — a pink moth on a stained pillowcase, moss eating a ballet shoe, a saint card fading behind glass, raw meat on floral china, a snail trail across a love letter.
- *Composition*: snapshot figure, curio still life, extreme textile close-up, figure dwarfed by a ruined room, grid collage; portrait or landscape; centered or carelessly cropped.
- *Lighting*: flat flash, overcast diffuse, dim tungsten, washed-over overexposure — any compromised light source.
- *Color handling*: pink-dominant with green intrusions, green-dominant with pink intrusions, brown-and-cream decay with a single pink object; accent red only in wound-sized doses.
- *Mood*: melancholy nostalgia, surreal whimsy, queasy revulsion, hushed abandonment.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — dual register, contaminated pastel, aged surface, archival light — not any reference image. A photograph of a mint-green abandoned kitchen with a single dusty pink teddy bear on the counter, or a macro of mold blooming across a satin ribbon, is fully Mochette without resembling any known example.

**Avoid**

- Saturated candy pastels, neon, or clean glossy digital color; HDR sharpness; studio lighting; pristine new objects.
- Gothic black, deep crimson, Victorian mourning dress, candles-and-crypt morbidity (that is Morute's fear register, not Mochette's nausea register).
- Sterile clinical whites and blues, gleaming medical equipment, hospital-as-clean-setting framing (Medicalcore).
- Wholesome cottagecore tidiness, kawaii cuteness without taint, sparkly whimsy without rot (Fairycore).
- Gore or injury staged to shock or frighten; the grotesque here is quiet, chronic, decayed — not fresh violence.
- Tidy modern interiors, minimalist styling, commercial product polish.
- Corvid-and-bone witchy earthiness without the feminine pastel half (Crowcore).
- Sexual or explicit content; the style's taint is expressed through dirt, stain and decay.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs. Morute**: both fuse cute with disturbing, but Morute seeks the dark, creepy, morbid — gothic horror, fear and unease, blacks and mourning lace. Mochette seeks revulsion and nausea: rot, mold, vermin, bodily trace, with heavier green and brown and more outdoor/natural decay. Test: does it feel like a haunted séance (Morute) or like a beautiful thing left too long in a damp drawer (Mochette)?
- **Vs. Medicalcore**: Medicalcore centers clinical apparatus, sterile or institutional framing, and medical imagery as subject. Mochette borrows hospital gowns, stitches and scars but embeds them in domestic decay and pastel femininity; its injury is aged, stained and personal, never clinical or clean.
- **Vs. Crowcore**: Crowcore runs on corvids, bones, taxidermy and dark earthy witch-adjacent still lifes, with little or no dollish femininity. Mochette may include bones and wet specimens, but only as one half of a pink-and-rot pairing; remove the pastel feminine register and Mochette collapses toward Crowcore.
- **Vs. Fairy Grunge**: Fairy Grunge mixes fairy motifs with grunge mud, mess and worn layers, but its mess is lifestyle grit, not deliberate disgust. Mochette's fairy elements (wings, elfin BJDs, flower crowns) must be tainted by decay, disease or vermin, and its palette stays faded pastel-sickly rather than grunge-earthly.
- **Vs. Fairycore**: Fairycore is sincere whimsy — bright or soft nature magic, clean cuteness, charm without contamination. Mochette uses the same dolls, lace and flora but insists on the rot: mold on the petals, larvae in the rice pudding. If nothing in the frame could cause a flicker of nausea, it is Fairycore, not Mochette.
