# Ghostcore

Ghostcore circles the dead who stay: ghosts, spirits, cemeteries and the eerie charge of places where something is present that is not supposed to be. It is an image-first culture born on Tumblr blogs and private Facebook pages, fed by Victorian spirit photography, creepypasta found-footage, horror manga and haunted-mansion games, and it is explicitly not fashion-centric. The community splits into two factions — one meditates on death and its philosophy, the other takes the macabre and makes light of it — and both are legitimate Ghostcore. Underneath both sits an identification with the ghost itself: formless, invisible, gentle, shy, watching from the edge of the room.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is a binary of sheet-white and soot-black, bridged only by muted natural mid-tones: sepia albumen, fog gray, gray-green stone, muddy umber. Every hue behaves as if faded, water-stained, or burned out by a camera flash.

- Soot and ink blacks: `#0b0b0a` – `#262421`
- Sheet, bone and paper whites: `#ddd9cf` – `#f2efe6` (flash-lit cloth may clip to pure white)
- Albumen sepia, stained vintage paper: `#a8906b` – `#d6c3a1`
- Fog, stone, gray-green: `#6d716a` – `#a3a79c`
- Dark rustic moss and umber: `#3f3a30` – `#5c564a`

One ordinary saturated color (a child's shirt, flash-lit grass) may intrude in the found-snapshot humour faction as dissonance — the mundane world glimpsed beside the uncanny — but it must read as accident, never as palette.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the text documents them)

1. **The ghost body**: floating bedsheets; transparent beings; black shadow silhouettes with white or black eyes and no corneas. Eye holes optional, absence of face equally valid.
2. **Cemeteries**: headstones, mausoleum visits, funeral formalwear, mourning dress.
3. **Haunted and abandoned architecture**: old houses, mansions, castles, vacant rooms, stairwells; windows, mirrors and doorways as thresholds where a presence appears.
4. **The photograph as evidence**: grainy ghost photos, unexplainable phenomena, blur and double exposure, figures that exist only inside the frame.
5. **Fog-bound emptiness**: forests, fields, graveyards and estates covered in mist and devoid of people.
6. **Handprints**: bloody handprints on mirrors, windows, any glass or painted surface.
7. **Draped fabric**: white sheets, flowing lightweight gowns in white or black, vintage nightgowns, Victorian-esque dress, the hoodie as modern stand-in.
8. **Discomforting stillness**: unsettling modeling positions — standing facing a wall, swaying, hovering just off the ground, head tilted too far, perched where a person should not be.
9. **Silence and night**: chilly air, quiet voices, being out after dark, hide-and-seek, séances, watching and being watched.
10. **Deadpan humour**: costume sheet ghosts caught mid-mishap, tabloid and zine collages joking about spirits, the macabre deflated by affection.

### Materials and textures

Gauze and cheesecloth weave of bedsheets; chiffon and thin cotton of nightgowns; funeral-wool suits; brick, ivy, weathered clapboard, peeling wallpaper; dust, fog, breath. On the image surface: film grain, digital noise, scratches, motion blur, sepia paper stains, flash falloff. Two secondary medium-layers exist: horror-manga ink with screentone (the style's related media runs from *Bleach* and *Mieruko-chan* to *Tokyo Ghoul*), and Victorian woodcut engraving as the historical mourning-culture layer.

### Recurring subjects

A solitary ghost figure; a tiny distant figure in a fogged landscape; ordinary people coexisting with or witnessing a presence; mirrors and reflections; statues and angels in collage; séances and occult paraphernalia. Companion fauna — cats, perched birds — recur in the image record as silent witnesses, though the text does not rank them centrally; treat them as permitted seasoning, not core motif.

### Composition and lighting

Emptiness leads: negative space, fog walls, vacant rooms, with figures small in frame or pushed to an edge. Framing reads as witness — amateur snapshot crops, long exposures, surveillance-like distance, off-center subjects. Vertical drift is characteristic: floating sheets, hanging gowns, feet hidden or just off the ground, figures on rooflines and branches. Thresholds organize the frame: doorways, windows, mirrors, stair landings. Lighting is dim available light, harsh night flash with rapid falloff, candle or séance glow, overcast fog scatter, moonlight; shadows crushed, highlights blown on white cloth. Monochrome or sepia is the default; color, when present, stays muted. Clean three-point studio lighting does not exist here.

## Mood & Values

Dread of "something that is not supposed to be there," held in the same hand as tenderness and joke. Ghostcore values individual freedom and expression, the wish to feel formless, otherworldly, invisible; haunting as identity rather than threat; occult play (séances, mirror-gazing, admiring your reflection); and humour as armour — the second faction openly lightens the macabre. The activity lore is surprisingly gentle: being shy but talkative with close friends, swaying, staying brave while easily scared, silence as a virtue. The mood range runs philosophical melancholy ↔ deadpan comedy ↔ quiet, shy softness, but never reaches glossy cheer or visceral terror.

## Era & Origins

Emerged in the 2010s on Tumblr (blogs like /ghostcore and "Sleeping in the Mausoleum") and private Facebook pages. It inherits from Victorian spirit photography and mourning culture — staged sheet ghosts, crinoline engravings — and from creepypasta found-footage, horror manga, haunted-mansion games (*I Spy Spooky Mansion*, the Nancy Drew haunt cycle), and films like *The Others*. It sits beside Goth, Gloomcore, Ethereal, Cottagegore, Cryptidcore and Goblincore, and reacts against polished digital brightness by insisting on the worn, grainy, dim image as proof of sincerity.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Suggest the presence, never explain it: the uncanny lives in blur, frame edges, eye holes, handprints.
2. Haunt the photograph itself: grain, underexposure, fog, motion blur, paper stain.
3. Keep the palette binary-plus-muted: black, sheet white, faded natural mid-tones.
4. Let fabric do the haunting: drape, float, hang, half-transparent.
5. Choose a faction per image — death-meditation or deadpan humour — but let neither become glossy.
6. Empty the stage: fewer figures, more fog, more silence.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: sheet ghosts, nightgown figures, shadow silhouettes with pupil-less eyes, transparent doubles, empty rooms marked by handprints, fogged graveyards, séance tables, pale spirits in ink manga panels, spirit-zine collages, clouded-eye close-ups, ordinary bystanders beside a presence.
- *Composition*: extreme distance (tiny figure in mist) to intimate crop (a hand on glass); centered altar-like stillness or off-kilter snapshot; horizontal fog landscapes or vertical floating figures.
- *Lighting*: night flash, moonlight, overcast scatter, candle glow, or a single lit cloth against void black; black-and-white, sepia, or muted color.
- *Medium*: found snapshot, long-exposure spirit photo, Victorian engraving, horror-manga ink, painterly fog study, collage.
- *Mood*: melancholy ↔ humour ↔ shy gentleness.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette, grain, presence-in-absence, draped or formless figure, silence — not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Saturated candy or neon color; pastel kawaii mascot ghosts.
- Glossy commercial studio light, crisp high-key clarity, clean minimalist interiors.
- Visceral gore: blood only as handprint, smear, suggestion — never splatter or anatomy.
- Crowded casts, mid-action poses, combat; Ghostcore is stillness.
- Furry cryptid anatomy (antlers, fangs, beast bodies): spirits, not creatures.
- Cozy clutter of bones, mushrooms and hoarded trinkets; warm golden-hour light; saturated green meadows.
- Modern tech clutter as subject: smartphones, LEDs, UI.
- Sharp fashion-editorial styling; this is not a fashion-centric aesthetic.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cryptidcore** hunts folkloric beasts in woods with field-research ephemera; Ghostcore haunts domestic and funerary space with human-shaped absences, and its monochrome/sepia photo grain and sheet fabric are the tell.
- **Dark Naturalism** depicts decay and muted nature with no supernatural agency; add a presence that should not be there — sheet, silhouette, handprint — and it becomes Ghostcore.
- **Ethereal** shares fog and flowing fabric but aims at luminous beauty and ascension; Ghostcore dims, grains and frightens. Ethereal glows; Ghostcore watches.
- **Gloomcore** is depressive interiority — rain, melancholy selfhood, no spirits; Ghostcore externalizes the uncanny as figure or haunted place, and admits humour.
- **Goblincore** is muddy maximalist clutter of frogs, bones and mushrooms with mischievous warmth; Ghostcore is sparse, silent, sheet-white and cold. Goblincore hoards; Ghostcore empties.
- **Goth** is fashion- and music-centric with sharp black styling; Ghostcore is photographic and found-image first, treating costume as evidence rather than outfit.

Where the record is thin — painterly fog studies and animal witnesses, for instance — the image corpus supports them but the text does not rank them; use them sparingly, as atmosphere rather than motif.
