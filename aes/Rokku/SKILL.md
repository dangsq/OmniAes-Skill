# Rokku (ロック / Rock Gal / Rokku Gal)

Rokku is the rock branch of gyaru: a Japanese street-fashion substyle of the mid-to-late 2000s that keeps the gyaru body — mini lengths, knee-high and over-the-knee socks, platform boots, big teased hair, doll-enlarged eyes — but dresses it in the hardware of goth, punk and visual kei. Where mainstream gyaru celebrated tan skin and sunny sweetness, Rokku answered with pale skin, black cloth, silver studs and a grin that is half cheeky, half menace. It was spread by gyaru and rock-leaning magazines (egg, Happie nuts, Koakuma Ageha, SOUL SISTER, KERA, SakuRinaStyle), sold through Shibuya 109 and Laforet Harajuku brands (GLAD NEWS, GHOST OF HARLEM, GILFY, DIABLE BAISER, HEAVEN and EARTH, BACKS, SWORD FISH, GLAVIL by tutuHA), and documented on Ameba and CROOZ blogs. Many of its wearers were Bangya — female visual kei fans — so the style reads as the fan-side, street-wearable cousin of Vkei stage costume rather than the costume itself.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

Black is the ground: it should occupy the majority of the surface area of any coordinate (tops, skirts, leggings, boots, jackets). Onto that ground goes one loud accent, most often a saturated crimson-to-scarlet red, plus metallic neutrals as trim and hardware. Secondary pops of pink or purple are permitted but optional; purple is documented only in the style's key-color list and appears rarely, so treat it as a rare variant.

- Ground black / charcoal: roughly `#0B0B0B`–`#202020`
- Primary accent red (crimson, scarlet, blood red): roughly `#A80F1E`–`#E23A2E`
- Metallics and neutrals: silver `#A8A8A8`–`#D8D8D8`, grey `#6E6E6E`–`#909090`, off-white `#EFEFEF`–`#FAFAFA` (graphic tees, bone prints, sneaker soles)
- Optional pops: hot pink `#E858A0`–`#F584B8`, violet `#5C3A8E`–`#7A4FBF`
- Prints carry their own colors: leopard/cheetah (tan `#C89A50` on black), red-black plaid, black-white stripes and skeletal bone prints

The rule: dark ground + one screaming accent + metal glitter. A palette that turns pastel-dominant, earth-toned, neon-bright or warm-neutral has left Rokku.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Skulls and crosses** — the core Gothic imagery: cross necklaces, cross appliqués, skull prints, skeletal bone graphics.
2. **Metal hardware** — studs, spikes, chains, safety pins, studded belts and chokers.
3. **Faux leather and ripped/distressed denim** — biker jackets, leather-look leggings, torn skinny jeans, distressed hems.
4. **Leopard / cheetah print** — as shoes, bags, trim or accent panels.
5. **Plaid, stripes and bone prints** — most characteristically red-black plaid in ruffled skirt form; black-white or red-black striped socks and sleeves.
6. **Biker imagery** — moto jackets, buckles, racing-style graphics.
7. **Gyaru-cute leakage (secondary):** hearts — sequined hearts, heart hardware, scribbled heart doodles — appear as the sweet counterweight inside the rock coordinate. This is the gyaru half of the hybrid; use it as an accent, never as the theme.

A faithful image should show at least two or three of motifs 1–6 simultaneously; a single band tee without hardware or Gothic imagery reads as casual, not Rokku.

### Materials and textures

The style is built on hard-soft collision: glossy faux leather, cold metal studs and chains, coarse fishnet and distressed denim set against soft ruffled chiffon or tulle tiers, fuzzy distressed knits, sequin patches and crimped or teased hair. Thick rubber platform and creeper soles ground everything. Expect cracked-print cotton tees, lace trim under ruffle hems, and visible wear (rips, fraying, safety-pin repairs) as intentional texture.

### Recurring subjects

Young women in full coordinates: solo standing poses showing the whole silhouette boot-to-hairpiece, pairs and small groups of friends, brand-shop interiors, night street snaps, and magazine coordinate spreads. Hair is a headline element: bleached platinum or blonde, ash, pink, orange or other dyed colors, styled in teased voluminous curls, crimped waves, layered spikey visual-kei-influenced cuts or heavy side-swept bangs. Small headpieces (mini hats, feather fascinators, ribbon clips) appear as accessorizing flourishes. Faces carry pale, luminous, untanned skin; heavy dark smokey eyes; bright or unnatural-colored circle lenses enlarging the iris; light pink, pale or deliberately dark moody lips; bleached brows or light sharp nose contour. Visible tattoos and piercings are common and welcome.

### Composition, lighting and medium

Rokku is native to three mediums, and a generated image may adopt any of them: the gyaru magazine scan (flat even lighting, coordinate cutouts, Japanese text columns and inset "hot item" detail boxes), the blog or shop snapshot (direct on-camera flash, slightly tilted angle, cluttered store racks or night city street behind, hard shadows and hot highlights on pale skin), and the plain-background coordinate cutout (full body centered on white or pale ground to document the outfit). Hand-drawn deco overlays — marker lettering, scribbled hearts, stickers, drawn wings or arrows — are a characteristic blog-photo finish and may be layered over any of the three. Lighting is therefore typically harsh, direct and unpolished: flash-bright skin, saturated reds, slight overexposure. Soft golden-hour glow or sculpted studio softbox lighting is foreign to the style's documented look.

## Mood & Values

Rokku values rebellion, counterculture, creativity and bold individuality, expressed through accessorizing rather than through expense or polish. Its emotional register is cute-aggressive: devilish playfulness (scribbled "DEVIL" lettering, heart-and-cross hybrids, cheeky grins and peace signs) layered over genuinely dark signifiers. It is a communal, fan-culture style — brand shops, live-house fandom, blog circles — so images often feel like snapshots shared between friends rather than authoritative fashion plates. Authenticity beats perfection: visible flash glare, cluttered backgrounds and DIY deco are features, not flaws.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in Japan in the mid-to-late 2000s, popularized by gyaru magazines and rock-leaning Shibuya/Laforet brands, with no single confirmed creator. It reacts against the tanned, sunny mainstream gyaru of the same period by refusing the tan entirely, and it borrows its darkness from goth, punk and — most intimately — visual kei, via the Bangya fan community. Iconic figures include Rina Sakurai (Sakurina), Chie and Chika Yoshikawa, Momoko Kuroki and Yuki Yamamoto. Its natural habitat is print scans and early Japanese blogging platforms, which explains its snapshot aesthetic.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Black ground first: most of the cloth surface is black; red (or rarely pink/purple) is the accent; silver metal is the trim.
2. Pale, luminous, untanned skin with heavy dark smokey eyes and circle-lens enlargement is the face rule; lips pale pink or moody dark.
3. Hard-soft collision in every coordinate: leather/studs/chains against ruffles, tulle, sequins or hearts.
4. Keep the gyaru silhouette: mini skirt or ripped skinny denim, knee-high or over-the-knee socks, platform/combat/studded creeper boots, big teased or dyed hair.
5. Motif density: show at least two or three documented rock motifs (skull, cross, studs, chains, leopard, plaid, rips) at once.
6. Medium honesty: flash snapshot, magazine-scan collage, or plain-background coordinate cutout; optional hand-drawn deco overlays. Slight overexposure and saturated reds are correct.

**Variation axes (all of this is allowed to vary)**

- *Subject:* solo coordinate, pair of friends, group shot, makeup or eye close-up, accessory still life, shop interior, night street, daytime street, magazine-style model page.
- *Composition:* full-body standing pose, tilted candid snap, centered white-background cutout, multi-image collage spread with text blocks and inset detail boxes, close crop on boots or hardware.
- *Lighting:* direct on-camera flash (day or night), flat editorial scan lighting, overcast daylight street; always fairly harsh and direct rather than sculpted.
- *Color handling:* red-dominant accent vs. near-monochrome black-silver with a pink pop vs. rare violet accent; pattern accent chosen among leopard, red-black plaid, stripes or bone print; hair any bleached or dyed color.
- *Mood:* cheeky cute-aggressive, cool and stoic, or playful group energy.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A rooftop night snap of a pink-haired girl in a studded moto jacket, bone-print tee and fishnets over platform boots is fully Rokku even if no reference shows it; a white-background cutout of a red-plaid ruffle coordinate is equally Rokku.

**Avoid (style-breakers)**

- Deep gyaru tan, bronzed or sun-glow skin.
- Pastel-dominant sweet gyaru or amaro palettes, neon gyaru brights, or any palette without a black ground.
- Natural or minimal makeup; bare faces; shimmery white gyaru eye liner without dark smokey weight.
- Modest, preppy, office or bohemian silhouettes; long flowing skirts; mori-girl layering; cottagecore florals; sportswear; oversized unisex streetwear that erases the gyaru silhouette.
- Gothic Lolita structure: bell-shaped petticoat skirts, blouses, bonnets, knee-length-or-longer modest coverage, porcelain doll makeup.
- Austere monochrome goth: Victorian romantic or fetish elegance, matte black-only refinement without gyaru cuteness or rock hardware.
- Western punk austerity: utilitarian unisex styling, anarchy signage, mohawk-only identity without gyaru face and silhouette.
- Visual kei stage costume as subject: corsets, historical coats, androgynous performer regalia (Rokku borrows Vkei hair and tees, not its stage wardrobe).
- Glossy high-fashion softbox lighting, matte earth tones, minimalist interiors, clean e-commerce luxury styling.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Decisive difference |
|---|---|
| Punk | Punk is utilitarian and largely unisex, with austere or anarchist signifiers; Rokku keeps the gyaru face (circle lenses, pale luminous skin, teased cute hair), feminine mini coordinates, ruffles and platform boots, and treats plaid as cute ruffle rather than rebellion flag. |
| Visual Kei | Vkei is musicians' androgynous stage costume — corsets, historical coats, theatrical staging; Rokku is the fan-side street style (Bangya), worn by gyaru in mini skirts and boots, photographed in shops and streets, borrowing only Vkei hair tease and band graphics. |
| Goth | Goth commits to monochrome black with romantic, Victorian or fetish maturity and elegant lines; Rokku injects red, pink and leopard, gyaru makeup and cuteness, flash-snapshot energy, and ruffled mini silhouettes that goth rejects. |
| Gothic Lolita | GoLoli builds a bell-shaped petticoat silhouette with blouses, head bonnets and modest coverage and doll-like makeup; Rokku is body-conscious mini length, ripped denim, bare shoulders, smokey eyes, with ruffles as tiered trim rather than a bell. |
| Gyaru / Tsuyome Gyaru | Mainstream and tsuyome gyaru keep tanned or warm skin, shimmery eye makeup, bright or sweet palettes and cute motifs; Rokku refuses the tan, darkens the eyes to smokey, grounds everything in black, and swaps bows for skulls, crosses, studs and chains. |

**Quick test:** pale skin + black-ground coordinate + two or more gothic/rock motifs + gyaru silhouette and eye makeup = Rokku. Remove the gyaru face and silhouette and it drifts to punk or goth; remove the black ground and rock motifs and it drifts back to gyaru; add petticoats and blouses and it becomes Gothic Lolita.

**Evidence notes:** purple appears only in the style's documented key colors and is rare in practice; tattoos and piercings are text-documented but understated in surviving imagery; biker imagery is listed among key motifs yet appears less often than skulls, studs and plaid; the style is documented as a gyaru (female-centric) substyle, so masculine presentations are outside its recorded range.
