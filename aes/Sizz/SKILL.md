# Sizz

Sizz takes its name from onomatopoeia — the hiss of static, of a signal breaking up — and its images look the way that hiss sounds: granular, smeared, partially lost in transmission. It is a photographic aesthetic of degradation applied to the real: a street, a figure, a horizon, a sheet of printer output, all pushed through noise, blur, artifacting and slight warp until reality reads as something estranged but still legible. The community defines it by ethos rather than rulebook — composition over technique, experimentation over purity, feeling over formula — and treats crediting artists as mandatory. Its lineage is borrowed and old: German Expressionism's abstraction-for-mood, film noir's emotional contrast, and Japanese are-bure-bokeh street photography's gritty, out-of-focus embrace of the imperfect frame.

## Visual Identity

**Palette.** The rule is monochrome-first with controlled exceptions. The core range runs from ink black (`#050505`–`#111111`) through fog grays (`#8a8a8a`–`#b8b8b8`) to paper white (`#e8e6e0`–`#f4f2ec`), handled either as crushing high contrast or as low-contrast fog where blacks dissolve into gray. Two documented departures: fully desaturated, washed-out color (pale sky `#cfd6d9`, dead grass beige `#b7a98f`, asphalt gray) where color exists but carries no cheer; and monochrome plus one hot accent — an ember orange/amber glow (`#d98a1e`–`#e8a020`) isolated in a black or cream field. Saturated multi-hue palettes do not occur.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how the text documents them.** (1) Noise: film grain, digital grain, fog haze — a sibilant surface over everything. (2) Artifacting: technological damage as content, including printer ink splatter and streaks, scan banding, glitch marks; a machine's dying output can be the entire subject. (3) Subtle deformation: slight warp, motion smear, soft-focus drift — never grotesque mutilation. (4) Abstracted realism: a recognizably real scene made strange, whether by degradation, by incongruity (a costumed figure doing something mundane in a solemn place), or by scale and void (a tiny falling body held still before an abstracted sun). Secondary motifs inherited from the named influences: chiaroscuro contrast and backlight silhouettes (noir), angular or radial compositional tension for mood (Expressionism), gritty unfocused street candidness (are-bure-bokeh).

**Materials and textures.** Film grain and fog; cloth folds and hanging shrouds; paper tooth and ink bleed; scanner glass flatness; halation blooming around a single light source; motion smear on a moving figure; overcast sky as a flat white ceiling.

**Recurring subjects.** Solitary human figures, blurred or masked or costumed; liminal outdoor space — bridges, paths, graveyards, fields, shorelines; minimal horizons reduced to two or three tonal bands with a small sun; suspended or falling figures; cloth and fog studies; machine-made mark-fields with no human subject at all.

**Composition and lighting.** Composition is the valued variable: one-point perspective down a path or walkway, a centered lone figure in a void, flat stacked horizon bands, radial lines converging on a glowing center, or an all-over texture field with no focal point. Lighting ranges from diffuse overcast and fog-scatter that flattens depth, to hard backlight and silhouette, to a single luminous source suspended in darkness. Focus is deliberately imperfect — soft, smeared, or missed — while framing stays intentional.

## Mood & Values

Uneasy quiet, melancholy, eerie stillness, the feeling of a memory degrading. Sizz values emotion over formula: an image succeeds by feeling, not by technical correctness. Titles and captions participate in the abstraction, often reframing what the picture means. The culture prizes exploration and credits its makers; purity of process is explicitly not a virtue.

## Era & Origins

Named around 2017 from Tumblr and Instagram degradation trends, organized on r/sizz by u/tiggerclaw, but deliberately descended from older movements: 1920s German Expressionist cinema, mid-century American film noir, and 1960s Japanese are-bure-bokeh. It reacts against clean, filter-polished digital photography by re-introducing hiss, grit and damage as expressive tools. Note honestly: the wiki's are-bure-bokeh slot is an unfilled placeholder, so grit-and-blur cues are read from the surviving works and the infobox rather than from a dedicated exemplar.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- Begin from a plausible real-world capture, then degrade the signal: grain, fog, blur, smear, scan or printer artifact, slight warp. The damage is the style.
- Keep the real world anchor visible. Abstracted realism means the subject survives its own degradation; even a pure artifact-field should read as a machine's physical output.
- Let composition carry the image: deliberate framing, negative space, strong horizontals or radial pull, even when execution is lo-fi.
- Choose one palette mode: high-contrast monochrome, fog gray monochrome, desaturated muted color, or monochrome plus a single ember accent.
- Make the mundane strange by context or scale, not by fantasy content.

Variation axes: subject (figure, street, graveyard, field, horizon, cloth, falling body, printer/scan artifact — anything ordinary); composition (centered figure, deep perspective, banded horizon, radial convergence, all-over texture); lighting (overcast flatten, fog scatter, hard backlight, lone glow in void); color handling (any of the four palette modes); degradation intensity (whisper of grain to heavy smear); mood (melancholy, eerie, serene void, anxious static). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid:
- Clean, sharp, noise-free digital rendering; glossy HDR clarity; crisp commercial lighting.
- Saturated, multi-hue, neon or pastel-gradient color.
- Literal monster lore as the point of the image (creature-as-subject belongs to Cryptidcore); costumed or shrouded figures appear only as estrangement devices.
- Painted angular sets, hand-drawn distorted geometry, brushstroke chiaroscuro (Expressionism's domain).
- Legible narrative cinema staging with pristine focus and sculpted shadow patterns (film noir's domain).
- Total non-representational abstraction with no real-world or machine-real anchor.
- Deformation so extreme the subject becomes unreadable gore or cartoon.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Cryptidcore:** Cryptidcore's interest is the creature — flash-lit night forests, cryptid silhouettes, bestiary lore. Sizz's interest is the image's own breakdown; its shrouded or antlered figures are cloth-and-fog studies or costumed people in daylight graveyards, and Sizz happily depicts horizons, bridges and printer marks with no creature anywhere. Ask: is the mystery the subject, or the signal?
- **Vs Expressionism:** Expressionism distorts the depicted world — jagged painted walls, impossible set geometry, tinted staged interiors. Sizz distorts the photograph: the scene's geometry stays photographic while grain, blur and artifacting corrupt the surface. Tinted expressionist set pieces appear in Sizz contexts only as cited ancestors.
- **Vs Film Noir:** Noir keeps glossy tonal gradation, crisp focus and story-legible staging; its hard light and blind-slat shadows serve narrative. Sizz borrows noir's emotional contrast but ruins the fidelity — fog eats the blacks, grain eats the detail, and its scenes are non-narrative, solitary or empty. Noir images are legible; Sizz images hiss.
