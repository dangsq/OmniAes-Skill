# Neoperreo

Neoperreo is a music genre and aesthetic that emerged in the mid-to-late 2010s as a DIY, internet-driven, underground alternative to mainstream reggaetón. Coined by Chilean artist Tomasa del Real and built with figures like Ms Nina, it restores the hard, dance-focused dembow "perreo" beat while fusing it with darker experimental electronics — deconstructed club, trap, grime, industrial, witch house — often intentionally lo-fi and distorted. That sonic distortion has a direct visual analogue: compressed flash photos, airbrushed renders, glitched net.art graphics and hand-made flyer collages that reject polished commercial design. The subculture is decentralized, feminist and LGBTQ+-centered, built as a safer club space where women and queer artists are the protagonists of their own sexuality, subverting reggaetón's traditional male gaze. Visually it merges futuristic internet-native art with the street style (*cultura de barrio*) of reggaetón's origins: "cybernetic and even goth, leaning towards a twisted Lolita."

## Visual Identity

**Color palette (rule, not recipe).** The infobox documents two registered modes, and both are canonical: (a) *bold and vibrant* candy-neon, and (b) *dark, gothic-inspired*. The candy-neon core runs hot magenta and pink (`#FF2E9A`–`#FF7BC0`), bubblegum and pastel pink haze (`#FFB0D6`–`#FBDCEF`), lavender and lilac (`#B98CFF`–`#E3C8FF`), candy blue (`#8FD4F5`), with acid green (`#A4FF3F`) as a clashing accent and chrome silver (`#D9D9E3`–`#F2F2F7`) as the metallic neutral. The gothic mode runs near-black (`#0B0B10`), oxblood and lacquer red (`#B00020`–`#E00040`), chrome and lilac glowing out of darkness. A third support register exists for net.art graphics: flat concrete grey (`#8C8C94`) or stark white fields carrying neon silhouettes and pixel type. Pink is the gravitational center but not obligatory; the binding rule is *saturated gloss plus metallic neutral*, or *dark lacquer plus chrome* — never muted, matte, or earth-toned.

**Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence.**
1. **Net.art internet graphics** (the infobox's key motif): desktop OS icons pasted onto skin, pixel checkerboards, glitch artifacts, comic panels, sticker-stack text layers, ornate blackletter and death-metal logotypes set against candy backgrounds, chaotic flyer collages where typography is a lead visual element ("world tour" posters, track lists, URLs).
2. **Body adornment as subject**: long false nails (stiletto and claw shapes, chrome-plated or airbrushed with art), exaggerated makeup (overdrawn glossy lips, graphic liner, frosted shadow, heavy blush), visible tattoos, piercings, grillz and tooth gems. Lips, teeth and hands are legitimate standalone subjects, not just attributes of a portrait.
3. **Y2K consumer tech fetish**: flip phones with holographic screens, chrome plastic keypads, cursor arrows, desktop icons — nostalgic consumer kitsch worn as jewelry, never sci-fi hardware.
4. **Fashion blend**: streetwear (crop tops, baggy pants, knee-high socks, platform boots) plus deconstructed high fashion plus goth/"twisted Lolita" (plaid minis, fishnets, mesh tops, spiked chokers, striped legwarmers).
5. **Doll and avatar renders**: 3D-rendered hyperfeminine dolls with elf ears, airbrushed plastic skin, star sparkles and pastel haze — the internet-native self-portrait.
6. **Club and barrio snapshots**: harsh on-camera flash in dark rooms, storefront streets, underground party crowds.
7. **Barrio airbrush iconography** (image-supported, tied to the text's *cultura de barrio*): chrome lowrider cars, flying banknotes, airbrushed script lettering, roses — chicano airbrush tradition rerun through Y2K chrome.
8. **Gloss macros**: liquid chrome, satin, vinyl and lip gloss as pure texture studies.

**Materials and textures.** Gloss and specular highlight everywhere: lip gloss, chrome plate, liquid metal, satin, vinyl, plastic doll skin, holographic film. Against that, deliberate lo-fi degradation: flash blowouts, compression artifacts, scan-line and zine grain, airbrush softness, pixelated type. The tension between wet shine and cheap compression is the texture signature.

**Recurring subjects.** Women and queer performers owning the frame; rendered doll-avatars; manicured hands holding phones or jewels; mouths with tooth gems; club friends; flyer layouts with no human at all. The subject returns the gaze — posed for themselves, not for a viewer's consumption.

**Composition and lighting.** Maximalist flyer collage: stacked photos, clashing text blocks, checkerboards, stars, sparkles, comic inserts, no negative space respected. Flat net.art poster: one silhouette or object on grey/white with pixel type. Extreme macro of mouth, hand or nail. Centered avatar portrait in soft glow. Tilted snapshot angle. Lighting ranges from direct on-camera flash with hard black shadows, to specular studio gloss on chrome and lips, to airbrushed gradient glow and emissive neon accents.

## Mood & Values

Unapologetic sexual liberation, queer joy and underground defiance, delivered with DIY anti-commercial roughness. Playful and menacing at once — candy pink holding a metal logo, a doll face with a deadpan stare, a gloss lip with a chrome fang. Inclusivity, feminism, LGBTQ+ empowerment and self-made production are the values the visuals must carry: everything looks self-shot, self-rendered, self-published.

## Era & Origins

Mid-to-late 2010s, internet-based, with strongholds in Chile, Argentina, Mexico and Spain; coined by Tomasa del Real. It reacts against the polished, commercialized, heteronormative presentation of mainstream pop-reggaetón, and against the male gaze of classic reggaetón imagery. Related aesthetics it borders: Bimbocore, Cyberpunk, Goth, Motomami, Neo-Y2K, Raxet, Reggaetonero.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
- Build the image as an internet artifact: a flyer, a render, a flash snapshot, an airbrushed cover — never as clean editorial design.
- Commit to one palette mode (candy-neon, gothic lacquer, or grey net.art base with neon accents) and keep gloss plus a metallic neutral present.
- Let typography participate: blackletter or death-metal logotypes, pixel fonts, chrome lettering, sticker text.
- Put adornment in frame: nails, lips, teeth jewels, tattoos, spikes, chokers.
- Keep the subject self-possessed; the gaze belongs to them.
- Add one layer of degradation or hand-made evidence: flash blowout, compression, airbrush bleed, collage misregistration.

**Variation axes.** Subject: portrait, macro of mouth/hand/nails, full-body fashion, club crowd, avatar render, object still-life (phone, jewels), text-only flyer. Composition: collage chaos, flat minimal poster, centered portrait, tilted snapshot, macro crop. Lighting: harsh flash, specular gloss, airbrushed glow, neon emissive, dark gothic key. Color handling: full candy-neon, pink-dominant with chrome, gothic dark mode, desaturated base with acid accents. Medium: 3D render, flash photo, airbrush illustration, vector flyer, scanned zine. Mood: playful candy, menacing goth, raw party documentary. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** Polished commercial pop-reggaetón gloss (clean luxury, beach-resort tropicalism, smooth streaming-cover gradients). Neutral beige, matte textures, tasteful restraint, corporate grid layouts. Clean studio beauty lighting without flash or gloss logic. Vaporwave props (Greek statues, palm-grid sunsets) standing in for net.art. Utilitarian sci-fi techwear, HUD interfaces, rain-slick megacity noir. Pure soft coquette pink with no chrome, spike, flash grit or metal type. Macho barrio documentary without queer/net.art intervention. Minimalist gallery abstraction. Anything that poses the subject as an object for an external gaze.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Bimbocore** shares pink, nails and hyperfemininity, but stays soft, plush and naive-cute; neoperreo adds goth metal logotypes, chrome, flash-lit club grit, barrio streetwear and queer defiance — the "twisted" in twisted Lolita.
- **Motomami** is minimalist and high-art: stark black/white/red, flamenco-modernist restraint, gallery negative space. Neoperreo is maximalist, candy-neon, DIY collage; restraint is the tell against it.
- **Raxet** overlaps in Spanish underground internet rap culture and bootleg graphics, but leans streetwear-skate rawness; neoperreo centers feminine/queer body adornment (gloss, nails, dolls) and perreo club context. Evidence here is thinner than for the other neighbors.
- **Reggaetonero** is classic 2000s barrio reggaetón realism: masculine-coded bling, sportswear, cars, documentary straightness. Neoperreo subverts it with queer protagonists, net.art layers and goth-Y2K fusion.
- **Cyberpunk** treats technology as dystopian infrastructure — utilitarian gear, megacity neon, teal-orange noir. Neoperreo's tech is nostalgic consumer kitsch (flip phones, OS icons, chrome logos) worn as jewelry, in candy pink, at a party, not in a dystopia.
