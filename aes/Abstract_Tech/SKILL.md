# Abstract Tech

Software is intangible, so culture invented a pictorial vocabulary for it. Abstract Tech is that vocabulary: a set of abstract motifs — concentric arcs, hexagons, circuit patterns, streams of code — used to signify "technology," "digital," "cyber," regardless of whether any real machine is present. It lives in science-fiction fantasy user interfaces (FUIs), heads-up displays and holographic touchscreens, depictions of cyberspace, game worlds, anime promotional art, event posters, desktop skins and widgets, and above all in the stock-image industry's idea of a "high-tech background." By the late 2010s its stock form had become self-consciously cliché, which is itself part of its cultural identity: this is the default costume that visual culture puts on the concept of computing.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is **one emissive hue family plus white, on a near-black ground**. The ground runs from pure black to deep navy (`#000000`–`#0A1220`); the default dominant hue is electric/cyan blue (`#0066FF`–`#4DE3FF`), justified in-universe as the colour of the artificial; highlights burn out to blue-white (`#EAF9FF`–`#FFFFFF`). Documented secondary modes: **phosphor green** (`#00E05A`–`#7CFF9B`) for raining code, wireframe schematics and simulation/hacking subject matter; **magenta–violet** (`#8A2BFF`–`#FF4BD8`) as an accent hue in game-world and anime lineages; **silver-white** for robot bodies and hardware shells. Warm orange, daylight and flat white grounds appear only at the boundary with DORFic and should be treated as outliers, not the core. Value contrast is extreme: luminous graphics against void, with little or no mid-tone ambient light.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Glowing lines**, blue or white, standing for energy and power — the style's primal element.
2. **Holographic / transparent touchscreen interfaces**: floating translucent panels, gestural screens, heads-up displays.
3. **Segmented concentric arcs and circles** of varying thickness — the "technological circle," radial tick rings, dial medallions with a bright core.
4. **Hexagons**, tiled or scattered, as the "efficient" futuristic shape.
5. **Printed-circuit-board traces**: right-angled tracks, nodes, pads, rendered as light.
6. **Binary digits and computer code**, raining columns, micro-labels, monospace readouts.
7. **Dots connected by lines** (plexus networks) signifying the Internet and connectivity.
8. **Robots, androids and robotic body parts**, artificial intelligence imagery.

Stock-image add-ons combine freely with the above: padlocks (cybersecurity), brain silhouettes (AI), and a businessperson tapping a floating touchscreen. Corner brackets, edge gauges, thin leader lines and tiny unreadable annotations are the connective tissue that turns any of these motifs into an "interface."

### Materials and textures

Glassy translucency, smooth vector and 3D-render surfaces, etched circuit copper glowing as pure light, wireframe meshes, scanlines and grid floors, glossy black planes that mirror the graphics above them, pixel/monospace typography. Everything is machined or rendered; there is no paper, cloth, wood, skin texture emphasis, grain or hand-made mark.

### Recurring subjects

The interface with no protagonist at all (abstract backgrounds, wallpapers, HUD medallions); cyberspace tunnels and game-world corridors seen first-person with gauge overlays; androids and robots posed in glowing chambers; silhouetted or rim-lit operators and stock businesspeople interacting with floating panels; anime idols and DJs framed by radial holographic rings; schematic sheets of wireframe objects with annotation leaders.

### Composition and lighting

Composition favours layered translucency and a clear focal geometry: a centred radial medallion; a full-bleed motif field (circuitry, plexus) with no centre; a first-person HUD frame wrapping an environment with corner brackets and side gauges; one-point perspective tunnels of receding light; a cloud of floating panels arranged around a standing figure. Lighting is **emissive only** — the graphics are the light source. Bloom and halos surround bright elements; screens cast rim light and coloured reflections onto faces, bodies and glossy floors; darkness reads as void rather than atmosphere. No sun, no sky, no practical lamps.

## Mood & Values

The style expresses technological advancement, digital transformation and connectivity: competence without friction, information made visible, progress as a clean blue glow. Its optimistic corporate register dominates (stock imagery, posters, product-keynote visuals), with a secondary eerie register — green code, wireframe voids — for simulated reality and hacking. A third, knowing register exists: the late-2010s awareness that this imagery is a cliché, used anyway because it communicates instantly.

## Era & Origins

Motif ancestry reaches back to the "technological circle" of 1970s space opera and to TRON (1982), whose glowing lines, circles and grids also seeded Synthwave; green raining code dates to The Matrix (1999). The style as documented codified in the **c. 1990s–2000s**, spreading with the popular internet and with sci-fi anime and film — Ghost in the Shell, Minority Report (2002), Iron Man (2008) — whose gesture interfaces became the template. Blue became the default because late-20th-century science fiction coded blue as the artificial. In the 2010s it spawned the hexagon-heavy, glossy Hexatron variant, and it persists in game FUIs, desktop customization and festival poster design.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Begin with the void: near-black or deep-navy ground; darkness is empty space, not mood fog.
- Light is data: every bright element is a graphic (line, arc, glyph, trace) that emits its own glow with bloom.
- Stack two to four motif families per image (e.g. arcs + circuit traces + micro-code), varying line weight and segmenting the arcs so rings read as instruments, not ornaments.
- Keep hue discipline: one saturated hue family plus white; blue-cyan default, green phosphor for code/simulation, magenta-violet as accent, silver-white for hardware.
- Make interfaces fantastically dense and non-functional: decorative telemetry, tick rings, corner brackets and illegible microtype are correct; a tidy real-world UI is not.
- Prefer hard geometry — circles, hexagons, right angles, sharp corners; curves only as arcs and rings.
- Treat figures as optional and secondary: a silhouette, an android, a gesturing operator, or nobody at all.
- Finish clean: 3D-render or vector crispness, glass translucency, glossy reflections, scanline subtlety.

**Variation axes.** Subject: pure abstract background ↔ interface medallion ↔ cyberspace tunnel ↔ game corridor with HUD ↔ robot portrait ↔ stock businessperson ↔ anime/music poster. Composition: centred radial ↔ full-bleed field ↔ HUD frame over a scene ↔ one-point tunnel ↔ panel cloud around a figure ↔ annotated schematic sheet. Lighting: pure emissive on black ↔ dim interior lit solely by screens ↔ bright hologram bloom. Colour: blue-cyan core, green phosphor sub-mode, violet-magenta accent, white/silver hardware; orange-daylight only as a DORFic-adjacent edge case. Mood: corporate optimism ↔ eerie simulation ↔ playful pop ↔ knowing cliché. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Nature: trees, water, sky, clouds, bubbles, grass, fish, lenses flare over meadows (Frutiger Aero territory).
- Dystopian street grit: rain, grime, crowds, multicolour neon signage, decay (Cyberpunk).
- Daylight, orange-dominant flat vector minimalism on white (DORFic).
- Chrome retro-leisure utopias, gleaming 1980s corporate futurism (Cyberprep).
- Sleek single-object product renders with restrained gradients and few motifs (Dark Aero).
- Functional flat-design UI, skeuomorphic glossy buttons, paper, film grain, hand-drawn line, serif or organic typography, warm incandescent light, steampunk brass dials.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cyberprep** depicts a lived-in retro-utopian world — chrome bodies, bright interiors, leisure — with reflected light; Abstract Tech depicts an abstraction layer, emissive graphics on void, with no world-building warmth.
- **Cyberpunk** is dystopian, urban, wet and poly-neon; Abstract Tech is clean, monochrome-cool, non-urban and neutral-to-optimistic.
- **DORFic** is daylight + orange + flat vector minimalism on light grounds; Abstract Tech is dark, glowing and motif-dense.
- **Dark Aero** is a restrained dark product-marketing design — one glossy object, subtle gradients; Abstract Tech fills the frame with symbolic interface ornament.
- **Frutiger Aero** fuses gloss with nature and sky-blue optimism; Abstract Tech explicitly omits nature and replaces sky with void.
- When hexagons and holographic gloss dominate a 2010s-flavoured piece, it leans toward the child style Hexatron — still within Abstract Tech's family, but sharper-edged and glossier.
