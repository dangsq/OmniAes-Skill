# Xpiritualism

Xpiritualism is a loosely defined internet outsider-art movement built on digital collage. It re-appropriates the "dark side" of the early-2000s web — spam folders, romance and miracle-cure scams, shock-site debris, bootleg Flash games, sim-date RPG sprites — and welds that material to esotericism, New Age spirituality and foreign internet culture. Coined on Instagram by 3rd.world.elite and crystallized around the net artist Yabujin and the "Azeroy" lore project (2018), it descends from James Howard's early-2010s "Schizocore" collages of scam-email imagery. The style aestheticizes a *broken* Web 2.0: 144p murk, screen-recorder watermarks, garbled audio implied by garbled visuals. Unlike Dreamcore's liminal comfort, Xpiritualism pursues "digital grotesquerie" — uncanny and sublime randomness, delivered with a straight face. Its tone is ethereal, mystical and unsettling but *serious*: world-building and hyper-sigil magic, not shitposting irony.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

Xpiritualism oscillates between two poles, and a single image may contain both:

- **Spam-gloss saturation pole:** hot magenta and fuchsia (#FF00CC–#CC0066), deep crimson and oxblood maroon (#8B0000–#4A0000), electric blue (#0000EE–#0066FF), acid green (#00CC00–#66FF00), and orange-to-gold gradient fills (#FF6600–#FFD700). Colors are over-driven, as if pushed through a cheap scanner or a saturation slider.
- **Corrupted-ethereal pole:** overexposed near-white bloom (#FFF8F0–#FFFFFF), pastel pink (#FFC0DB), soft cyan halo-glows (#00E5FF), laid over murky JPEG beige-grey (#B9B2A6) and washed-out low-bitdepth shadows.

Bridging devices: rainbow fades and conic spectral gradients, lens-flare whites, and neon glow outlines (magenta or green) around cutout figures. Desaturation is allowed only as compression damage, never as a mood choice.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the text documents them)

1. **Digital collage of old-web debris** — the defining method: heterogeneous fragments (photos, sprites, diagrams, clipart) pasted with hard edges, drop shadows and outer glows.
2. **Esoteric / New Age signifiers** — halos and sparkle rings over heads, angels and wings, love-potion and spell advertisements, miracle-heal product pitches, numerological sigils (1616 for chaos/evil, 8888 for good/magic, 777), constructed-language glyphs.
3. **Spam and scam imagery** — the glossy, overly perfect ad look: testimonial text blocks, "100% guarantee" promises, product buckets, dyed-banknote fraud gloss, roleplay figures with California tans and neon-white teeth.
4. **Broken-media markers** — "Unregistered Hypercam 2"-style watermarks, 144p pixelation, screen-capture cursors and timestamp overlays, bootleg game title screens.
5. **Foreign and garbled text** — overlapping scripts (Arabic, Cyrillic, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese), zalgo-corrupted Unicode, ornamental Unicode usernames, scrolling text bands; legibility is optional, meaning is deliberately withheld.
6. **Medical imagery** — ultrasound scans, surgical diagrams, "under the knife" themes, contrasted against angelic healing iconography.
7. **Anime and bootleg-game sprites** — visual-novel and sim-date RPG character art, Flash-era game renders, manga-style line figures, often the sole "clean" layer in a dirty field.
8. **Conspiracy and fringe-science ephemera** — diagrammatic overlays, paranoid annotation, pseudo-technical schematics.
9. **Glitter-graphics clipart** — heart and star cutouts, floral photo frames, rose borders, sparkle GIF-style stars, cursive script flourishes.
10. **Software UI chrome** — media-player skins, photo-editor windows, forum and gallery thumbnails framing the artwork from inside the screen.

### Materials and textures

JPEG compression blocks, chroma bleed, pixel upsampling fuzz, scan-to-screen flatness, MS Paint/Photoshop cutout edges, WordArt metallic serif lettering, cursive script overlays, spray-paint scribble marks, scribbled pen-line doodles over prints, mirrored kaleidoscopic shard slices of photographs, floral and heart-shaped vignette frames. The substrate is always a screen (or a print of a screen); paper-craft textures do not belong.

### Recurring subjects

Portraits of strangers and net-artists glow-treated into icons; grey alien or mannequin humanoid figures; anime youths with swords or wings; street and crowd snapshots with faces blurred or blacked out; couples from romance-scam stock photos; religious and occult iconography; medical scans; product shots; gallery walls of printed collages; desktop and editor windows.

### Composition and lighting

Composition refuses unity: no shared perspective, scale or light source across layers. Common structures include the spam-landing-page grid (central text column flanked by thumbnail rails), the centered shrine (one figure ringed by glow, border vignette and corner clipart), the UI window as frame, the wide banner strip, and full-bleed chaotic stacking. Typography clashes by rule: metallic serif WordArt titles, handwritten cursive, garbled Unicode and foreign script coexist in one field. Lighting is screen-native: flat scanner light, overexposed flash bloom, radial halo glows, lens flares, neon saturation with no physical source, and compression murk in the shadows.

## Mood & Values

Confusion, discomfort, foreignness, nostalgia, lack of context, surrealism, the uncanny. The work feels like a transmission from a web that never existed — sincere devotion expressed through corrupted tools. Scam gloss produces unease precisely because it is *too perfect*; the esoteric layer produces awe precisely because it is *too broken*. Values: outsider authorship, lore-building and shared mythology, multicultural drift, and reverence for internet trash as sacred material.

## Era & Origins

Early-2010s roots in Net.art and James Howard's "Schizocore" scam-collages; crystallization 2018–2022 with Yabujin, the Azeroy video series and the NKWeb community; codification and naming in the 2020s on Instagram. Source material reaches back to the Flash-era web (1998–2006): sim-date RPGs, bootleg games, spam email, shock sites. It reacts against both polished commercial design and, later, against its own sanitized TikTok offshoot (Yabujincore), preserving the dense, lore-heavy, "serious" collage practice.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Assemble, don't photograph: layer at least three ontologically different sources (a photo, an anime or game sprite, a diagram or clipart, a UI fragment) with hard cutout edges, glows and drop shadows.
- Damage the signal: apply one degradation pass — pixelation, JPEG blocking, upscale fuzz, a recorder watermark or timestamp.
- Fuse scam and spirit: pair one miracle-promise text block or product pitch with one esoteric signifier (halo, angel, sigil number, spell name).
- Let text misbehave: mix at least two clashing typefaces and one foreign or zalgo-corrupted script; meaning may be withheld entirely.
- Keep the tone sincere: eerie devotion, not meme irony.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be anything the old web touched — portraits, street snaps, medical scans, religious icons, nature, product ads, game renders, interior rooms, gallery walls. Composition may range from tidy spam-page grids to full-bleed chaos, square album covers to wide banners to cramped 4:3 captures. Lighting may sit anywhere between overexposed halo bloom, flat scan light and murky low-res night. Color may lean to any point on the magenta-crimson-electric-blue saturation pole or the pastel-overexposed ethereal pole, including maroon occult fields. Mood may run glossy-uneasy, mystical-soft or grotesque. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Single-source coherent photography with consistent perspective, lighting and lens behavior.
- Clean vector minimalism, modern flat UI, tasteful type systems, premium gradient-mesh design.
- Paper-craft analog collage (torn paper, tape, xerox grain) — the degradation must be digital.
- Dreamcore liminal-space comfort and soft nostalgia warmth.
- Desaturated rainy gloom (Draincore) or physical retail print cheapness (Dollar Store Vernacular).
- Explicit gore or shock-site viscera as the payload.
- Ironic TikTok-trend formats: stretched jumpstyle dancers, "Epic Face" memes, repetitive low-effort edits.
- Pristine 4K rendering with no compression artifacts or watermarks.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Blogspot Scene** keeps the personal blog as artifact: template sidebars, diary posts, a legible authorial voice and domestic intimacy. Xpiritualism erases authorship and context; its layouts mimic spam landing pages and shrine-collages, and its content is occult or scammatic, never diary-like.
- **Dollar Store Vernacular** locates cheapness in the physical world: printed signage, off-brand packaging, fluorescent shelf light. Xpiritualism's cheapness is screen-native — compression, screen captures, WordArt — and carries an esoteric layer the retail vernacular lacks.
- **Draincore** is desaturated, wet, urban-melancholic, usually a single photographic mood. Xpiritualism is saturated, maximalist and multi-source; its sadness, if any, glows magenta.
- **Fotonight Web** is candid flash-lit night photography and photo-dump intimacy, photographically whole and socially warm. Xpiritualism may ingest such snapshots but only as fragments among clipart, sprites and garbled text.
- **Goreweb** delivers visceral blood and transgressive shock as content. Xpiritualism's discomfort is semantic and uncanny — broken meaning, scam promises, medical eeriness — framed by spirituality rather than violence.

(Note: the movement's sonic half — garbled "defective ocarina" audio, jagged jumpstyle — is documented in the source culture but cannot appear in still images; likewise, peripheral NSFW strands in the community are not defining visual motifs.)
