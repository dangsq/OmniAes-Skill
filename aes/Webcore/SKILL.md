# Webcore

Webcore is an act of digital reminiscence: it takes the discarded visual language of the early consumer internet — GeoCities homepages, Windows 95/98/XP interfaces, dial-up-era clip art, system error dialogs, crude screensaver 3D — and re-stages it as surrealism. It emerged in the late 2010s and was codified around 2020 by the animated series *ENA*, whose low-polygon worlds and non-linear interface navigation gave the style its dreamlike grammar. Unlike mere retro reproduction, Webcore always applies a modern artistic filter: pastel washes, glitch distortion, impossible recursion, or unsettling text dropped into innocent scenery. Its emotional core is *anemoia* — nostalgia for a web the viewer may never have used — and a quiet critique of the curated, corporate internet that replaced it.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is: **bright system primaries, optionally degraded by pastel filters or digital distortion.** Two families coexist in almost every image:

- **Saturated system color:** pure web blue and BSOD blue (`#0000FF` → `#0000AA`), hyperlink blue (`#0000EE`), Windows XP sky blue (`#2E7CD6`–`#7EC0EE`), Bliss lawn green (`#4C9F4C`–`#57A64C`), alert red (`#FF0000`), smiley yellow (`#FFD800`), magenta/hot pink (`#FF00FF`–`#FF69B4`) for lyric text and glitter accents.
- **Hardware neutrals:** Windows 95 chrome gray (`#C0C0C0`), dialog beige (`#D4D0C8`, `#EFEFD9`), yellowed plastic white (`#E8E0D0`), plain white.

Over these, a distortion layer is common and legitimate: lavender/baby-pink/mint pastel washes (`#C9B6E4`, `#FFC0CB`, `#BFF0D0`), monochrome blue fields, or grayscale dither. A correct image may sit anywhere on this spectrum — a fully saturated BSOD-blue text field and a lavender-tinted window collage are both Webcore.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Dated operating-system interfaces:** sharp-edged, beveled Windows 95/98 gray windows with gradient-blue title bars and `_ □ ×` glyphs; Windows XP's rounded blue theme, Bliss hill-and-sky wallpaper, taskbar and Start button. The desktop itself is the stage.
2. **Pop-ups and error messages:** scam "Congratulations, you are the 100,000th visitor!" dialogs, warning-triangle icons, OK/Cancel buttons, blue-screen crash text, monospace BIOS-style setup menus on pure blue.
3. **Browser windows and interface navigation as narrative:** windows inside windows, non-linear clicking-through as story (the *ENA* inheritance).
4. **GeoCities-era amateur graphics:** animated-GIF energy, glitter starbursts, disco balls, clip art (clovers, smileys, doves), tiled backgrounds, chibi anime / OS-tan overlays pasted onto wallpapers.
5. **Pixel art and early 3D:** low-polygon humanoids and smiley-sphere avatars, vertex-lit flat shading, checkerboard planes, empty rendered highways and lawns, screensaver space scenes, avatar-picker grids.
6. **Surrealist deconstruction:** infinitely receding nested windows, UI elements adrift in skies or voids, repeated search-query text ("am i an angel") treated like incantation, melancholy sentences typed over dithered starfields.
7. **Obsolete hardware:** beige CRT monitors, towers and keyboards photographed on domestic desks, often captioned with speech bubbles containing underlined hyperlink-blue text.
8. **System-font text as image content:** white Arial/Helvetica on flat blue fields, monospace menus, underlined blue links, WordArt-flavored lyric text over sky footage. Text is not caption here; it is subject.

### Materials and textures

CRT phosphor glow, scanlines and moiré from photographing a screen; GIF dither and hard pixel edges; JPEG crunch; flat vertex-lit polygons with visible facets; matte beige plastic. The only gloss permitted is the OS's own title-bar gradient. Everything reads "rough around the edges" by conviction, not accident.

### Composition and lighting

Composition is flat and stacked: orthographic UI planes layered over a full-bleed wallpaper, centered modal dialogs, hard rectangular crops, naive one-point perspective inside 3D scenes, and deliberate clunkiness (misaligned margins, tiled repeats). Lighting is screen-born: uniform emissive flatness, phosphor bloom around bright glyphs, or — in the photographic branch — dim indoor ambient light with amateur flash on beige plastic. There is no depth of field and no soft shadowing; drop shadows, when present, are 1-pixel hard offsets.

## Mood & Values

Webcore feels like remembering a room you never lived in: warm, slightly wrong, faintly lonely. Its values are amateur sincerity over professional polish, the utopian myth of a decentralized handmade web, irony played straight (the scam pop-up as poetry), and creativity expressed through remix and digital archeology. Unease appears, but it is always tied to obsolete technology — a friendly smiley avatar grid that stares a beat too long — never to free-floating dread.

## Era & Origins

Sources: the Old Web of approximately 1995–2005. Emergence: late 2010s, popularized May 2020 by Joel G.'s *ENA*; carried on YouTube, TikTok and SoundCloud, with community life in Neocities, SpaceHey and Flashpoint archives. It reacts against Web 2.0's centralized, algorithmic, corporate internet, and inherits Vaporwave's nostalgia while swapping malls and marble for desktops and dial-up.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Mediate the subject through an obsolete interface: whatever you depict, let it appear inside, behind, or as a desktop artifact — window, dialog, wallpaper, clip art, avatar, crash screen.
2. Let text carry meaning: system fonts, hyperlink blue underlines, white-on-blue fields, monospace menus.
3. Degrade faithfully: dither, scanlines, low resolution, hard edges, facetted polygons. Resolution loss is a material, not a defect.
4. Collage naively: layer GIF glitter, clip art and anime over wallpapers with amateur confidence; sincerity beats taste.
5. Surrealize the familiar: recurse windows to infinity, float UI in voids, set tender or existential sentences in scam-pop-up or search-bar voices.
6. Keep light screen-born: emissive flatness, phosphor glow, or dim domestic flash on beige hardware.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any — portraits as avatar pickers or OS-tans, landscapes as wallpapers or low-poly renders, still lifes as CRT desk photos, pure abstraction as BSOD text fields or checkerboard planes. Composition: full desktop scene, single centered dialog, text-only color field, stacked collage, photographed monitor. Lighting: flat emissive, CRT bloom, pastel wash, indoor flash. Color: full primary saturation, monochrome blue, grayscale dither, XP naturalism, lavender/pink filter. Mood: playful, ironic, melancholic, gently uncanny. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid:** modern flat/material/glassmorphism UI, rounded-corner dark mode, smartphone or social-feed chrome; high-fidelity PBR renders, ray tracing, smooth subdivision surfaces; clean vector illustration and corporate branding; crisp 4K anti-aliased text; modern emoji idioms in place of period smileys and clip art; cyberpunk neon futurism; vaporwave's marble statues and palm sunsets as primary language; analog-photo dread with no technological mediation.

## Discrimination Cues

- **2010s Meme Maximalism:** deep-fried JPEGs, Impact font, Twitter/Vine screenshots, ironic excess. Webcore uses pre-2005 system fonts and UI, and its irony is wrapped in genuine yearning.
- **Cybercore:** sleek futuristic HUDs, neon circuits, forward-looking tech. Webcore is backward-looking, beige, clunky, consumer-grade.
- **Demoscene:** real-time technical virtuosity — plasma effects, Amiga intros, scrolltext as flex. Webcore appropriates consumer interfaces and personal-homepage kitsch, not demo bravura.
- **Dreamcore:** soft-focus liminal photography of comforting dream spaces. Webcore's dreams are screen-mediated and era-specific; its uncanny is a dialog box, not a hallway.
- **Frutiger Aero:** 2004–2013 glossy skeuomorphism — aqua glass, bubbles, auroras, high-res sheen. Webcore occupies the earlier, flatter, dithered decade and celebrates amateur roughness over corporate gloss.

Where evidence is thinner — notably the photographic CRT branch and the musical side (instrumental IDM/ambient built from modem and notification sounds) — treat them as legitimate but secondary expressions: the music's mood translates to imagery as quiet, atmospheric emptiness inside the machine, not as literal depiction.
