# Choni

Choni is the aesthetic of the Spanish urban working-class girl, the *choni* "de barrio": a subculture (and a pejorative label she sometimes wears back as a badge) that formed in Spain in the 1990s and peaked in visibility in the 2000s. It is a loud, maximalist, deliberately "cheap"-looking glam built from fast-fashion leopard print, fluorescent color, oversized gold jewelry and sportswear, assembled with total confidence. Its masculine counterpart is the Cani; its earlier ancestor is the Spanish *hortera* (tacky); its modern reclaiming descendant is Raxet, the trap-era style of singers like La Zowi and Bad Gyal. Media touchstones that fixed its image in the public eye are the TV show *Aída*, the film *Yo soy la Juani* (2006) and the reality show *Gandia Shore* (2012). Everything about the look broadcasts abundance on a budget: more print, more gold, more pink, more volume, never less.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is **bright, fluorescent or metallic, always against a black or white-satin base, never a single neutral family alone**. A correct image holds at least three of: a hot pink/red pop, a fluorescent or metallic accent, an animal print, and visible gold shine.

Clashing is structural, not accidental: pink sits next to red, leopard next to zebra, gold next to silver. Muted, tonal or earth-tone handling of these hues breaks the style.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how firmly the text documents them)

1. **Oversized gold accessories** — large hoop earrings, layered necklaces and medal pendants, and above all **oversized sunglasses**, gradient or shield-lensed, worn indoors, at night, anywhere. Jewelry must read at arm's length.
2. **Animal prints** — leopard first, snake/zebra second, on tops, skirts, bag panels and trim; inexpensive-looking print is part of the point.
3. **Revealing, tight-fitting clothing** — mini shorts, mini skirts, tight dresses, crop tops; bare midriffs and low necklines.
4. **Fluorescent and metallic colors** — volt yellow, hot pink, gold and silver laminate sheen.
5. **Clashing patterns** — print against print, print against stripe.
6. **Heavy makeup** — defined, often thin-drawn or heavily filled brows, lined or smoky eyes, glossy or matte red lips; plus long nails.
7. **Tracksuits** — a staple: satin bombers, zip jackets, striped track pants; sportswear fused with glam.
8. **Fanny packs** and flashy handbags carried openly.
9. **Sneakers and boots** as footwear.
10. **Body modifications** — monroe and eyebrow piercings, lower-back tattoos; exaggerated fake tanning is documented but noted as less frequent.
11. **Hairstyles** — the text only says "specific hairstyles"; the image corpus supplies the working rules: volume and length. Teased quiffs and backcombed mass, high or side ponytails, crimped or flat-ironed lengths with contrast streaks, headscarves and bandanas. Treat these as corpus-derived defaults rather than textual canon.

### Materials and textures

Shiny polyester satin, laminated metallic fabric, faux fur and feather trim, rhinestone and plastic-bead jewelry, gold-tone metal, acrylic nails, stretch jersey and track nylon. The surface language is **gloss over matte**: light should catch satin shoulders, metallic threads and gold hoops. "Cheap" is a material fact here — visible seams, thin fabric, costume-grade shine — and it is worn with pride, not hidden.

### Recurring subjects

Young working-class women, solo or in tight groups of friends: on neighborhood streets and shopping strips, outside bars, leaning on or piled around a car, on TV talk-show sets, at terraces with plastic cups and straws, walking with handbags or rolling suitcases. The subject is always *presented*, never caught unaware: even candid-looking frames are posed with attitude toward the lens.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

The photographic vernacular is the vernacular snapshot: on-camera flash with hot shine on forehead and nose and dark falloff behind; blown-out midday sun with washed skies; flat, saturated TV-studio or broadcast light; slight horizon tilt; casual cropping. Framing runs from close-up face portraits to mid-shots and full-body street shots; groups cluster shoulder-overlapping into one mass. Poses are declarative: chin up, pout or duckface, open-mouthed laugh or taunt, finger pointed at the camera, arms akimbo. Props are everyday-loud: drinks with straws, fanny packs, big bags, suitcases, car bonnets, storefront signage.

## Mood & Values

The mood is **defiant abundance**: perceived vulgarity worn as a flag, aggression as armor, pride in working-class roots, and cheapness converted into maximum visible effect. There is humor and bravado in it — the pout, the taunt, the group pose — but also solidarity: choni images are often about girls together, loyal and loud. The style values being *seen*: volume, shine and scale over subtlety, taste-by-budget over taste-by-class. Any reading of the look as shame or apology is wrong; the choni owns the stereotype.

## Era & Origins

Spain, emerging in the 1990s from lower-socioeconomic urban neighborhoods, gaining national prominence in the 2000s through fast-fashion chains like Bershka and Stradivarius and through TV and film parody (*Aída*, *Yo soy la Juani*, *Gandia Shore*). It descends from the older Spanish insult *hortera* (tacky) and sits beside the male Cani culture; its slang (dropped "s", "la Vane", texting like "tOh LoKoh") is part of the stereotype. In the 2010s–2020s the lineage is reclaimed and remixed by Raxet and Spanish trap, which updates the same gold-and-leopard grammar with trap-video lighting and designer-logo irony. Core choni, however, reads pre-smartphone: digicam flash, broadcast saturation, street and set.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Maximalism mandate.** Every element stacks: print over print, chain over chain, pink plus red plus gold. Nothing is understated; negative space is for backgrounds only.
2. **Cheap-glam materiality.** Satin, laminated metallic, faux fur, rhinestone, acrylic. Light must bounce off something.
3. **Clash logic.** Pair at least two patterns or color families that "should not" go together; resolve nothing into tonality.
4. **Silhouette tension.** Tight-and-short (mini skirt, crop top, tight dress) or sporty-loose (tracksuit, bomber, striped track pants) — or both at once, jacket open over bare midriff.
5. **Face rule.** Makeup legible at two meters: strong brow, lined or smoky eye, red or glossy lip. Oversized sunglasses override all of it and are always acceptable.
6. **Jewelry scale rule.** Hoops of conspicuous diameter, layered chains, medal pendants; if the jewelry is delicate, it is not choni.
7. **Hair volume rule.** Teased, crimped, high-ponytailed, streaked, scarfed — mass and length over sleekness.
8. **Attitude rule.** Confrontational tenderness: pout, taunt, point, group loyalty. The subject addresses the camera as an equal or a rival.
9. **Snapshot vernacular.** Flash shine, blown daylight, or saturated studio/TV light; casual tilt and crop are features, not flaws.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter: solo portrait, best-friend duo, group of four; street walk, car pose, bar terrace, market stall, TV set, beach promenade, bedroom or club snapshot; sincere documentary register or exaggerated parody register; ages from teens to thirties. Composition: close-up to full body, centered or tilted, clustered group or single figure against a painted-sky studio backdrop or a plain street wall. Lighting: direct flash, overexposed noon, flat saturated studio light, night street with flash falloff. Color handling: full rainbow clash, or a disciplined pink-and-gold-on-black scheme, or white satin with volt accents — as long as one fluorescent or metallic note and visible gold survive. Mood: pride, parody, solidarity, tabloid candor. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Beige, cream, earth-tone or Scandinavian minimalism; quiet luxury; matte muted grading.
- Couture tailoring, discreet monograms, fine delicate jewelry as the only jewelry.
- No-makeup "clean girl" faces; soft ring-light beauty-influencer gloss (that is Baddie).
- Pastel cottagecore, boho linen, grunge flannel, goth matte black without shine.
- Men-centered sportswear groups (that is Cani); US mall or Miami signage and Spanglish props (Chonga); Marseille harbor and rhinestoned-denim meridional cues (Cagole).
- Avant-garde chrome minimalism, biker-leather futurism, art-school irony (Motomami).
- Pristine old-money props: yachts, luxury hotel lobbies, heritage cars.
- Moody underexposed cinematic color; desaturated film emulation.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Cagole** (Marseille): shares leopard, pink, hoops and rhinestone. Tells apart: French meridional setting and props, strass-overloaded denim, rooty tinted balayage as the signature hair; choni keeps Spanish barrio streets and TV sets, tracksuit stripes, fluorescent yellow and gold medal pendants.
- **Cani**: the male counterpart. If the frame centers young men in caps or faded cuts, tucked tracksuits, squatting by tuned cars, it is Cani; choni centers women and the makeup-jewelry-hair code. Mixed groups are fine when the feminine code dominates.
- **Chonga** (US, esp. Florida): near-identical hoops, makeup and track jackets. Tells apart: US brand-logo density, mall and Miami pastel sportswear contexts; choni reads Spanish fast-fashion cuts, leopard-plus-gold layering and Spanish broadcast saturation.
- **Motomami**: the contemporary art-pop remix of the same roots. Tells apart: sparse composition, chrome and biker leather, Y2K futurism, ironic detachment; choni is unironic (or parodic) maximalism in visibly cheap materials. Raxet, choni's direct trap-era descendant, borrows choni gold and leopard but stages it with trap-video lighting and logo irony — core choni stays digicam-flash and street.
- **Pokero**: Southern Cone cumbia-adjacent flash; documentation here is thin, so decide on scene and geography — cumbia dancehall and Argentine/Uruguayan football-shirt props versus choni's Spanish barrio streets, fast-fashion leopard and gold hoops. When in doubt between neighbors, geography and setting props decide before any single garment does.
