# Femboy

Femboy is a gender-expression aesthetic born in online communities (TikTok, Reddit, earlier imageboards and the 2001 "Boi Fancy" hub) and codified into a highly recognizable fashion grammar in the late 2010s and early 2020s. Its subject is a man or boy — most often cisgender, male-identified — who deliberately adopts attire culturally coded as feminine: thigh-highs, pleated skirts, pastels, animal ears. The aesthetic's engine is **contrast**: feminine garment language worn on a body that keeps masculine or youthful-boyish features, so that every image reads as a performed juxtaposition rather than as conventional womenswear. Nothing about the style denotes sexual orientation or gender identity; it is a vocabulary of softness, gender play and defiance of rigid masculinity, frequently staged as an intimate self-shot photo in a bedroom.

## Visual Identity

### Color

The palette is pastel-forward with a black counterweight. Pinks carry the scene — from flag-magenta (around `#C75B9B`) through rose and blush (`#E9A8C9`, `#F3C9D8`) — supported by lilac and lavender purples (`#B494D6` to `#D8BFE8`), the community flag's light blue (`#55CDEF` to `#7FD8F0`), and whites and cream off-whites (`#F5F7F8`). Black (`#141414`–`#262626`) never leads but always punctuates: black thigh-highs, black chokers, black oversized tees, black fishnet over pink skin. The rule: **pastels set the temperature, black sets the edge.** An all-black frame drifts goth/alt; an all-saturated-neon frame drifts cyberpunk; femboy sits in soft, slightly desaturated domestic color, often with a pink or magenta LED wash tinting walls and bedding.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **Thigh-high socks and stockings** — the single most recognizable piece; black, pink or purple, frequently striped, frequently layered with fishnet for texture. Sometimes the sole focus of a detail crop on the legs.
2. **Pleated skirts and short dresses** — the iconic lower silhouette, anime-schoolgirl derived, contrasting with a plainer or male-coded upper body.
3. **Maid outfits** — the style's named key motif; frilled apron, puff sleeves, headpiece, treated as a beloved "extreme" register rather than a costume-shop uniform.
4. **Animal-ear headbands** — cat ears and bunny ears, signaling playful innocence; often the only accessory on an otherwise casual outfit.
5. **Chokers** — simple bands or spiked collars framing the neck; the spiked variant tips the look toward alt.
6. **Soft cozy tops** — oversized hoodies, coquettish sweaters, sleeves swallowed over the hands; crop tops and, at the far edge, lingerie as the "extreme case."
7. **Fluffy, voluminous hair** — medium to long, curled, pigtails, clips, headbands, bows; doll-like volume is the goal, in natural browns, black bobs, bleached or split-dye blonde/black, or pastel wigs.
8. **Light, eye-focused makeup** — eyeshadow, mascara, eyeliner, tinted lip balm, soft bronzer; ranges from barely-there to a full feminine beat.
9. **Feminine footwear** — women's boots, platform shoes, simple heels.
10. **The pride flag's stripe grammar** (pink / white / light blue, c. 2017) as graphic accent: striped socks, bedding, or the flag itself as prop or backdrop.

Lower-frequency observed variants: black cloth face masks borrowed from alt-kid wardrobes, body harnesses or chains over black layers, gaming chairs and streamer setups as set dressing.

### Materials and textures

Ribbed knit sock cotton, fishnet mesh, fleece and brushed hoodie interior, pleated uniform suiting, cotton apron starch and satin ribbon on maid pieces, plush bedding and stuffed animals, glossy wig fiber. Skin and fabric typically carry a phone-filter softness — slight smoothing, mild bloom on highlights — rather than crisp editorial texture.

### Subjects and the contrast rule

The recurring subject is a youthful, male-identified figure in feminine dress; the contrast *is* the subject. A flat chest, boyish jaw, broad-ish shoulders or unshaven softness beside pigtails and pleats is not a flaw in the image, it is the statement. Secondary subjects: the bedroom itself (unmade pastel bed, plushies, string lights, mirror on a door), a phone held up in mirror reflection, a desk or streaming rig. Outfit-detail crops — legs in thigh-highs on a bed — function as standalone subject matter.

### Composition, medium and lighting

The medium is vernacular self-portraiture: vertical phone-frame crops, mirror selfies with the phone partially masking the face, high-angle selfies looking down at a seated or kneeling figure, full-length door-mirror stands, webcam or stream stills. Poses lean coy and small: knees together or pigeon-toed kneeling, hands hidden in sleeves, hair twirls, glances over the shoulder. Lighting ranges from soft diffuse window daylight to dim room ambience washed in pink/magenta LED, ring-light catchlights in the eyes, or bare on-camera flash in a dark room; occasional monochrome treatments exist. Fidelity is deliberately amateur: slight motion blur, compression, filter smoothing, cluttered domestic backgrounds. There is no professional studio tradition in this style.

## Mood & Values

Softness, innocence, and play — gender treated as a toy box rather than a uniform. The mood is coy and intimate, a performance for a community that understands the codes ("programming socks," #FemboyFriday). Underneath the cuteness sits a value statement: effeminacy and gender fluidity as defiance of toxic masculinity. A fetishized, hyper-sexualized register exists at the style's edge (fishnet, harnesses, lingerie), but the documented core is non-sexual self-expression; even the edgier frames keep a tender, domestic, almost doll-like vulnerability rather than hard glamour.

## Era & Origins

The slang term dates to the 1990s (parallel to "tomboy"), with the first dedicated community, Boi Fancy, in 2001. The aesthetic as documented here is a creature of the late 2010s–early 2020s: TikTok and Reddit virality, anime and manga's feminine male characters (Astolfo, Venti, Hideri Kanzaki, Chihiro Fujisaki, Felix Argyle, Angel Dust) supplying cosplay source material, and the c. 2017 pride flag supplying the color script. It reacts against traditional norms of masculinity; note that extremist groups have occasionally attempted to co-opt the look, and the mainstream community explicitly rejects them — political iconography of any kind is foreign to the aesthetic.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Build the image around juxtaposition: at least one masculine/youthful physical signifier against at least one strongly feminine garment signifier. The tension is the composition.
- Dress bottom-up in the style's grammar: thigh-highs plus pleated skirt or dress as the anchor, then choose a top register — cozy hoodie (casual), sweater or crop top (sweet), maid frills (extreme).
- Keep the palette pastel-dominant with black punctuation; let pink or lilac tint the environment, not just the clothing.
- Shoot it like a phone, not a studio: vertical crop, domestic clutter, mirror or high-angle selfie logic, soft filter smoothing.
- Stage softness everywhere: fluffy hair volume, plush bedding, sleeves over hands, coy small poses.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range from a hoodie-and-thigh-highs figure at a gaming desk, to a full maid costume, to a cosplay of an anime femboy character, to an outfit-detail crop of striped socks on a bed, to a flat arrangement of flag-striped garments. Composition may range from mirror selfie to kneeling full-body to seated bed portrait to leg detail. Lighting may range from daylight to pink LED dim to ring-light to flash snapshot to monochrome. Color handling may range from pastel-dominant to black-dominant-with-pink-accents. Mood may range from cozy innocence to playful coyness to alt-edged (spiked choker, fishnet, mask). **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**

- Removing the gender-signifier contrast in either direction: a purely masculine outfit (that is Soft Boy or plain casual), or a presentation that reads as conventional adult womenswear glamour with no boyish/youthful counterpoint.
- Goth or alt-kid all-black with occult or punk hardware as the dominant language; black here is accent, not world.
- E-girl grunge signage: plaid-dominated outfits, chain belts, band tees, heavy drawn-under-eye liner as the main event.
- Saturated neon cyberpunk palettes, seamless studio backdrops, hard key lighting, glossy editorial retouching.
- Formal, business, or mature-glamour clothing; heritage or workwear fabrics.
- Political or extremist symbols of any kind; the community defines itself against them.
- Explicitly pornographic framing: the style's documented heart is clothed, playful self-expression; sexualization exists only as a stigmatized edge, not the rule.

| Maid | The uniform is the entire subject, any gender, café or cosplay context | If the image is about the costume alone with no male-coded/feminine-garment tension or bedroom self-shot framing, it is Maid; femboy treats maid as one register among many |

| Tomboy | The inverse vector: female-identified person in masculine clothing | Boxy shirts and short hair on a girl; femboy is feminine clothing on a male-identified person |

When in doubt, ask one question: is the image staging feminine dress on a boyish body as a playful, soft, self-aware performance? If yes — pastel palette, thigh-highs, pleats, ears, bedroom light — it is femboy.
