# Romantic Goth

Romantic Goth (also "Romantigoth") is the tender, literary wing of the goth subculture. Born in the 1990s from ethereal wave and the second wave of gothic rock, it borrows its soul from 19th-century Romanticism and the Gothic novel — Poe, Mary Shelley, the Brontës, Shakespeare, Anne Rice — and translates that inheritance into clothing, portraiture and fantasy imagery. Where other goth strands pursue shock, austerity or club aggression, Romantic Goth pursues *tragic beauty*: grief rendered gracefully, passion held under restraint, decay treated as ornament. Its figures mourn, daydream, read, and love badly on purpose; its rooms smell of candle wax and old paper; its gardens are overgrown and its roses are dead or black.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The colour rule is simple and strict: **black is the ground, jewel tones are the accent, ivory and silver are the light.** Most of any frame sits in near-black or stone-neutral value; at most one deep jewel tone carries the emotional charge, optionally answered by a pale ivory or cool silver counterpoint. Wine red is the most characteristic accent and may read rich and saturated; all other hues stay low-to-mid saturation. Matte finishes dominate — colour should look like velvet and stone, not vinyl and neon.

| Role | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ground black | `#0A0A0C` – `#1E1E22` | soft, light-absorbing black; never glossy patent |
| Ivory / bone / white | `#EDE8DE` – `#F5F2EA` | pale skin, poet shirts, lilies, candle wax |
| Silver / pewter / weathered stone | `#8E8E93`, `#B7B2A9` | jewellery, grave granite, staircases |
| Wine / burgundy / oxblood | `#4A101C` – `#7E2231` | the signature accent: drapery, gowns, red wine |
| Forest green | `#1C352A` – `#2E4A38` | corset panels, ivy, night gardens |
| Sapphire / indigo night | `#16244A` – `#24365E` | moonlit sky, dark stones |
| Dead-rose mauve | `#6D4A56` | withered blooms, faded upholstery |
| Optional ethereal mist | lavender `#6F6A8C`, teal-cyan `#2F4E54` | a cool wash seen in ethereal-wave-adjacent artwork; secondary, never dominant |

Warm colour appears only as tiny candle flames or a faint wine glow. Any neon, candy pastel, bright primary or sunny high-key treatment breaks the style instantly.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Flowing garments** — the defining motif: long velvet gowns, lace dresses, poet shirts with ruffled cuffs, tailcoats, suits and formal attire of any cut, all in soft, moving silhouettes. Trains, veils and sleeve-drapes pool on floors, steps and grass, extending the body's line beyond itself.
2. **Corsetry and lace** — corsets over blouses or bare shoulders, lace chokers, lace overlays and underskirts, tiered ruffles.
3. **Romantic-gothic settings** — cemeteries (especially moonlit), ruined castles, gardens at night, nighttime generally; stone staircases, arches, rose windows, Celtic crosses and wrought iron as framing architecture.
4. **Mourning props** — black and dead roses, candelabras and candle rows, ravens, bones, red wine; gothic jewellery: crosses, rosaries, cameos, black or dark-stoned pieces in silver.
5. **Beauty canon** — pale foundation, smokey or swirling eye patterns, cat's-eye liner, dark lips; long hair straightened or in soft waves, elegant dramatic updos, 1890s curls. Men appear in tailcoats, poet shirts and long or swept-dark hair.
6. **Allegorical figures** — angels (often winged, kneeling or grieving), faeries with dark or wine-red wings, dream-trapped sleepers; the style readily expresses itself through painted fantasy figures, dolls and figurines as much as through living models.

### Materials and textures

Velvet (deep pile, light-swallowing), lace (patterned, semi-transparent), chiffon and georgette (drapes that pool and float), satin and brocade as secondary sheens; matte resin and painted porcelain in figurine work; weathered granite, moss and ivy, fallen leaves, old wood and tarnished silver in the environment. Texture must be *legible*: the eye should read the lace pattern, the velvet nap, the chiffon transparency. Surfaces are matte to soft-sheen; hard gloss belongs to other substyles.

### Recurring subjects

Solitary women in mourning or reverie; couples in formal black (seated on staircases, entwined in grief); allegorical angels, faeries and spirits; poets and readers; musicians of the ethereal-wave milieu; groups of friends in romantic black at gatherings; and, as commercial variants, catalog and runway models presenting the garments. Dolls, figurines and game characters dressed in the canon also count as legitimate subjects — the style tolerates being embodied by an object rather than a person.

### Composition and lighting

Composition favours the vertical full-length figure, centred or slightly off-centre, with fabric spreading beyond the body into the lower frame. Framing devices recur: draped curtains behind studio sitters, arches and rose windows behind painted figures, staircases and gates organizing depth, a flat full-moon disc pinned in a dark sky. Close crops on garment detail, jewellery or a single rose stem are acceptable; catalog grids and runway walks are acceptable commercial framings. Lighting is soft and directional-less or gently modelled: overcast daylight on pale stone, window light in dim rooms, candle glow as warm pinpoints inside cool darkness, cool moonlight rimming figures against deep shadow, or studio soft light against a saturated draped backdrop. High-contrast monochrome is a permitted extreme. Shadows keep their detail; falloff is gentle; the overall exposure sits low-to-mid key with pale skin and ivory fabric as the brightest notes.

## Mood & Values

Dark romance, tragic beauty, melancholy, individualism, artistic expression, reverence for nature and for history. The emotional register is *tender grief and restrained passion*: crying over lost lovers, daydreaming, falling in love doomward, staying home with records and sad books rather than chasing clubs. Nothing in the image should feel aggressive, ironic or casual. Beauty is located in decay — withered roses, leaf-strewn stone, ruined walls — and in self-styled elegance: every figure looks deliberately, artfully composed, as if authoring their own portrait of sorrow.

## Era & Origins

Emerging in the 1990s from ethereal wave and second-wave gothic rock — bands whose medieval/Victorian dress and Celtic-flavoured vocals (Requiem in White, Miranda Sex Garden, Dead Can Dance, Black Tape for a Blue Girl and kin) supplied the template — Romantic Goth briefly crossed into mainstream fashion during that decade, appearing on runways and worn by adult women outside the subculture, and peaked in popularity in the 2000s. Its deeper ancestry is the 19th-century Romantic movement and Gothic literature; its musical companions are neoclassical darkwave, dark cabaret and operatic ethereal wave. The style defines itself *against* rigidity: it softens Victorian Goth's strict historical recreation into mood rather than costume, and rejects mallgoth teen casualness in favour of adult formality.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Black-first palette: 60–85% of the frame in near-black and stone/ivory neutrals, with at most one jewel accent (wine above all) plus optional silver or ivory counterpoints.
2. Soft, flowing silhouette: fabric must move or pool — trains, drapes, veils, ruffled cuffs — never stiff, boxy or body-clinging latex.
3. At least one romance-gothic signifier from the motif list: lace, corset, poet shirt, black rose, candle, raven, graveyard, ruin, moon, rosary, draped curtain.
4. Light as mood, not illumination: moonlight, candlelight, overcast stone-light or soft studio light; low key, matte, shadow-retaining.
5. Melancholy posture: stillness, downcast or distant gaze, embrace, mourning, reverie; passion under restraint.
6. Texture legibility: velvet nap, lace pattern, chiffon transparency, weathered stone.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a living portrait, a couple, an allegorical angel or faerie, a doll/figurine/game character, a still life of roses-candles-rosary, a landscape of ruin-graveyard-garden, or a runway/catalog presentation. Composition may range from full-length vertical portrait to garment-detail crop, painterly narrative scene, catalog grid or monochrome study. Lighting may be moonlit night, candlelit interior, overcast daylight on pale stone, draped-backdrop studio, or high-contrast black-and-white. Colour handling may be pure monochrome, black-plus-wine, black-plus-green or sapphire, or black cooled by a lavender/teal ethereal mist. Medium may be photography, digital painting, illustration, figurine product shot or toy render. Mood may slide from quiet contemplation to overt grief to tender love. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.**
- Neon, candy pastels, bright primaries, sunny high-key cheerfulness.
- Punk/industrial hardware as focus: spikes, studs, safety pins, ripped fishnets as the main event, PVC/latex fetishwear, dominant platform boots, chain harnesses.
- Cybergoth signage: neon hair falls, goggles, circuitry, club strobes.
- Strict period recreation: bustled crinolines, bonnets, sepia antique-photo patina (that is Victorian Goth).
- Dark Academia props: tweed blazers, plaid, satchels, daylight lecture halls and warm oak libraries.
- Dark Fantasy apparatus: plate armour, swords, battle, creatures, saturated magical glow.
- Ethnogoth layering: coin headdresses, folk embroidery, tribal silver, ethnic woven textiles, steppe or desert settings.
- Mallgoth casualness: band tees, plaid skirts, studded belts, slouched teen posture.
- Glossy commercial glamour: airbrushed shine, pop saturation, hard on-camera flash.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Victorian Goth:** Victorian Goth is archival and austere — rigid period silhouettes, high collars, bustles, strict recreation, sepia patina. Romantic Goth keeps the *mood* of the 19th century but softens the silhouette, uses 1990s goth fabrics, and centres emotion (grief, love) over historical accuracy.
- **Vs Antwerp Six:** Both love black, but Antwerp Six deconstruction is intellectual avant-garde tailoring — asymmetry, raw edges, conceptual volume, no lace, roses, moons or mourning props. Romantic Goth is ornamental, literary and prop-rich.
- **Vs Dark Academia:** Dark Academia lives in daylight tweed, plaid, warm browns and olive, campus interiors and scholarly optimism-tinted-with-melancholy. Romantic Goth is nocturnal, black-and-jewel, and its books are read by candlelight over a grave, not in a library carrel.
- **Vs Dark Fantasy:** Dark Fantasy narrates epic conflict — armour, weapons, monsters, magical spectacle. Romantic Goth is intimate and garment-centred; its drama is interior, its "magic" at most an angel's wing or a moonlit ruin.
- **Vs Ethnogoth:** Ethnogoth stacks folk and tribal signifiers — coins, embroidery, ethnic silver, layered woven textiles, earthbound palettes. Romantic Goth's lineage is Western European Romantic literature: velvet, lace, corsetry, rosaries, and the Gothic novel's castles and moors.

**Recognition checklist:** black flowing gown or poet shirt + pale skin and dark lips + one jewel accent (wine first) + soft matte light (moon, candle, overcast stone) + a signifier of mourning or ruin + tender, still, grief-tinged posture = Romantic Goth.
