# Trojan Skinhead

Trojan Skinhead (Traditional Skinhead, "Trad", "Spirit of '69") is the dress and image culture of the original British skinhead subculture of the mid-to-late 1960s, built jointly by black and white working-class youth in inner-city London (Brixton, Hackney) around imported Jamaican ska, rocksteady and early reggae — the sound of Trojan Records, founded 1968. It is a sharp, clean-cut, utilitarian look formed in explicit rejection of middle-class hippie "flower power", and it carries an explicitly anti-racist, multiracial, working-class-solidarity ethic. Everything visual in the style follows from those two facts: dress is neat, hard-wearing and heritage-branded; imagery is casual, communal and documentary rather than staged or glamorous.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** The wardrobe core is four neutrals — tan, forest green, black, white — worn against rigid denim indigo and oxblood/burgundy leather. Workable ranges: tan `#C9A876–#D8C29A`, forest green `#2E4B3F–#40604A`, black `#101010–#1C1C1C`, off-white/cream `#F2EFE6–#E8E2D2`, denim indigo `#4F6D8C–#7A93AC` (fading to pale blue `#AFC4D8` in worn or washed cloth), oxblood `#5E2B2B–#7A3B34`. Small saturated accents (a red dress on the dance floor, a burgundy bomber) are acceptable punctuation, never the field. Photographic treatment mutes everything further: faded colour film with lifted blacks and a slight magenta-green drift, or mid-tone grainy black-and-white. A correct image reads desaturated, weathered and un-glossy even when in colour.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the text documents them.**
1. **The haircut:** a 2–4 grade clipper crop — very short but visibly present, never shaved bald. This is the single most defining visual characteristic.
2. **Jamaican music paraphernalia:** vinyl records and sleeves, dance halls, ska parties, sound systems and live bands; Trojan Records iconography as quiet signage.
3. **Shirts:** button-down Ben Sherman shirts and Fred Perry polos; diamond jacquard and houndstooth weaves; collars buttoned, sleeves often worn long or neatly folded.
4. **Braces (suspenders)** worn over the shirt, frequently visible as a back or full-body detail.
5. **Outerwear layers:** Harrington jackets, bomber-style jackets, Crombie-style overcoats, cardigans.
6. **Trousers:** fitted suits and, most characteristically, cuffed (turn-up hemmed) blue jeans exposing boot shaft or sock.
7. **Footwear:** sturdy smooth-leather boots and moccasin-type loafers / brogues, polished but scuffed by pavement; feet and hems are a recurring focal point.
8. **Heritage brand grammar:** Ben Sherman, Fred Perry, Gabicci Vintage, Trojan Records Clothing — labels implied by cut and tipping, not shouted as logos.

**Materials and textures.** Cotton oxford and jacquard shirting, wool cardigans and mohair-toned knits, cotton-gabardine Harringtons, heavy wool Crombies, rigid denim with a pressed turn-up, smooth booted leather and suede loafers, set against brick mortar, rendered pebbledash, wet paving slabs, concrete steps and paper record sleeves. The style lives in the contrast between pressed cloth and rough municipal surfaces.

**Recurring subjects.** Multiracial groups of young working-class people: crews leaning on railings or parked cars, couples in doorways, dancers mid-skank at a party, bands on small stages, friends photographed feet-together in a circle of boots and loafers. Women appear in the same sharp grammar — buttoned shirts, pencil skirts or jeans, opaque tights, loafers — though the text details mainly the menswear uniform, so female styling should borrow its neatness rather than invent new codes. Solo portraits are equally valid: arms crossed, weight on one hip, chin level, pride without threat.

**Composition and lighting tendencies.** Snapshot-documentary grammar: full-body or three-quarter standing figures framed by ordinary architecture (brick walls, window sills, doorframes, estate balustrades); detail frames of hems, boots and braces; loose rows or clusters for groups rather than posed symmetry. Light is flat overcast British daylight, dim interior window light, or direct on-camera flash at night and in clubs. Grain, slight blur and colour fade are welcome; studio gloss is not.

## Mood & Values

Dignity through neatness: clothes pressed, boots brushed, hair cropped — sharpness as working-class self-respect against the "grey" reality of London estates. Joy and movement: the upbeat Caribbean offbeat is the subculture's heartbeat, so dancing, gigs and record-collecting carry the mood. Solidarity: black and white bodies sharing frames, clothes and dance floors as a matter of principle — anti-racism, apolitical or left-wing views, multiracial unity. Nostalgia without costume-party irony: revivalist images (1980s onward) deliberately echo 1969 rather than parody it.

## Era & Origins

Emerges mid-to-late 1960s United Kingdom from the meeting of British working-class youth and the Windrush-generation Caribbean community; codified as a named subset in the 1980s–90s (Glasgow Spy Kids, George Marshall's *Spirit of '69: A Skinhead Bible*) against later politicized skinhead offshoots. Reacts against hippie long hair and psychedelia on one side and, retrospectively, against white-power skinhead imagery on the other. Directly inspired the 2 Tone revival of the late 1970s.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Cut the hair to a visible 2–4 grade crop before anything else; without it the look collapses into Mod or prep.
2. Anchor every outfit at the hem: turn-up cuff, boot shaft or sock, polished leather. Feet are a legitimate hero subject.
3. Layer heritage grammar: buttoned collar, braces over shirt, Harrington or Crombie on top; patterns limited to jacquard, houndstooth, subtle checks.
4. Keep the palette inside the muted rule above; let denim and oxblood do the colour work.
5. Set scenes in British working-class fabric — brick terraces, estate walls, doorways, railings, modest interiors, small clubs — or at the dance hall / gig.
6. Shoot like a friend with a 35mm: candid stance, flat or flash light, grain and fade; never seamless-studio perfection.
7. Cast and group for multiracial unity and easy camaraderie; posture is proud, relaxed or dancing, never menacing.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo portrait, couple, crew, dancers, band, still life of vinyl and boots, overhead feet circle. Composition: full-body, three-quarter, hem/foot close-up, group row, candid club frame. Lighting: overcast daylight, window-lit interior, harsh night flash, stage light. Colour handling: faded colour film, saturated flash colour at night, or grainy black-and-white. Era inflection: 1969 original, late-70s/80s revival, contemporary trad. Gender: male uniform as documented; female equivalents borrowing the same shirts, knitwear, tights and loafers. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Shaved-bald heads (Oi! signifier) and long or hippie hair; any racist, far-right or white-power insignia, slogans or flags — absolutely exclusionary, contrary to the style's core values; 2 Tone checkerboard as a dominant graphic; punk mohawks, studs, safety pins; modern athletic sneakers, hoodies, baseball caps and streetwear logo culture; neon or digitally saturated palettes, glossy studio lighting, seamless backdrops; florals, tie-dye and psychedelia; American prep or Ivy contexts; biker or cowboy leather; aggressive confrontational posturing that reads as violence rather than pride.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs 2 Tone:** 2 Tone (late 1970s) wears its black-and-white checkerboard branding, sharper mod-cut suits and pork-pie-hat revivalism on the sleeve; Trojan predates it, skips the checkerboard, and leans on braces, turn-up jeans, boots and warm faded film rather than graphic monochrome styling.
- **Vs Mod:** Mod keeps a longer, combed hairstyle, parkas, scooters, tailoring and desert boots; Trojan's clipper crop, braces-over-shirt and boot-and-denim workwear edge are the tell.
- **Vs Oi! Skinhead:** Oi! (1980s) shaves bald or near-bald, heavyizes the boot, adopts football-casual or aggression-coded posture and often far-right imagery; Trojan shows visible hair grade, smarter heritage layers, multiracial company and ska cues.
- **Vs Rude Boy:** Rude Boy is the Jamaican source culture — slim three-button suits, thin ties, sunglasses, Caribbean street context; Trojan absorbs it but adds the British crop, braces, Crombie and estate backdrop.
- **Vs Skinhead (generic):** the umbrella term spans all eras and politics; Trojan is specifically the anti-racist, multiracial, Trojan-Records-loving '69 lineage — confirm via crop grade, heritage brands, Jamaican music props and communal, non-hostile mood.

*Evidence note:* the text fixes haircut, garments, brands, palette keys and values; exact colour placement per garment and women's full uniform are looser, inferred from imagery, and should be treated as open variation within the rules above.
