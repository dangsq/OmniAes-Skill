# Sovietwave

Sovietwave is an aesthetic and music microgenre that emerged in the 2010s on the Russian and wider post-Soviet internet, as a branch of synthwave turned toward Soviet imagery of the 1980s. Where mainstream synthwave imagines hyper-capitalist utopias or dystopias, Sovietwave preserves the memory of socialism's promised future: the communist tomorrow that never came, the space program as humanity's collective ascent, the Cold War as backdrop of sublime power. Its nostalgia is sincere, not ironic — many of its adherents were born after the USSR's 1991 collapse and inherit the longing second-hand, from parents' generation of Young Pioneers. The music is lo-fi, ambient, new wave and synth-pop, slower than synthwave to build awe but faster than vaporwave's dream-drift; the visuals carry the same tempo: grand, slow, wistful, slightly degraded, as if recovered from a tape recorded in 1986 and played again today.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The key triad is **red, white, yellow**, set against deep field colors. Treat these as roles, not fixed swatches:

- **Ideological red** (#B0121B–#E23A2E): banners, stars, sickle-and-hammer marks, slogan type, eye-catching accents on an otherwise muted frame.
- **Sun amber / gold** (#E8933A–#FFC94D, gilt up to #D9A441): low giant suns, gilded allegorical figures, warm horizon glow.
- **Paper and craft white** (#EAE3D3–#F7F3EA): poster paper, mural plaster, spacecraft hulls, snow-light.
- **Space blue and void black** (#0A1730–#14306B; #050505–#101010): starfields, night skies, lo-fi interiors.
- **Oxide rust** (#9E4A28–#C96F3B): Martian regolith, weathered metal, old roofs.
- **Analog-fade accents** (milky cyan #9CC7C4, dusty rose #D8A9AE): only as chromatic-aberration fringes and washed-out highlights, never as design colors.

Rule: red and yellow always read as *signal* against white paper or black space; the fade accents exist solely to imply a decaying medium. A frame may be almost monochrome black-and-white with a single red accent, or fully saturated poster color — both are inside the palette range.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the source documents them)

1. **Space program and cosmonauts** — the most documented theme: rockets with needle noses and bulbous stages, landers with stilt legs and antenna whips, probes on rust-red planetary soil, starfields with radiant flare-stars, children gazing at space murals. Spacecraft carry CCCP stencils and red stars.
2. **Soviet state iconography and slogan typography** — hammer and sickle, red star, Cyrillic proclamation blocks ("НАША МЕЧТА!"-style exclamation slogans), spaced monospace Latin words used as title cards, banner cloth with party slogans.
3. **Soviet Futurist allegory** — monumental gilded figures reaching toward stars, rays and orbit lines, humanity personified as a soaring body beside rocket contrails; earnest replication of utopian poster art.
4. **Red Army and Cold War hardware** — parade columns of tanks, uniformed silhouettes, military might displayed with awe rather than critique.
5. **Panel houses and everyday Soviet urbanism** — dithered apartment-block backdrops, courtyard murals, cracked plaster walls; documented in text but visually thin, so keep domestic interiors marginal and public space central.
6. **Analog media decay** — VHS scanlines, chromatic aberration (red/cyan edge fringing), pixel dithering, tape haze, print grain, halation; the lo-fi filter that ties the image to the music.
7. **Optional synthwave apparatus** — perspective grid floors, giant scanlined suns, silhouette-with-glowing-emblem framing; borrowed from vaporwave/synthwave but always subordinated to Soviet content.

### Materials, textures, medium

Weathered mural plaster with cracks and weeds at its base; gouache and airbrush poster paper; stencilled metal hulls; banner cloth; tape-static and raster scanlines; dithered pixel screens with monospace dialogue boxes. The medium range is genuinely wide: painterly poster illustration, degraded photograph, pixel-art game screen, and flat vector synthwave graphics all belong, because the style defines itself through subject and filter rather than one technique.

### Recurring subjects and composition

Small human against vast machine, cosmos, or sun — a child with a paper airplane before a rocket mural, a lone caped silhouette before a skyline, a probe dwarfed by planetary terrain. Poster-flat planes with a slogan block anchored to a corner; diagonal thrusts of ascending rockets; symmetrical monumental centers; horizon-grid recession; UI-framed lo-fi vignettes. Text behaves as a compositional element, not caption: slogan banners, title words, dialogue boxes.

### Lighting

Backlit amber suns swallowing skylines; star-black fields punctured by a single flare-star; overexposed, haze-washed parade daylight; dim screen-glow interiors; flat poster light with radial gradients emanating from stars or suns. Light always feels remembered rather than observed — softened, bloomed, slightly wrong in the way tape memory is wrong.

## Mood & Values

Melancholic utopianism. The style expresses communism, utopianism, militarism, patriotism and nostalgia as *longing*: awe at the might of the USSR layered with grief for the future that died in 1991. Triumph is always tinged with fade; even a parade glows like a last recording. Sincerity is the core value — Sovietwave replicates Soviet utopian art earnestly, and its quietness distinguishes it from both state bombast and internet irony.

## Era & Origins

2010s, Russian and post-Soviet internet, growing out of the electronic music scene's Soviet-nostalgia current. It reacts against synthwave's capitalist retro-futurism by substituting a socialist one, and against vaporwave's dreamy detachment by keeping a faster, awestruck pulse. Visual sources it recycles: 1960s–80s Soviet space posters, propaganda reproductions, Eastern European New Wave album culture, and the analog media of late-Soviet childhoods.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Sincerity first: no parody, no wink. Replicate propaganda grandeur earnestly.
2. Red as signal: one or few ideological red accents carrying the eye; yellow/amber as warmth; white or black as ground.
3. Monumental scale contrast: tiny human, enormous machine, sun, or idea.
4. One analog decay dialect per image — VHS fringe, dither, print grain, or scanlines — applied as atmosphere, never as destruction.
5. Retro-futurist technology vocabulary: bulbous landers, needle-nose rockets, analog dials, stencilled Cyrillic metal. Nothing sleek, glassy, or contemporary.
6. Text as graphic element: Cyrillic slogan or spaced monospace word, legible and solemn.
7. Melancholy under awe: every triumph carries a hint of loss through haze, fade, or emptiness.

**Variation axes.** Subject may range across space hardware and cosmonauts, propaganda-poster allegory, parade militarism, panel-house courtyards and murals, lone silhouettes, planetary landers, or lo-fi game-screen vignettes. Composition may be poster-flat, painterly diorama, degraded photo document, pixel UI frame, or vector grid horizon. Lighting may be amber backlight, starfield black, washed daylight, or dim interior glow. Color handling may span full poster saturation to near-monochrome with a single red accent. Mood may span solemn awe, warm childhood nostalgia, and eerie lo-fi loneliness. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Irony, absurdism, meme mockery (that is Laborwave).
- Western vaporwave/synthwave stock: Greek busts, palm trees, Miami neon, malls, magenta-cyan neon dominance without the red-yellow Soviet anchor.
- Modern technology, smartphones, contemporary spacecraft or fashion.
- Ruin porn, dread, horror-tinted decay (Doomer/Stalker territory); Sovietwave's decay is gentle fade, not collapse.
- East German consumer kitsch — Trabants, Ampelmännchen, GDR packaging (Ostalgie).
- Snow-first compositions and winter mundanity as the subject (Slavic Winter/Northerncore); snow only incidental.
- Sterile clean 3D renders with no analog texture or poster flatness.
- Generic sci-fi without Soviet marks: unmarked spacecraft break the style.
- Glitch overload and shopping-core collage.
- Present-tense triumphalism with no melancholy filter — that drifts into Heroic Realism.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Doomer:** shares panel houses and post-Soviet melancholy but strips all red-gold utopianism and space ambition; grey, nocturnal, despairing, no propaganda grandeur. Sovietwave keeps hope's skeleton: rockets, stars, slogans.
- **Heroic Realism / Socialist Realism:** original state art in muscular oil-paint present tense; no tape decay, no synth palette, no retrospective grief. Sovietwave is the same monumentality seen through 1980s analog memory and mourning.
- **Northerncore:** cold northern landscape minimalism and rural isolation without Soviet iconography, space program, or ideology.
- **Ostalgie:** domestic GDR nostalgia centered on consumer objects and warm kitsch; Sovietwave is USSR-scaled — cosmos, army, propaganda — not East German household goods.
- **Slavic Winter:** seasonal mundanity, snowbound courtyards, grey-blue cold as subject; Sovietwave may include snow but its heart is amber suns, starfields, and red ideology.
- **Laborwave (bonus cue):** overlapping communist iconography deployed ironically and absurdistly; Sovietwave's tell is always sincerity plus melancholy awe.

**Quick recognition check:** red-white-yellow over space blue or sunset black + earnest Soviet emblem or slogan + 1980s analog decay + monumental scale + wistful utopian mood = Sovietwave.
