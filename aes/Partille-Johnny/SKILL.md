# Partille-Johnny (PJ)

Partille-Johnny is a Swedish youth subculture and fashion code of the late 2000s, named after Johnny Shalaimon of Partille, a suburb of Gothenburg, whose look peers copied until it hardened into a nationwide trend with strict rules. It is the male counterpart and sub-variety of the Fjortis phenomenon: teenage boys investing enormous time and allowance money in hair wax, self-tanner and lip-whitening ointment to achieve a bronzed, stiff-haired, hyper-groomed ideal. The style blurs gendered grooming norms — makeup and hair products marketed to women, consumed heavily by boys — but its intent is "guido" / metrosexual status, never androgyny. Its natural habitat is the early social-media snapshot: dim living rooms, bathrooms, hallways and house parties captured on cheap compact cameras and uploaded to Bilddagboken, accompanied by Hands Up and Fjortisdunk at 140+ BPM.

## Visual Identity

### Colour language

The palette is a triad plus accents, and it should read as *artificial* rather than sun-kissed:

- **Orange bronze (fake tan):** the skin tone anchor, from muddy carrot in shadow to saturated orange under flash — roughly `#8A5A32` → `#C87A3B` → `#E0915A`. Always slightly too orange, with visible streaking at jawlines and hairlines.
- **Stark white:** graphic tees, whitened lips, and flash blowouts — `#F2EFE9` → `#FFFFFF`. The pale lip against dark tan is a mandatory contrast pair.
- **Denim blue:** bootcut and flared jeans, big-logo Evisu — `#2B3A55` → `#46618C` → `#6E87A8`.
- **Neon hi-vis:** reflex-ves yellow-green `#D6E900` → `#CCFF33`, cut by silver-grey reflective tape `#B9BEC2`.
- **Fjortis pink accent:** hearts, girls' tops, plastic cups — `#F0A8BC` → `#E86F95`.
- **Domestic neutrals:** beige wallpaper `#D8C6A6`, cracked brown leather sofas `#6B4A2F`, cream bathroom tile; black for sunglasses and hoodies `#141414`.
- **Hair:** bleached straw-blond `#D9B36A` → `#E8D39A` with dark roots, or waxed chestnut brown `#6B4E33`.

Rule: orange tan + white + denim must coexist; neon and pink are optional accents; backgrounds stay warm, cheap and domestic.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the culture documents them)

1. **Wax-sculpted hair** — short-to-medium hair spiked into chaotic, gravity-defying clusters or slicked back, permanently wet- and stiff-looking; D:fi (green tub) and Dax Wax are the canonical products; consumption of up to two jars a month is part of the lore.
2. **Rubber bands / hair ties on the calves** (*gummisnoddar*) — stacked over the bottom of pant legs, from a few up to twenty climbing the shin; count is a status symbol.
3. **Fake tan plus white lips** — "brun-utan-sol" or dark liquid foundation paired with Idominsalva or pale foundation on the lips for stark contrast.
4. **Hi-vis reflex vests** worn as fashion at any hour or season, over hoodies and tees.
5. **Brand-coded clothing** — Evisu jeans with large logos, Wesc and Björn Borg tees and hoodies, coloured chinos, flared/bootcut denim, canvas sneakers; Lacoste consciously avoided as "overused."
6. **Oversized smartphones and compact cameras** in mirror selfies; **snus** (portion tobacco) as a prop of the in-group.
7. **Photo-supported secondary motifs** (common in surviving snapshots, silent in the written record, therefore optional): indoor sunglasses, slogan and heart graphic tees, check-pattern scarves and short-sleeve plaid shirts, plastic party cups, and mixed groups with Fjortis girls — platinum straightened hair, pink tops, heavy makeup.

### Materials and textures

Glossy wax clumps with stiff separated tips; matte, slightly cakey foundation skin with tan lines at the ears and neck; soft cotton jersey; raw denim seams and big embroidered logos; polyester vest mesh and mirrored reflective tape; ridged rubber bands compressing jersey sweatpants; cracked leather upholstery; grout-lined bathroom tile; sensor noise and flash sheen over everything.

### Recurring subjects

Teenage boys roughly 14–18, solo or in packs of two to four groomed to near-identical standards; mixed groups with Fjortis girlfriends; mirror-selfie performers; outfit-check full-body shots that frame the shins; sofa and hallway group huddles with arms over shoulders; pouts, duckfaces and smirks aimed straight into the lens.

### Composition, lighting and medium

Snapshot grammar, never editorial: centre-weighted, crammed framing with cropped heads and elbows; selfie arms entering from a high side angle; mirror reflections with the camera or phone blocking the face; full-body checks angled down the legs. Light is direct on-camera flash in dim rooms — blown highlights on foreheads and white tees, red-eye, rapid falloff to black corners, occasional flare blobs in mirrors — or flat bathroom-mirror flash. The medium is low-megapixel compact digital: soft detail, warm yellow cast, shadow noise, sometimes a white upload/print border. Candid awkwardness is a feature.

## Mood & Values

Validation, conformity, meticulous grooming, attention-seeking. The look is described by adherents as armour: without bands and wax they feel invisible. Status runs on accumulation — more wax, more bands, darker tan. There is communal tenderness inside the performance (leaning heads, draped arms) and defiant pride under mainstream ridicule that calls the style ugly and unnatural; within the group it proves you care and can afford to. The emotional register is loud, party-keyed, self-cebratory: Fjortisdunk thump, plastic cups, house-party heat.

## Era & Origins

Partille, Gothenburg, Sweden, late 2000s; popularized by Johnny Shalaimon, spread through schools and early social media, peaking across one spring–summer–autumn cycle before codifying into strict brand and grooming rules, with minor spill into neighbouring Scandinavia. Musically rooted in Hands Up and Electro House (Dirty Dutch from c. 2009), Basshunter, Cascada, Sidney Samson's "Riverside," Tune Up!, ItaloBrothers, Dj Mangoo. It reacts to nothing avant-garde: it is a peer-imitation validation economy, and it inherits Fjortis brand codes while pushing male grooming into deliberately artificial territory.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Snapshot first: flash-lit, low-fi, domestic, slightly awkward — the image must look uploaded, not shot.
2. Grooming outranks setting: wax spikes, orange tan, pale lips must survive any crop.
3. Show accumulation logic: band counts, wax shine, tan depth as visible status.
4. Keep the bronze/white/denim triad; add neon or pink only as accents.
5. Conformity over individuality: groups look like copies of one template.
6. Era honesty: 2007–2010 devices, brands and interiors only.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo boy, boy pack, mixed group with Fjortis girls, mirror selfie, calf-band outfit check, party or schoolyard scene, bathroom or hallway portrait. Composition: crammed huddle, high selfie angle, mirror reflection, full-body leg framing, close face with sunglasses. Lighting: dark-room flash, mirror flare, overcast daylight outdoors with the vest still on, blown bathroom light. Colour: tan intensity from carrot to deep bronze; blond or brown waxed hair; pink accent present or absent. Mood: boastful pout, deadpan smirk, party euphoria, affectionate group lean. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Modern slim smartphones, ring lights, selfie sticks, Instagram-era filters or clean HDR; studio softboxes, golden-hour outdoor glow, film grain or medium-format polish; natural skincare, beards, adult bodies; androgynous or drag reading, emo/scene fringes and neon hair streaks; gold chains, muscle tees and club-bouncer physiques; Japanese deco layering and platform boots; rave lasers, DJ booths and crowd hands; minimalist interiors, luxury fashion styling, tidy composition.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Fruittari:** shares the Scandinavian rubber-band and accessory accumulation, but Fruittari skews female and candy-bright — dyed multi-colour hair, cluttered bracelets, sweet prints — where PJ keeps a male, bronze-and-white guido core with wax spikes and safety vests.
- **Fjortis:** the parent culture, broader and more gender-balanced, with darker emo-lite tones and attitude; PJ is its male sub-variety, distinguished by fake tan, white lips, calf bands and reflex vests.
- **Guido:** the American tanned-club cousin — older, muscular, gold jewellery, faded club haircuts, spray-tan bronze; PJ is teenage, suburban-Swedish, flash-snapshot, with makeup-derived orange tan and no gym-body coding.
- **Gyaruo:** the Japanese tanned-and-bleached male style, but fashion-editorial: layered deco, prints, platform boots, street-styled poses; PJ is anti-editorial, sportswear-branded, domestic and low-fi.
- **Hands Up:** a music-event visual culture of neon, lasers and club crowds; PJ merely soundtracks itself with Hands Up — its images stay in hallways, sofas and bathrooms, lit by a camera flash, not a strobe.

Where the record is thin — reflex vests and snus appear strongly in text but rarely in surviving photos, while indoor sunglasses and heart-graphic tees are the reverse — treat those elements as period-plausible options rather than mandatory markers.
