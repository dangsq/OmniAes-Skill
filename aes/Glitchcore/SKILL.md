# Glitchcore

Glitchcore, also called **Eyestraincore**, is a 2020s internet aesthetic defined by the maximalistic use of visual glitches and distortion: heavily saturated colors, rainbows, flashing patterns, pixelation, and deliberate eyestrain. It peaked on TikTok in 2020–2021, carried strongly by the Danganronpa fandom, and it feeds on borrowed childhoods — characters and artwork lifted from cartoons, anime, and video games, then run through a corruption filter until they vibrate. The name doubles as a microgenre of music adjacent to Hyperpop and Digicore (high-pitched vocals, sharp 808s, stuttering glitch effects; pioneers like 100 gecs), and the visual style carries that album-cover energy: everything at maximum volume at once. Glitchcore is not glitch used sparingly as an effect; glitch is the entire habitat.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette
The rule is saturation pushed to clipping point, with hues chosen to clash rather than harmonize. The working range spans the full hue wheel at maximum chroma: hot magenta and bubblegum pink (#FF00FF–#FF2D95), electric cyan (#00FFFF–#00CFFF), acid lime and chartreuse (#00FF00–#C8FF00), ultraviolet purple (#8A2BE2–#9400FF), and signal yellow (#FFFF00), frequently stitched together as rainbow gradients that cycle the entire wheel in a single band or swirl. Against this, the style's one documented neutral rule: **black and white, but not gray** — pure #000000 and pure #FFFFFF appear as letterbox bars, checker fields, halftone dots, and high-contrast manga linework, never as muddy midtones. Acceptable color treatments within the range include: full rainbow clash; a single dominant neon field (magenta or cyan) peppered with contrary accents; two-color duotone posters (pink/yellow, magenta/cyan); blown-out overexposure where saturation clips toward white; and black-void frames in which only glowing neon type or symbols carry color.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)
1. **Glitch and distortion artifacts** — the defining layer: pixelation, pixel sorting, datamosh smears, RGB channel split / chromatic aberration, JPEG and MPEG corruption, scanlines and tracking noise.
2. **Highly saturated color and rainbows** — rainbow arcs, rainbow gradient hair or fills, prismatic lens flares.
3. **Anime, cartoon, and video-game characters** — magical girls, twintailed vocaloid-type idols, mascot hedgehogs and ponies, sanrio-style cute icons, game sprites and pixel-game interiors; corrupted-cartoon imagery in the lineage of *Come and Learn with Pibby!*, *Problem Solverz*, and the Spamtopia episode of *Smiling Friends*.
4. **Pattern fields** — checkers and checkerboard transparency grids, stripes, waves, diamond lattices, flashing repeating tiles.
5. **Pixels as visible unit** — chunky pixel clusters, dither and halftone dot textures, low-res sprite edges left unsmoothed.
6. **Black-and-white extremes** — pure black voids, pure white blowouts, high-contrast ink passes with no gray ramp.
7. **Sticker symbols** — peace signs, hearts, stars, smiley faces, sparkles and starbursts scattered like clipart confetti.

Secondary motifs corroborated across the visual record: operating-system and VHS interface text (Windows-style error dialogs, "ERROR", glowing "PLAY" on a pause screen), repeated emo or ironic caption lines set in wordart ("i feel sick", "click to forget", "it hurts so much"), chain-link graphics, holographic gradient fills, and Japanese text blocks on poster-style layouts.

### Materials and textures
Everything reads as screen-native rather than physical: pixel-sorted smears, datamosh bleed, compression artifacts, dithered halftone dots, checkerboard alpha grids, MS Paint clipart layering, plastic smiley stickers, hologram foil gradients, VHS bloom and scanlines, manga-scan paper white blown out by a copier-bright light. There is no paper, fabric, or wood grain that survives un-corrupted.

### Recurring subjects
Fandom characters (anime girls, cartoon mascots, game heroes), cute-icon brands, toys and 3D renders of chibi figures, pixel-game scenes — but the subject range legitimately extends to **no character at all**: pure abstract corruption fields, all-over pattern grids, and text-only screens ("PLAY", "ERROR", lyric lines) are equally valid Glitchcore images.

### Composition and lighting
Composition tends toward full-bleed maximalism: a central character orbited by sticker symbols and chain graphics, often inside a checkerboard or patterned border frame; or an all-over mosaic with no resting focal point; or a letterboxed video-still frame; or a flat poster duotone. Layers stack with deliberately confused depth — foreground clipart, midground character, background noise all competing at equal volume. Lighting is never natural: it is emissive screen light — glow, bloom, VHS flare, overexposed blowout — or a black void in which neon elements self-illuminate. Flashing, repeating pattern fields are used intentionally to produce eyestrain; visual comfort is not a goal.

## Mood & Values

Glitchcore feels like sugar-rush overload with a bruise underneath. The candy-cute iconography (smileys, hearts, mascots) is routinely paired with sick-sweet or despairing text — "i feel sick", "it hurts so much", "click to the pain" — producing an irony of adorable suffering. The style values more-is-more, anti-taste, and deliberate ugliness-as-affection: eyestrain is a virtue, minimalism is the enemy. It expresses digital-native nostalgia for 2000s–2010s cartoons, clipart, and early-web clutter, and a fandom belonging tied to TikTok, Danganronpa, and the hyperpop/digicore music scene. Sensory overwhelm is treated as comfort and as community signal.

## Era & Origins

A 2020s aesthetic that peaked on TikTok in 2020–2021. It descends from Glitch Art, Vaporwave/Synthwave screen nostalgia, Scenecore's neon excess, Kidcore's sticker clutter, and 2010s Meme Maximalism's layering habits, but reorganizes all of them around glitch grammar and candy neon. Its soundtrack is the Glitchcore/Hyperpop/Digicore microgenre (100 gecs and peers), plus adjacent Krushclub, Lolicore, and atmospheric drum-and-bass/Glitchbreak scenes. Television touchstones of corrupted-cartoon imagery — *Come and Learn with Pibby!*, *Problem Solverz*, *Smiling Friends*' Spamtopia — sit in the same visual family.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Glitch is the habitat, not an accent: commit to pixelation, datamosh, RGB split, or dither across the whole frame.
2. Push saturation to clipping; let complementary hues clash; a rainbow element is always welcome.
3. Stack at least three systems at once — character or subject, pattern field (checker/stripes/waves), sticker symbols (stars, hearts, smileys, peace signs), and optionally interface or emo text.
4. Keep the pixel visible: chunky pixels, halftone dots, pixel-sort smears; never smooth everything out.
5. Use only pure black and pure white as neutrals; avoid gray ramps.
6. Light everything emissively: screen glow, bloom, flare, overexposure — no daylight, no softbox.
7. Optionally add wordart captions, error dialogs, or VHS pause-screen type as graphic elements.

**Variation axes**
- *Subject*: any anime, cartoon, or game character or mascot (original or fandom), toy or 3D render, pixel-game interior, cute-icon creature — or no subject at all: abstract corruption field, pattern lattice, or glowing text screen.
- *Composition*: centered sticker-collage with border frame; all-over mosaic without focal point; letterboxed video still; flat duotone poster; radial sparkle chaos.
- *Color handling*: full rainbow clash; single-neon dominant field; two-color duotone; blown-out pastel overexposure; black void with luminous neon.
- *Lighting*: emissive glow, VHS bloom, copier-bright blowout, or void darkness with self-lit elements.
- *Mood*: euphoric candy rave, sick-sweet despair, or ironic deadpan.
- *Medium*: digital collage, clipart layering, 3D render, pixel art, VHS capture, dithered manga scan.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — saturation, glitch grammar, pattern fields, symbol clutter, emissive light — not any reference image. An image of a completely original robot, fruit, or cityscape can be perfect Glitchcore if it obeys these rules.

**AVOID**
- Muted, earthy, or desaturated palettes; beige, cream, wood tones; tasteful neutrals.
- Minimalism, generous negative space, a single clean object on a calm background.
- One restrained glitch accent on an otherwise pristine photograph (that is purist Glitch Art).
- Mid-gray tonal mud, soft natural light, uncorrupted photographic realism.
- Elegant serif typography, restrained branding, orderly corporate grids.
- Industrial horror gloom, broken concrete, blood-on-black dread (Glitchbreak).
- Fashion-photo framing, scene-hair and bracelet portraiture (Scenecore), e-girl wardrobe portraits (2020 Alt).
- Warm analog film fade, sunset gradients, classical sculpture (Vaporwave's soft side).

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Their tells | How Glitchcore differs |
|---|---|---|
| 2010s Meme Maximalism | Deep-fried JPEG crud, impact-font meme scraps, ironic pileups of meme references, photographic meme sources | Glitchcore's chaos follows glitch grammar — pixel sort, datamosh, RGB split, checker/dither fields — and is stocked with cute anime/cartoon icons and rainbow neon rather than meme punchlines |
| Glitch Art | Corruption as the subject; one figure or object on a calm field; source palette often kept or muted; gallery restraint | Glitchcore treats glitch as wallpaper, not subject: clipping saturation, symbol clutter, and character collage on top of the corruption |
| Glitchbreak | Dark, atmospheric, industrial/horror; broken UI on black; acid green or red on void; drum-and-bass dread | Glitchcore is bright, cute, and eyestrain-rainbow; its despair is sugar-coated in smileys and pink, not industrial |
| Scenecore | 2000s scene-kid photography: dyed hair, bracelets, party-rock clothing, Myspace-angle portraits, neon on photographic subjects | Glitchcore is screen-native collage of animated characters and glitch texture, not fashion or portrait photography |
| 2020 Alt | E-girl/alt portraiture, black wardrobe with a single neon accent, chains and eyeliner, moody bedroom light | Glitchcore rejects single-accent restraint: every hue at once, pattern fields everywhere, no photographic wardrobe focus |

When in doubt, apply the eyestrain test: if the image feels like a candy-colored screen malfunction lovingly assembled by a fandom kid at 2 a.m., it is Glitchcore.
