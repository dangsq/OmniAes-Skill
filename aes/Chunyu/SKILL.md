# Chunyu (纯欲 / 純欲)

Chunyu — literally "pure-desire" — is a paradox worn on the body: "chun," innocent and cute, held in the same frame as "yu," sexy and alluring. The style is often summed up by the old Chinese saying "angel face, devil body," or described as "sweet and salty." Its governing law is effortlessness: the wearer must look beautiful without appearing to have tried, styled yet casual, classy yet girl-next-door. Emergent on Xiaohongshu (China's Instagram analogue) in the early 2020s as a makeup language before growing into a full fashion aesthetic, Chunyu is digital-native femininity — phone-camera intimacy, soft light, and a body grammar calibrated to Chinese beauty standards (defined collarbones, aegyo sal / "wocan" under-eye fullness, small face with V-shaped chin, slender hourglass figure, long legs, good posture).

The paradox is resolved by division of labor: the face carries the "pure" (doll-like blush, glossy lips, lush natural hair), while the silhouette carries the "desire" (figure-hugging knits, bared shoulders, collarbones, midriff or legs) — but never too much. Heavy cleavage is avoided because it breaks the pure half of the equation.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette rule is **milky pastels and warm reds over nude neutrals, with black only as hair or a thin accent layer**. Saturation stays low and value stays high across the frame; the only saturated note is a warm red reserved for small doses — lips, a single knit, a rose. Backgrounds are muted and desaturated so the skin and pastels read luminous.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how firmly the style documents them)

1. **The doll-like makeup face.** Clear pale skin; red or warm blush swept around the eyes and sometimes onto the nose tip (a lineage from Chinese opera makeup); emphasized aegyo sal; arched eyebrows; spiky separated fake lashes; glitter touches; glossy warm-red or peach lips. This is the style's origin point and its strongest tell.
2. **The cute-sexy wardrobe balance.** Oversized sweaters, knitted dresses (cable and rib knits), cardigans, spaghetti-strap singlets and camisoles, short skirts, cropped T-shirts, high-waisted jeans, faux fur coats, hair accessories. A cute piece and a sexy piece always coexist: an off-shoulder cardigan sliding over a bodycon knit dress, a cropped bolero over a slip-style mini.
3. **Collarbone and shoulder display.** Defined collarbones are a beauty ideal and are accentuated with highlighter; necklines obligingly expose them — halter, off-shoulder, square neck, open shirt collars. Arms, shoulders, midriff and legs may be bared; deep cleavage may not.
4. **Lush natural hair.** Black or dyed brown only; soft, voluminous, slightly unkempt strands that preserve a feeling of realism against the doll-face perfection.
5. **Elegant nail art.** Long almond or stiletto nails in milky translucent nude, sparsely decorated with rhinestones, crystals and fine glitter — precious but restrained.
6. **Whisper-thin jewelry.** Fine chain necklaces and chokers with small charms (moons, stars, tiny pendants) that rest on the collarbone rather than compete with it; delicate hair accessories. Statement jewelry belongs to the offspring style, Chunye, not here.
7. **Traditional-dress fusion.** Chunyu makeup worn with qipao, hanfu or hanyuansu (modernized hanfu) is text-documented and trendy, though absent from the small surviving image record — treat it as a legitimate but under-evidenced branch.
8. **The unposed pose.** Casual hand placement, a slight tilt, a selfie arm's-length angle, a half-turned stance: body language that reads as caught, not directed.

### Materials and textures

Cable knit and rib knit wool; brushed sweater fleece; faux fur; sheer mesh and chiffon sleeves over opaque jersey; crisp shirt cotton; ruched stretch jersey; fine jacquard or checkered knit; quilted leather only as a small bag accent; rhinestone and glitter on nails; fine metal chain. Everything invites touch — soft, fuzzy, sheer — against smooth porcelain skin.

### Recurring subjects

Almost exclusively young women (the term can apply to any sex, but the practice and the entire visual record are female; male Chunyu is under-documented). Subjects range from face-forward beauty portraits to outfit studies to fragment close-ups: hands and nails, neck and collarbone with a necklace, shoulders under a sliding cardigan. Celebrity associations include Jang Won-young, Jennie Kim, Nana Ou-Yang, Yi Mengling, Yu Shuxin and Zhao Lusi.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical, phone-native framing. Light is soft, diffused and low-contrast — window daylight, softbox, or warm interior lamp — often slightly hazy or gently overexposed so skin glows and shadows stay milky. Backgrounds are simple and muted: gray seamless, white tile, beige bedroom, a café corner with flowers. Framing ranges freely across: face-forward selfie close-up; macro detail of nails, jewelry or collarbone; three-quarter or full-body outfit shots in which the face is often cropped at the nose or mouth, shifting emphasis to silhouette, fabric and posture. ## Mood & Values

Chunyu expresses controlled contradiction: youth and womanliness, innocence and allure, polish and carelessness. Its core value is *effortless* beauty — looking beautiful without seeming to try — which is why hair keeps a few stray strands and poses stay casual. It values femininity and class alongside a tomboyish girl-next-door accessibility, and it encodes contemporary Chinese beauty standards (collarbones, aegyo sal, V-chin, slender hourglass, good posture) as aspirational norms. The emotional register is intimate and warm with a quiet undercurrent of seduction: sweet on the surface, salty beneath.

## Era & Origins

Early 2020s, China. Chunyu spread first as a makeup style among beauty gurus on Xiaohongshu, then expanded into hair and fashion as Chinese-style aesthetics gained global momentum; TikTok carried it to a worldwide audience in 2022, and it gained traction in Japan alongside styles like Chaiborg. Internationally it was often mislabeled "Douyin makeup," though within China it is simply "Chinese makeup" (中国妆) — a misattribution that itself became a point of criticism and debate. Its blush-around-the-eyes technique descends from Chinese opera makeup. In late 2022 it spawned **Chunye** (纯野, "pure-wild"), its more mature, glamorous "older sister": heavier contouring, more glitter, bolder colors, more jewelry, luxurious sexy fashion.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Hold the paradox.** Every image must carry at least one "chun" cue (doll blush, glossy lips, soft knit, pastel, messy lush hair) and at least one "yu" cue (bodycon silhouette, bared shoulder or collarbone, thigh line, ruched fabric hugging the waist). Neither half may win outright.
2. **Respect the exposure boundary.** Shoulders, arms, midriff, legs, collarbones: yes. Deep cleavage: no — it collapses the "pure" half.
3. **Keep it effortless.** Slightly unkempt strands, casual hand placement, lived-in or plain interiors, selfie-grade intimacy. Nothing may look labored or stage-directed.
4. **Obey the palette rule.** Milky pastels and nude neutrals dominate; warm red only in small doses; black only as hair or a thin accent garment/accessory.
5. **Light soft.** Diffused, low-contrast, slightly hazy or gently overexposed; muted, simple backgrounds.
6. **Finish with whisper-details.** Milky rhinestone nails, a fine chain on the collarbone, a touch of glitter, highlighter on the collarbones.
7. **Frame phone-native.** Vertical orientation; close-up, detail macro, or face-cropped body emphasis all valid.

**Variation axes** — a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

- *Subject:* beauty selfie; outfit study; hand/nail or neck/jewelry macro; qipao or hanfu fusion; casual cropped tee with high-waisted jeans; tomboyish girl-next-door variant; faux-fur-coat winter variant; café, bedroom, bathroom-tile or seamless-studio setting.
- *Composition:* face-forward close-up, three-quarter, full-body, fragment macro, face cropped to emphasize silhouette.
- *Lighting:* window daylight, soft studio, warm lamp, overcast exterior — always diffused.
- *Color handling:* pink-led, cream-led, or red-accent-led within the stated ranges; backdrop gray, tile white, or warm beige.
- *Mood dial:* sweeter (more pastel, more doll) ↔ saltier (more bodycon, more bared shoulder), never leaving the paradox.

**Avoid**

- Heavy contouring, sculpted cheekbones, smoky eyes, matte nude lips, abundant glitter-and-gold glamour (that is Chunye or Western glam).
- Bold saturated jewel colors, statement jewelry, overt luxury-logo opulence.
- Deep cleavage or overtly sexual posing.
- Unnatural hair colors (platinum, pastel dye), sleek sculpted hair; tanned skin or heavy bronzer.
- Harsh direct flash, high-contrast chiaroscuro, dark moody grades, heavy film grain.
- Opaque bold or chrome acrylic nails (ABG territory).
- Vintage kitsch overload: lace, pearls, ribbon bows, ballet flats, cherries (Coquette).
- Sunlit meadows, prairie florals, analog fade and pastoral romance (Nymphet).
- Stiff editorial haute-couture posing; anything that reads as visibly "trying."

When in doubt, apply the litmus: *does this image look innocent and sexy at once, and does it look like it didn't try?* If yes — Chunyu.
