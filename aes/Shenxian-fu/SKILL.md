# Shenxian-fu (神仙服)

Shenxian-fu is a revivalist dress aesthetic: a 2020s Chinese Han-yuansu (Han-element) fashion that asks "what did a xian — a female Taoist immortal — look like in our ancestors' imagination?" and answers in cloth. It has no fixed historical court behind it; instead it gathers every traditional place where Chinese culture pictured immortal women: Wei–Jin era silk scroll painting and murals from the Taoism-prevailing dynasties, later temple mural ensembles, the Chang'e printed on mooncake tins, the celestial court dancers of 1980s television. Its silhouette borrows mostly from Wei and Jin (220–420 CE) dress. The style surged after runway appearances at the Guofeng Dadian (National Style Grand Ceremony) and through social-media backlash against the homogeneous, dedication-lacking costumes of xianxia film and TV — Shenxian-fu is deliberately the opposite of disposable costume-drama cloth: an antiquarian, garment-obsessed dream of transcendence.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: mineral warm–cool contrast
The constitutional rule is a warm–cool pair held in contrast: a cinnabar/vermilion red family against a turquoise/malachite/azurite blue-green family, mediated by ivory and cream silk and edged with ochre gold.

| Family | Role | Hex range |
|---|---|---|
| Reds (cinnabar, vermilion, rouge) | skirts, outer robes, ribbons, bodice blocks | #8E2323 – #C0392B – #E4573D |
| Blue-greens (azurite, malachite, turquoise) | underskirts, facings, bixi grounds, ribbon reverses | #1F6F78 – #3E9C9C – #7FC6BC |
| Ivory / aged silk | inner layers, sleeves, painting grounds | #F4EBDA, #E9DDBF, #D9C79A |
| Ochre / gold | brocade bands, crowns, mural grounds | #B98A44 – #D4AF37 |
| Ink | hair, contour line | #1C1B1A |

Saturation is a sliding scale, not a fixed value: at one pole, faded mineral pigment on cracked, beige-aged silk (scroll and mural registers), where reds read as soft cinnabar and greens as pale malachite; at the other, fully saturated stage silks in modern reconstruction photography, where red and teal hit full chroma. Both poles are valid. What is invalid is a palette lacking the red-versus-blue-green tension — all-white, all-pastel, or monochrome earth tones break the style.

### Signature motifs and garments, ranked by textual grounding
1. **Red-and-turquoise color blocking** — the infobox key colours and first visual note; the single strongest identifier.
2. **Draping fabric in motion** — wide sleeves, looping pibo ribbons, trailing hems; flowing cloth is the style's main event.
3. **Zaju chuishao fu** — the Wei-Jin skirt system with mixed-pattern panels and hanging sash tails or pointed hem tabs that trail at the sides and between the feet.
4. **Banbi with pleated facing** — a half-sleeve short jacket worn over the inner ruqun, its front edge pleated.
5. **Bixi** — an embroidered ornamental panel or knee-cover suspended from the waist over the skirt.
6. **Hewei (合围) outer dress** — a wrapping outer layer closing the torso.
7. **The female xian herself** — nymph, moon goddess (Chang'e), celestial court dancer; the subject the whole wardrobe exists for.

Secondary motifs, evidenced in modern renditions rather than ranked by the text: high coiled or double-loop topknots and, in the photographic register, gold phoenix crowns with dangling ornaments; round团 fans; props such as painted scrolls and lanterns; cloud and mist stage sets; vertical calligraphy titles in poster-format renditions; mural-register ensembles of several robed immortals on gold ground. The source garment list is flagged provisional, so treat the named pieces as characteristic vocabulary rather than a mandatory checklist — but the layered logic (inner ruqun + wrapping outer dress + half-arm jacket + waist panel + trailing skirt) should always read.

### Materials and textures
Lightweight silk gauze and crepe that drapes and floats; fine pleated skirt folds; brocade bands and hems edged in gold thread; dense satin-stitch embroidery on bixi panels; ribbons thin enough to arc in mid-air. In painted registers: mineral pigment (cinnabar, azurite, malachite) laid on silk or plaster, fine even iron-wire contour line, gold-leaf ground in mural ensembles, and an aged patina — beige silk, faint cracking, softened edges.

### Recurring subjects
A solitary immortal standing, walking or dancing; a modern wearer modelling reconstructed hanfu; an illustrated Chang'e on commodity packaging; a mural fragment of several robed immortals; occasionally a hybrid with neighbouring folk dress (a Tibetan-style brocade hybrid is recorded) so long as the layered red-turquoise core survives.

### Composition and lighting
Vertical, full-length figure dominant; motion described by ribbon arcs, flung sleeves and turning bodies rather than camera tricks. Settings range widely: plain seamless studio backdrops in ivory, beige or white; heritage architecture — temple corridors, balustrades, timber halls; gardens with rockery and greenery; reed marshes; artificial cloud and mist sets; dark interiors lit by candle or spot. Lighting accordingly ranges from even soft commercial light, to open daylight, to dramatic low-key spot against shadowed architecture, to diffuse glow inside mist. Painted registers use flat picture-plane light: no cast shadow, no single source, colour itself doing the modelling.

## Mood & Values
Reverie with scholarship: the feeling is ancestral imagination of transcendence made cloth — weightless dignity, serene poise mid-movement, ritual formality softened by floating ribbon. It carries guofeng (national-style) pride and an ethic of dedication: every pleat, panel and band is evidence of care, posed against disposable costume-drama minimalism. Dreamlike but not ghostly — this immortality is saturated, jewelled, warm.

## Era & Origins
Emerged in 2020s China within the hanfu / Han-yuansu revival. Source imagery reaches back to the Wei and Jin dynasties (220–420 CE), when Taoism prevailed and painters of the Gu Kaizhi lineage pictured the Nymph of the Lo River in trailing ribbons; it also absorbs later temple murals, twentieth-century commodity packaging (mooncake tins) and 1986 television's celestial court. The immediate catalyst was reaction: criticism of homogeneous xianxia film and TV costumes fuelled a social-media trend of reconstructing "what xian looked like in our ancestors' imagination," and runway exposure at the Guofeng Dadian turned it into fashion. The origin narrative is flagged needs-verification in sources; treat the runway and social-media catalyst as widely reported but provisional.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Layer the garment as a system, not a dress: cross-collared inner ruqun, wrapping hewei outer dress, banbi half-arm with pleated facing, bixi panel at the waist, zaju chuishao fu skirt with hanging tails, pibo ribbons over all. Not every piece must appear, but the stacked, tied, trailing logic must.
2. Constitution colour as red-versus-blue-green contrast; let one family lead and the other counter, with ivory silk as breathing room and gold as edge.
3. Give cloth weight and air: hems that trail, sleeves that pool, ribbons that describe arcs; stillness with implied motion.
4. Pick one register and stay coherent in it: mineral-on-silk painting, mural on gold, modern reconstruction photography, or commercial/packaging illustration.
5. Hair up and ornamental: coiled or double-loop topknots; painted registers favour bare simple coils, photographic registers allow gold crowns and dangling pins.
6. Keep posture dignified and otherworldly: standing, turning, slow dance — never casual modern body language.

**Variation axes (what may freely change)**
- Subject: any female immortal or modern wearer; solo or ensemble; nymph, moon goddess, celestial dancer, anonymous model; with fan, scroll, lantern or empty-handed; even a packaging illustration or mural fragment. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.
- Composition: full-length vertical, three-quarter, or close crop of the layered garments; centred or offset; figure in empty studio, architecture, garden, field, mist, or flat painted ground; poster layouts with vertical calligraphy titles optional.
- Lighting: even studio softbox, open daylight, low-key candle or spot, mist diffusion, or flat picture-plane light in painted registers.
- Colour handling: anywhere between faded mineral on aged silk and full-chroma stage silk; red-dominant or teal-dominant schemes both valid so long as the contrast pair exists.
- Mood: from quiet contemplation to ceremonial spectacle.

**Avoid (style-breakers)**
- All-white, pastel-only or monochrome ethereal minimalism — that reads as Xian-fu, the xianxia-fiction fashion this style is explicitly not.
- Any palette without red-versus-blue-green tension; neon accents or desaturated grey-beige austerity.
- Stiff costume-shop fabric with no drape; visible modern fasteners (zippers, velcro, plastic sequins).
- Silhouettes from other hanfu eras as the primary read (Tang chest-high ruqun, Ming standing-collar aoqun), Qing qipao, Japanese kimono cues, or Western fantasy corsetry.
- Generic unlayered "ancient Chinese dress" with no trailing tails, no half-arm, no waist panel — the dedication to garment construction is the point.
- Modernized sexualized cuts: short hems, bare shoulders, sheer bodices.
- Male armour or battlefield gear as subject; the wardrobe is female xian garb.
- Mixing registers unintentionally (photographic spotlight on a mural gold ground, aged-silk cracking on a studio photo).

## Discrimination Cues
The wiki records no formal neighbour list beyond two relations, so discriminate on those plus construction:
- **Versus Xian-fu (xianxia-inspired fashion):** Xian-fu leans white/pastel, simplified, fiction-derived ethereality; Shenxian-fu insists on saturated mineral red-turquoise contrast and named, layered, historically referenced pieces.
- **Versus plain Wei-Jin reconstruction (its recorded related aesthetic):** reconstruction aims at plausible everyday historical dress in muted earth tones; Shenxian-fu theatricalizes the imagined immortal — ribbons, trailing tails, ritual ornament, mural and packaging glow — history as dream, not document.
- **Versus generic guofeng hanfu photography:** look for the construction vocabulary — hanging sash tails of the zaju chuishao fu, pleated banbi facing, waist-hung bixi, looping pibo — and the red/turquoise blocking; their absence usually means another hanfu category.
- **Edge cases:** recorded hybrids (e.g., with Tibetan-style brocade) remain Shenxian-fu so long as the layered silhouette and warm-cool mineral contrast survive.
