# Metal

Metal is a music-first subculture whose visual identity was forged on album sleeves, band promo sheets, fanzine xeroxes and the backs of its fans' jackets. The genre developed in the late 1960s and early 1970s in the United Kingdom and the United States out of blues rock, psychedelic rock and acid rock, crystallized by Black Sabbath in the early-mid 1970s, then diversified through the New Wave of British Heavy Metal into doom, power and thrash, and further into death and black metal by the late 1980s and early 1990s. Its imagery matches its sound: thick, distorted, loud, and deliberately confrontational. Lyrics and performance carry aggression and machismo; the occult strand (from Coven and Blue Öyster Cult onward) and the 1980s moral panic over "Satanic" content fed a visual vocabulary of demons, inverted crosses and blasphemy, while the community itself codified belonging through battle jackets, patches and the horns gesture. Fans are headbangers, metalheads — and the pictures look like it: heavy, dark, analog, and unapologetically morbid.

Note on evidence: the written source documents the genre broadly, but the surviving image record skews toward the extreme end (death, black and thrash metal cover art and promo photography). Classic-era heavy metal imagery sits inside the variation space described below even where it is underrepresented in the surviving sample.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule: **black is the ground, never merely an accent.** Everything else is either desaturated toward bone and ash, or injected as a single violent accent.

| Role | Range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Void black (ground) | `#000000`–`#1c1c1c` | Backgrounds, clothing, shadow mass; occupies 50–90% of frame |
| Bone / sepia cream | `#d9c7a7`–`#e8dcc0` | Aged paper, anatomical plate toning, faded print |
| Ochre decay | `#8a6d3b`–`#b08d4f` | Stained, rusted mid-tones on illustrated covers |
| Blood / signal red | `#7a0c0c`–`#c1121f`, logo red up to `#e01a1a` | Logotypes, dripping blood, robes, title text |
| Rot green | `#4f7942`–`#8fbf3f` | Sparse decay accent: necrotic lettering, slime |
| Cold funeral grey-blue | `#2f3a42`–`#5a6b7a` | Winter landscapes, monochrome-adjacent toning |
| Ash greyscale | full black-to-white scale | Grainy high-contrast monochrome mode |

Two dominant color handlings coexist: (1) **monochrome mode** — grainy, contrast-crushed black and white, blown highlights, blocked shadows; and (2) **decayed tint mode** — sepia/ochre fields with one saturated accent (almost always red) reserved for logo, blood or garment. Full-color illustrated covers exist but keep the field muted so the red or green accent reads as a wound in the image.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual documentation)

1. **The band logotype as graphic object.** Spiky, symmetrical, near-illegible death/black metal letterforms, often in blood red or bone white, placed top-center or corner-anchored over the artwork; treated as an emblem, not readable type.
2. **The battle jacket / patch vest.** Black denim or leather vest densely sewn with band and album patches, studs and spikes — documented as a core feature of the metal community; patches function as a worn résumé of allegiance.
3. **Long hair and the black uniform.** Waist-length hair, full beards, plain black t-shirts or black shirts; hair is the silhouette-defining element in portraits and group shots.
4. **Occult and morbid iconography.** Skulls, skeletons, flayed anatomical (écorché) figures, robed cultists, inverted crosses, horns, demons, blasphemous parody of religious scene-painting — the visual echo of the genre's occult rock roots and the Satanic panic it provoked.
5. **Corpse paint (black-metal lineage).** White-painted faces with blackened eye sockets and gaunt shading, sometimes with horned or spiked regalia; strictly monochrome contexts.
6. **Gore and decay tableau.** Corpses, rot, dripping blood, clinical anatomical exposure in illustrated cover scenes.
7. **The horns hand gesture.** Clenched fist with index and little finger raised, often with spiked wristband — the community's greeting and emblem.
8. **Instruments as props.** Electric guitars (frequently held vertically or angled across the chest), drum kits and band rooms in promo and live contexts.

### Materials and textures

Worn black denim and leather, metal studs and spikes, woven patch cloth with frayed edges, chains; analog film grain, photocopier/xerox degradation, scan artifacts and print misregistration; matte ink on card (vinyl sleeve surface), ink bleed and drip effects in logos; illustrated covers show painterly or pen-and-ink plate texture (anatomical chart stipple, cross-hatch); environmental textures of bare winter forest, concrete underpasses, dust and rust.

### Recurring subjects

Band group portraits (members in a row or wedge against void black); solo musician portraits with instrument; illustrated album-cover scenes (anatomical figures, robed cults, funeral landscapes, cosmic or hellish tableaus); garment still lifes (the patch vest displayed flat or on a hanger); emblematic iconography (horns fist, skull devices); desolate nature — leafless trees, snow, fog — in the black-metal lineage; candid rehearsal and live grit.

### Composition and lighting

- **Low-key studio:** pure black backdrop, a single hard key light carving faces, hair and beards out of darkness; group arranged in a shallow wedge or line with the frontman centered and nearest camera.
- **Illustrated sleeve layout:** dense central tableau, logotype block anchored top, album title in a contrasting accent color at bottom edge; square vinyl-sleeve format.
- **Lo-fi snapshot:** harsh direct flash or available light, tilted or off-center framing, heavy grain, crushed blacks — rawness as credibility.
- **Flat document light:** even, shadowless lighting for vest and patch still lifes, object centered like an exhibit.
- Across all modes: contrast is pushed, mid-tones are scarce, and empty black space is compositional, not accidental.

## Mood & Values

Heaviness, aggression and machismo; fascination with death, decay and the occult; transgression as identity (the imagery deliberately courts the moral panic it caused); underground authenticity — grain, xerox degradation and self-made patches signal truth against commercial polish; communal belonging expressed through worn allegiance (patches, horns, the uniform of black); solemnity and funeral gloom in the doom and black-metal registers. The picture should feel loud, serious and slightly dangerous.

## Era & Origins

Emergent 1968–1975 in Britain and the US from heavy blues and occult rock; crystallized by Black Sabbath; expanded by NWOBHM in the late 1970s–early 1980s alongside thrash, doom and power metal; death metal's illustrated gore sleeve flourished in mid-1980s–early-1990s Florida and beyond; black metal's monochrome corpse-paint imagery arose in early-1990s Scandinavia. Visual era markers are analog: film grain, pre-digital print registration, vinyl square formats, photocopied zine aesthetics. The style persists into the 21st century but keeps these analog signatures as stylistic law.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Begin from darkness: black occupies most of the frame; light is a blade, not a wash.
- Restrict saturation: color appears as accent (blood red, rot green) or as decayed tint (sepia, ochre, cold grey-blue), never as a full cheerful spectrum.
- Degrade the signal: grain, contrast crush, print bleed, xerox coarse-ness; perfection reads as fake.
- Treat the logotype as image: spiky, symmetrical, semi-illegible emblem lettering integrated into the composition.
- Dress the subject in the uniform: long hair, black cloth, denim/leather with studs and patches.
- Let morbidity carry meaning: skulls, skeletons, anatomical exposure, occult paraphernalia, funeral landscapes.
- Keep the human stance confrontational or solemn — never casual-cheerful.

**Variation axes**
- *Subject:* band group portrait; solo musician with instrument; illustrated cover tableau (anatomical, religious-parody, gore, cosmic, funeral); garment/patch still life; emblem iconography (horns fist, skull device); desolate winter forest or concrete wasteland; live or rehearsal candid.
- *Composition:* centered wedge or line of figures; bust or three-quarter solo portrait; dense illustrated tableau with logo block; flat centered still life; tilted snapshot diagonal.
- *Lighting:* single-key chiaroscuro on black; harsh direct flash; grainy available light; flat document light; fog-diffused daylight in forest scenes.
- *Color handling:* pure high-contrast monochrome; sepia/ochre decay; black field plus one red accent; desaturated cold grey-blue; muted full-color illustration with accent wound.
- *Mood:* menacing, mournful/funeral, aggressive, ritualistic, clinical-anatomical.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A studio portrait of an unknown band, an invented anatomical plate, or a still life of a patched vest can all be perfectly on-style without reproducing any existing sleeve or photo.

**AVOID**
- Neon magenta/cyan gradients, chrome futurism, perspective grids, sunset outrun skies (darksynth territory).
- Glitter, satin, sequins, platform boots, candy color, camp theatrical sparkle (glam rock).
- Motorcycles, chrome machine polish, open-road Americana, eagle-emblem club cuts as the subject (biker).
- Pastel palettes, bright daylight leisure, beach or café casualness, smiling snapshot warmth.
- Clean digital smoothness, glossy commercial pop lighting, corporate minimalism, crisp sans-serif type as the centerpiece.
- Solemn monochrome broken by unrelated rainbow color; black used only as a thin outline rather than as ground.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Biker:** both wear leather and studs, but biker imagery centers machines — chrome, motorcycles, highway, club patches, warm brown leather, Americana sun. Metal centers music allegiance: band patches and logotypes, instruments, corpse paint, occult iconography, black denim, and needs no vehicle at all.
- **Vs Black Metal:** black metal is a strict subset — mandatory monochrome, corpse paint, raw winter forest or fog, xerox degradation, anti-relious symbols. Parent metal additionally admits color illustrated gore sleeves, sepia anatomical plates, studio group portraits in black shirts, and rot-green accents; if color or clean studio light appears, it is metal broadly, not black metal specifically.
- **Vs Darksynth:** darksynth is synthetic and retro-futurist — neon, chrome, VHS scanlines, night-city grids. Metal is organic and analog — grain, ink, denim, bone, forest; no neon, no futurism, no synthetic sheen.
- **Vs Death Metal:** death metal is the gore subset — anatomical and necrotic illustration, dripping illegible logotypes, rot green and blood red over bone fields. Parent metal also includes non-gore modes: patch vests, horns emblems, plain band portraits, doom funeral landscapes; death metal never leaves the mortuary, metal may.
- **Vs Glam Rock:** glam flaunts glitter, androgyny, satin, platforms and saturated color as spectacle. Metal's spectacle is darkness: unadorned black cloth, natural long hair, denim and studs, morbid rather than glamorous theater; where glam shines, metal corrodes.
