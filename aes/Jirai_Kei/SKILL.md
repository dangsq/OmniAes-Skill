# Jirai Kei (地雷系 / "Landmine Type")

Jirai Kei is a Japanese subculture of the 2020s, born in the nightlife of Kabukichō, Tokyo, that wraps mental illness, addiction and romantic obsession in hyperfeminine kawaii packaging. The name means "landmine type": a girl who looks sweet and dainty but is stereotyped as a ticking time bomb — volatile, obsessive, self-destructive. The aesthetic lives on that contradiction: frilled blouses and Sanrio plushies posed against box cutters, pill capsules, glued-together energy drink cans and rain-slick neon streets. It is fashion as warning label, and subversion is its core value.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette: the two-color discipline
The defining rule is restriction, not hue: a coordinate typically contains only two colors. Neutrals are black and white; the supporting color is muted or pastel pink for girly looks, grey or a stronger dark tone for edgier ones. Work within these ranges:

- Black: #0E0E12 – #1C1A20 (dominant neutral; skirts, ribbons, cardigans, knee socks)
- Off-white / filter-white: #F4F0F3 – #FBF7F9 (blouses, bedding, overexposed backgrounds)
- Muted pink family: pale filter blush #F7E3EB, dusty rose #D9AABB, deep berry #B0768C (blouses, backpacks, plushies, haze)
- Grey: #A8A2AB – #6E6A72 (skirts, hoodies, edgier variants)
- Accent red: #D8001E – #FF2D55 (neon signage, nail polish, small ribbons; never a main outfit color)
- Rare cool blue as a minor accent only

Night scenes add saturated red-magenta neon glow bleeding into black; daytime and flat-lay scenes dissolve into a pinkish-white haze. Any coordinate using three or more competing colors has left the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the culture documents them
1. **Ribbons, bows, lace, ruffles, Peter Pan collars** — the fashion skeleton; satin bows at the collar, in the hair, on shoes and bags.
2. **Heart-shaped hardware and crosses** — heart buckles on corset belts, heart charms on bag straps, cross pendants, faux-pearl and rhinestone brooches.
3. **Sanrio characters, especially Kuromi and My Melody** — plushies, backpacks, pencil pouches, phone cases, plushies dressed in miniature coordinates.
4. **Energy drinks, alcohol and cigarettes** — color-scheme-matched cans (Monster, Strong Zero), often with a straw inserted to protect lipstick; cans stacked and glued into machine-gun shapes; cans as "dinner."
5. **Medical imagery** — pills, capsules, syringes, blister packs, coffin-shaped cases; stylized prop still-lifes rather than explicit injury.
6. **Fake weapons** — box cutters, razors, pocket knives, fake guns held or laid among lace and ribbons.
7. **Pleading-face ("pien") emoji, crying emojis, manga speech bubbles and text stickers** overlaid on photos; faces hidden behind emoji or bars.
8. **Night neon signage** — vertical Kabukichō signs, convenience-store glow, host-club lightboxes.
9. **Brand and luxury bags** — logo-coated canvas backpacks, luxury handbags signaling host-club spending; ita-bags heavy with charms and merch.
10. **Plushie piles and merch hoards** — consumerism as coping, displayed en masse.

### Materials and textures
Synthetic and fast-fashion by nature: polyester satin ribbons with a hard sheen, chiffon and lace ruffles, faux pearl and rhinestone clusters, patent leather Mary Janes and platform shoes, coated canvas backpacks, plush fleece, long acrylic nails with charms, aluminum cans, foil blister packs, lace curtains and cozy bedding in interior shots.

### Recurring subjects
Young women in coordinate outfits (twin tails, hime-cut bangs); flat-lay outfit grids arranged on fabric; bedrooms in pink and white with ruffles, cosmetics, lace curtains, sometimes anime figures or gaming setups; crouching or loitering poses on night streets; hands holding cans, knives or syringe-charms; plushie still-lifes; illustrated coordinate guides and bag-contents diagrams.

### Composition and lighting
Two lighting regimes dominate. (a) Hazy high-key: overexposed pale-pink/white wash, soft flash, low contrast, items or bodies floating in blush fog. (b) Neon night: deep black shadows, harsh on-camera flash or phone flash, red-magenta signage blooming behind crouching figures. Composition favors flat-lay grids, mirror selfies with the face obscured by stickers, close-cropped prop hands, and full-body street shots from a low or crouching angle. Editing is heavy and intentional: anime-proportioned enlarged eyes, dark-circle lenses, pale smoothed skin, thinned elongated limbs, sticker and emoji overlays — the goal is eerie and puppy-eyed, not natural.

### Hair and makeup (the culture calls makeup its true definition)
Hime-cut bangs (straight fringe, chin-length side locks); black hair most common, with bleached-blonde or pink highlights as rebellion; twin tails (low, high, or half-up) with curled ends decorated by ribbons and jeweled bows; rectangle headdresses and charm bobby pins. Makeup: emphasized "namida bukuro" tear bags with pink undereye blush for a sickly, about-to-cry look; downturned eyeliner and straight, slightly downturned brows for puppy sadness; optional "jirai line" drawn lower lash line with the real lash line left nude; long spiky fake lashes; dark-circle contact lenses; pink blush and sparkly highlighter high on the cheekbones; glossy or matte pink lips.

## Mood & Values

Subversion is the key value: sweetness weaponized. The mood is cute-yet-menacing, melancholic, deadpan, deliberately eerie — a pleading "pien" sadness paired with implied volatility. Beneath it sits loneliness, escapism (binge drinking, clubbing, shopping, host clubs, fandom/"bangya" devotion), and, importantly, community: the subculture gives its members solace and a hobby. Consumerism is both practice and meme — brand-specific coords, merch piles, ita-bags. The aesthetic romanticizes self-destruction as gimmick; treat its darker props as stylized symbols, never as glorified real harm.

## Era & Origins

2020s, Kabukichō, Tokyo. The term derives from "jirai onna" ("landmine woman"), a derogatory label for women with mood disorders; it went viral through the March 2020 "psycho girlfriend" dress-up challenge during COVID restrictions, entwined with the Toyoko Kids runaway scene of Kabukichō. The look evolved out of Japanese Girly fashion (its darker "Dark Girly / Sweet Girly" variant), later absorbing Goth Punk, Jersey Maid and other alternative substyles under the same umbrella (subgenre: Subcul Kei). Brands such as Ank Rouge, Liz Lisa, MA*RS, Noemie, Rojita, Travas Tokyo, Maison de Fleur, MCM and Vivienne Westwood supply it; media like *NEEDY STREAMER OVERLOAD* and *Ashita, Watashi wa Dareka no Kanojo* depict it.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Enforce the two-color coordinate: one neutral (black/white) plus one support (muted pink, grey, or a dark accent).
- Always stage a contradiction: dainty femininity against a menacing or medical prop, or against a night street.
- Choose one lighting regime: hazy pink-white high-key, or black night with red-magenta neon and flash.
- Apply the processed look: pale smoothed skin, oversized glossy eyes, sticker/emoji/speech-bubble overlays, obscured faces acceptable.
- Dress the hair: hime-cut bangs, twin tails with satin bows; makeup with pink undereye tear-bag blush and downturned liner.
- Props speak the dialect: color-matched cans with straws, Kuromi/My Melody plushies, heart buckles, box cutters and capsules as still-life objects.

**Variation axes (what may change)**
- *Subject*: fashion portrait, flat-lay coordinate grid, bag-contents diagram, bedroom interior, plushie still-life, prop still-life (cans, pills, knives), night street scene, illustrated coordinate guide.
- *Composition*: grid flat-lay, mirror selfie, crouching street pose, close-up hands, pile/hoard arrangement, collage with text.
- *Lighting*: overexposed blush haze; dim room with pink lamp; neon night with flash; rare cool daylight kept washed-out.
- *Color handling*: pink-dominant "sweet girly," black-dominant "dark girly," grey/edgy variants; red only as accent or signage.
- *Mood*: playful menace, melancholic pleading, deadpan eerie, maximalist consumerist joy.
- *Medium*: heavily edited photo, clean product-style flat-lay, anime-style illustration.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — palette discipline, cute/dark juxtaposition, processed surface — not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Coordinates with three or more competing colors; rainbow or candy-multicolor layering.
- Warm beige, brown, boho or cottagecore naturals; linen and wool textures; gold hardware instead of silver/pearl/heart hardware.
- Tan gyaru skin, white eyeshadow, oversized loose socks of gyaru fashion.
- Grunge denim, distressed flannel, chain belts, rainbow hair streaks and LED-strip gamer rooms (E-Girl territory).
- Mint, lavender, baby-blue pastel medical graphics as the whole palette (Yami Kawaii territory).
- Purple-dominant Kuromi-only merchandise scenes without a coordinate (Kuromicore territory).
- Clean, unfiltered commercial photography with natural skin texture and no overlays.
- Daytime nature settings, offices, minimalist Scandinavian interiors.
- Explicit depiction of injury or real self-harm; keep weapons and pills as stylized props.

## Discrimination Cues

- **E-Girl**: Western, grunge/skater-derived; graphic tees, chain belts, colorful hair streaks, nose-blush, LED-lit bedrooms. Jirai Kei keeps a restricted two-color ultra-feminine coordinate, Kabukichō neon or pink haze, Sanrio plushies and medical props.
- **Girly Kei / Larme-adjacent girly fashion**: same ruffles and femininity but no dark undercurrent — no weapons, pills, cans or night streets; warmer, mature, soft-glam palette and unedited glamour. Jirai adds the menace and the eerie editing.
- **Kuromicore**: character-core built entirely on Kuromi in purple/black, playful and cartoonish. Jirai uses Kuromi as one accessory inside a fashion coordinate and nightlife context.
- **PEEPS**: shares Japanese street-snap DNA but leans brighter Y2K/gyaru-revival candy tones and retro flash; the wiki documents it only as related, so judge by Jirai's palette restriction, night-neon setting and medical/violent prop layer, which PEEPS lacks.
- **Yami Kawaii**: shares pills, syringes and sick-cute tone, but sits in pastel mint/lavender/baby-blue with graphic "menhera" prints and decora-like layering; Jirai is black-anchored, fashion-coordinate-first, tied to Kabukichō nights, host-club bags and energy-drink culture.

**Quick recognition check**: two-color black/pink/white coordinate + ribbons and heart hardware + twin tails with tear-bag makeup + one menacing or medical prop or neon night backdrop + hazy/edited surface = Jirai Kei.
