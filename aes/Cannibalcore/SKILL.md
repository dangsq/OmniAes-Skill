# Cannibalcore

Cannibalcore treats consumption — eating, being eaten, offering a piece of yourself — as the truest vocabulary of love. Born on Tumblr around 2020 from the analytical revival of *Hannibal* (2013), it reads murder-as-devotion, appetite-as-obsession, and sacrifice-as-proof of feeling. It is fundamentally metaphorical: nobody in this aesthetic actually eats anyone. What you see instead is a love letter written in meat — gore that is optional, restrained, and always paired with a token of tenderness (lace, a valentine, a poem, a pie). The emotional register is saccharine menace: sweet surfaces (blush pink, sailor collars, dessert, handwriting) carrying predatory confessions ("I beg you, eat me up," "I love you just a little too much," "a kiss is the beginning of cannibalism").

## Visual Identity

### Color palette: one red axis
Every color in Cannibalcore plots onto a single hue axis stretched from pale to black: **bone/cream** (#EFE8DC, #E6DCCB) → **blush and flesh pink** (#F2D3D3, #E4A9AD, #D98C93) → **cherry/arterial red** (#B3202C, #8E0F1E) → **oxblood/maroon** (#4A0D12, #2E080A) → **black** (#0A0A0C, #1A1416). A given image anchors on one or two stations of this axis and uses the rest as accents: a pastel-pink diagram with cherry line-work, a black night interior with one oxblood stain, a bone-white cup with a maroon drip. Vintage variants drift sepia-desaturated; painterly variants warm the flesh tones toward umber. Off-axis color is rare and deliberate: a deep teal cinematic night grade, a sage-green skirt, an acid-green outline on a saturated red field — always a complementary ground chosen to make the red read louder, never a palette of its own.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)
1. **Blood** — stains, smears, drips and soaks on pale fabric, porcelain, skin, paper. Rarely pooled splatter; usually absorbed, wiped, or trailing.
2. **Meat and flesh** — raw cuts, glistening muscle, and the butcher's-chart device: a human silhouette divided into labeled meat cuts on flat pastel pink.
3. **Anatomically correct hearts** — held in hands, painted, offered, or spelled out in crust letters on a pie; the aesthetic's central valentine.
4. **Teeth and mouths** — bared teeth, smeared red lips, bite marks, open mouths mid-confession; "bare your teeth / go for the throat" as handwriting.
5. **Red wine** — glasses, decanters, a poured dark liquid standing in for blood at the table.
6. **Cutlery and utensils, especially knives** — laid beside a plate, glinting at a candlelit supper, held loosely rather than swung.
7. **Bones and severed body parts** — restrained and often collaged: an arm in a clamp, a hand offered on a dish; implication over autopsy.
8. **Love-related imagery** — embraces, foreheads touching, intertwined fingers, hands at a throat or waist, kisses, valentines, confessional second-person text.
9. **The table as love letter** — vanitas still lifes and food photography where the meal is the message: pie, cup, plate of meat, wine glass arranged like an offering.

The wiki also names obsession, sex and ownership of the body as conceptual motifs; visually these stay understated — bared shoulders, lace, a grip on a wrist or throat, a choker — rather than explicit.

### Materials and textures
Loose impasto oil paint with visible dragging brushstrokes; watercolor and ink bleeding into cream paper; grainy, soft-focus cinematic film; sepia vintage photograph and collage paper with torn edges; porcelain, lace, linen and satin; wax candlelight on glass and meat; handwritten scrawl in pink or red ink; clean serif type on plain card stock; flat diagrammatic vector line on pastel ground. Surfaces feel handled — smeared, soaked, bitten, pressed — even when the image is typographic.

### Recurring subjects
Lovers entwined or consuming each other; a solitary devotee offering or withholding themselves; the pale feminine figure as either the consumed or, in the revenge reading (*Jennifer's Body*), the consumer; still-life vanitas tables; desserts and meals carrying text; anatomical specimens treated as relics; diagrams of the body as meat; quote cards and poem cards as standalone images.

### Composition and lighting
Intimacy of scale: tight crops on mouths, hands, hearts, a cup, a plate — the frame close enough to feel breath. Centered typographic cards with generous margins. Flat, labeled diagram layouts. Baroque vanitas table arrangements in deep shadow. Candlelit chiaroscuro with a single warm source; cold cinematic night grades (teal-black shadows, pale lit skin, one red soak); flat even light for diagrams, cards and food; soft window light in painterly interiors. Multi-panel collages and quote-superimposed edits ("web-weaves") are a native format, inherited from Tumblr photo-set culture.

## Mood & Values

Devouring tenderness. The aesthetic confesses a love so total it wants to ingest its object, and a devotion so total it offers its own flesh: sacrifice, codependency, the shadow side of merger. It values metaphor over shock, beauty over disgust, confession over narration. A feminist current runs through it — the woman consumed by roles and appetites turning consumer, the "Good for Her" revenge plate — so the same blood on lace can read as wound or as trophy. Underneath everything: the conviction that being eaten and being loved are the same sentence.

## Era & Origins

Tumblr, circa 2020, before and after the pandemic, when *Hannibal* (2013) was re-read through literary and film analysis rather than pure fandom, popularizing cannibalism-as-romance via photo sets, quote edits, collages and fanfiction. Its visual ancestry reaches back through the horror canon — *Texas Chainsaw Massacre* (1974), *Silence of the Lambs*, *Ravenous*, *Jennifer's Body* (2009), *Raw*, *Bones and All*, *Fresh*, *Yellowjackets*, *Tokyo Ghoul* — and its tone is tuned by a dark-pop playlist (Hozier, Kesha, Melanie Martinez, Jazmin Bean) of sweet voices singing appetite.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Metaphor first: every gruesome element must read as an act of love, devotion or reclaiming — never as accident, crime or torture.
2. Pair each wound with a tenderness token: blood needs lace, a heart needs a hand holding it, a knife needs a table set for two.
3. Keep gore optional and soft. Stain, smear, imply. The aesthetic explicitly does not require extreme gore.
4. Speak in the second person or the confessional first person; text, when present, is a vow or a hunger, set in serif or handwriting.
5. Commit to one medium register per image (impasto paint, film grain, watercolor, collage, diagram, type card) and keep its textures consistent.
6. Light as ritual: candle flame, cold night, or flat archival evenness — never neutral documentary daylight.
7. Stay on the red axis; use off-axis color only as a complementary ground that intensifies red.

**Variation axes.** Subject: lovers, solitary devotee, avenging consumer, still life, dessert, diagram, type card, collage. Composition: extreme close crop, centered card, flat diagram, baroque table, cinematic medium portrait, multi-panel weave. Lighting: chiaroscuro candle, teal-black night, flat even, soft window. Color handling: pink-dominant, red-dominant, black-dominant, or bone-dominant with red accent. Gore level: none (wine, lace, quote only) → implied (stains, bites, smears) → moderate (severed part as collage object, heart as specimen). Mood: devotional, possessive, vengeful, melancholic. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a diagram of a body as pastry cuts, a watercolor of two hands only, or a black card with one serif couplet are all equally valid Cannibalcore.

**Avoid**
- Extreme, forensic, disgust-engineered gore: evisceration close-ups, splatter piles, autopsy realism.
- Shock without romance: gore with no love token or confessional frame (drifts to Gorecore/Horror).
- Industrial butcher-shop realism — plastic wrap, meat lockers, slab photography — with no human body metaphor (Meatcore).
- Anime kawaii register: meme-menace schoolgirls, cartoon splat, big-eye jealousy (Yandere).
- Threat POV: monsters, chase framing, jump-scare lighting, victim-fleeing composition (Horror).
- Off-axis palettes: neon cyber cyan/magenta, boho terracotta-sage wholesomeness, primary pop colors.
- Glossy commercial product photography or clean brand/UI design; the graphic voice is handmade — scrawl, serif card, xerox zine.
- Clinical medical-illustration context (blue-white diagrams, labels in sans-serif medical style); the anatomical heart here is a relic, not a textbook figure.
- Literal endorsement of violence: crime-scene staging, real-world weapon branding; the feast must stay metaphorical and periodless.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Its tell | Cannibalcore's difference |
|---|---|---|
| Gorecore | Disgust is the point; explicit wounds, harsh flash or desaturated grime, no narrative of affection | Gore optional, soft, and yoked to love iconography and confessional text |
| Meatcore | Meat as material study: slabs, wrap, butcher realism, no human story | Meat always stands for a body and a relationship: hearts as valentines, butcher charts of human silhouettes, meat on a candlelit table for two |
| Horror | Fear, threat, monster, dread composition, victim POV | Desire POV: intimate, consensual-feeling, devotional; the "victim" offers themselves |
| Yandere | Anime kawaii surface, jealous violence, pastel-plus-splat meme energy | Painterly/literary/cinematic register; violence as consumption and communion, text as poetry rather than menace |

Where evidence is thin, say so: the aesthetic's conceptual motifs of sex and bodily ownership appear visually only as restraint-level intimacy — a grip, a bared shoulder, a choker — and the green-accent zine variant is a minority dialect, not the core voice.
