# Vectordelia

Vectordelia is the peak of "humanist maximalism" in computer-aided graphic design: the look of mid-2000s to early-2010s club flyers, CD covers, iPod silhouette commercials, Xbox 360 dashboards, Just Dance menus, laptop lids, toy packaging and school yearbooks. It is vector software (Adobe Illustrator CS2/CS3-era tooling) pushed to ornamental excess — flat, non-photorealistic shapes with crisp, geometrically neat outlines, stacked into dense layered arrangements that flow outward from a focal point and gain complexity as they grow. Researcher Evan Collins named it Vectordelia, framing it as a digital evolution of 1960s "Corporate Hippie" psychedelia (Peter Max) rebuilt with glossy gradient tools; the online alias "Frutiger Metro" is a retrospective misnomer, since the style predates and contradicts Microsoft's minimalist Metro language. Where Frutiger Aero renders skeuomorphic glass and water, Vectordelia stays deliberately flat-but-elaborate: sheen without realism, chaos without mess.

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** A high-contrast CMYK-plus set: cyan (roughly #00AEEF–#00C8FF), magenta/hot pink (#EC008C–#FF2E91), lime green (#8DC63F–#76FF03), orange (#F7941E–#FF9900), yellow (#FFDE00), against black (#000000–#141414) or white/near-white grounds. The ground is monochrome by rule — solid black, solid white, or a single-hue gradient — never a depicted scene. Two licensed poles vary this: a dark pole where saturated shapes glow self-luminously off black, and a soft pole (the Vector Música / Vectorgarden end) of pastel gradients — baby pink, lilac, mint, sky — with white flourishes and sparkling bokeh. Hot pink may dominate entirely in the feminine sub-aesthetics. Saturation stays high at both poles; mud and mid-tone greys appear only as grunge texture, never as the palette's center of gravity.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.** First tier: swirling vines and calligraphic flourishes; ink splats and splatter clusters; solid silhouettes of dancers, crowds and jumping figures; flowing gradient blocks and ribbons; striped or concentric circles; halftone dot fields; stars (four-point glints and five-point bursts); sunburst ray fans; arrow clusters. Second tier, the music cluster: speakers, speaker cones, headphones, turntables, DJ mixers, microphones, music notes and staff lines. Third tier, the nature cluster: abstract flowers, butterflies, birds, bubbles and aurora arcs, strongest at the feminine pole. Grunge variants add black blots, drips and distressed overlays; rave variants add zebra print and electric auroras.

**Materials, texture, medium.** Everything reads as vector output: smooth Bézier curves, clean closed paths, no hand-drawn wobble, no brush grain. Fills are flat color or smooth linear/radial gradients; gloss is a gradient sheen plus a white specular crescent on spheres, bubbles and badges, not refractive glass. Translucent alpha overlaps let shapes tint each other. Halftone dots, checker dots and dot-grid fades supply texture; splats have crisp vector edges even when they imitate paint. Photographic elements, when present, are flattened into duotone cutouts or silhouettes and collaged as just another layer. Sparkle crosses and bokeh discs stand in for light.

**Recurring subjects.** Dancing and partying humans in silhouette; DJs at turntables; crowds with raised arms; music hardware; club-night and compilation-cover layouts with bold display type; consumer-tech surfaces (MP3 players, laptop lids, console dashboards, CD discs and jewel cases); advertising spreads with cutout figures; flowers, butterflies and birds for the feminine pole; purely abstract ornament fields with no subject at all.

**Composition and lighting.** Maximalist by default: dozens of overlapping forms, edge and corner framing with flourishes, diagonal arrows driving motion, sunburst rays or dot bursts behind the focal point, and a radial logic where ornament erupts from a center or corner and complicates outward. A single solid silhouette often anchors the chaos. Lighting is non-naturalistic: glow comes from bokeh, sparkles and gradient luminance on the dark pole; the light pole uses airy pastel gradients and soft translucent veils. Hard drop shadows are rare; depth comes from layering and transparency, not perspective.

## Mood & Values

Optimism, dynamism, youth and party energy; "organized chaos" as a virtue — abundance that still resolves into clean geometry. Digital humanism is the core value: human joy (dancing, jumping, playing music) expressed through pure digital shape, sincere and pre-ironic, commercially friendly and aimed at teens and young adults. Even its grunge edge is decorative rebellion, never cynicism.

## Era & Origins

Emergent mid-2000s, peaking late 2000s through early 2010s in advertising (iPod silhouette spots, Cox and Windows XP campaigns), music packaging, broadcast idents (MTV, Nickelodeon, Cartoon Network Noods), game UIs (Just Dance, DJ Hero, de Blob, LittleBigPlanet, Persona) and kids' products. It inherits 1960s psychedelic corporate art and merges it with Illustrator-era gloss. It declined as brands pivoted to flat minimalism (Windows 8 Metro, iOS 7), and resurged in the 2020s through nostalgic edits and music videos. Sub-aesthetics map its range: Vectorfunk (party silhouettes), Vector-Electro (rave gear, hot pink), Vector Música (pastel bokeh elegance), Vectorgarden (floral feminine flourish), Vector Grunge (black splats, emo/scene overlap).

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
- Build only from vector grammar: crisp closed Bézier shapes, flat fills, smooth gradients, clean outlines. No painterly or photographic rendering.
- Lay a monochrome ground first — black, white, or one hue's gradient — then erupt ornament over it.
- Layer abundantly: flourishes, splats, stars, striped circles, halftone fields, sunburst rays, arrows. Aim for organized chaos, not sparse arrangement.
- Include human presence as solid single-color silhouettes (dancer, crowd, jumper, DJ) when the subject calls for people.
- Add gloss sparingly but visibly: gradient sheen, specular crescents, translucent overlaps, bokeh discs, sparkle glints.
- Drive composition radially or diagonally: burst from center or corner, frame edges with vines, point arrows through the frame.
- Set any type in bold display sans or beveled chrome lettering, integrated as a vector layer.

Variation axes: subject (music/party, tech product, floral-nature, animal, advertising, packaging, or pure abstraction); color pole (CMYK-on-black rave, CMYK-on-white pop, hot-pink feminine, pastel Vector Música, black-heavy grunge); composition (central burst, corner burst, all-over pattern, edge-framed panel); lighting (flat matte, glossy sheen, bokeh glow); mood (euphoric, whimsical-elegant, grungy-edged). A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid: photorealism and skeuomorphic glass/water with refraction or lens flare; hand-drawn wobbly lines, brush strokes, watercolor bleed; aerosol spray texture, wildstyle graffiti lettering, wall grain; film grain, paper texture, vintage print decay; muted earth-tone or desaturated palettes; minimalist flat-design layouts, grid tiles, generous negative space; meme imagery, impact fonts, deep-fried raster artifacts; fully rendered 3D CGI or photographic humans as the focal treatment; Victorian or ornate serif ornament.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Vectordelia |
|---|---|
| 2010s Meme Maximalism | Meme maximalism is raster, ironic and cluttered with internet imagery and joke fonts; Vectordelia is clean vector Bézier, sincere, ornament-driven, with no meme iconography. |
| Colorful Pop | Colorful Pop stays flat, simple and evenly candy-colored; Vectordelia adds gradients, gloss, halftone fields, splats, flourishes and black-or-white high-contrast grounds. |
| Frutiger Aero | Aero renders photoreal glass, water, sky and refraction with skeuomorphic depth; Vectordelia's sheen is a gradient on flat shapes, with opaque silhouettes and crisp outlines, never realistic material. |
| Graffiti Pop | Graffiti Pop shows hand-sprayed paint, drips, aerosol fuzz and wildstyle lettering on walls; Vectordelia's splats are geometrically neat vector paths and its type is clean display or chrome. |
| Groovival | Groovival revives 60s–70s psychedelia with warm earth tones, grain and retro type; Vectordelia shares the psychedelic ancestry but is digital-crisp, CMYK-bright and grain-free. |
