# Ukiyo-e (Pictures of the Floating World)

Ukiyo-e is the mass-produced visual culture of Edo (modern Tokyo), flourishing 1603–1868 and rooted in earlier Yamato-e court painting. Its name is an ironic blessing of transience: the "floating world" of kabuki theatres, teahouses and pleasure districts, recorded for townspeople rather than elites. A print was collaborative — publisher, artist's drawing, carver cutting one woodblock per colour, printer rubbing water-based ink into damp washi — and this production chain *is* the style: every visual rule below descends from what a carved block and a limited set of pigment plates can do. Masters: Hokusai, Hiroshige, Utamaro, Sharaku, Kuniyoshi; later revitalized as Shin-hanga (Hasui Kawase), which keeps the subjects but softens the light.

## Visual Identity

**Line and plane.** Form is carried by a confident, calligraphic black keyline of varying thickness; interior colour is flat and unshaded. There is no chiaroscuro, no cast shadow, no volumetric modeling. The only permitted modulation is *bokashi*: a soft wipe-gradation at a plane's edge (sky darkening upward, water deepening, snow-lit dusk), produced by wiping pigment on the block.

**Palette rule.** Behave as if 4–8 carved colour plates plus the paper itself. Pigment families, aged by time:

| Plate | Hex range | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Sumi black | #201d1a–#3a3630 | keylines, hair, timber, silhouettes |
| Prussian/indigo blue | #1e3f66–#4a7ba6; night #16222e–#2b3a4a | water, sky, wave bodies, dusk |
| Beni red / vermilion | #a63a2b–#c0503a; pale #d89a86 | kimono, seals, accents, lips |
| Malachite green | #5c7f5a–#7fa07a | robes, foliage |
| Ochre / yellow | #c8a24a–#e0c07a | garments, interiors, gold grounds |
| Muted purple | #5f5068–#6b5a72 | kimono, twilight |
| Paper beige | #e9dfc6–#d8c69a; gold leaf #cfa14a–#e3c078 | ground, sky, negative space |
| Reserved white | #f6f1e4 | snow, foam, faces, unpigmented paper |

Saturation is mineral, never neon; the beige paper counts as a colour and should breathe through large areas. Early works may sit on a gold-leaf ground; deluxe editions occasionally add mica flecks or blind embossing (rare, optional — the evidence here is thin).

**Texture and medium.** Washi fibre tooth, faint woodgrain showing through flat planes, slight plate misregistration at edges, matte water-based pigment, trimmed sheet with a title cartouche and artist seal in calligraphic script. Flatness must feel *printed*, not vector-sterile.

**Motifs, ranked by textual prominence.** 1) *Bijin-ga*: courtesans and geisha at leisure — kimono patterned with komon dots, kanoko shibori, floral crests; hair as fine parallel line bundles with ornamental kanzashi; sometimes cropped to a large-head close-up. 2) *Yakusha-e*: kabuki actors in mie poses, dramatic gesture, patterned stage robes. 3) *Fūkei-ga*: landscapes and travel — waves with claw-fingered foam, Mount Fuji or a castle as a tiny distant anchor, snow villages, rain, bridges, teahouse rows, pine and plum. 4) History and folklore: witches, skeletons, warriors, spectres in theatrical confrontation. 5) Sumo wrestlers. 6) Flora and fauna (birds-and-flowers). 7) Shunga erotica and street-life genre scenes (festivals, interiors, entertainments).

**Composition.** Asymmetry as law: masses pushed off-centre, a looming foreground diagonal (wave, ribcage, roofline, utility pole) against a small distant motif; oblique projection looking down into streets, rooms and canals; high, low or absent horizon; figures cropped by the sheet edge; generous negative space. Formats vary: vertical ōban portrait, horizontal landscape, multi-sheet triptych whose image runs across seams, wide screen/handscroll frieze on gold ground.

**Lighting.** Default: none — local colour and convention, not observed light. Time and weather are coded: indigo bokashi dusk, deep night blue with reserved-white snow, snow as unpigmented paper plus scattered flake dots, rain as fine parallel streaks. A warm lamp-glow in a window belongs only to the Shin-hanga edge of the style.

## Mood & Values

Celebration of transient pleasure: theatricality, wit, urban pride, fashion as news, and a quiet, attentive reverence for season and weather — snow falling on a village, a wave cresting mid-swell. Even the grotesque (skeletons, witches) is staged with decorative poise. The mood floats between lyrical calm and kabuki drama, never moralizing, never permanent.

## Era & Origins

Emergent in 17th-century Edo from Yamato-e roots, peaking 18th–19th centuries as cheap collectible prints for commoners and samurai alike; declining after the 1860s under photography and modernization, then exported to Europe where it ignited Japonisme and fed Impressionism and Post-Impressionism. 20th-century Shin-hanga revived its subjects with European atmospheric light; Sousaku-hanga broke from it entirely toward modernist abstraction.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:
1. Draw with the knife: every shape bounded by a decisive sumi contour; contour carries form, shading never does.
2. Colour as plate: flat unmodulated areas; gradation only as bokashi wipes at plane edges.
3. Budget your blocks: 4–8 pigment families plus paper; repeat hues across the sheet for print coherence.
4. Compose asymmetrically with oblique projection; let one diagonal dominate and one motif stay tiny and distant.
5. Leave the paper working: negative space, reserved white for snow/foam/skin highlights.
6. Pattern inside pattern: textile designs, foam fingers, flake dots, roof tiles — ornament confined to surfaces and nature, never to borders.
7. End with print artifacts: washi tooth, slight misregistration, cartouche and seal, sheet format.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any floating-world or natural subject — a beauty, an actor, a wrestler, a ghost tale, a bird on a branch, a snowbound street, a storm sea, a festival crowd. Composition: vertical portrait, horizontal vista, triptych spread, frieze on gold ground, close-up head crop, bird's-eye street view. Lighting: flat convention, bokashi dusk, indigo night, snow luminosity, or (Shin-hanga edge) a soft atmospheric glow. Colour: from near-monochrome sumi with one red accent, through muted aged palettes, to bold Prussian/beni contrast, to early gold-leaf grounds. Mood: serene, theatrical, eerie, satirical. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid:** Western single-point perspective and centered symmetry; volumetric shading, cast shadows, chiaroscuro, glossy 3D rendering; photographic depth of field or photorealism; neon or unlimited digital palettes and smooth airbrush gradients; visible painterly impasto or broken brushstrokes; anime/manga eyes and pop-mascot iconography; ornamental whiplash borders; anachronistic clutter (cars, signage, skyscrapers) unless deliberately working the early-20th-century Shin-hanga edge, where a lone utility pole or wire is permissible; sterile vector flatness lacking paper tooth and keyline variation.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Japonisme:** European hands borrowing Japanese props — oil or lithograph medium, modeled faces, retained perspective and shadow beneath the kimono. Ukiyo-e never models form and always thinks in carved plates.
- **Superflat:** shares flatness but is digital-smooth, neon-saturated, anime-derived and consumer-ironic; lacks calligraphic keyline, mineral palette, paper age and Edo subject matter.
- **Art Nouveau:** line as sinuous ornament everywhere, gilded decorative borders, Byzantine mosaic richness, European decadence; ukiyo-e line describes form, ornament lives only in textiles and nature, composition is asymmetric rather than curvilinear.
- **Impressionism:** outline dissolved into broken brushwork and optical colour mixing, light observed plein-air; ukiyo-e keeps contour, flat local colour, and light as convention.
- **Post-Impressionism:** expressive impasto and structural colour (Van Gogh's swirls) yet still Western modeling and space; ukiyo-e remains linear, planar, printed.
- **Internal neighbours:** Shin-hanga keeps ukiyo-e subjects but adds soft European atmosphere and glow (acceptable edge); Sousaku-hanga's abstraction and personal-expression monochrome is outside the style; Yamato-e ancestor shows gold cloud bands and courtly narrative without the urban pop energy and bold print keyline.
