# Cluttercore

Cluttercore is an interior-design aesthetic built on a philosophy of "organized chaos": the artful, abundant display of personal belongings that hold sentimental value and narrate the inhabitant's life. It is a form of curated maximalism coined on TikTok in 2020 by @mDugy, which surged in popularity as people spent pandemic years at home and wanted spaces that felt personal, comforting and authentic. It stands in direct opposition to minimalism: blank walls are rare, every surface — walls, shelves, tables, mantels, stair risers — is treated as a canvas for display. Crucially, it is not hoarding and not mess; the abundance is deliberate, grouped and cherished, an "emotional security blanket" or "literal cocoon" made of objects.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)

The defining color law is **saturation and variety, never a unified scheme**. Any hue is permitted, and several usually appear at once; rainbow logic recurs (color-coded book spines, neon rainbows, prism-like accent runs). A typical room grounds itself in warm woods and soft plaster neutrals, then stacks saturated accents from across the wheel on top:

| Role | Range |
|---|---|
| Grounding woods & neutrals | honey oak `#C89A5B`, walnut `#6B4A2F`, cream plaster `#F4EBDA` |
| Saturated accents (use 4+ hues simultaneously) | magenta `#E23E9C`, violet `#7C4DFF`, teal `#1FA8A0`, mustard `#E9B33C`, tomato `#C63D2F`, leaf green `#3F7D3B` |
| Light tints | tungsten amber `#FFB347`, fairy-light warm white `#FFE9C4`, pink LED wash `#FF5EC4`, violet wash `#8A5CF9` |

Pastel kitsch variants (pink-painted cabinets, heart decals, toy kitchens) sit inside the range as long as density and variety hold. What is forbidden chromatically is restraint: a two-tone or tonal designer palette reads as a different aesthetic.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how strongly the style documents them

1. **Curated collections in quantity** — the heart of the style: books, vintage cameras, figurines, dolls, plush toys, ceramics, jars, displayed as groups, not single tokens.
2. **Overflowing bookshelves** — shelves packed past capacity, often with spines color-sorted into rainbow runs, stacked horizontally as well as vertically.
3. **Gallery walls** — dense grids and clusters of framed art, personal photographs, postcards, prints, mirrors; blank wall space is treated as failure.
4. **Plants** — potted, trailing, hanging; monstera and pothos masses softening the object density.
5. **Layered textiles and patterns** — velvet against wool, patchwork quilts, floral rugs, draped fabric, mismatched cushions.
6. **Eclectic mismatched furniture** — antique, modern and thrifted pieces coexisting; no matching sets.
7. **Sentimental objects** — travel souvenirs, family heirlooms, childhood toys, handwritten notes, shadowbox compartments of small treasured things.
8. **Small warm light sources as decor** — lampshades, string/fairy lights, neon signs woven through the clutter.

### Materials and textures

Wood shelving and ladders, rattan and wicker (pendant lamps, baskets), ceramic and glass jars, plush fur and wool knits, velvet upholstery, paper in bulk (book blocks, prints, postcards), brass clock faces, plastic toys, neon tube, dried flowers. Texture is layered deliberately: hard ceramic beside soft plush, glossy glass beside matte paper.

### Recurring subjects

Domestic interiors: bedrooms, studies and home libraries (sometimes shop-dense, with staircases between shelves), sunrooms and conservatories, craft and hobby rooms, kitchens, attic studios, play corners. Equally valid is the close vignette: a shelf altar of dolls, figurines and framed photos filling the frame edge to edge.

### Composition tendencies

Fill from floor to ceiling; stack vertically; compartmentalize within grids (shadowboxes, cubby units, drawer banks) so the chaos reads as curated. Cluster like with like, or by color. Layer depth: foreground objects partially occlude mid-ground shelves, implying the collection continues beyond the frame. Cropping is often tight and slightly cramped. There is no empty focal void; the eye is meant to travel cluster to cluster.

### Lighting tendencies

Multiple small warm sources instead of one even overhead light: pooled tungsten lamplight through fabric shades, strands of fairy lights, neon accents. Two accepted alternates: bright diffuse daylight filtering through foliage in plant-filled sunroom variants, and saturated colored LED washes (pink, violet) over bedroom clutter. Close collection shots may use direct flash for an intimate, snapshot feel. Shadow pockets and glow points coexist; uniform studio lighting never appears.

## Mood & Values

Comfort, safety, nostalgia, authenticity, individuality, storytelling. The room functions as a cocoon and an emotional security blanket; objects are valued for memory, not money or trend. The inhabitant is the curator of their own life, celebrating personal history over conformity. Joy is the test: the abundance must feel cherished and deliberate, which is exactly what separates it from hoarding's distress.

## Era & Origins

Coined 2020 on TikTok (@mDugy); popularized through the COVID-19 pandemic as home time increased. It is a direct reaction against stark, impersonal minimalist design and the sterile showroom ideal of the home. It belongs to the maximalist family but with different intent: traditional maximalism seeks bold opulence through a cohesive designed scheme, while cluttercore seeks personal expression and emotional resonance, producing a look that feels intimate, lived-in and organically evolved rather than designed at once.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Fill every surface; treat blank wall or empty tabletop as an error to correct.
- Make curation visible: group by type, by color, or in compartment grids; "organized chaos," never scatter.
- Let collections, not single objects, be the subject: quantity is the message.
- Layer: textiles over furniture, frames over frames, plants over shelves, lights through everything.
- Mismatch furniture openly; mix antique, modern, thrifted.
- Light with many small warm or colored sources; allow glow points and dim corners.
- Embed narrative: photographs, postcards, souvenirs, heirlooms, handwritten scraps.

**Variation axes (a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image)**

- *Subject*: bedroom, library, sunroom, kitchen, craft room, attic studio, play corner, or a tight shelf-altar still life.
- *Composition*: wide room view, corner vignette, or edge-to-edge close-up of shelving; cramped crops are fine.
- *Lighting*: daylight through plants, warm tungsten pools, fairy-light strands, neon, saturated pink/violet LED wash, or flash-lit intimacy.
- *Color*: any hue family, including pastel kitsch, provided saturation and multi-hue variety persist; rainbow color-coding optional.
- *Density*: from moderately filled shelf-walls to attic-level saturation, as long as no surface reads as intentionally empty.
- *Object era*: vintage dolls and cameras through contemporary toys and prints.

**Avoid**

- Minimalist negative space, bare walls, empty surfaces, sparse "one shelf" coziness.
- Matching furniture sets, uniform built-in cabinetry, flat-pack coherence.
- A single cohesive designer palette or opulent luxury scheme (that is maximalism).
- Genuine mess: trash, spills, grime, distressed disorder — hoarding cues break the style.
- Sterile real-estate staging or showroom emptiness.
- Outdoor pastoral scenes as the subject; cluttercore is domestic interior (a plant-packed sunroom or porch counts as interior).
- Nature-found objects only (bones, mushrooms, moss) with no sentimental personal objects — that drifts to goblincore.
- Even, shadowless studio lighting.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Maximalism**: maximalism is a deliberately designed, cohesive scheme of rich color and luxurious materials aiming for opulence; cluttercore is organically evolved, mismatched, sentimental, and prioritizes emotional story over aesthetic harmony. Test: does the room look designed at once (maximalism) or collected over years (cluttercore)?
- **Vs Cottagecore**: cottagecore is pastoral and romantic-rural — muted natural palette, handmade crafts, floral dresses, fields and farms; cluttercore is interior-domestic, saturated, collection-driven, and urban-compatible.
- **Vs Goblincore**: goblincore hoards nature-found objects (bones, stones, mushrooms, moss) in earthy muddy tones, often outdoors or in dirt-floor dens; cluttercore's hoard is sentimental consumer and personal objects (books, dolls, photos, plants) in warm, saturated domestic interiors.
- **Vs Grandmacore**: grandmacore is vintage domestic softness — crochet, doilies, floral teacups, cream and pastel nostalgia in a tidy grandmotherly kitchen; cluttercore is denser, more individualistic, more saturated, and centered on personal collections rather than inherited domestic craft alone.
- **Vs Cacareco Girl**: the closest sibling, documented mainly as a related aesthetic; evidence here is thin. Treat the split as emphasis: Cacareco Girl leans into kitschy everyday trinkets and knick-knack accumulation, while cluttercore foregrounds curated sentimental collections, gallery walls and storytelling display with visible organization.
- **Quick tell**: if shelves overflow but everything is color-sorted, grouped, and lit by warm lamps or fairy lights, and the walls carry photographs and art in dense clusters — it is cluttercore.
