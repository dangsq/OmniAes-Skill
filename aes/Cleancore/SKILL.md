# Cleancore

Cleancore is an aesthetic organized around clean objects and recently cleaned environments: antibacterial soaps, sanitizing sprays, UV lights, antiseptic creams, suds, bathwater, tile, and the packaging of household hygiene brands. It borrows its glossy commercial sheen from Frutiger Aero-era detergent advertising, but redirects that sheen toward domestic sterilization and the emotional management of contamination anxiety. The style lives online as Tumblr/TikTok edits and "stim boards" — collages of cut-out product PNGs, bubbles, taps, bathtubs and blue skies assembled to feel clear, fresh, sterile and slightly surreal. It carries two registers: **High Cleancore**, mature and sterile, built on commercial cleaners, hand sanitizer, latex gloves, steam cleaners and PPE, embracing capitalist brand imagery with a cynical wink and overlapping Medicalcore; and **Low Cleancore**, a kidcore twist on the same vocabulary — rubber duckies, bath sponges, baby shampoo, powder pouffes, shower caps, Sanrio-adjacent kawaii mascots and pastel stickers. Both registers share one promise: a dreamy, flawless, sanitary world where nothing is dirty and nothing can hurt you.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette
The rule is cool, wet and high-key. White or porcelain white dominates the frame; pale blue and mint/teal carry the mid-tones; a pastel accent set (pink, lavender, lemon) supplies warmth without heat; saturated vivid colors appear only as *contained* accents — a liquid, a label, a toy, a trigger head — never as ambient light or atmosphere.

- Porcelain white: `#FFFFFF` – `#F2F7FA`
- Pale blue: `#CFE6F5` – `#A9D3EA`
- Mint / teal: `#BFE9DE` – `#59C9C0`, deepening to pool teal around `#1FA8A0` in water shots
- Pastel accents: pink `#FAD5E3`, lavender `#DCD2F0`, lemon `#FCEFB8`
- Vivid contained accents: hot pink `#F0509C`, cyan `#2AB7E9`, bubblegum `#FF9EC4`

An image reads cleancore when its overall temperature is cool and its value key is high. A minority of water and wet-tile subjects dip into deeper teal or shadowed tile; this is acceptable variation as long as the surface still reads wet, wiped and clean rather than moody.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)
1. **Cleaning and sanitizing products**: trigger spray bottles, detergent jugs, hand sanitizer, antimicrobial soap, shampoo / conditioner / body wash bottles, deodorant, humidifiers.
2. **Bubbles, foam and suds**, including foam treated as a drawable surface (smileys, shapes traced in suds).
3. **Clean water**: bathwater, pool water, tap streams, rippling surfaces with sun caustics.
4. **Sterile environments**: glazed tile floors and walls, freshly wiped counters, white voids.
5. **Children's hygiene and bath toys** (Low register): rubber ducks, bath sponges, baby shower caps, powder pouffes, baby-brand bottles, kawaii mascots.
6. **Laundry and dish tools**: laundry pods and scent beads, sponges, scrub brushes.
7. **PPE and cleaning wear** (High register): latex and rubber gloves, surgical and gas masks, safety goggles, scrubs, hospital gowns, hazmat suits, reflective vests, aprons.
8. **Post-bath softness**: pajamas, robes.
9. **Brand and packaging imagery**: cheerful mascot labels and commercial logos, kept visible and loved cynically.
10. **Edit vocabulary**: cut-out PNGs, star and sparkle stickers, blue-sky cutouts, soft glow outlines around objects.

### Materials and textures
Glossy molded plastic, translucent gel liquids, foam microbubbles, glazed ceramic tile and porcelain, chrome taps, water surfaces patterned by caustic light, vinyl and latex gloves, soft terry cloth. Everything looks wet, wiped, sealed or freshly opened. Dust, fingerprints, scuffs and residue are absent by definition.

### Light
Shadowless, high-key, commercial. Light is even and cool, often overexposed toward white, with a soft bloom or haze haloing object edges — the glow of an airbrushed edit or a 2000s product render. Water scenes are lit from above so caustics dance on tile and submerged objects. No chiaroscuro, no golden hour, no candlelight.

### Composition
Product-shot logic governs: rows of near-identical bottles in pastel sequence; a single hero object centered on a white void; top-down flat lays; stim-board collages of glowing cut-outs with sticker stars; all-over patterns of tile grids or water surface filling the frame; vector-commercial vignettes where an object is crowned with sparkle bursts and droplets. Negative space is white, foam or water — never clutter.

## Mood & Values

Calm, mental clarity, reassurance. Cleancore images function as visual disinfectant: orderly, saturated with softness, deliberately surreal in their flawlessness (a smiley drawn in foam; a sponge with a face). Beneath the sweetness runs the aesthetic's honest core — anxiety about contamination and unsafe systems, soothed by ritual order — plus nostalgia for childhood bath time in the Low register and a cynical, affectionate embrace of consumer capitalism in the High register, where brand labels become comfort objects. The founding blog's sign-off, "Stay Safe," names the mood exactly: vigilance disguised as tenderness.

## Era & Origins

Coined in April 2014 on the Tumblr blog *Safety Corp* by Redeem Pettaway, within the net.art community, as a response to post-9/11 cultures of protection and false safety; aliases include Safetycore, Hygienicore, Safety Goth, Showercore and Antibacterial. Its visual vocabulary was inherited from mid-to-late 2000s cleaning commercials of the Frutiger Aero period. The COVID-19 pandemic amplified its imagery (PPE, sanitizer, disinfection rituals) without creating it. It persists as an edit and stim-board practice on Tumblr and TikTok.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Light first: cool, shadowless, high-key; let whites bloom slightly at the edges of objects.
2. Anchor the frame with at least one hygiene signifier — a cleaning product, suds, clean water, tile, PPE — even in surreal or abstract arrangements.
3. Keep surfaces wet, glossy or sealed: plastic, porcelain, chrome, gel, foam, water.
4. Hold vivid color inside objects (liquids, labels, toys); keep the surrounding air pale and cool.
5. Add one touch of surreal flawlessness: foam drawn into a face, impossible cleanliness, floating cut-outs, sticker stars, a sparkle burst.
6. Choose a register and stay coherent: High (sterile commercial, sanitizer, gloves, masks, brand jugs) or Low (kawaii bath time, ducks, baby bottles, pastel stickers).
7. Treat packaging and brand-like mascots as legitimate subject matter, not clutter.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be any cleaning or hygiene object, cleaned space, bath or pool water, PPE garment, or post-bath textile. Composition may range from product-row still life, flat lay, centered hero shot, stim-board collage, to full-frame water or tile pattern. Lighting may range from blown-out white studio key to sun-caustic underwater light to soft cool window light. Color handling may lean pastel-dominant, white-dominant, or teal-water-dominant, with vivid accents anywhere inside objects. Mood may tilt sterile-cold (High) or sweet-nostalgic (Low). Medium may be stock-style photography, airbrushed soft-focus edit, 2000s vector commercial render, or kawaii illustration. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**
- Dirt, grime, stains, clutter, wear, used or opened messiness; any narrative of cleaning *in progress* that shows the dirt.
- Warm earthy palettes (brown, terracotta, olive), sepia, candlelit spa organicism, wood, rattan, dried flowers.
- Heavy shadows, low-key noir lighting, dramatic contrast.
- Ice, frost, snow, frozen surfaces (Icepunk territory).
- Neon magenta/cyan grids, glitch, classical statues, retro computer UI (Vaporwave).
- Ocean horizons, glossy gadgets, auroras, tropical fish utopias (Frutiger Aero).
- Empty liminal pools with sun-faded warm tile and no hygiene signifier (Poolcore).
- Nursery plushies, pacifiers and cribs with no cleaning or bath context (Babycore).
- Clinical procedures, blood, illness, hospital drama; cleancore's medical overlap stops at masks, gloves and scrubs.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Neighbor keeps | Cleancore keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Frutiger Aero | skies, oceans, auroras, glossy tech, eco-futurism | domestic hygiene objects, foam, brand packaging, white voids |
| Icepunk | frost, ice, survivalist cold, industrial desaturation | wet (not frozen) coolness, suds, sanitized calm |
| Poolcore | empty liminal pools, sunlit water, nostalgic emptiness | pools only as *clean water*, plus products, suds, tile shine |
| Vaporwave | neon dusk, glitch, statues, 80s–90s irony | high-key white, soft bloom, sincere-cynical brand love, no glitch |
| Babycore | nursery softness, plushies, pacifiers | bath-time subset only, always tied to soap, water, hygiene gear |

Quick tells: if the frame smells of disinfectant — glossy plastic, suds, tile, a mask or a duck, all under shadowless pale light — it is cleancore. If it smells of ocean, frost, neon or talcum powder alone, it is one of the neighbors.
