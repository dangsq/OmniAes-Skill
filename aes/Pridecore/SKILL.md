# Pridecore

Pridecore is an internet aesthetic and community practice born on Tumblr in the late 2010s. Its core operation is not displaying pride flags but *transforming* them: taking the discrete stripe palette of a specific flag — bisexual, transgender, asexual, pansexual, aromantic, aroace, abrosexual, lemonade-colored lesbian variants, "moon lesbian" customs and dozens of other microlabel designs — and rebuilding whole images out of those hues. Moodboards, glittery GIF "stimboards," character icons, fan-art recolors, pixel graphics and custom flag designs all follow the same rule: one flag's palette becomes the entire color world of the piece. The aesthetic is made by and for LGBTQ+ people as self-expression, identity exploration and community validation; that sincerity is part of the visual register and should never be parodied.

## Visual Identity

### Palette: the flag-first rule

The palette is never invented; it is borrowed wholesale from a single pride flag. Hues are locked to that flag's stripes, and every dominant color in the frame — hair, fabric, sky, food, fur, neon gas, background field — must belong to the chosen set. Neutrals (white, black, grey) appear only when the flag itself contains them. Stripe order often survives as horizontal banding or as a stacked swatch block, but smooth rainbow smears that mud the hues together break the logic: Pridecore keeps its hues separable, like stripes.

Custom and microlabel flags extend this list indefinitely; the rule is "one flag, its stripes, its hue discipline," not any fixed table. Saturation handling is an allowed dial: flag-exact saturation, pastelized candy versions, neon-tube intensifications, or darkened moody versions all read as Pridecore so long as hue identity survives.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how the text documents them

1. **Pride flag palettes applied transformatively** — the defining act; recoloring of subjects, scenes and characters rather than pasting a flag rectangle.
2. **Moodboards and stimboards** — curated grids of images and GIFs that all share one flag's scheme; classically a 3×3 tile layout with a flat color-swatch panel at the center; stimboards imply shimmer, sparkle bursts and sequenced motion.
3. **Character icons and fan edits** — square avatar-scale art in which a character's hair, clothing or background is recolored to a flag as "headcanon" expression.
4. **Custom flag designs** — personalized or niche flags (moon lesbian and similar) created for self-expression.
5. **Glitter and sparkles** — glitter grain, sequin shine, star-point sparkles as a unifying overlay.
6. **Pixel graphics and original digital art** — flat, grid-visible pixel illustration in flag pastels.

Supporting motifs visible across the corpus: neon signage and handwritten neon phrases in flag hues; symmetrical flat-lay mandalas of hue-matched natural objects (crystals, petals, herbs, seed pods on dark wood); candy and food styled to palette (scoops of ice cream, donuts, macarons); hue-curated nature photography (mossy forest paths, auroras, flower crowns, backlit silhouettes against bark); metallic jackets, fur and sequins in glam-rock boards.

### Materials and textures

Glitter grain and sequin sparkle; metallic foil and lamé; plush fur; neon tube glow against dark walls; visible pixel grid; flat vector fills with clean outlines; candy gloss on sweets; matte botanical surfaces (petals, moss, leaves); rough wood grain under dark flat-lays; soft photographic grain in curated photos.

### Composition

Recurring structures, any of which may carry the style: the 3×3 grid collage with a central swatch tile; a single centered subject on a solid pastel field; horizontal stripe banding or stepped gradients that walk through stripe order; symmetrical radial flat-lays; centered portrait busts on plain grounds; full-bleed glow fields (neon walls, auroras, starfields). Square crops and icon-scale framing echo Tumblr dashboard culture.

### Lighting

The wiki text is silent on lighting; the corpus shows three consistent modes. **Flat mode:** even, shadowless illustration light for pixel and vector pieces. **Glow mode:** neon bloom, sparkle highlights, auroras and backlit silhouettes on dark grounds. **Dappled mode:** shaded forest light with sun flecks for curated nature photography. Mixed lighting inside one board is acceptable — hue unity, not light unity, holds the piece together.

## Mood & Values

Pride, self-expression, identity, community, celebration, validation. The mood is declarative and tender: "this is me," stated in color. Boards can swing euphoric-glam (sequins, neon, metallics), cozy-mystical (crystals, herbs, moonlit grass), or playful-sweet (pixel desserts, candy), but all keep an earnest, celebratory temperature. Stimboards add sensory comfort: shimmer and repetition as soothing texture. Insider specificity matters — choosing an obscure microlabel palette is itself a statement of belonging.

## Era & Origins

Late 2010s Tumblr, growing out of 2010s flag-design culture: as new flags for specific identities proliferated beyond the rainbow, users began treating each palette as raw creative material rather than an emblem to repost. Pridecore sits in the Tumblr aesthetic ecosystem beside Glitter Graphics (its glitter-GIF technique), Scenecore, Kidcore, Lovecore and Webcore, and remains live in fandom spaces wherever characters get recolored as headcanon.

## Generation Guidance

Principles:

1. **Flag first, subject second.** Choose one flag palette before choosing content; lock its hues.
2. **Hue lockdown.** Every dominant color belongs to the stripe set; allow only the flag's own neutrals plus white sparkle points.
3. **Transform, don't sticker.** Recolor the world — hair, clothing, sky, food, fur, neon gas — instead of pasting a flag decal; a literal swatch block or flag may appear as one element, but the palette must also live inside the subjects.
4. **Stripe logic as composition.** Prefer discrete bands, stepped gradients and swatch stacks over smooth rainbow blends.
5. **Pick a format family.** Grid board with swatch heart; single-subject flat illustration; character portrait edit; pixel graphic; neon sign set; symmetrical natural flat-lay; glitter stim frame.
6. **One finish layer.** Glitter grain, pixel grid, neon bloom, pastel matte, or photographic hue-curation — a single finish unifies mixed sources.
7. **Sincere tone.** Text fragments, if any, are affirmations or yearning phrases in neon or handwriting, never irony.

Variation axes: **subject** — anything that can carry hue: desserts, flora, crystals, fish and birds, clothing, signage, landscapes, auroras, smoke, fur, characters; **composition** — grid, centered single, flat-lay mandala, portrait, full-bleed field; **lighting** — flat even, neon glow, dappled shade, backlit silhouette, sparkle-lit; **color handling** — flag-exact, pastelized, neonized, or darkened while keeping hue identity; **mood** — glam-euphoric, cozy-mystical, sweet-playful, declarative. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

Avoid:

- Generic rainbow gradient smears with no chosen flag's discrete stripe logic (reads as stock pride merch).
- Palettes not traceable to any pride flag: earth tones, single-hue monochrome, corporate duotones.
- Hues outside the chosen flag beyond its own neutrals and white sparkle highlights.
- Corporate-minimal pride branding (clean logo plus a small rainbow).
- Neighbor-style content without flag discipline: toy-clutter nostalgia (Kidcore), heart-and-rose romance (Lovecore), scene-fashion neon chaos (Scenecore), retro UI clutter (Webcore), glitter alone (Glitter Graphics).
- Parody, trivialization or outsider-gaze framing; the style is community-owned self-expression.
- Incidental-color photorealism with no evident curation; the hand of the palette-chooser must be visible.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Lovecore:** Lovecore runs on hearts, roses, cupids and red-pink-white romance. Pridecore pinks only appear inside a flag stripe set with companion hues (magenta with purple and blue, pink with yellow and blue), and it happily uses unromantic palettes — aro greens, ace greys and blacks — that Lovecore would never touch.
- **Vs Kidcore:** Kidcore is toy clutter, crayon primaries and childhood nostalgia. Pridecore can be sweet (pixel sundaes) but its hue set always maps to a flag, and it admits adult glam textures: sequins, fur, neon signage.
- **Vs Scenecore:** Scenecore is 2000s scene fashion — chaotic multi-neon on black, raccoon tails, snapshot angles. Pridecore neon is flag-hued and disciplined, serving identity declaration rather than subculture costume.
- **Vs Webcore:** Webcore nostalgia is technological: dialog boxes, pixel cursors, chrome UI. Pridecore pixel work is illustration and icon art, not interface paraphernalia, and its organizing logic is a flag palette, not retro software.
- **Quick tell:** ask "which flag is this?" If a specific stripe palette explains every hue in frame, it is Pridecore; if the colors serve romance, nostalgia, fashion or interface instead, it is a neighbor.
