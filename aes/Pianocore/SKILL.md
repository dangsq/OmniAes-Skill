# Pianocore

Pianocore (also Piano Aesthetic, Piano Girl, Music Note Fashion) takes the structured high-contrast geometry of the piano keyboard and the glyph language of sheet music and treats them as printable, embroiderable, paintable ornament. It is not a "musician lifestyle" aesthetic: the piano is not documented, it is *worn*. The style grew out of 1990s Italian playful maximalism and 2000s Japanese Lolita border-print coordinates, and resurfaced in the 2020s through Pinterest, TikTok and Roblox avatar culture. Its emotional register is elegant whimsy — classical reverence filtered through cute, hyper-feminine craft.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette is a rule, not a suggestion: stark two-value monochrome, optionally warmed into aged-manuscript territory.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Ink black | `#0b0b0b`–`#202020` | Keys, note glyphs, leather, tights, bows |
| Paper white | `#ffffff`–`#f3efe4` | Key fields, backgrounds, blouses, socks |
| Cream / ivory | `#f0e7d0`–`#e4d6b4` | Vintage key panels, ribbon satin, lace |
| Sepia / aged paper | `#d9c7a1`–`#8a6a45` | Manuscript toning, parchment backdrops, vignettes |
| Antique brass / bronze | `#8a6a3d`–`#b5915c` | Charms, corner filigree, pendant frames, ball chains |
| Oxblood crimson (pinpoint only) | `#6e1a24` | Tiny embroidered roses or seals; never a field colour |
| Cocoa base (rare variant) | `#4b2e2a`–`#5f3a33` | Skirt or bag ground, only when cream keys carry the surface |

Mid-greys appear only as photographic shading or halftone in collages, never as palette blocks. Any hue outside this set breaks the style.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual authority

1. **Piano-key borders** — the defining device: a horizontal band of alternating key rectangles (short black tabs over a white/cream field with fine vertical key divisions) running along skirt and dress hems, hosiery tops, bag flaps, shoe platforms and soles.
2. **Lace** — cotton or chemical lace trim edging key panels, hems, collars and collage frames.
3. **Musical staves** — five-line staves that flow organically, curved or ribbon-like, across tights, blouses, bag surfaces and edit backgrounds.
4. **Treble clefs** — the focal glyph: metal charms on chains, hair-bow centres, printed oversize on edits and pendant inserts.
5. **Eighth and beamed notes** — scattered glyph confetti over hosiery, nails, backgrounds and overlays.
6. **Violins** — text-documented as violin-shaped bags and held props; image-rare, treat as an occasional accent, not a core requirement.
7. **Metronomes** — text-documented as necklace charms; sparse in imagery, use sparingly.

Supporting motifs evidenced in practice: nail art sets mixing keys, staves, roses and clock charms; harlequin diamond bands and ornate oval frames in monochrome collage edits; cursive script embroidery ("bella musica"-style italic handwriting); black parasols; striped or key-print over-the-knees and leg warmers.

### Materials and textures

Patent and embossed/toolled leather (bags, shoes); printed cotton twill and satin for key borders and note prints; matte cotton lace and grosgrain or satin ribbon; antique-finish metal charms on ball chain; glossy nail-art gel with 3D appliqués; paper sheet music as backdrop or insert; wrinkled linen or cloth as flat-lay ground. Surfaces read hand-finished: painted keys, stitched lace, embroidered script.

### Recurring subjects

Hyper-feminine coordinates (jumperskirt or pleated miniskirt with key hem, note tights, Mary Janes or platform pumps with bows); accessory still lifes (satchels with key panels and lace, treble-clef hair bows, pendant necklaces on sheet-music paper); leg-and-hosiery crops where the tights are the canvas; nail-art arrays; scrapbook-style digital collages and phone/UI themes built from key strips, bows and glyphs; anime or manga illustration and doll/figure stagings in the same coordinate grammar.

### Composition and lighting

Composition ranges from full-body coordinate portraits, to vertical leg crops, to overhead flat-lays on cloth, to dense layered collages with glyph overlays and lace frames, to flat vector graphic layouts. Lighting ranges from high-key studio white and soft diffuse window daylight (product and flat-lay work), to desaturated overcast outdoor light for coordinates, to sepia-toned vignetting for the aged-manuscript variant, to low-key flash snapshot mood in edit culture. Contrast stays crisp: blacks stay black, whites stay paper-bright.

## Mood & Values

Elegance, whimsicality, classical appreciation, performative art. Pianocore values the *gesture* of musicianship — notation as jewellery, keyboards as hemlines — over documentary realism. It carries 90s playful maximalism (the piano skirt as statement piece), Lolita narrative craft (coordinates as composed stories), and a nostalgic reverence for aged manuscripts. The mood is poised yet mischievous: a curtsey in key-print socks.

## Era & Origins

Precursors: 1990s Moschino Cheap & Chic piano-key gown, popularized as a minidress on *The Nanny*. Consolidation: 2000s Japanese Lolita community, where brands like Angelic Pretty and Alice and the Pirates used piano-key border prints in narrative coordinates; parallel digital precedent in Vocaloid design (MAYU's key skirt, Hatsune Miku's Gothic module). Revival: 2020s Roblox avatars, Pinterest and TikTok edits blending Twee and Lolita layering with musical motifs. Related media: Vocaloid, *The Nanny*, *Your Lie in April*.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Notation must be *applied* to a wearable or graphic surface: printed, painted, embroidered or collaged onto garment, accessory, nails or layout. A piano merely standing in the room is not Pianocore.
2. Keep the value contrast extreme: black glyphs and key tabs on white/cream fields, or the inverse.
3. Place at least one keyboard border on an edge — hem, cuff, hosiery top, flap, sole — whenever clothing or footwear is present.
4. Let staves and notes move organically: curved, flowing, scattered, never in rigid grid wallpaper.
5. Finish with Lolita-adjacent craft grammar: lace trim, ribbon bows, Mary Janes or platforms, antique metal charms.
6. Choose one temperature register per image: crisp monochrome, cream-ivory warmth, or sepia aged-manuscript. Do not mix sepia toning with clinical white in one frame.

**Variation axes.** Subject: coordinate portrait, leg crop, accessory flat-lay, nail art, collage edit, UI/graphic theme, anime illustration, doll staging, sepia portrait with violin. Composition: full-body to macro charm close-up; symmetric flat-lay to chaotic scrapbook layering. Lighting: high-key studio, soft daylight, overcast outdoor, sepia vignette, flash snapshot. Colour handling: pure black-white, cream-warmed, sepia, rare cocoa base, optional pinpoint crimson embroidery. Mood: playful maximalist, elegant vintage, grunge-tinged monochrome edit, hyper-feminine avatar cute. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.**
- Any hue outside the palette: rainbow or pastel keyboards, pink/sax-blue sweet-Lolita bases, neon, saturated primaries.
- Instruments as lifestyle props without applied notation (concert-hall documentary, practice-room realism, stage lighting, audiences, lens flare).
- Earthy tweed, oxblood leather, library staging and scholarly melancholy (Dark Academia drift).
- Chocolate-and-sweet motifs without notation (Choco Girl drift); celestial blur and pastel dreaminess (Wishcore drift); illustrated woodland animals and pastel childlike whimsy (Whimsy Twee drift).
- Crosses, coffins, black-on-black somber goth ornament replacing musical glyphs.
- Modern minimalist sans-serif branding, gold glitter glamour, photorealistic instrument-catalogue shots.
- Colour grunge; distress is acceptable only as monochrome collage texture keeping glyphs legible.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Classic Lolita:** same silhouette (JSK, lace, Mary Janes), but Classic Lolita surfaces are muted florals, damask and solids; Pianocore demands legible notation or keyboard border print as the coordinate's focal surface.
- **Choco Girl:** shares cream-and-cocoa warmth, but Choco Girl's motifs are sweets, knitwear and chocolate tones; Pianocore's cocoa variant still carries cream keys and glyphs, and its default is black-white, not brown.
- **Whimsy Twee:** twee is pastel, illustrated, softly childlike; Pianocore is graphic, two-value, vector-crisp, with notation as its only "illustration."
- **Wishcore:** wishcore dissolves edges in soft-focus pastel yearning and celestial symbols; Pianocore keeps hard glyph edges, printed surfaces and high contrast.
- **Dark Academia:** DA treats music as studied practice — violins on desks, sheet music on stands, tweed and lamplight in earth tones; Pianocore treats music as worn ornament in monochrome, cute and performative rather than scholarly.
- **Gothic Lolita (near neighbour):** goth stacks black-on-black with roses and crosses; Pianocore always preserves the white key field and musical glyphs, and its mood is whimsical, not funereal.

**One-line test:** if you can remove every piano-key band, stave, clef and note glyph and the image still reads as the same aesthetic, it was never Pianocore.
