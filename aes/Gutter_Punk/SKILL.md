# Gutter Punk

Gutter Punk is the visual language of punk adopted as a full life situation rather than a music scene: transient, often homeless young people whose clothing, grooming and gear declare anti-consumerism, rejection of conventional labor, and nomadic anti-authoritarian freedom. It synthesizes punk's confrontational hardware with the drift of Bohemianism, but strips boho of all romance — everything is worn out, sewn back together, and lived in publicly, on the pavement. The subculture is international (Traveler Punk, Punk Hobo, Crusty, punkabbestia, punk-à-chien, perroflauta), and its imagery is overwhelmingly candid street documentation: people sitting low in public space, surrounded by belongings, companions and the working fabric of the city.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)
The palette is *dirt-desaturated*: black plus weathered earth tones plus the gray of municipal concrete, with at most one or two loud accents that still read sun-faded or stained.
- Base: near-black and charred fabric `#101010–#22201d`; asphalt and concrete gray `#6f6f6c–#a9a8a4`.
- Body: olive drab and surplus camo `#4a4f38–#6b7050`; khaki and sun-bleached tan `#8a7a58–#b3a184`; rust, brick and dried-mud brown `#6e3a2a–#96543c`; dusty denim `#4f5b66`.
- Accents (sparse, never crisp): blood red `#8e1f1f`, loud shirt green `#3f7a4e`, bleached-blond hair `#c7b08a`, box-dye orange `#b85c2a`, bandana paisley red or blue.
- Whites are never white: bone, sweat-stained cream `#d8d2c4`. Night flash pushes highlights hot and shadows to tar; overcast days compress everything toward mid-tone grime.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the culture documents them)
1. **Heavily customized tattered clothing**: sewn-on patches, metal studs, safety pins, visible repairs; torn hems, grime, layers worn in defiance of season.
2. **Deliberately unkempt grooming**: long matted hair, dreadlocks, mohawks; facial tattoos (often rough, self-made looking), numerous piercings, stretched ear gauges.
3. **Animal companions**: dogs above all — curled at the side, draped over the shoulders, held on the lap, leashed to a backpack (the naming core of punk-à-chien and perroflauta). The photographic record also shows cats riding shoulders; any companion animal in physical contact qualifies.
4. **Travel gear**: oversized knapsacks and backpacks, bedrolls, sleeping bags, cardboard, bundled possessions stacked beside the body.
5. **Street-economy props**: acoustic guitar, banjo, mandolin or flute for busking; open case or cup for coins; cardboard panhandling signs ("spanging").
6. **Ground-level loitering**: sitting on curbs, steps, doorways, plaza brick, canal edges; squat circles of several people facing inward.
7. **Headwear and cloth scraps**: beanies, camo and bucket hats, bandanas (face-covering or neck-worn), flannel and plaid hood linings.
8. **Consumption debris**: beer bottles, plastic cups, cigarette packs and smoke — the hedonist strand the culture openly acknowledges.
9. **Anarchic street backdrop**: graffiti tags and circled-A spray paint as environment, not subject; transit motifs (hitchhiking, freight yards) are textually central but rarer in surviving imagery — treat them as licensed subject matter, not required props.

### Materials and textures
Frayed cotton and flannel, faded band-print jersey, surplus camouflage nylon, worn leather boots and straps, matted hair and dusty animal fur, corrugated cardboard, cracked asphalt and brick, photocopied paper. Everything should look handled for years: seams blown, fabric thinned at the knees and elbows, metal hardware dulled.

### Recurring subjects
Solo portraits locked with an animal companion; two-to-eight person squat clusters; buskers mid-song on a sidewalk; panhandlers with cup and sign; walkers seen from behind with packs and leashed dogs; festival or protest margins where the tribe gathers; night porch or doorway circles with smoke and a guitar case; occasional high-contrast photocopy stencil posters of a member's face with torn paper edges and a typed caption.

### Composition, camera and lighting
Candid, unposed, witness-level framing: eye-level standing camera looking down at seated subjects, or crouched tight for face-plus-animal portraits. Groups fill the frame edge to edge with imperfect crops (feet cut, heads near borders), slight tilt, snapshot awkwardness. Backgrounds are municipal and specific: wrought-iron fencing, paneled doors, brick porches, concrete plazas, canal walls, storefront glass. Lighting is either direct on-camera flash at night (hard shadow behind bodies, hot skin highlights), flat overcast daylight, or harsh noon sun with short shadows; warm interior spill onto a night porch is acceptable. Medium reads as amateur compact-camera documentary of the 1990s–2010s, grain and mild blur included, or as xeroxed zine artifact. Passersby in mainstream clothing may cross the frame as unconscious contrast.

## Mood & Values

Defiant pride in squalor: the dirt is the statement. Beneath it, communal tenderness — arms around dogs, bodies packed in circles, shared smoke, someone's head on someone's knee. The mood is present-tense and hedonistic, anti-aspirational, allergic to polish; it communicates DIY self-sufficiency, anti-consumerism, refusal of wage labor, and a found-family ethic that extends to animals. The gaze should be documentary and humanizing, neither pitying poverty porn nor glamorizing decay. Internal authenticity anxiety ("oogles", "scumfucks") means the look must read earned, never costumed.

## Era & Origins

Emergent from the 1970s–1980s punk diffusion in North America and Western Europe, crystallizing through squat, traveler and crust scenes of the 1980s–90s; documented in *The Decline of Western Civilization III* (1998) and later named into public debate as perroflauta during Spain's late-2000s/early-2010s crises. It reacts against mainstream capitalism, mass consumer culture and conventional labor, and borrows mobility from the older American hobo tradition (freighthopping, hitchhiking).

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Build the figure in layers: tattered base garments (flannel, camo, band tee, overalls) → customization layer (patches, studs, pins, crude repairs) → grooming (dreads/mohawk/matted hair, face tattoos, gauges) → gear (backpack, bedroll, cup, instrument) → companion animal in physical contact.
- Seat or stall the subject in public space; the ground is their living room.
- Clutter the frame with belongings, animals and urban texture; emptiness is wrong.
- Light like a witness, not a stylist: night flash, flat overcast, or blunt noon sun.
- Grade dirty: desaturate, sun-fade, add grime; permit one loud accent only.
- Keep the camera amateur: snapshot crop, mild tilt, compact-camera grain; or switch fully to xerox-stencil poster mode.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo animal portrait, squat circle, busker, panhandler, hitchhiker at a road edge, freight-yard hopper, protest/festival margin, canal or porch sitter, indoor-outdoor couch cluster. Composition: tight face-plus-animal, mid-distance group, wide street tableau, stencil poster. Lighting: night flash, overcast, noon, warm porch spill, or black-and-white photocopy. Color: full monochrome dirt up to one red/green/dyed-hair accent. Mood: tender, nihilistic sprawl, defiant glare, communal warmth. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**AVOID**
- Clean, new, branded or color-coordinated clothing; intact hems; pressed fabric.
- Studio lighting, softboxes, golden-hour glow, creamy bokeh portraiture, editorial fashion posing.
- Groomed hair, makeup, salon tattoos presented glossily; any hint of styling intent.
- Pristine interiors, cafés, tidy domestic rooms, minimalist negative space, symmetrical composition.
- Concert stages, mosh pits, PA stacks, band-merch walls (that is crust/folk punk's territory).
- Boho-chic signifiers: maxi dresses, macramé, dreamcatchers, jewel tones, camper-van vanlife.
- Neon or cyberpunk saturation, pastel filters, teal-orange cinema grades.
- Crisp tactical/military gear; camo only as worn surplus.
- Generic homelessness documentary without the punk customization layer (patches, studs, dreads, mohawks).
- Companion animals as distant leash props; the animal must share the body's space.
- Anarchist propaganda-poster layouts as the subject (graffiti stays background).
- Current glossy lifestyle tech (ring lights, selfie sticks) as focal props.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Gutter Punk |
|---|---|
| Anarcho-Punk | Ideology-first: black bloc dress, slogan patch jackets, demos and gig flyers, usually housed subjects. Gutter Punk adds transience — backpack, bedroll, cup, dog, curb — and politics are optional. |
| Crust Punk | Near-identical clothing, but crust is a music identity (D-beat, anarcho politics) pictured at gigs and squats with sound systems. If the frame centers a stage or patch-wall back, it's crust; if it centers a sidewalk economy and animal companion, it's gutter. |
| Folk Punk | Acoustic-punk as festive culture: taverns, festivals, album-art warmth, wool and tweed, cleaner grooming. Gutter Punk's instrument is a busking tool with an open case for coins, played for rent money on pavement. |
| Bohemian | Consumable romantic vintage fashion: flowing natural fabrics, artisanal jewelry, curated warmth. Gutter Punk is anti-fashion — grime, metal hardware, torn synthetics, no prettiness, no purchase-new anything. |
| Landevejsriddere | Scandinavian road knights: rural roads, hand carts and bicycles, functional outdoor wear, craft-sale props, pastoral vagabond nostalgia. Gutter Punk is urban sidewalk, punk hardware, squat circles and dogs; no carts, no countryside romance. |

Where evidence is thin, say so: freight-train and hitchhiking imagery is textually central but scarce in surviving photographs, and shoulder cats appear in the photo record though the text names dogs — both are licensed variations, not core requirements.
