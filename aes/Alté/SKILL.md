# Alté

Alté (from Nigerian Pidgin for "alternative") is a youth-led counterculture movement that emerged in Lagos, Nigeria in the mid-2010s, built on non-conformity, creative freedom and radical self-expression outside traditional societal norms. Visually it is a thrift-store collage logic: vintage graphic tees, jerseys and baggy denim fused with corsets, mesh, Ankara wax print, studs, chains and platform footwear, carried by grooming that is itself a statement — neon-dyed hair, bleached brows, locs, piercings, painted nails. It is a digital-native style, born on Snapchat, TikTok and Instagram, and its photographic voice is the phone camera: direct flash in cramped rooms, mirror selfies, backstage clutter, festival grounds, friends clustered in frame.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule: **black is the ground, green and purple are the signature signals, neon is the spark.** Outfits and frames are anchored in deep blacks (leather, denim, tees), then broken by saturated accents — canonically green and purple, plus the reds, blues and pinks that thrift availability supplies. Hair and nails carry the most extreme hues: bleach blonde, honey, pink, green.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Bold, sleek outfits built by eclectic layering** — mesh tops over or under thrifted corsets, oversized baggy jeans, vintage graphic tees, sleeveless shirts; the outfit as assembled collage rather than matched set.
2. **Daring grooming** — neon hair colors, bleach-styled brows, dyed locs, spiky hair, facial piercings, painted nails.
3. **Bold accessories** — retro sunglasses (narrow ovals, wraparound Y2K shields), stacked heavy jewelry (chains, beads, rings, bracelets), quirky bags and crossbody straps, studded and rhinestone belts, platform boots and high heels.
4. **Gender-fluid silhouettes** — kilts, nail polish, non-conforming cuts, shared wardrobes across genders; cutout and distressed tops, cropped halter tops, mesh sleeves worn regardless of gender.
5. **Jerseys and sportswear fragments** — numbered jerseys, track fabrics, visible branded waistbands, trucker caps and bucket hats.
6. **Ankara print and traditional textile fragments** — wax-print cotton used as a spliced accent inside an otherwise subcultural outfit, never as conservative head-to-toe traditional dress.

### Materials and textures

Cracked-ink vintage cotton tees; distressed and ripped denim; leather and pleather with contrast topstitching and flame or tribal graphics; mesh and fishnet; corset brocade and boning; Ankara wax cotton; knit cardigans; nylon jersey and track fabric; metal hardware everywhere — studs, rhinestones, chains, belt grommets; chunky rubber platform soles. Texture contrast is the point: soft thrift cotton against hard studs, sheer mesh against heavy leather.

### Recurring subjects

Nigerian youth, alone but more often in crews of two to four friends; models backstage among clothing racks; party and salon interiors; festival crowds and stage fronts; mirror-selfie trios in dressing rooms. Subjects pose with nonchalant cool — chin down, sunglasses on, attention split between the camera, the mirror and their phones — or are caught mid-gesture in candid clutter.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical, phone-native framing dominates. Mirror-selfie geometry (phone occluding part of the face, doubled reflections), tight group clusters that fill the frame, tilted or low casual angles, and environmental truth left in shot: curtains, sofas, clothing racks, wood-panelled walls, stage banners, sanitizer bottles on shelves. Lighting is direct on-camera flash in dim interiors, vanity or room light in mirrors, flat overcast daylight outdoors, occasionally even semi-editorial light for lookbook-style poses. Grain, high-ISO noise, slight blur and flash hotspot are authenticity markers, not flaws.

## Mood & Values

Rebellion and self-expression are the declared values, and the mood carries them: cool detachment layered over playfulness, confidence without aggression. Alté prizes DIY thrift ingenuity over luxury consumption, fluidity over rigid gender codes, and community over lone genius — the crew shot is its moral as much as its composition. It is joyfully serious about being itself: daring grooming and gender-crossing garments worn with total nonchalance, as everyday fact rather than costume.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s Nigeria. The term derives from Nigerian Pidgin English for "alternative" and was popularized around 2014 by the Lagos music collective DRB LasGidi (TeeZee, BOJ, Fresh L). The movement's roots run from the late 2000s through the 2010s in Lagos, gaining momentum as wider internet access let young Africans challenge rigid traditions through art and reject conservative cultural expectations. Its home platforms are Snapchat, TikTok and Instagram, which explains its snapshot aesthetics and fast remixing of thrift finds.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Anchor the frame in black, then signal with green or purple and one neon or saturated thrift-pop accent; never let the palette drift to muted neutrals.
- Build each outfit as a collage of at least three logics: vintage thrift (cracked graphic tee, baggy denim), subcultural hardware (studs, chains, platforms, retro shades), and one spliced traditional or unexpected note (Ankara fragment, corset, kilt).
- Treat grooming as half the outfit: bleached brows, dyed locs or crops, piercings, nails.
- Let silhouettes cross gender lines naturally — cutouts, mesh, heels, skirts, shared pieces — worn with nonchalance, not theatricality.
- Shoot like a phone: vertical crop, direct flash or flat daylight, grain and clutter retained, friends clustered, mirrors and phones welcome in frame.
- Keep the social body present: crews, backstage queues, festival grounds, party rooms.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: solo portrait, duo, crew, backstage fitting, thrift-market haul, festival crowd, salon party, semi-staged lookbook pose, even an outfit still-life — any subject within the culture's daily life.
- *Composition*: mirror selfie, candid snapshot, tilted group cluster, centered editorial pose, environmental wide with stage or street context.
- *Lighting*: harsh on-camera flash in dim rooms, mirror vanity light, overcast outdoor daylight, clean even studio light for lookbook mode.
- *Color handling*: black-dominant with a single accent, or busier multi-accent thrift chaos; green and purple privileged but reds, blues and pinks fully licensed.
- *Mood*: defiant cool, joyous play, tender intimacy between friends, bored backstage languor.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A garden courtyard portrait, a market stall scene or a clean studio lookbook can all be perfectly Alté if the palette rule, collage dressing, grooming statement and fluid silhouettes hold.

**Avoid**

- Beige, cream or muted Scandi minimalism; quiet-luxury tonal dressing.
- Head-to-toe designer monogram or logo luxury; Alté is thrift-led, not boutique-led.
- All-black goth or punk austerity with no color pop, no fluidity and no play.
- Glossy, retouched, seamless-backdrop campaign perfection as the default register (lookbook mode may be clean-lit but keeps casual styling and lived-in texture).
- Conservative traditional Nigerian formalwear presented straight; Ankara appears only as subverted fragment.
- Afrofuturist chrome, sci-fi costumery or cyberpunk gloss.
- Pastel e-boy / soft-grunge tweeness; cottagecore or pastoral styling.
- Caricatured, costumed gender play — fluidity here is lived daily wear, not drag parody.
- Neat symmetrical compositions and empty, styled-minimal interiors; clutter and closeness are characteristic.

## Discrimination Cues

No neighboring styles are recorded in the source taxonomy, so in practice distinguish Alté by conjunction rather than by any single element:

- Against **Western Y2K / McBling revival**: Alté adds Nigerian thrift eclecticism, Ankara fragments, gender-fluid cuts and a flash-snapshot social-native texture, not glossy nostalgia.
- Against **goth / punk**: Alté is colorful, playful and fluid where goth is austere, black-coded and rigid in silhouette.
- Against **e-boy / soft grunge**: Alté swaps pastel grunge tweeness for jerseys, platform boots and heels, heavy stacked metal and African urban context.
- Against **mainstream Afrobeats glam**: Alté rejects coordinated luxury styling for deliberate thrift collage and countercultural grooming.
- Against **K-fashion genderless style**: Alté is louder, flash-lit, hardware-heavy and rooted in Lagos crew culture rather than soft tailored minimalism.

The fastest recognition test: black-grounded thrift collage + statement grooming (bleached brows, dyed locs) + retro shades and stacked chains + gender-fluid silhouette + phone-flash candidness, situated in Nigerian youth life. Where several of these fire together, the style is Alté.
