# Pink Princess (Rocococore / Modern Rococo)

Pink Princess empathizes girlish innocence, elegance, compassion, and a life of pleasure and luxury. It borrows imagery from many periods but leans hardest on the Rococo, via Marie Antoinette, filtered through mid-20th-century French chic: think Dior couture and Audrey Hepburn grace wearing a pink bow. It is a sub-aesthetic of Princesscore, but where Princesscore dreams of being royalty, Pink Princess dreams of being *upper class* — the luxury is domestic and personal (bedrooms, baths, vanities, tea tables) rather than thrones and crowns. Its governing phrase, spread on Tumblr by the blog *laduree-et-cigarettes*, is "Take your pleasure seriously." Everything below serves that idea: pleasure, staged beautifully and without guilt.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule
Pink is the regime, not an accent. The pink family should hold the dominant colored area of any image, ranging from near-white blush (`#FBE9EC`) through ballet pink (`#F4C2CD`) to dusty rose (`#D89AA4`), with optional hot-pink spikes (`#E4568E`) used sparingly for emphasis — a dress, a frosting, a lipstick. White, cream and ivory (`#FFFDF9`–`#F4E8DC`) supply the ground, the breathing room and the linens. Gold and brass (`#C8A24B`–`#8E6F3E`) are the default metals: gilt mirrors, ornate cutlery, chandelier arms, locket chains. Secondary pastels (powder blue, mint, butter yellow, lilac) may support but never lead; silver appears only occasionally and always reads as the lesser metal. Black is rationed to functional doses — shoes, a man's skinny tie or tailored suit, an embedded black-and-white photograph as wall ephemera. Fully saturated non-pastel hues sit at the outer edge of the palette and are acceptable only when a motif or stage context carries them (a swan-embroidered couture gown, an opera or ballet stage); they are never the default for rooms or wardrobes.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence
1. **Core motifs:** pink bows and ribbons (in hair, tied on cats and small dogs, knotted around packages and ponytails); white long-haired / Persian cats; swans; bunnies; ballerinas and ballet; silk pajamas and dressing gowns; champagne; macarons; luxury chocolates and truffles; cake with pink frosting; pearls; diamonds.
2. **Settings and objects of pleasure:** luxurious feminine bedrooms with canopy, brass or tufted beds; clawfoot bathtubs and pedestal sinks with petal baths, candles and champagne; dressing tables with silver or gold mirror-brush-comb sets; perfume bottles with atomizers; fancy tea sets and tea parties; croissants, pain au chocolat and French pastry; cupids and cherubs; Rococo artwork in gilt frames; marble columns and Neoclassical statuary; Victorian dolls, dollhouses and vintage fashion dolls / Barbies; lap and purse dogs; baby animals (lambs, kittens); figure skaters; picnics on white blankets with pink roses; Ladurée macaron boxes; candles, record players, coffee-table books; black-and-white photographs, vintage movie posters and celebrity autographs as framed wall ephemera.
3. **Fashion grammar:** berets; pastel bouclé suits; pleated, tulle, chiffon and organza skirts; silk slip dresses; corset tops; lacy dresses; cashmere sweaters; silk and cotton blouses; lacy socks and stockings; white leather or lace gloves; silk or satin and velvet hair bows; tiaras; gold heart-shaped lockets; charm bracelets; pearl and diamond jewelry; pink ladylike handbags (Lady Dior type); 1950s–60s French-chic silhouettes; Marie-Antoinette-style costume reserved for costume parties, not daily wear.

### Materials and textures
Satin and silk sheen; velvet for bows and upholstery; lace and eyelet broderie openwork; chiffon, organza and tulle volume; cashmere and fine wool matte softness; gilt gold, brass and occasional silver plate; marble, porcelain and crystal coupes; pearl luster; fluffy white fur; frosting swirls and laminated pastry; pom-pom and piped trim; painted white wood and caned furniture. Surfaces should look touchable and expensive: pressed, piped, curled, frosted, polished.

### Recurring subjects
Groomed women of any ethnicity — styled hair with a bow or a soft quiff, clean polished nails — at leisure: reclining, bathing, sleeping, pouring tea, frosting cake, pirouetting, skating, reading in bed, lying on a lawn among cut roses. Men are peripheral by design: the Ken to her Barbie, groomed and clean-shaven, in a body-tailored early-1960s-style suit with a skinny tie in black, gray or blue, complementing never overshadowing. Animals appear pampered and accessorized: white cats in tiaras or bows, purse dogs, rabbits. Objects frequently star alone as still-life heroes — a vanity set on satin, a macaron tower, a pink-jacketed book, a labeled bottle.

### Composition, lighting, medium
Composition tends toward staged symmetry and dollhouse tidiness: paired canopy beds, a centered vanity, mirrored arrangements, nothing stray in frame. Frames-within-frames recur — gilt mirrors, canopy drapes, marble columns, proscenium arches. Reclining diagonals express leisure; back views exist to show off a bow or a dress closure; centered still lifes breathe against cream negative space. A graphic strand also belongs: vintage fashion-plate illustration, sewing-pattern envelopes, handwritten script panels on dusty pink grounds. Lighting is soft and diffused — daylight through sheer curtains, high-key creamy exposure, candlelight for baths, gentle theatrical or projector glow, snowy high-key exteriors, lawn sun with tender shadows. Contrast stays low; the grade is pastel and slightly matte with fine film grain, echoing film stills, Sofia Coppola and Wes Anderson perfume commercials, editorial couture photography and mid-century fashion illustration.

## Mood & Values

Pampered tenderness. Pleasure treated as discipline and self-love: the bath, the nap, the chocolate, the silk sheet. Despite being wealth-coded, the style professes kindness, compassion and generosity — luxury with a soft heart and an inclusive door. Innocence without naivety, wealth without edge, nostalgia without melancholy. Play is central: dolls, Barbies, cake, bows, tiaras on cats. The overall feeling is being cherished in a beautiful room where nothing is urgent.

## Era & Origins

The recorded decade of origin is the 1930s — the era of its earliest touchstone films (*Marie Antoinette*, 1938; *Ninotchka*, 1939) — but the visual grammar reaches further in both directions: backward to 18th-century Rococo court dress and Neoclassical marble, forward to 1950s–60s French chic (Dior, Grace Kelly, Audrey Hepburn, Catherine Deneuve in *Les Parapluies de Cherbourg*, the Sissi trilogy). The named aesthetic and its phrasebook crystallized in the Tumblr/blog era (*laduree-et-cigarettes*, Blushing Ingenue, Sweet Macaron, Pink Ballerinas). Interior decor draws on Shabby Chic and Neo-Rococo. Culturally it reacts against hustle and austerity by insisting, deliberately and prettily, on rest.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Build the palette first: pink family dominant, cream/white ground, gold hardware; pastels support, silver rare, black rationed.
2. Groom everything — pressed linens, piped frosting, curled hair, polished silver; tidiness is moral.
3. Let ornament structure the composition: a bow, gilt frame, canopy or column should anchor the frame.
4. Show leisure as a verb: bathing, sleeping, tea-pouring, frosting, pirouetting, skating, reading in bed.
5. Keep light soft: diffused daylight, high-key cream, or candle glow; never harsh flash or noir contrast.
6. Use a vintage medium feel: film grain, pastel grade, fashion-plate line work, handwritten script.
7. Wealth without crowns: upper-class domestic luxury, not throne rooms or regalia.
8. Keep the tone kind and playful — tender, never cynical, never sexualized.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter: any scene or object of pampered leisure — interior, still life, portrait, animal, athlete (ballet, skating), picnic, costume party, illustration or product shot; any era costume within the 18th-century-costume to 1960s-chic to modern-silk-pajamas range; any ethnicity and age presentation of the feminine subject; men only as groomed supporting figures. Composition: symmetric interior, centered still life, reclining portrait, back view, flat pattern or script panel, fashion plate. Lighting: sheer-curtain daylight, candlelight, theatrical glow, snowy high-key, lawn sun. Color handling: cool blush through warm rose; hot-pink accents allowed; secondary pastels in small areas; a rare saturated couture hue only when a motif carries it. Mood: playful ↔ serene ↔ dreamy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid:** black- or dark-dominant palettes; neon, plastic gloss or emoji-era consumer clutter; grunge, dirt, distress, decay; modern tech (phones, laptops) as hero props; sportswear and athleisure; Scandinavian minimalism or industrial materials (concrete, chrome, bare plastic); harsh flash, high-contrast noir; overt sexualization; castles, thrones and crown-jewel regalia; academic libraries and lecture halls; Paris landmark street scenes as the primary subject; pinup red-and-black retro kitsch; misty desaturated wilderness; earthy matte tones (terracotta, olive, rust); clutter without curation.
