# Melodycore

Melodycore, also called MyMelodycore, is a light, hyper-feminine aesthetic built in devotion to the Sanrio rabbit My Melody — the white rabbit in a pink, flower-trimmed hood. It coalesced in the early 2020s on TikTok, Instagram and Japanese image boards as one of the most prominent sub-aesthetics under the Sanriocore umbrella, and it defines itself structurally as the bright counterpart of Kuromicore: where Kuromicore owns black and edge, Melodycore owns everything soft, sweet and innocent, borrowing its silhouettes from Girly Kei, Ryousangata (mass-produced trendy Japanese fashion) and Soft Girl, with occasional Lolita touches. Its images function less as documentation than as affection: shelves of plushies arranged like altars, ribbons tied onto everything, a haze of glitter over the whole frame.

## Visual Identity

### Palette
The palette is a three-value family — pastel pink, white, cream — held at high luminance and low contrast, with deep pink or red permitted only as small accent punctuation (a bow center, a lip, a printed heart, a hood flower). Treat it as a ratio rule, not a fixed swatch: roughly three quarters or more of any frame should sit inside the pink–white–cream family, from near-white tinted pink (#FDF3F5, #F9E4E8) through shell and blossom pink (#FADADD, #F6C9D3, #FFD9E0) to warm cream (#FFF6EC, #F4EADB); accent raspberry-to-red (#E0507A to #D22B2B) never becomes a field color. Two legitimate ends of the range exist: a whited-out, near-monochrome end where pink survives as a whisper over white, and a fuller candy-pink end typical of product and cosmetics photography. What never varies is the absence of dark values — no black, no charcoal, no saturated cool hue as structural color.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)
1. **The My Melody character herself** — the hooded rabbit as plushies, printed apparel, cosmetics packaging, ear headbands, character backpacks, phone wallpapers, metal charms.
2. **Bows and ribbons** — satin and grosgrain bows on collars, hems, hair, gifts and flat lays; script-printed ribbon; ribbon lacing threaded through knits.
3. **Lace and frills** — lace collars and trims, frilled hems, lace cloth used as flat-lay underlay, tulle.
4. **Hearts** — pearl-bead heart charms, heart cutouts, heart graphics stamped over photos.
5. **Sweet foods** — cakes, strawberries, parfaits and confections rendered in the same pink key. The style's own texts name sweets a key motif even though surviving imagery leans heavily toward merchandise and fashion; desserts are therefore fully in-range subjects.
6. **Sparkles and glitter** — glitter particles on lids and nails, iridescent flakes, star-cross sparkle overlays, rhinestones.
7. **Dreamlike imagery** — haze, bloom, soft focus, milky white vignettes; the filter itself is a motif.

### Materials and textures
Satin and grosgrain ribbon, pearl beads and pearl chains, chemical lace and eyelet, quilted vinyl on small backpacks and handbags, plush minky and faux-fur sleeves, chiffon and tulle frills, fine-gauge knit arm warmers and cardigans, glossy cosmetic compacts, pale silver- and gold-tone charms, iridescent glitter on nails. Surfaces must read touchable and soft; even hard objects (compacts, charm metal) gleam gently rather than sharply.

### Fashion and beauty grammar
Silhouettes come from Girly Kei and Ryousangata with Lolita touches: oversized hoodies and T-shirts, frilled dresses and skirts for childlike comfort; white stockings or knee-high socks; platform boots, Mary Janes or loafers for height. Signature accessories are My Melody ear headbands and small character backpacks, with Girly Kei labels such as Lafary, Liz Lisa, Maison de Fleur, Noemie and Rojita as the high-end register. Makeup is a natural nude base in the Japanese TikTok mode — glossy lip, large innocent "pien" eyes, healthy youthful glow — decorated with glitter; nails are heavily built with charms and 3D decals; hair is adorned with bows and pearl clips.

### Recurring subjects
Character-goods displays (shelves and tabletops crowded with My Melody plushies and boxes, arranged like shrines); flat lays of accessories, cosmetics and stationery on lace or fur; hands with decorated nails holding a plush; eye-makeup and nail close-ups; back-of-head hair shots with bows; neck-down or partially cropped outfit shots; pink bedroom and vanity interiors; multi-panel collages and decorated wallpapers in image-board grammar; character-branded cosmetics; sweet foods.

### Composition and lighting
Composition tends toward either crowded maximalist accumulation (the altar shelf, the packed flat lay) or intimate close crops that keep the face partial or absent, letting garments, hands and objects carry the image. Centered symmetry suits altars; scattered arrangement suits flat lays. Depth of field is shallow; edges often dissolve into a white vignette or haze. Lighting is diffused and high-key — window daylight or softbox, deliberately overexposed, with bloom or mist lowering contrast further. Decorative overlay stamps (hearts, sparkles, snowflakes, script) and collage grids are native grammar, inherited from Japanese image-board editing.

## Mood & Values
The governing value is kawaii: cuteness as sincerity, not irony. The mood is dreamlike, romantic, hyper-feminine, committed to delicate innocence — the aesthetic explicitly rejects darkness. Comfort and childlike softness (oversized hoodies, plush company) coexist with meticulous decoration (built nails, ribbon-laden hair), expressing fandom devotion as care: collecting, arranging, tying, glittering. There is gentle performativity — peace signs, posed hands — but never aggression, cynicism or edge. Sweetness is the moral center.

## Era & Origins
Melodycore emerged in the early 2020s on TikTok, Instagram and Japanese image boards, though its mascot is a decades-old Sanrio staple whose anime lineage (*Onegai My Melody*, later *Kuromi's Pretty Journey*) kept her in circulation. It is fandom-native: its brand ecosystem spans Sanrio character goods and Girly Kei / Ryousangata labels, and its beauty grammar comes from Japanese TikTok trends. Structurally it exists in symbiosis with Kuromicore, its dark twin; the characters' rivalry lets an occasional fleeting dark accent appear as a narrative wink, but the field stays light.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
- Lock the palette before the subject: pink–white–cream at high key; deep pink/red as punctuation only.
- Light is the style: diffuse, overexposed, bloomed; shadows exist only as the softest gradients.
- Carry the character or her grammar: a My Melody plush, print, headband or charm — or, in her absence, her visual language of hooded-rabbit softness expressed through bows, pearls and hearts.
- Stack at least two or three motif families per frame (ribbon + lace + heart; plush + glitter + frill).
- Keep textures soft and touchable: satin, plush, pearl, lace, knit, quilted vinyl.
- Finish with the native edit: lowered contrast, milky haze, optional sparkle/heart stamps, optional white vignette or collage grid.
- Style bodies and faces innocently: modest comfort silhouettes, glossy nude makeup, cropped or softened framing.

**Variation axes**
- *Subject matter:* goods altar, wearable fashion, beauty close-up (eyes, nails, hair), sweets, room interior, stationery or wallpaper design, product photography — any is correct.
- *Composition:* flat lay, hand-held close-up, neck-down outfit crop, wide interior, centered altar, multi-panel collage; centered or scattered.
- *Lighting:* bright window daylight, even studio softbox, or heavily dreamlike overexposed wash; haze from subtle to near-dissolving.
- *Color handling:* anywhere between the whited-out monochrome end and the fuller candy-pink end; accent present or absent but always small.
- *Mood:* serene and hushed versus playful and candy-bright; rarely, one tiny dark accent (a Kuromi charm) as rivalry play.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A strawberry parfait on lace with bloom and a bow, unseen in any reference, is fully Melodycore; a copied reference layout with hard lighting is not.

**Avoid (style-breakers)**
- Dominant black, charcoal, deep purple; goth hardware (chains, spikes, studs) — crosses into Kuromicore.
- Cool pastel dominance (mint, baby blue, lavender) or infant props (pacifiers, bottles, cribs) — Babycore or blue-tinted Sanriocore territory.
- Multi-character Sanrio ensembles with equal yellow/blue/red presence — generic Sanriocore.
- Hard flash, deep shadows, high contrast, heavy film grain, sepia, analog grime.
- Industrial or minimalist materials: concrete, brushed steel, matte black plastic, bare Scandinavian interiors.
- Mature glamour coding: smoky eyes, heavy contour, stiletto heels, bodycon silhouettes, sexualized posing.
- Earth tones: mustard, olive, terracotta, brown-dominant palettes.
- Saturated red or hot pink as a field color rather than an accent.
- Cynical, grunge or horror elements; torn paper, rust, decay.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates it from Melodycore |
|---|---|
| Babycore | Infantile props (pacifiers, cribs, onesies, bottles) and a poly-pastel palette including blue, mint, yellow; Melodycore keeps pink–white–cream monochrome and dresses teens and adults in Girly Kei silhouettes, not baby roleplay. |
| Girly Kei | A fashion style first: frills, lace and pink without character IP and without the filtered, sticker-stamped photo grammar; Melodycore always carries My Melody merchandise or devotion plus the dreamlike edit, and Girly Kei tolerates accent colors and cleaner catalog lighting. |
| Kuromicore | The dark twin: black, purple and hot pink, edgy hardware, Kuromi as mascot; Melodycore excludes darkness except a fleeting rivalry cameo, and its mascot is the hooded rabbit, not the devil-eared one. |
| Larme Kei | Magazine-derived mature sweetness: beige, lavender and brown mixes, tweed and fur textures, posed model editorial lighting; Melodycore is higher-key, pinker, character-anchored, and uses amateur smartphone / image-board edit grammar. |
| Sanriocore | The umbrella: any Sanrio character and the full multi-color palette (Hello Kitty red, Cinnamoroll blue, Pompompurin yellow); Melodycore narrows to one rabbit and one pink–white–cream devotion. If the frame's color story is multi-character parity, it is Sanriocore. |

Quick recognition summary: a frame almost entirely pink–white–cream, glowing with diffuse overexposure, crowded or cropped with bows, lace, pearls and hearts, and carrying the hooded rabbit My Melody on plush, print or packaging — is Melodycore.
