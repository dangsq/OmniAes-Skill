# Shamate (杀马特)

Shamate is an edgy, self-invented youth aesthetic born among migrant factory workers in southern China (Dongguan and surrounding industrial belt) in the late 2000s, peaking 2009–2013. It is a Visual Kei / Emo / Scene silhouette rebuilt from cheap materials: spray dye, backcombs, hair lacquer, imitation-brand streetwear, and a camera phone. Because it grew on internet forums and personal photo albums, the *processed photograph* is as much a part of the style as the outfit — cranked saturation, glow, flares, stickers and borders are native shamate elements, not post-production accidents. The style is deliberately eye-catching and deliberately non-realistic: a fantasy self-portrait made in a grey concrete world.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The governing rule is **neon head, desaturated world**: vivid artificial hair colors colliding against black clothing, pale makeup, denim, and grey Chinese urban infrastructure.

Any unnatural dye in the neon family is acceptable; multiple clashing dyes within one crew (or one head) are typical. Natural hair color alone never reads as shamate.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Sculpted spike hair** — the text names hair "the most recognizable element": exaggerated volume, sharp radiating spikes, asymmetrical cuts, heavily textured backcombed forms, dyed red, yellow, blue, purple or platinum. The hair silhouette should exceed the head's width and height; it often partially eclipses the face.
2. **Dark eye and lip makeup** — heavy black eyeliner rimming the eyes, smudged shadow, dark or deliberately pale lips, over a light foundation base; makeup exists to amplify the face for the camera.
3. **Shock accessories** — dog collars and studded chokers, chains, religious symbols (crosses), skull/death motifs, padlocks, fingerless gloves; statement pieces worn over ordinary clothes.
4. **The digital edit layer** — extreme saturation, high contrast, glow/bloom, lens flares, decorative borders and frames, layered graphics, stickers, stylized typography and watermarks; portraits pushed toward a heightened, fantasy-like unreality.
5. **Non-canonical cheap clothing** — brightly colored garments, imitation luxury-brand logos, tank tops, hoodies, skinny jeans, checkered shirts or scarves, canvas sneakers. The text is explicit: no single clothing canon defines shamate; identity lives in the hair and the photo.
6. **Crew and street context** (image-supported) — groups of friends perched in rows on curbs, steps and plaza ledges, crouching clusters, cigarettes, hand gestures aimed at the camera; backdrop of tiled plazas, apartment blocks, park benches, factory-dormitory streets; occasionally a cheap saturated vinyl studio backdrop.

### Materials and textures

Crusty backcombed hair with a matte spray-dye finish and lacquered spike tips; cheap synthetic polyester and cotton; worn denim; thin metal chains and plastic studs; low-megapixel sensor noise, compression artifacts, flash blowout — or, on the edited pole, over-sharpened halos and airbrushed glow.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Amateur, eye-level, center-weighted snapshot framing; full-body or three-quarter group rows; tight face close-ups where hair fills the frame; posed-but-casual body language (sitting, squatting, leaning, hands in pockets). Lighting is typically flat overcast daylight or harsh on-camera flash; studio variants use a single saturated backdrop color. Rim light and glow are usually *added in editing* rather than lit on set.

## Mood & Values

Defiant joy and self-made fantasy against monotonous factory life; entertainment as resistance to boredom. Pride under stigma — the style knowingly invites mainstream dislike and wears that rejection as proof of identity. Community belonging (crews, forums, albums) over individual luxury. A melancholy undercurrent: blank stares, cigarettes, grey skies beneath the neon. Individuality and visual impact are valued over realism; the edited portrait is truer than the mirror.

## Era & Origins

Emerges late 2000s–early 2010s China, particularly rural-origin migrant workers in southern industrial cities; creator Luo Fuxing, one of his village's first internet users, copied Visual Kei imagery without knowing its music. The name derives from the English word "smart." Peak 2009–2013 (~20,000 adherents), then decline under factory hiring bans and mainstream hostility, driving the style underground; a modest revival follows on TikTok. It reacts against grey factory routine and respectability politics, and borrows its silhouette from Visual Kei, Emo, Scene and punk while substituting phone cameras, spray dye and forum culture for stages and salons.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Hair is the subject. Build the silhouette first: volume beyond the skull, radiating spikes, asymmetry, crusty backcombed texture. If the hair reads as an accent, the image fails.
2. Neon-on-grey clash: saturated artificial hair against black clothing and desaturated concrete surroundings.
3. Commit to one of two native media poles: the candid low-fi camera-phone snapshot, or the hyper-processed net portrait (saturation, glow, flare, border, sticker, typography). Halfway between them reads as generic.
4. Mundane body, extraordinary head: clothing stays cheap, bright, mismatched, imitation-brand; never coordinated designer wear.
5. Face: pale foundation, eyes rimmed in black liner, dark or bleached lips.
6. Self-presentation: subjects pose knowingly for the camera, alone or as a crew row; the image is meant for an online album.
7. Environment is ordinary Chinese urban public space — plaza tile, steps, apartment blocks, park benches — or a cheap saturated studio backdrop.

**Variation axes.** Subject: solo close-up, duo, crew of six, dance pose, studio portrait, street candid. Composition: hair-filling-frame close-up, seated curb row, crouched cluster, standing loose group, angled snapshot. Lighting: flat overcast day, night on-camera flash, single-color backdrop studio, edited bloom and flare. Color handling: near-natural snapshot color through maximum-saturation net edit. Mood: defiant, playful, bleak, tender, boastful. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a shamate portrait in a rain-slick night market or against a painted studio sky is equally valid if the hair, makeup, clothing register and photo idiom hold.

**Avoid**

- Professional editorial lighting, retouching, or coherent high-fashion styling.
- Natural hair colors, or hair treated as a small accent.
- Shaved-sides punk mohawks, goth Victorian/Lolita costumes, metal corpsepaint, grunge flannel.
- Smooth, precise cosplay wigs — shamate hair is crusty, backcombed, spray-matte.
- Muted earth-tone or Scandinavian palettes; clean minimalist backgrounds; luxury genuine logos worn as actual high fashion.
- Western suburb, nightclub, or concert-stage settings; instruments and band-photo staging (that is Visual Kei, not shamate).
- Flawless skin texture and pristine fabrics; the style's material truth is cheap and worn.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Kmeng Steav (Cambodia):** same family, but glossier Facebook-sticker editing, smoother gradient dyes, slimmer K-pop-inflected tailoring and duckface posing; hair volume is smaller and spikes softer. Shamate keeps bigger radial backcombed bursts, pale foundation with heavy liner, and Chinese plaza/factory-dorm context.
- **Pokemón (Chile):** flat-ironed, side-swept fringes over a black base with neon streaks; the silhouette is flat and angular, emo-lineage, with skinny jeans and converse. Shamate's silhouette is volumetric and radiating, not straightened.
- **Too Cool:** overlaps in bright sporty streetwear and posed coolness, but keeps hair comparatively conventional; in shamate the hair is the load-bearing element and the clothing is an afterthought.
- **Trẻ trâu (Vietnam):** younger teens, dyed fringes rather than sculpted spike architecture, hoodies, flip-flops, motorcycle props and meme-bravado posing; lacks the edited fantasy-portrait culture and the backcombed volume.
- **Yabi:** adjacent Chinese online goth/visual-kei-flavored look with more coordinated dark-romantic outfits; shamate stays streetwear-cheap and hair-maximal. Documentation for Too Cool and Yabi is thin, so treat these cues as tendencies rather than hard borders.
- **Visual Kei / Emo / Scene proper:** stage costumes, instruments, band photography, Western suburbia. Shamate is the same silhouette photographed on a plaza curb with a phone, then glowing in a QQ album.
