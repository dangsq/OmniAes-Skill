# Brazilian Carnival (Carnaval Brasileiro)

Brazilian Carnival is the annual four-day festival before Ash Wednesday (February–early March), descended from the Portuguese Shrovetide game of *entrudo*, transformed by African drumming into samba and marchinha, and dressed with French masquerade-ball imports — confetti, streamers, masks. Visually it is a doctrine of abundance: no surface left unornamented, no color left desaturated, no body left still. It manifests regionally — Rio's sambadrome samba schools, Salvador's *trios elétricos* and axé/samba-reggae blocos, Recife and Olinda's frevo and giant papier-mâché dolls, Manaus's Carnaboi with its Bumbá Meu Boi ox figures — so the style is a family of festival registers united by one visual grammar: high-chroma polychrome, gold, feathers, glitter, and crowd density.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not swatch copy)
- **Primary register:** high-saturation, medium-to-high-brightness hues used in deliberate clash — scarlet and cherry reds (#D62828–#E63946), golden yellows (#FFD000–#F4A300), emerald and leaf greens (#00A86B–#06A77D), turquoise and teals (#00B4D8–#40E0D0), royal blues (#2457C5–#1D4ED8), hot pinks and magentas (#FF477E–#FF2E93), flame oranges (#FF7A00–#FF8800). Any hue is admissible if it stays saturated; muting breaks the style.
- **Metallic anchor:** gold (#D4AF37–#FFD700) as embelishments, mask leaf, fringe, float trim, body paint. Gold is the one near-mandatory accent tying the polychrome together.
- **Secondary register:** pastel confetti fields — pale pink, baby blue, mint, lilac — appearing only as scattered paper, streamer curls or bokeh, never as calm wall color.
- **Night ground:** near-black sky (#0A0A0F–#141419) against which floodlit costume glows; daylight ground: hot sun-bleached street whites and vivid painted facades.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by textual prominence)
1. **Parades and parade floats** — sambadrome avenues, receding rows of costumed blocks, towering float tableaux.
2. **Costumes with feather headdresses and adornments** — radial plume fans, beaded crowns, fringed collars; costumes of any theme (mythic figures, animals, characters) without restriction.
3. **Masks** — masquerade-ball inheritance; ornate half-masks with feathers, gold leaf, gems.
4. **Glitter** — on skin, hair, costume; a stated trademark of the festivity.
5. **Confetti and streamers** — airborne and ground-strewn paper particles.
6. **Colorful frevo umbrellas** — small, multicolored paneled parasols held aloft or spun.
7. **Percussion and music bodies** — drum lines, blocos (Olodum-type), dancers mid-samba.
8. **Regional effigies** — Olinda giant dolls, *trio elétrico* sound trucks, Bumbá Meu Boi ox figures with fireworks.
9. **Fashion grammar** — bejeweled bikini/swimsuit bases under adornment (women), expansive coats, dresses and skirts, gold embelishments (men), prints and patterns, bracelets and earrings, high heels, boots, sandals.

### Materials and textures
Feathers (long stiff plumes radiating outward plus soft ostrich fluff), sequins, rhinestones and beadwork in dense grids, loose body glitter with specular sparkle, metallic lamé and satin, gold-painted leaf and trim, raffia/straw skirts and collars, papier-mâché (masks, giant dolls, boi heads), paper confetti and silk streamers, feathered parasol silk. Surfaces should read touchable and costly-handmade, never matte plastic.

### Recurring subjects
Smiling costumed dancers (close portrait or mid-stride), grouped revelers blowing kisses or raising arms, uniform costume blocks marching in rows, percussionists carrying or striking drums, street crowds under bunting and painted facades, mask still-lifes on confetti beds, frevo steps with umbrella in hand, float crews in themed unity, giant dolls and ox effigies above crowds.

### Composition and lighting tendencies
- **Density first:** frames filled edge-to-edge with ornament, people or particles; negative space appears only as night sky or confetti field.
- **Upward silhouette:** headdresses and plume fans expand above and beyond the head; shoulders widened by fringes, epaulettes, feather wings.
- **Receding geometry:** parade rows and avenue shots use one-point perspective down the samba pass; aerial views show massed color blocks.
- **Gesture:** dance stride, spin, raised arms, blown kisses — bodies caught mid-motion, occasional motion blur in feet and fringe.
- **Lighting regimes (pick one coherently):** (a) night floodlight/hard flash against black sky, colors glowing and glitter sparking; (b) bright tropical daylight, slightly hot highlights, saturated facades; (c) soft even studio light for mask-and-confetti still-lifes. Glitter and sequin speculars must catch the light in all regimes.

## Mood & Values

Joy, festivities, fun, excitement, harmony, charm. The style expresses collective rather than solitary pleasure: ornament is generosity, the body is celebrated rather than concealed, and the crowd is the hero. Beneath the glitter sits a people's-voice tradition — marchinha lyrics carried social critique — and a syncretic pride in Portuguese, African and Indigenous roots. Nothing mournful, ironic or austere belongs here.

## Era & Origins

Introduced in the 1800s via Portuguese Shrovetide *entrudo* (water, flour, eggs); African enslaved communities fused drumming with Portuguese music, birthing marchinha and samba (first marchinha "Ó Abre Alas", Chiquinha Gonzaga, 1899). Upper-class rejection of entrudo's mess imported French masquerade confetti, streamers and masks; 1930s convertibles spread confetti-throwing; from the 1960s samba dominated. Regional forms (frevo, axé, samba-reggae, Carnaboi) and purpose-built sambadromes diversified the visual family. Evidence base is photographic — event, portrait and still-life photography — so the style reads as lived festival documentation rather than illustration, though illustrated renderings obeying the same rules are valid.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Saturate everything; clash hues deliberately; anchor with gold hardware.
2. Build the silhouette upward and outward with feathers, fringes, crowns.
3. Make at least one surface glitter: skin, hair, sequins, mask leaf.
4. Fill the frame with ornament, crowd or confetti particles.
5. Include gesture or motion — dance, spin, raised arms, kiss blown.
6. Give costume blocks internal coherence (one hue family or theme per row/group), rainbow across groups.
7. Choose one lighting regime and commit; let speculars spark.
8. Show skin adorned, not armored: midriffs, shoulders, backs carrying ornament (regional effigies and coated figures excepted).

**Variation axes** — subject (dancer portrait, parade avenue, percussion line, street crowd, float, mask still-life, frevo umbrella study, giant doll or boi effigy, backstage/barracão preparation); composition (tight overlapping group, single proud portrait, receding rows, aerial mass, flat-lay still-life); lighting (night floodlight, noon sun, soft studio); color handling (full rainbow vs. a single dominant hue block with gold, e.g. an all-yellow bateria); mood (euphoric dance, poised smile, masked playfulness); setting (sambadrome, historic street, seaside avenue, workshop, confetti table). **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.** A lone frevo umbrella spinning over a confetti-strewn street, or a night portrait of a drummer in white and gold, reads as Brazilian Carnival if saturation, material logic and festive density hold.

**Avoid**
- Desaturated, earthy, matte or beige-minimal palettes; large empty negative space.
- Venetian dominance: bauta/moretta full-face masks, tricorn hats, baroque brocade cloaks, gondolas, candlelit salons.
- Easter pastels with eggs, bunnies, quiet domestic spring scenes.
- Halloween horror, gore, gothic gloom; winter snow or Christmas props.
- Solo pinup woman with fruit headdress in Hollywood studio light (Brazilian Bombshell) unless embedded in parade/crowd context.
- Generic corporate party: balloon arches or plain streamers with no feathers, glitter, masks or costume.
- Mandrake street-funk bravado: tracksuits, sneaker culture, gold chains without carnival costume.
- Manguebeat grit: mud, crabs, psychedelic grunge, murky band stages.
- Static, emotionless faces; formal closed portraiture without adornment.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Venetian Carnival:** restrained palettes (black, white, burgundy, antique gold), full-face porcelain masks, 18th-century attire, canals and candlelight, mood of mystery and decorum. Brazilian Carnival shows skin, sweat-heat daylight or floodlit night, feathers, glitter, crowds and smiles.
- **Brazilian Bombshell:** single glamorous woman, fruit-and-turban headdress, 1940s Hollywood studio gloss, pinup pose. Brazilian Carnival is collective, costumed-for-parade, feather-radial rather than fruit-still-life.
- **Easter:** pastel-only palette, eggs, bunnies, spring domesticity, calm composition. Shares confetti pastels but never feathers, glittered bodies or parade density.
- **Mandrake:** urban Brazilian funk flash — sportswear, branded logos, slicked hair, gold chains, favela/street backdrop, posed cool. No feathers, no parade, no confetti.
- **Manguebeat:** Recife 1990s movement imagery — mud, crabs, psychedelic retro-grunge, earth tones with acid accents, band iconography. Gritty and conceptual where Carnival is glittering and bodily; overlap only in Pernambuco frevo motifs, which in Carnival appear as umbrellas and street dance in full polychrome.

**Quick recognition checklist:** saturated polychrome + gold accent; feather radial headdress or mask; glitter speculars; confetti/streamer particles or crowd density; festive gesture; night floodlight or hot daylight. Four or more present → Brazilian Carnival.
