# Vector Música

Vector Música (catalogued earlier as Música Metro; the name is simply "vector" plus the Spanish word for music) is a subgenre of Vectordelia that emerged in the late 2000s and peaked into the early 2010s as the house style of music-themed media for girls and teenagers. Where parent Vectordelia is urban and high-contrast — bright CMYK inks, jagged splatters, stark black breakdancer crowds — Vector Música softens every one of those impulses: pastel gradients replace primaries, smooth curves replace splatters, sparkling bokeh replaces grit, and solitary dreamers with headphones replace the crowd. It lived on anime idol promo art (Suite PreCure♪, Winx Club's Musa, Project DIVA backgrounds), Disney Channel transition graphics (Hannah Montana, Victorious, Violetta), Just Dance routine backdrops, karaoke interfaces, Sanrio and Claire's back-to-school stationery, and even mall wall murals. Its cultural job was to make music visible as harmless magic: melody drawn as ribbon, sound as sparkle.

## Visual Identity

**Color palette.** The field is always high-key and pastel: pastel pink (#FFD9E8 to #F7B8D0), blush-cream and near-white (#FFF4F8, #FFFFFF), lavender (#E3D7F0 to #C9A9DD), and baby blue (#BFE3F2 to #A9D3EA), usually blended in soft linear or radial gradients rather than flat blocks. Against this whisper-light field, magenta (#E6399B to #D6218F) acts as the strongest line color — the ink of staves, clefs, swooshes and script logos — so the style reads as "magenta drawing on pink mist." Sparse candy accents (lemon, mint, peach, turquoise at tint strength) may appear as individual notes, bubbles or rings, and silhouetted figures render in soft grey-mauve (#B9A7B4, #8E7C8A) or tinted gradients, never solid black. Rule of thumb: no hue ever reaches full saturation across a large area; saturation is reserved for thin lines and small motifs.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them.**
1. *The curved musical staff* — the primary compositional element: a five-line stave bent into a smooth Bézier sweep that flows across the canvas and guides the eye. Everything else hangs off it.
2. *Scattering music notes and treble clefs* — eighth notes, beamed pairs, oversized clefs — riding the staff's curve or drifting free of it, in magenta fills, pastel gradients, or outline-only strokes.
3. *Hearts and stars* — small punctuations of affection and magic among the notes.
4. *Floral filigree* — tendrils, blossoms and line-art petals borrowed from Vectorbloom, intertwining with the staff.
5. *Solitary figures* — a single listener in headphones, a singer at a microphone, a dancer mid-twirl, or an idol/magical-girl character; rendered as soft grey or tinted-gradient silhouettes, or as clean promo-art characters placed atop the vector field.
6. *Bokeh effects* — soft out-of-focus light circles, radial white glows, four-point sparkle glints, and halftone dot screens fading at the edges.

Supporting vocabulary observed across the style's range: glossy translucent spheres and thin rings clustered like bubbles along a curve; wireframe wave-meshes of hairline parallel lines; pearl-string arcs; an occasional piano-keyboard strip used as a decorative band; rainbow-gradient ribbons; cursive script wordmarks ("Music," character names) in magenta or white.

**Materials and texture.** This is flat digital vector work of the Adobe Illustrator era: crisp Bézier paths, linear and radial gradient fills, transparency layering, halftone dot screens, and Gaussian-soft bokeh discs. Surfaces are immaculate — no paper grain, no brushstroke, no photo texture. At most a faint speckle or watercolor-wash tint may breathe in the background field; gloss appears only as gradient highlights on orbs and bubbles, never as photorealistic glass or water.

**Recurring subjects.** Abstract notation fields with no figure at all are fully canonical and common. Beyond them: headphones as a hero object; a lone singer or dancer silhouette; idol and magical-girl characters in frilled costumes; product-like vignettes (headphones, microphones); environmental graphics scaled to walls, banners and interfaces.

**Composition and lighting.** The staff is the spine: it enters one edge and exits another as an S-curve, diagonal sweep, or horizon band, with motif density graded along it (a dense cluster at one crest thinning to scattered stragglers). Negative space is generous and deliberate — white or the palest tint left open for a logo or script word. Layering is done with opacity, not shadow. Lighting is uniformly high-key and diffuse: a radial white glow blooming from a corner or center, bokeh discs standing in for light sources, tiny cross-glints as sparks. There are no cast shadows, no directional sun, no night.

## Mood & Values

Harmony, whimsy, elegance, melody, magical transformation. The style expresses music as a gentle, feminine magic: sound made visible as ribbon and sparkle, never as noise or rebellion. It values optimism, youth, friendship and idol-stage dreaminess; its kindness is commercial and welcoming — the visual language of stationery racks, karaoke screens and mall murals meant to make a teenager feel the room is hers. Even at its most energetic (idol promo art, dance-game backdrops) the energy is buoyant, not aggressive.

## Era & Origins

Late 2000s, peaking into the early 2010s, in the orbit of Japanese magical-girl and virtual-idol franchises (Mermaid Melody through PriPara; Hatsune Miku: Project DIVA), Western teen-sitcom branding (Disney Channel and Violetta), and rhythm games (Just Dance). It arose as a deliberate softening of Vectordelia's urban starkness and rode the same vector-tooling boom as Vectorbloom and Frutiger Aero, sharing their gradients and gloss while keeping its subject strictly melodic. Its decline tracks the mid-2010s fade of flat pastel vector stock art, though successor idol media (Soy Luna) kept the vocabulary alive.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Lay down a high-key pastel gradient or white field first; light is diffuse and shadowless.
2. Draw at least one curved five-line staff as the compositional spine, sweeping edge to edge.
3. Populate it with scattering notes, a treble clef, and small hearts/stars; vary scale and let some motifs be outline-only.
4. Add one or two embellishment families maximum from: floral filigree tendrils, bokeh glow discs, halftone dot fade, glossy bubble cluster, sparkle glints.
5. If a figure appears, keep it solitary and soft — grey-mauve silhouette, tinted gradient, or clean idol character — never a crowd, never solid black.
6. Keep all edges vector-crisp and all fills gradient-smooth; reserve magenta for linework and small pops.
7. Optional cursive script wordmark in a corner of reserved negative space.

**Variation axes.** Subject may range from pure abstract notation → headphones or microphone vignette → lone singer/dancer silhouette → idol or magical-girl character → environmental mural or interface backdrop. Composition may range from diagonal S-sweep → centered radial glow with orbiting notes → horizontal banner bands (with or without keyboard strip) → vertical wallpaper column → sparse minimal corner cluster on white. Color handling may range from monochrome magenta-on-white → pink-dominant with lavender mist → full pastel-rainbow tint accents → cream/blush field. Lighting may range from flat even high-key → central white bloom → dense bokeh-field diffuse. Mood may range from calm elegance to buoyant pop energy. Aspect ratio is free: widescreen banner, square jacket, vertical mobile wallpaper, mural proportion.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A field of nothing but bubbles and a single clef on lavender mist is as valid as an idol portrait on pink staves, provided the palette, vector medium and melodic spine logic hold.

**Avoid.** Stark black silhouettes, crowds and breakdancers; jagged ink splatters, grunge distress, torn edges; saturated CMYK primaries on black or white urban layouts; photorealistic gloss, water droplets, glass, fish, skies or grass (Frutiger Aero territory); rhinestones, chrome type, hot-pink-with-black blocks, velour and logo-mania (McBling); photographic pianos, candlelight, aged sheet-music paper, dark moody interiors (Pianocore); painterly brushwork, impasto, collage, film grain; hard drop shadows, bevels, full 3D renders, neon-on-black; hard-edged glitter instead of soft glints; character-first cel-shaded narrative scenes with no vector notation field.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | The tell that separates it |
|---|---|
| Frutiger Aero | Shares gloss and bokeh, but Frutiger Aero is skeuomorphic and photoreal — water, glass, fish, sky, grass, eco-tech utopia. Vector Música is flat vector notation on pastel mist, with no photoreal nature at all. |
| Magical Girl | Shares idol characters and sparkles, but Magical Girl is character-first cel-shaded narrative art (costumes, transformation poses). Vector Música is background-first: the staff-and-note field defines it, and it remains Vector Música with no character present. |
| McBling | Shares pink and sparkle, but McBling is early-2000s hot pink plus black, chrome, rhinestones and hard glitter. Vector Música is pastel, not hot; its sparkle is soft bokeh, and it never uses black contrast blocks. |
| Pianocore | Shares music, but Pianocore is acoustic and classical — photographic or painterly pianos, paper sheet music, candlelit cream-and-black rooms. Vector Música is digital-pop, abstract, high-key, and its instruments are stylized vector glyphs at most. |
| Vectorbloom | A sibling vector-ornament style whose spine is floral: blossoms, vines, leaves, in any saturation. Vector Música borrows its filigree, but the spine must be musical notation; if flowers dominate and notes vanish, it has crossed into Vectorbloom. |
| Vectordelia (parent) | The stark original: black silhouettes, CMYK brightness, jagged splatters, urban energy. Vector Música is precisely the softening of each of those traits. |

When in doubt, apply the two-part test: is the image flat vector with a pastel high-key field, and is curved musical notation its structural spine? Both yes → Vector Música.
