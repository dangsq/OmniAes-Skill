# Grunge

Grunge is the anti-fashion subculture that grew out of the Pacific Northwest underground of the mid-1980s and briefly owned the 1990s mainstream. Born in Seattle around the Sub Pop label, it fused punk's DIY rawness with the sludgy weight of early heavy metal, and its visual side followed the same logic: thrift-store clothing, working-class utility, and a graphic language of torn paper, photocopy decay and eroded type borrowed from gig posters, zines and album covers. Everything in grunge looks handled, weathered and slightly broken on purpose — flannel gone soft at the cuffs, denim split at the knee, posters wheatpasted over each other, photographs blurred by motion and darkroom abuse. It is a style of deliberate imperfection, opposed point-for-point to the glossy excess of 1980s glam metal and consumer culture.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The rule is desaturation toward earth and shadow. The working range sits between muddy browns (#5C4632–#8A6F4D), army and olive greens (#4A5320–#6B705C), concrete and photocopy greys (#8C8C88–#C4C2BC), burgundy and oxblood (#5E1F2A–#7A2E3A), and faded denim blues (#7C8B99–#A9B4BE), anchored by near-black (#121212) and newsprint off-white (#E6E0D2). Tan and mustard appear as leather-boot accents (#B98A44), rust as decay. A fully monochrome variant is canonical: high-contrast black and white with crushed blacks and blown stage highlights. Saturated color is allowed only as a puncture, never a field — one fuchsia spray mark, one red cigarette pack, one magenta hand-drawn symbol dropped into an otherwise muted frame. The soft/pastel grunge subgenre may wash the base toward lilac, mint and dusty pink, but the muted, faded quality must survive the pastelizing.

### Signature motifs and objects, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Flannel shirts** — oversized, worn open over a tee or tied at the waist; plaid is the style's pattern anchor.
2. **Ripped, faded, baggy denim** — frayed holes at knee and thigh, washed-out indigo, wide slouchy legs.
3. **Vintage band t-shirts** — soft, cracked prints, layered under plaid shirts or babydoll dresses.
4. **Combat boots and sneakers** — Dr. Martens, work boots, worn Converse; heavy footwear against thin legs or baggy hems.
5. **Oversized knit sweaters and slouchy beanies** — moth-eaten, pilling, swallowed hands.
6. **Long unkempt hair and androgynous, unstyled bodies** — no salon finish, no posed musculature.

Secondary motifs documented in the style's graphic and photographic record: torn paper edges, ink splatters, scratches and photocopy degradation; spray-painted and hand-scrawled type; collage layering of crooked snapshots; skateboards, graffiti walls, VHS tapes, toe rings; cigarette packs, beer cans and cassette clutter as period props (present in the photographic record rather than ranked as key motifs); and the album-cover iconography rules — the distressed beauty queen with tiara and smudged mascara, figures half-buried in cracked desert earth, inverted or blurred band portraits over burning forest, anti-consumerist symbols such as a dollar bill on a fishhook, and somber stagings of candles and lilies for acoustic, funeral-quiet scenes.

### Materials and textures

Wool flannel brushed soft, faded cotton jersey, denim frayed to white threads, scuffed leather biker jackets, canvas sneaker toes, pilling knit wool. Paper matters as much as cloth: newsprint, xerox copies gone grey, torn poster edges, halftone dots, typewriter pages. Surfaces are cracked concrete, damp wood, rust, stained plaster, dirt and dry earth. Every medium carries analogue damage — film grain, dust, scratches, misregistered print, scan lines.

### Recurring subjects

Musicians mid-lurch onstage and mosh pits in motion; slacker portraits slumped on floors, curbs, stairwells and benches; deadpan group band poses against handmade backdrops; thrift-store racks and heaped piles of worn clothing and objects; object studies of boots, jeans or packs laid on weathered ground; Pacific Northwest vernacular exteriors — parking lots, basements, garages, wooded clearcuts, peeling house walls; zine spreads, gig posters and magazine layouts; distressed prom-queen figures.

### Composition and lighting

Frames behave like snapshots: uncropped edges that cut limbs, tilted horizons, off-center subjects, motion blur and missed focus, cluttered foregrounds. Collage layouts layer images crookedly with uneven baselines and no grid. A deadpan frontal symmetry is equally acceptable — the posed group portrait or the centered object on cracked concrete — as long as the materiality stays worn. Lighting is found, never built: overcast diffuse daylight, a single harsh on-camera flash in a dark room, high-contrast black-and-white stage light, dim tungsten interiors, or the flat washed exposure of a VHS still.

### Typography and graphic language

Type is damage: handwritten scrawl, typewriter keys, ransom-note cutouts, spray-painted tags, letters stretched, blurred, photocopied and eroded. Layouts reject symmetry and white space in favor of collage, overlap and torn edges. Clean corporate sans-serif is the enemy; even advertising of the era ("corporate grunge") faked its authenticity with dirty backgrounds and ink splatters.

## Mood & Values

Apathy, social alienation, angst and confinement, held together by a fierce claim to authenticity. Grunge values the thrifted over the bought, the worn over the new, the slouched over the posed; it mistrusts corporations, media and authority, and wears its disillusionment as identity. The emotional register is melancholy and sardonic rather than rageful — boredom as armor, decay as honesty, imperfection as proof of being real.

## Era & Origins

Emergent mid-1980s in Seattle, Washington around Sub Pop, as a reaction against 1980s glam metal glamour and consumerist excess; mainstream explosion 1991–92, decline by 1997. Its visual code was fixed early-90s by album covers, Ray Gun magazine's rule-breaking design, and high-contrast documentary concert photography, then recycled through 90s advertising, independent cinema and fashion catalogues. Afterlives include post-grunge, nu-grunge, and the Kinderwhore, soft grunge and pastel grunge subgenres.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Wear everything: no garment may look new — fade, fray, pill, stain, moth-eat.
- Mute the field: desaturate toward earth tones; a saturated note is a single printed or sprayed accent, never a lighting scheme.
- Damage the medium: grain, blur, crooked frame, photocopy decay, torn edge — include at least one analogue flaw.
- Slouch the body: weight collapsed into one hip, knees drawn up, shoulders folded; refuse heroic posture.
- Clutter honestly: heaps, racks, scattered objects beat minimal arrangement.
- Treat type as wreckage: eroded, handwritten, cut-and-paste; never crisp corporate lettering.
- Use found light: overcast sky, bare bulb, direct flash; never softbox glamour.

**Variation axes.** Subject may range across live performance, solo or group portrait, object still life, graphic layout, street or interior scene, and decayed landscape. Composition may range from chaotic snapshot to layered collage to deadpan centered portrait to overhead scatter. Lighting may range from overcast daylight to club flash to monochrome stage contrast to dim tungsten to VHS flatness. Color may range from the full earth palette to strict black and white to faded film casts to pastel-grunge washes that keep the muted base. Mood may range from bored apathy to raw anguish to tender melancholy to deadpan humor. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Glam-metal gloss: hairspray, spandex, chrome, neon, airbrushed skin, sunburst stadium sheen.
- Clean corporate design: symmetric grids, generous white space, crisp sans-serif branding, stock-photo lighting.
- Pristine clothing: pressed creases, stiff new denim, luxury logos, shining jewelry (a humble toe ring excepted).
- Frame-wide candy or jewel-tone saturation.
- Runway staging, fitness poses, manicured interiors, sunny beach light, overt cheerfulness.
- Sterile digital sharpness: no HDR clarity, no flawlessly clean sensor look.
- Neighbor-style overload: mohawks and safety-pin hardware (punk), blackletter band heraldry and patch vests (metal), editorial supermodel gauntness (heroin chic).

## Discrimination Cues

- **Graffiti Pop** shares spray marks and street walls, but graffiti pop is candy-bright, cartoonish and celebratory with clean vector energy; grunge keeps spray as one dirty accent inside a muted, decaying field.
- **Heroin Chic** shares 90s flash snapshots and pale gauntness, but heroin chic is fashion-editorial — professional models, designer clothing, magazine gloss; grunge is unstyled thrift worn by musicians and crowds, earth-toned and anti-fashion by intent.
- **Indie** shares alternative lineage and analogue warmth, but indie is twee and tidy: pastels, neat symmetry, earnest quirk; grunge is heavier, dirtier, distressed, angsty. Indie sleaze keeps party glamour and saturated flash where grunge keeps boredom.
- **Metalhead** shares long hair, leather and loud guitars, but metalhead is heroic and precise: patch vests, blackletter logos, pyrotechnic spectacle; grunge slouches in flannel, uses eroded handwritten type, and meets spectacle with irony.
- **Punk** shares DIY ethics and rebellion, but punk is fast and sharp: mohawks, safety pins, black-and-neon, crisp stencil politics, confrontational stance; grunge is slow and sludgy: baggy silhouettes, muted earth palette, apathy instead of open anger.

**Recognition test:** if every object in the frame looks thrifted, every surface looks weathered, the palette reads as mud and denim and newsprint, and the image itself carries a flaw of film or paper — it is grunge.
