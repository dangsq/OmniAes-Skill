# Heroic Realism

Heroic Realism — also called Propaganda Graphic Design — is the persuasive visual language of the twentieth century's mass mobilizations: recruitment drives, war-bond campaigns, labour plans, space-race triumphs and civic crusades. It borrows the technique of Realism but not its intent: everyday accuracy is replaced by believable idealization, so that a worker, soldier or citizen reads as a hero worth emulating. Figures are rendered realistically yet exaggerated to larger-than-life proportion; compositions surge with diagonal movement; colour is bold and emotionally loaded; and a legible typographic message almost always seals the argument. The style served democracies and dictatorships alike, which is why its grammar — not its politics — is the recognizable artifact: the same raised chin, the same radiating aura, the same imperative headline appear on a British recruiting sheet, a Soviet labour poster, a North Korean state print and a modern electoral screenprint.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The palette obeys spot-printing logic: a small set of flat, saturated inks laid on a paper ground, with shading achieved through tonal steps of the same hue or through painterly washes — never through photographic continuous gradient.

| Role | Hex range | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Paper ground | #F2E9D6 → #E6D9BC | aged cream / off-white; counts as one of the spots |
| Persuasion red | #C8102E → #8E1A1A | vermilion to oxide; banners, headscarves, sky fire |
| Institutional blue | #24408E → #3A5FA0; slate #5A6B7D | uniforms, work shirts, shadowed smoke |
| Ink black / warm black | #141210 → #2A2620 | text bands, silhouettes, outlines |
| Signal yellow / gold | #F5C518 → #C89B3C | fields of light, stars, accent ground |
| Secondary earth & sky | salmon #E8A089, olive #6B7A3A, sepia #6E4A2F | dawn skies, battlefield, engraving tones |

Rule: three to five spots including the ground. A red-dominant sheet, a blue-dominant sheet and a cream-ground sheet with red and black are all equally correct; monochrome engraving plus one spot (the postage-stamp variant) and modern three-tone posterization also sit inside the range.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the style documents them

1. **The idealized hero figure** — the core of the style: strong jaw, upright spine, flawless proportion, expression of resolve or serene confidence.
2. **Persuasive typography** — a caption or slogan integrated as design architecture, not overlaid decoration.
3. **Commanding hand gestures** — the pointing index finger at the viewer, the raised fist or clenched hand, the uplifted arm holding tool, flag, torch or rifle, the formal salute.
4. **National personifications** — Uncle Sam, Marianne, John Bull, Mother Russia, Germania: the nation as a body that can look at you and ask.
5. **Symbolic imagery** — flags, banners, stars, eagles, political icons; defeated enemy emblems trampled or torn.
6. **Dynamic diagonal composition** — movement as moral energy: striding legs, whipping fabric, angled flagpoles.
7. **Monumental scale jumps** — the hero towering over factories, battlefields, city skylines; allegorical size rather than optical size.
8. **The hero of labour** — wrench, hammer, sickle, sheaf, overalls, rolled sleeves, polka-dot or plain headscarf; production as frontline.
9. **Stylized animal emblems** — panther, horse, eagle standing in for a unit's force.
10. **Silhouetted masses** — aircraft formations, factory roofs, marching columns, smoke plumes behind the protagonist.
11. **Radiating rays and aura** — the surreal element: light bursting from behind the head or hand, sanctifying the figure.
12. **The threatened home vignette** — domestic interior or burning village posed as the stake of the argument.

### Materials, texture, medium

Lithograph and offset print on matte paper are the native surfaces: visible brushwork in skies and smoke, flat ink fields elsewhere, paper grain, faint edge wear, occasional slight misregistration of spots. Engraved line work appears in stamp and certificate variants. Silkscreen posterization — hard-edged tonal blocks with no brushwork — is the style's modern extension. Ink stays matte; there is no varnish, no gloss, no photographic grain.

### Composition and lighting

Vertical format dominates. The figure claims roughly half to four-fifths of the height, seen from a low viewpoint so the viewer looks up. The horizon sits low; diagonals carry the eye from headline to gesture to slogan band. Headlines occupy a top or bottom band, sometimes an angled ribbon; borders and rules are optional framing devices. Lighting is moral: a directional key from above-front sculpts cheekbone and jaw, a rim or halo separates the hero from the ground, rays or smoke build depth, and corners vignette toward darkness. Flat, even, shadowless light is equally valid in the posterized branch, where modelling is replaced by tonal blocks.

## Mood & Values

Awe, admiration, urgency, moral certainty. The style expresses patriotism, duty, strength, sacrifice and national pride, and treats labour as virtue and the individual as the embodied collective. Fear appears only as a spur to action — a burning home, a looming enemy — never as despair. Irony is absent; the poster believes itself completely.

## Era & Origins

Born in the First World War's mass recruitment and atrocity sheets, peaked in the 1920s–1930s as state doctrine — Soviet Socialist Realism's "New Soviet Man," Germany's classicizing idealism — and expanded through the Second World War's bond, enlistment and factory campaigns. It persisted through Cold War space-race and civic posters and survives in electoral screenprints and retro pastiche. It defines itself against two neighbours: modernist abstraction, which it rejects as illegible, and neutral Realism, whose technique it keeps while swapping accuracy for idealization.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

- Decide the imperative first — enlist, produce, endure, celebrate, remember — and let layout argue for it.
- Build one idealized protagonist (or a symbolic pair); anatomy noble, posture upright, gaze at the viewer or toward a radiant horizon.
- Make the gesture the verb: point, clench, raise, salute, stride.
- Limit yourself to three-to-five spot inks on a paper ground; shade with tonal steps and washes, not gradients.
- Keep print logic: matte ink, paper grain, flat fields, painterly sky or smoke where depth is needed.
- Use monumental scale relations — hero above machines, masses, cities — rather than realistic perspective alone.
- Light the figure as if light were approval: key from above, halo or rays behind, vignette at edges.
- Set type as architecture: bold condensed all-caps grotesque or slab in a band; a brush-script accent line is permitted; legibility is non-negotiable.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject*: soldier, sailor, pilot, nurse, factory worker of any trade, farmer, engineer, scientist, cosmonaut, athlete, national personification, stylized animal mascot, family or home under threat; peacetime causes (literacy, savings, vaccination, space exploration) are fully in range.
- *Composition*: bust, three-quarter, or full striding figure; single hero, symbolic pair, or hero above silhouetted masses; centred monument or offset diagonal; with or without narrative vignette; framed stamp-format variant.
- *Lighting*: painterly chiaroscuro with rays through to flat even screenprint light; storm-grey or salmon dawn skies.
- *Colour*: red-dominant, blue-dominant, cream-ground with red and black, engraved monochrome plus one spot, or three-tone posterization.
- *Mood*: triumphant, urgent warning, solemn duty, joyful labour.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — idealized figure, limited spot palette, print logic, monumental scale, message hierarchy — not any reference image.

**Avoid**

- Photographic candor: fatigue, asymmetry, casual posture, snapshot cropping, lens flare, bokeh, HDR microcontrast, muted cinematic grades.
- Irony, camp, satire, meme captions, any wink at the viewer.
- Neon glow, synth gradients, VHS decay, holograms, chrome, glossy 3D render sheen.
- Pastel consumer palettes and googie shapes; watercolor softness; impressionist brushwork; non-objective abstraction.
- A minimal single-object layout with no figure and no imperative (that is Plakatstil, not this).
- Machine fetish: rivets, oil grime and speculative analog tech as protagonist (Dieselpunk).
- Sentimental ornamental leader-worship décor with no message hierarchy (Totalitarian Kitsch).
- Editorial layouts led by small serif body copy; handwritten scrawl as primary type.
- A leaderless crowd with no focal hero, or victimhood with no call to action.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbour | Shares | Decisive difference |
|---|---|---|
| American Kitsch | Mid-century US print lineage, bold type | Kitsch sells leisure: diners, tailfins, chrome, pastel mint-pink-turquoise, humour. Heroic Realism sells duty: monumental figure, flag spots, imperative text. |
| Socialist Realism | Idealized heroes, red banners | SR is Heroic Realism's Soviet child: hammer and sickle, wheat, tractors, golden harvest light, hero of labour. Those icons mark SR; the parent style also covers recruitment, personifications and non-Soviet national colours. |
| Sovietwave | Soviet iconography, cosmonauts | Sovietwave is nostalgic dream: neon cyan-magenta glow, grid horizons, VHS haze, wistful mood, no imperative. Heroic Realism is opaque ink, radiating rays, sincere forward thrust. |
| Totalitarian Kitsch | Monumental leaders, state symbols | TK sentimentalizes or ironizes the language as décor — porcelain gloss, ornamental excess, camp repetition — and drops message clarity. Heroic Realism remains functional persuasion on matte paper. |
| Dieselpunk | Wartime machinery, period dress | Dieselpunk makes riveted machines and grime the subject in a speculative fiction. Here machines are background silhouettes serving a human hero, and the surface is clean print, not grease fetish. |

When in doubt, ask three questions: does a single idealized human body carry the message; is the colour a small set of flat inks on paper; and does the composition argue an imperative? Three yeses is Heroic Realism.
