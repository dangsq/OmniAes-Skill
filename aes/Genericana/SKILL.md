# Genericana

Genericana (also called Contemporary-Conformist or Industriana) is the look of a brand inventing itself a grandfather. It borrows the commercial vernacular of pre-war America — hand-lettered shop signs, fruit-crate labels, apothecary bottles, trade cards, felt pennants — and rebuilds it as contemporary packaging, posters, logos and storefronts, producing a deliberately fake sense of premium quality and heritage. It is design as provenance cosplay: every ribbon banner and "Est." date is a claim of longevity that the object does not actually have. The style surfaced in the 1980s, ran through the early 2010s, and reached saturation as the house language of hipster-era branding during the Great Recession, when craft, thrift and analog honesty became marketable virtues. Its tell is not a subject but a *method*: lettering behaves like the image, and every surface behaves like a material you could splinter, crease or dent.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The ground is almost always either a warm paper cream or a deep ink black; saturated color arrives as a limited set of heritage inks layered on top, rarely more than two per piece.

- **Ink black / coal:** `#171310` – `#2B2723` — label fields, cover grounds, pennant felt.
- **Cream / off-white paper:** `#F6F0DF` – `#E9E0C8` — the default "printed stock" color; also the lettering color when the ground is dark.
- **Brick to coral red:** `#A3262C` – `#E5796A` — banners, poster fields, circular badges, stamp inks.
- **Heritage blue:** navy grounds `#1C2A47` – `#27506F`, with a mid printing-blue `#2E6DA4` as accent. Blue is a declared key color but in practice behaves as ground or secondary ink rather than the dominant field.
- **Light brown / kraft / ochre:** `#C9A876`, `#B9854C` – `#D99A3D` — cardboard, wood, tan label stock, ochre cover fields.
- **Supporting greens (optional, secondary):** bottle green `#1E5C4A`, muted sage `#A9B89C` — apothecary and mercantile accents.

Rule of thumb: cream or black carries the piece; red, blue and brown appear as inks and substrates; nothing is neon, nothing gradients, and white is never a cool optical white — it is always paper with a temperature.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the style documents them

1. **Vintage typography as subject.** Sign-painting fonts, bold serifs, Victorian wood type, arched and curved baselines, chalkboard hand-lettering. In Genericana the lettering *is* the picture: type specimens, alphabet posters, titles built into silhouettes, names arched over emblems.
2. **Old-school packaging lockups.** Re-created badges, mid-century labels and crests: circular or oval emblems, name plus descriptor plus founding date, symmetrical flourishes, framed borders with corner ornaments.
3. **Skeuomorphic analog marks.** Ribbons, banners, postage stamps with perforated edges, seals, tickets and stitched felt imported into physical and digital branding to manufacture authenticity — a digital layout will still pretend to be stamped, tied and perforated.
4. **Rustic industrial substrate.** Reclaimed wood planks, exposed brick, rusted steel, Edison bulbs and gooseneck lamps; hanging oval or round storefront signs; the workshop and the old-shop interior as stage set.
5. **The container canon.** Apothecary glass bottles, round pomade and tobacco tins, jars, kraft boxes and booklets, memo books, felt pennants — small handheld objects that give the label something to wrap.

### Materials and textures

Letterpress ink squeeze and slight misregistration; paper tooth and kraft corrugate; felt and stitched edges; wood grain with knots and nail holes; brick mortar; rust bloom on steel; rolled tin rims; the dull sheen of glass. Suraces stay matte — gloss appears only as a faint highlight on a bottle shoulder, never as a finish.

### Recurring subjects

Label and packaging design (spirits, pomade, sanitizer, jars, tins); posters and album or book covers built from lettering; logo lockups and crest designs; type specimens and stamp sheets; menus and infographic sheets laid out like trade broadsides; storefronts and shop interiors with hanging signs and wooden fit-outs; pennants and ephemera pinned to weathered walls; orderly product rows photographed against plain grounds.

### Composition and lighting

Composition is frontal, centered and symmetrical: an emblem or name on a vertical axis, arched type above, ribbon or descriptor below, ornate rule borders framing the field. Variants include the diagonal sash banner across an illustrated field, the all-over type-specimen grid, the lettering packed inside a silhouette shape, and the environmental shot of a shopfront or interior where signage hangs in rows. Lighting is either soft, shadowless, catalog-even studio light for labels and products; warm pooled tungsten from Edison bulbs and gooseneck fixtures for interiors; or diffuse overcast daylight raking across weathered wood. Contrast is moderate, highlights are soft, and the whole image reads as printed matter or documented craft, never as dramatic photography.

## Mood & Values

Manufactured heritage, sincerely worn. Genericana sells permanence to a disposable era: small-batch honesty, trade craft, thrift-era solidity, the reassurance of a name that claims to have existed since 1922. It is nostalgic without being dreamy — the nostalgia is commercial, aimed at trust and premium perception rather than memory. The emotional register is confident, workmanlike and slightly ceremonial, like a handshake from a company that prints its own labels. Irony sits just under the surface (the heritage is invented), but the visual language itself commits fully; a wink would break the spell.

## Era & Origins

Declared origin in the 1980s, with the style's active span running from the 1980s to the early 2010s; its source material is the American commercial vernacular of the 1920s–1950s — sign painters' alphabets, crate labels, trade cards, mercantile signage. It gained mass popularity inside the hipster trend during the Great Recession, when recession-era consumers equated hand-made-looking with trustworthy. It reacts against glossy digital minimalism, mass-produced disposability and frictionless tech branding by insisting on analog texture at every layer. Neighboring aesthetics include Decoplex, Hipness Purgatory, Hipster, Live Laugh Love, Paperback Chic and Soft Colonial Wanderlust.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. **Lettering leads.** If the image contains type, the type must be sign-painting serif, wood type, ornate Victorian display or chalkboard hand — arched, curved, flourished, locked into emblems. Modern sans-serif minimalism is outside the style even as body text.
2. **Invent a history.** Give the design provenance machinery: a badge or crest, a ribbon banner, an "Est." date, a seal or stamp, a descriptor line ("Sharp · Stylist · Wings · Fangs" logic), symmetrical ornaments.
3. **Show the substrate.** Let the material be visible: kraft paper edge, felt stitch, wood grain, brick course, tin rim, glass thickness. The object should look produced by a trade, not by a factory line.
4. **Hold the palette.** Cream or ink-black ground, one or two heritage inks (brick red, navy/mid blue, ochre/kraft), optional bottle green or sage support. No gradients, no neon, no cool digital white.
5. **Skeuomorph even in flat layouts.** Posters, menus and digital-looking sheets should still carry stamps, perforations, ribbons and printed-matter artifacts.
6. **Light like a catalog or a workshop.** Even shadowless studio light for objects; warm Edison tungsten for interiors; soft daylight on weathered wood. Keep finishes matte.
7. **Center and symmetrize.** Default to a frontal, balanced, emblem-on-axis composition; break symmetry only with a diagonal sash or a specimen grid.

**Variation axes (what may change)**

- *Subject:* any packaged good, poster, album or book cover, logo lockup, type specimen, stamp sheet, menu broadside, pennant, storefront, shop interior, product row, or plain emblem on a wall — the style is a branding language, not a subject list.
- *Composition:* centered circular badge, ornate framed border, diagonal banner over illustration, silhouette filled with lettering, all-over specimen grid, environmental signage shot, flat-lay row on a plain ground.
- *Lighting:* flat even studio, warm pooled tungsten interior, diffuse daylight on raw wood; all low-gloss.
- *Color handling:* cream-dominant, black-dominant with cream lettering, red-field poster, navy-ground lockup, kraft/ochre cover; green or sage as secondary field is acceptable.
- *Mood:* carnival-ornate (circus lettering), austere trade (black and cream only), apothecary-delicate (sage, pink, thin serifs), rugged industrial (brick, steel, bulbs).

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — heritage lettering, provenance machinery, honest substrate, restricted palette — not any reference image.

**Avoid (style-breakers)**

- Neon colors, gradients, chrome, holography, glossy plastic, lens flare, drop shadows.
- Minimalist Swiss grids, geometric sans-serif branding, flat tech-startup logos, modern UI elements, QR codes and smartphone props.
- Flat mid-century cartoon illustration of domestic or atomic-age life (that is Hipness Purgatory's territory).
- Art Deco sunbursts, stepped zigzags, brass-and-gold glamour geometry (Decoplex).
- Painted pulp scenes on yellowed, dog-eared paperback stock (Paperback Chic).
- Cursive inspirational platitudes on whitewashed shabby-chic wood with burlap and mason jars (Live Laugh Love).
- Heavy grunge distressing, splatter and torn-poster decay: wear in Genericana is material honesty (grain, crease, rust bloom), not texture-overlay ruin.
- Cool white backgrounds, saturated candy color, and any ironic parody that winks at the viewer.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs. Decoplex:** Decoplex revives Art Deco luxury — symmetrical sunbursts, zigzag steps, brass and gold on lacquer black, ballroom glamour. Genericana is vernacular trade, not deco luxe: ribbons, badges, wood type, kraft and brick, the workshop rather than the foyer.
- **Vs. Hipness Purgatory:** Hipness Purgatory is flat mid-century illustration — muted teal, mustard and avocado fields with deadpan atomic-age figures and domestic scenes. Genericana is lettering-driven commercial label work rooted in pre-war trade signage; it illustrates products and emblems, not suburban life.
- **Vs. Paperback Chic:** Paperback Chic is the painted pulp cover — dramatic illustrated scenes, worn yellowed paper, pocket-book proportions. Genericana book and album covers are typographic constructions: lettering arranged into silhouettes, ornate titles, framed rules, with no painterly narrative scene.
- **Vs. Hipster:** Hipster is the wider lifestyle umbrella — fashion, lo-fi snapshot photography, irony, fixed-gear bicycles. Genericana is specifically hipster-era's *branding and design arm*: crafted trade-sign typography and heritage packaging. A lo-fi casual photo of a coffee cup is Hipster; a letterpressed crest on a kraft cup sleeve is Genericana.
- **Vs. Live Laugh Love:** Live Laugh Love is mass-market home décor sentiment — script platitudes, distressed white paint, burlap, mason jars, saccharine warmth. Genericana shares rustic materials but keeps commercial logic: product names, descriptors, founding dates, trade-card structure, and bold sign-painting serifs instead of inspirational cursive.

**Quick recognition test:** arched bold serif or wood-type lettering + badge/ribbon/seal provenance machinery + cream-or-black ground with brick red, navy or kraft inks + a visible honest substrate (wood, brick, kraft, tin, felt) + matte, even or tungsten-warm light = Genericana.
