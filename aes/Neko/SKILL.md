# Neko

Neko is the visual culture of the cat-person: an otherwise ordinary human body annotated with cat ears, a tail, and often paw-soft mittens, then wrapped in the softest materials available. The word simply means "cat" in Japanese; *nekomimi* is the accurate term for cat-people, and *kemonomimi* for animal-eared people generally. As a modern aesthetic, Neko crystallized in 2000s otaku culture and surged in the 2010s through meme and stan edits, Picrew avatar creation, TikTok, and the Kawaii Gamer and E-Girl communities, where adding ears and a tail to any portrait became a staple gesture of self-fashioning. Its charm is exaggerated childlike femininity — playful, cuddly, occasionally bratty kitten behavior performed through fashion rather than identity: wearers of Neko dress up and roleplay, and most explicitly do not consider themselves furries or cats. The aesthetic recurs inside Erokawa, intersects Lolita and Maid fashion through its dresses and maid-café context, and touches petplay (kittenplay) at its edges, though the majority of its imagery is simply cute.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The color rule is pastel-first, high-key, with black used only as a spice. The base range runs from cherry-blossom and blush pinks (`#FFD9E8`, `#F9C5D5`, `#F4A7C3`) through lilac and lavender (`#D8BFD8`, `#C8A2C8`) into cream and off-white (`#FFF8F2`, `#FFFDFD`), with soft butter yellow (`#FFF3C4`) and rose gold (`#B76E79`) as warm support. Metallic gold (`#D4AF37`) appears almost exclusively as small hardware — bells, rings, clasps — catching the light. Black (`#141414`) is permitted as a contrast accent (opaque stockings, a cat silhouette, a pastel-goth ribbon) but never as the ground color. A saturated blush pink (`#FF8FB3`) functions as an effect color: cheek glow, blush stickers, tinted bloom. In illustration, a legitimate handling is pink monochrome duotone — line and tone in a single pink (`#F06292`-family) on paper white, sometimes with screentone and hand-lettered sound effects. Yellow and rose gold are documented in the style's key colors but surface sparingly in practice, mostly as metal and confectionery tones; treat them as optional warm notes, not pillars.

### Signature motifs, ranked by how firmly the style documents them

1. **Cat ears and tail on a human figure** — the defining mark. Pointed triangular ears, often with visible fluffy inner-ear fur, worn as headbands, clips, or drawn in; a long thin flexible tail behind.
2. **Fluffiness as a thesis** — over-sized pom-pom hoodies and sweaters, woolen coats, plush surfaces everywhere; the silhouette should look soft to touch.
3. **Paws** — big fluffy mittens shaped like cat paws with padded pink toe beans, long fluffy gloves, paw prints printed on fabric, paw pads on the soles of stockings.
4. **Bells** — small round bells dangling from chokers, ear headbands, and ribbon knots.
5. **Chokers and collars**, typically carrying either a bell or a heart-shaped ring.
6. **Bows and ribbons**, frequently attached to the choker or the cat ears themselves.
7. **Stockings** — long, with cat-like patterns, cat shapes, or paws on the bottoms.
8. **Frills, maid dresses, maid-café context** — the Lolita and Maid intersection: aprons, ruffled hems, frilled bonnets (the bonnet may even go on a real cat).
9. **Bright pink blush and effects** on people and characters — flushed cheeks, heart and sparkle overlays.
10. **Cartoon catgirl drawings and actual cats** as companions or subjects — cat-shaped sweets, cookies, hoodies with ear hoods, paw-print ponchos.
11. **General kawaii imagery** — hearts, stars, glitter, confectionery.

### Materials and textures

Faux-fur pile and plush minky for mittens, ears and tails; chunky rib-knit wool and fleece for hoodies, sleeves and coats; satin for ribbons; lace and cotton frill for maid and Lolita layers; opaque nylon for stockings; polished brass or gold for bells and heart rings; soft appliqué padding for paw beans. Surfaces should read matte, piled, or gently lustrous — never glossy latex, never hard plastic or metal beyond the small bell punctuation.

### Recurring subjects

Catgirls are the core subject; feminized catboys (traditionally feminine clothes or softened features, though masculine clothing appears depending on media and person) are the documented secondary one. Beyond people: real cats dressed in frills or posed as companions; accessory still-lifes (ear headbands, mittens, chokers arranged as product); cat-shaped food and merchandise; avatar-style characters in the Picrew idiom; and manga or anime catgirl illustrations, including flat pink duotone panels.

### Composition and lighting tendencies

Portraits framed so the ears break or crown the top edge; hands raised in a curled "paw pose" at cheek or chest height, fingers tucked; extreme close-ups of mittened hands or a single accessory treated as the whole subject; full-body fashion shots in soft domestic interiors (beds, blankets, pale walls); flat-lay and product arrangements; manga panels with hand-lettered sound effects. Framing tends centered, close, and intimate, with shallow depth of field. Lighting is high-key and diffuse: overexposed whites, soft window light, bloom and haze, sparkle cross-screen flares and glitter sticker overlays. Illustration handles light flatly — clean line with even tone, or duotone with screentone — rather than modeled shading.

## Mood & Values

The governing value is cuteness, pursued without irony. Neko expresses exaggerated childlike femininity and kitten personality: playful, cuddly, a little bratty. It is an aesthetic of self-invention — avatar creation, photo edits, and cosplay let anyone author themselves as a cat-person, which is why the style holds particular meaning in transfem communities as an image of an ideal self. It values softness as comfort: plush textures, pale light, and small tinkling bells construct a world that cannot hurt you. Roleplay is consensual play, not identity: wearers enjoy dressing up without claiming to be cats, and most Neko fashion carries no petplay meaning at all, even though collars and the owner/pet dynamic of kittenplay sit nearby at the style's border.

## Era & Origins

The trope is old: Japanese mythology's cat yōkai (bake-neko, neko-mata), a 1700s misemono spectacle depicting a cat/woman hybrid ("nekomusume"), and Kenji Miyazawa's 1924 book *The 4th of Narcissus Month*, the first modern catgirls. Catwoman and Cheetah (1940) carried the idea into comics; anime picked it up with *Ousama no Shippo* (1949) and, decisively, *GeGeGe no Kitarō* (1968), whose Neko Musume likely popularized "neko" for cat-people. The 1980s built the trope's modern grammar — *Star of the Cottonland* (1984), the Puma sisters of *Dominion Tank Police* (1988), *Ranma ½* (1989) — and the late 1990s–2000s fixed its current look via *Hyper Police* (1997), *Outlaw Star* (1998), *Tokyo Mew Mew* (2002) and *Soul Eater* (2008), after which the only real innovation was catboys. The aesthetic as a fashion and internet style belongs to the 2000s onward, exploding in the 2010s with otaku, meme, stan, E-Girl and Kawaii Gamer culture.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Human first, cat added: a human face and body with catness applied as ears, tail, mittens, or printed motifs — never an animal skull or fur-covered body.
2. Pastel ground, black spice: build the image on pink/lilac/cream, and let black appear only in accents like stockings or a silhouette.
3. Fluff is non-negotiable: include at least one piled or knitted surface (fur mitten, pom-pom hoodie, wool coat, plush blanket).
4. Small metal punctuation: a bell or heart ring that catches the light anchors the accessory language.
5. Blush and sparkle: pink cheek glow, heart stickers, glitter flares are welcome post-effects.
6. Playful gesture: curled paw hands, head tilts, tucked fingers, pouty-bratty or blissful expressions; the body language of a kitten, not a model.
7. High-key softness in photography (diffuse, overexposed, hazy); flat clean line or pink duotone in illustration.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may be any cat-person (girl or boy), a real cat in kawaii framing, cat-shaped sweets or merchandise, an accessory still-life, an avatar, or an illustrated catgirl — ordinary objects cat-ified (ear-hood ponchos, paw-print cookies, bell chokers on a hanger) are fully in range. Medium may be cosplay photography, softie lifestyle photo, anime cel-style illustration, manga duotone panel, chibi icon, 3D avatar render, or product flat-lay. Composition may run from macro close-up of mittens to full-body fashion shot to arranged flat-lay to comic panel. Lighting may be bright diffuse daylight, warm soft interior, sparkle-filtered haze, or entirely flat illustration light. Color handling may mix the full pastel range, narrow to pink-and-lilac, adopt pastel-plus-black, or collapse into pink monochrome. Mood may be playful, cuddly, bratty, shy, or dreamy. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image — a catboy in a grey paw-print poncho outdoors and a duotone manga panel of a blushing catgirl are equally valid Neko.

**Avoid.**
- Full fursuits, anthropomorphic muzzles, fur-covered bodies, digitigrade legs — that crosses into Furry.
- Floppy dog ears, round dog tails, bone motifs, harness-and-muzzle gear — that is Puppygirl.
- Fox, wolf, or bunny ears when the intent is Neko (other kemonomimi have their own traits).
- Overt sexualization, lingerie-only framing, or bondage hardware beyond a cute bell/heart-ring collar — that drifts to Erokawa or petplay-centric imagery.
- Dark, gritty, or horror lighting; grime, desaturated gloom, gore.
- Saturated non-pastel primaries (fire-engine red, electric blue, neon green) as base colors.
- Hard tech surfaces, mecha, cyberware; glossy latex, spikes, studs (a kawaii cat-ear headset is fine, armor is not).
- A realistic wild cat presented documentary-style with no kawaii framing or dress-up.
- Hyper-masculine, muscular presentation as the default (catboys lean feminized, though masculine clothing is occasionally legitimate).

## Discrimination Cues

**Against Erokawa:** Erokawa fuses erotic and cute — seductive poses, lingerie, suggestive framing, glossy dark accents. Neko keeps the body clothed in cozy knits and frills, the gaze playful or shy rather than seductive, blush reading as embarrassment not arousal; its collar carries a bell, not a leash hook as focal hardware. Because Neko recurs inside Erokawa, judge the ratio: cuteness dominant means Neko.

**Against Puppygirl:** check the animal signifiers. Pointed triangular ears with inner fluff, a long thin tail, and paw-bean mittens are Neko; floppy ears, a stubby round tail, bone prints, and heavier petplay equipment are Puppygirl, which also leans harder into owner/pet power imagery.

**Against Animecore:** Animecore is an overload of anime imagery itself — screenshot collages, 90s cel palettes, glitch and sparkle edits across any fandom — with no required cat features and no required pastel palette. Neko always carries cat-person signifiers and a kawaii pastel ground; remove the ears and tail and Animecore can survive, Neko cannot.

**Against Furry:** Furry depicts anthropomorphic animals — animal face, muzzle, fur-covered body, fursuit construction. Neko keeps human skin and face; catness is additive prosthetic (ears, tail, mittens), and the community explicitly distinguishes the two.

**Against Moe:** Moe is the broad affective cuteness of anime — large eyes, small mouth, soft cheeks — with no animal requirement. Neko is moe plus the cat apparatus; an image with only wide-eyed sweetness and no ears, tail, paws, or bell is Moe, not Neko. (Neighboring pastel styles like Yume Kawaii and Sanriocore similarly share the palette but lack the embodied cat-person: dreamy medical-creep or character-goods focus respectively.)

**Honest gaps:** the infobox's yellow and rose gold appear mostly as bell metal and confection tones rather than full garment colors; maid-café settings are well documented in text but sparse in imagery; and petplay elements (collars, rings) are visually minor compared to the overwhelming emphasis on fluff, paws, and pastel. Weight generation accordingly.
