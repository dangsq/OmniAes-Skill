# Liminal Space

Liminal Space (also liminality, liminalcore, anemoiacore, liminal horror) is the aesthetic of the threshold: places built to be passed through — hallways, waiting rooms, parking lots, highway rest stops, escalator banks, airport gates — photographed or rendered at the hour when nobody is passing through. It emerged as an internet subculture in the late 2010s, catalyzed by The Backrooms creepypasta ("what you see when you no-clip out of reality," 2019), but its subject matter is deliberately older: the architecture, furniture and decor of roughly 1970–2005, the built background of a collective childhood. Its intellectual ancestor is the "non-place" of supermodernity; its cinematic ancestors are the empty sprawling hotel of *The Shining* and the deserted airport of *The Langoliers*. The aesthetic's core operation is subtraction: take a space whose entire meaning is human transit, remove the humans, and let the leftover familiarity turn uncanny.

## Visual Identity

### Palette rule

The palette is the color of ordinary buildings, not of design. One image typically lives inside a single family below, plus shadow; saturation stays muted except in the two licensed exceptions (play-area memory color, and the surreal render branch). A faded, slightly off cast — greenish fluorescent, magenta film drift, washed-out highlight — is the norm.

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **Empty transitional interiors** — corridors, hallways, stairwells, waiting rooms, hospital corridors at night, mall concourses, escalators, airport gates. The space must read as *between*: origin and destination, never the destination itself.
2. **Empty transitional exteriors** — parking lots, highway rest stops, playgrounds, roadside attractions, vacant houses, abandoned malls, suburban street corners at no particular hour.
3. **Period building fabric** — linoleum and vinyl composite tile, drop-ceiling acoustic tiles, cool-toned fluorescent troffers, beige ceramic tile, wood paneling, patterned contract carpet, institutional furniture (plastic chairs, porcelain sinks, toilet stall partitions, lamppost-style mall fixtures).
4. **Absence as subject** — no people, ever; a frozen, waiting appearance; optionally a trace of recent use (one chair askew, a forgotten cup) that sharpens the absence.
5. **White noise** — the infobox's second key motif: visual grain, camera noise, compression artifacts, soft blur, date stamps, the implied hum of fluorescents; media degradation is content, not damage.
6. **Subtle wrongness** — nonsensical architecture, misplaced elements, a door opening onto nothing, one door too many, moody lighting tricks; light Vaporwave/psychedelic/glitch editing is permitted but heavy editing destroys liminality.
7. **Empty game-space look** — rudimentary 3D renders, Source-engine emptiness, PS1-era geometry, server maps with no players; a legitimate branch, not an imitation.
8. **Still water rooms** — tiled pools, changing rooms, shower banks (the Poolcore overlap); water present but unmoving, unswum.
9. **Childhood-memory objects** — foam cubes, ball pits, cloud murals, play blocks, slide structures; innocence preserved under glass, with the children missing.

### Materials and textures

Sheen and staleness: buffed linoleum reflecting ceiling panels, grout lines, fissured acoustic tile, carpet-tile seams, plastic laminate, aluminum-framed glass, plasterboard, faded paint, safety netting, foam, porcelain. Surfaces look maintained-but-unattended — cleaned yesterday, touched by nobody since.

### Composition and lighting

The text prescribes no composition; the corpus converges on a still, naïve camera: one-point perspective down a receding corridor, flat frontal elevations of a sealed empty room, centered symmetry or slightly off-kilter snapshot framing, thresholds (door, stair, escalator, gate, window) held inside the frame. Lighting does the emotional work: even cool fluorescent wash; flat overcast daylight; dead night with isolated pools of light and a glowing exit sign; blown-out window light erasing the outside; direct flash falling off into dark interior. Shadows are black-and-grey and swallow ceiling cavities and corridor ends.

## Mood & Values

Uncanniness and comfort in the same frame. The style's stated values — uncanniness, surreality, nostalgia, familiarity, discomfort, comfort — are not contradictions but a single braid: anemoia, nostalgia for rooms one half-remembers or never lived; hauntology, the future that stopped arriving; the dread of a place whose purpose is people, standing empty. There is no threat in frame, only the expectation of one. The emotional dial runs from warm melancholy (a Christmas tree glowing in a dark living room) to cold apprehension (a windowless corridor of identical doors), and both ends are correct.

## Era & Origins

Etymologically from Latin *limen*, threshold. As subculture: 2019 onward, from The Backrooms 4chan post, spreading through r/LiminalSpace, found-footage video, and empty multiplayer game maps. As subject: the late 20th and early 21st century — 90s and early-2000s decor, mall and school and hospital stock of the 70s–90s. It reacts against seamless contemporary digital polish by weaponizing obsolete media fidelity (compression, grain, date stamps) and against present-day busyness by subtracting every person.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Remove all people; absence is the subject, not a staging choice.
2. Make the space's transitional function legible — it must be a between-place, or a once-lived-in place now vacant.
3. Dress it in period building fabric: drop ceilings, troffers, linoleum, beige tile, contract carpet, institutional fixtures.
4. Let lighting carry the unease: fluorescent evenness, overcast flatness, night pools of light, blown highlights, flash falloff.
5. Degrade the medium one notch: grain, noise, soft compression, optional date stamp. Stop before it becomes a glitch edit.
6. Keep the camera still and amateur-plausible; no drone sweeps, no hero angles.
7. Allow at most one wrong note — an impossible door, a misplaced object, geometry that almost parses.
8. Set the dial somewhere between comfort-nostalgia and dread; never land on neutral documentation.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be any interior or exterior in the motif list, including renders and game-map emptiness. Composition may be receding perspective, sealed frontal room, or cropped threshold. Lighting may span harsh noon, overcast, dusk, deep night. Color handling may sit in any single palette family above. Medium may be lo-fi film, early camera phone, VHS still, or rudimentary 3D render. Mood may slide from tender to foreboding. A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.

**Avoid.** People, crowds, pets, human silhouettes. Monsters, gore, visible threats — dread stays ambient. Heavy glitch, vaporwave grids, psychedelic overlays, scanline abuse (too much ruins liminality). Contemporary 2020s interiors: smart-home devices, matte-black fixtures, LED strips, Scandinavian minimalism. Cozy lived-in clutter and hygge warmth. Glossy staged real-estate photography light. Overlaid meme text or dense narrative signage. Dreamcore floating objects and collage surrealism. Motion blur, traffic light trails. Pure wilderness with no built threshold. Neon cyberpunk saturation.

## Discrimination Cues

- **After Hours** also loves empty night spaces, but it seeks peace: tranquil neon, rain sheen, an invitation to linger. Liminal Space's night is apprehensive and dreamlike; the emptiness presses you out instead of drawing you in.
- **Dreamcore** decorates the dream — pastel surrealism, floating imagery, overt dream-logic. Liminal Space keeps plausible real architecture and subtracts people; its wrongness is a whisper, not a collage.
- **Mizuiro** is bright, airy blue-white freshness with youthful optimism. A tiled liminal washroom or pool may share its surfaces, but Mizuiro's air is clean and hopeful; liminal air is stale, humming, and watched.
- **Poolcore** celebrates pools: sparkling water, summer light, tile joy. Liminal borrows the pool room only when the water is still, the deck empty, the light dead or clinically overbright — the echo after the swimmers left.
- **Precisionism** paints architecture with crisp heroic geometry, clean edges, no grain, no nostalgia. Liminal Space is photographic or lo-fi rendered, its geometry banal or subtly wrong, its meaning generated entirely by absence and media decay.

**One-glance test:** an ordinary threshold-place from the recent past, empty of people, lit by fluorescents or dead night, slightly degraded in fidelity, familiar enough to remember and wrong enough to leave — that is Liminal Space.
