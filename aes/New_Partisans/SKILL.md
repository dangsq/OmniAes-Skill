# New Partisans (Novi Partizani)

A short-lived music-and-visual movement of the mid-1980s Yugoslav rock scene, centered in Sarajevo and carried by Bijelo Dugme, Plavi Orkestar and Merlin. New Partisans was a deliberately pro-Yugoslav, anti-nationalist statement made at the moment when post-Tito state narrative felt hollow and Western consumerism and ethnic nationalism were both rising. Its visual language was codified almost entirely through **album sleeve design**: the juxtaposition of Yugoslav socialist heritage (Partisan photography, socialist realist art, state symbols) with Western pop culture (Beatles-style group montage, Hollywood star portraiture) and classical or Romanticist painting. The result is a grand, slightly kitsch collage aesthetic in which state-sanctioned heroism and commercial stardom are blurred until they look like the same thing.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette (rule, not recipe)
The ground is **muted military tone**: olive drab and wool grey (`#4A5320`–`#6B705C`), khaki drab (`#8A7D5A`), charcoal (`#232323`), and the silver-grey or sepia of archival print (`#B7A98C`, paper white `#F1EDE2`). Into this ground, **red is the punctum and never mere decoration**: flag red and enamel-star red (`#B01E24`–`#E03A3E`) appear as the star on a cap, a flood of duotone ink, a slogan block, a banner or a painted flag. Two *quotation registers* may temporarily override the muted ground, because the style is built on citation: (a) the **Romanticist oil register** — earth ochres, embroidered cream linen, cerulean sky (`#8FB4D9`, `#A9743F`) — when a 19th/early-20th-century painting is reproduced; (b) the **international-leftist pop register** — saturated poster blocks of magenta, lemon, grass green and cyan, or a monochrome red flood — when the sleeve quotes screenprint pop or socialist poster art. Gold bullion (`#C9A227`) appears only as braid, epaulettes and aiguillettes on tunics. A correct image keeps the muted-ground-plus-red discipline even when one quotation register dominates a zone of the frame.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the movement documents them)
1. **The red star, petokraka** — enamel pin on a cap, backdrop emblem, typographic ornament; the movement's single most re-appropriated symbol.
2. **The Partisan uniform kit, stylized rather than reenacted**: the *titovka* side cap or peaked cap, JNA-mirroring tunics, heavy military boots, and *partizanke* — hand-knitted wool socks — often worn under leather rock jackets.
3. **Historical WWII photographs recontextualized**: the grainy portrait of a smiling female Partisan (the Kozarčanka type) lifted from archive into sleeve, duotone or split-cover treatment.
4. **Slogans and logotypes**: "Bratstvo i jedinstvo", "Smrt fašizmu", band names in bold condensed uppercase, Latin or Cyrillic, often in a band across the top edge or a red block.
5. **Socialist realist art quoted**: mass choreographed tableau (ballet, choir), heroic painted figures, painted flags.
6. **Classical/Romanticist painting quoted**: the Kosovo-Maiden type — embroidered folk dress, a wounded soldier, open sky — reproduced full-bleed under a modern logotype.
7. **Western pop parody and the heroism/stardom split**: Sgt.-Pepper-style assemblies of historical figures, or a diptych opposing a glamour star portrait to a Partisan portrait.
8. **Folk layer**: woven wool, embroidery, Balkan folk costume fragments beneath the military gear.
9. **Montage devices**: torn-paper edges, photo strips collaged along a bottom margin, grid panels of repeated screenprinted portraits, split covers.

### Materials and textures
Offset-litho print grain and halftone dots; duotone and screenprint ink; torn paper edges; archival photograph grain, scratches and soft focus; the varnished craquelure of reproduced oil painting; wool felt and hand-knit wool; worn leather boots and jackets; the hard gloss of an enamel star; bullion braid and brass buttons. Everything reads as **printed matter or worn cloth**, never as a clean digital surface.

### Recurring subjects
Band group portraits in modified uniform; sleeve montages and split covers; live stage tableaux in uniform and partizanke; object still-lifes of the cap or star; reproduced paintings; choreographed mass tableau; collaged crowds of historical personalities.

### Composition and lighting
Square sleeve format is the natural frame. Composition favors **juxtaposition structures**: split/diptych opposition, torn-layer montage with a sepia photo strip, portrait grids, full-bleed painting capped by a type band, or a frontal group lineup against a painted arcade, flag or star backdrop. Lighting is print-mediated and legibility-first: flat frontal studio flash for sleeve photography, hard stage spots against black for live shots, diffuse daylight in archival photos, even museum light on painting reproductions — or no lighting nuance at all once a duotone conversion flattens the image. High contrast (black-and-white, red duotone) or flat studio evenness; never moody atmospheric gradient light.

## Mood & Values
Earnest romanticism delivered with a wink of parody; nostalgic longing for the anti-fascist struggle and for "Brotherhood and Unity"; pacifist sentiment beneath heroic posing; the humility of the "soldier of the people" fused with stadium-scale stardom. There is melancholy under the kitsch grandeur — a unity being celebrated precisely because it was already failing. Values expressed: pro-Yugoslavism, anti-nationalism, pacifism, shared memory over ethnic division.

## Era & Origins
Sarajevo, Yugoslavia, roughly 1984–1988. Precedent set by Bijelo Dugme's 1984 sleeve quoting a Romanticist painting; consolidated through 1985–86 sleeves by Plavi Orkestar and Merlin; dissolved by 1988 as ethno-nationalist politics overtook the unity narrative. It reacts simultaneously to the vapid late-state socialist narrative, to Western commodity culture, and to rising ethnic nationalism — and it was itself accused of kitsch and of Communist-Chic-style commercial appropriation, a tension that is part of its character.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Build the image as **montage of two registers**: at least one socialist-heroic or archival element colliding with one commercial-pop or classical-art element.
2. Keep **palette discipline**: muted olive/grey/khaki ground, red as star/flag/slogan/flood; one quotation register (Romanticist oil or saturated poster pop) may claim a zone.
3. Assert **print materiality**: halftone, grain, torn edges, duotone, square sleeve logic.
4. Treat **type as icon**: condensed uppercase slogans or logotypes, Latin or Cyrillic; a chrome or script pop logo is a valid counterpoint.
5. Costume is **modified, not reenacted**: gold braid over olive wool, leather jacket over tunic, knitted socks under boots.
6. **Blur hero and star**: pose pop performers like Partisans and Partisan portraits like pop stars.

**Variation axes (a correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image)**
- *Subject*: band portrait, mass tableau, object still-life of cap/star/socks, painting reproduction, archival portrait, crowd collage, stage scene.
- *Composition*: split cover, torn montage, portrait grid, full-bleed painting with type band, frontal lineup, slogan band header.
- *Lighting*: flat studio flash, hard stage spot on black, archival daylight, painterly diffuse, or duotone flattening.
- *Color handling*: muted full color, black-and-white grain, red or monochrome duotone flood, saturated poster blocks as quotation.
- *Mood*: earnest romantic, wry parody, melancholic nostalgia, celebratory unity.

**Avoid**
- Generic Communist-Chic décor with no Yugoslav Partisan specificity (lone hammer-and-sickle or Che motif as fashion, empty irony).
- Faithful state-propaganda poster or heroic reenactment without pop/commercial framing (that is Socialist Realism).
- Post-1990 war imagery, ethnic-nationalist insignia, combat violence or blood (the movement's values are pacifist and unifying).
- Modern digital gloss: HDR, seamless gradients, clean vector minimalism, smartphone-photo realism.
- Contemporary camouflage patterns, tactical gear, or accurate museum reenactment costume.
- Punk/new-wave anarchy signage, neon, safety pins; likewise garage-flyer crudeness and slang humor (New Primitivism's territory).
- Retrospective Yugo-nostalgia memorabilia: Tito portraits, blue pioneer caps, baton-relay souvenirs, soft café sepia.
- Anachronistic typography (2000s default sans, web fonts) and modern saturated color photography outside the quotation registers.
- Cynical fashion-editorial emptiness that strips the symbols of all romantic charge.

## Discrimination Cues
- **Communist Chic** consumes communist symbols globally and ironically (Che faces, red stars as décor) with no Yugoslav content; New Partisans is always Yugoslav-specific — titovka caps, petokraka, Kozarčanka-type photos, partizanke, Cyrillic/Latin unity slogans — and always framed inside rock-stardom packaging and sleeve montage.
- **New Primitivism**, its Sarajevo contemporary, is anti-glamour: urban mahala slang, deliberate bad taste, flyer roughness, comedy; it lacks the uniformed heroics, Romanticist quotation and grand kitsch of New Partisans.
- **Socialist Realism** is the original state art: earnest idealized heroes, no irony, no pop citation, no commercial logotype; New Partisans quotes it but breaks the frame with torn paper, duotone, star-portrait splits and band branding.
- **Yugo-Nostalgia** is retrospective post-1990s warmth — Tito portraits, pioneer iconography, gentle sepia memorabilia; New Partisans is contemporaneous 1980s production, militarized Partisan rather than pioneer imagery, aggressive red and print collage rather than sentimental souvenir softness.
