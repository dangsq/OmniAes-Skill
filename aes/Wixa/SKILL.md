# Wixa

Wixa is a Polish youth subculture built around fast, hard electronic music (hardcore, gabber, hard trance, hands up, "neo-gabber") and a fashion aesthetic of deliberate bad taste. It lives in two eras: the fluorescent rave hedonism of the late 1990s Ekwador club scene, and the 2010s Warsaw revival led by WIXAPOL S.A., which retooled that hedonism as ironic, post-shame social satire. Visually, Wixa is a collision of three wardrobes — Polish working-class Dresiarz streetwear, classic rave gear, and football-ultras paraphernalia — photographed with the cheapness of a pocket camera flash or a compressed club video. Nothing in it is sincere; everything in it is committed to at 180 BPM.

## Visual Identity

### Color palette

The infobox core is **black, white and yellow**, and the image record confirms yellow as the scene's identifying hue: acid yellow knit scarves, yellow jersey panels, yellow smiley discs. Around that core sits a sanctioned neon accent range inherited from rave fluorescents and color-block sportswear.

| Role | Range | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Core black | `#0A0A0A`–`#1C1C1C` | tribal prints, caps, mesh, night backgrounds |
| Core white | `#F2F2F2`–`#FFFFFF` | jersey tees, socks, balaclavas, blown flash highlights |
| Signature yellow | `#FFD400`–`#FFE600` | ultras scarves, smiley discs, logo ground |
| Neon magenta / hot pink | `#FF00AA`–`#FF4FA0` | pixel video text, tracksuit panels, gloves |
| Acid green | `#B6FF00`–`#39FF14` | glowsticks, vests, UV accents |
| Electric blue / teal | `#0047FF`–`#00C2C2` | windbreaker blocks, club light beams |
| Violet / purple | `#7A00FF`–`#9D00FF` | jacket panels, stage light |

Rule of thumb: a Wixa image always carries the black–white–yellow spine; neon accents and pastel-free color-block panels (pink, red, teal, purple) enter as sportswear blocks or light, never as a tasteful gradient.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how strongly the culture documents them)

1. **Football-ultras scarves ("szalikowcy" scarves)** — the scene's "colors": knitted acrylic scarves, most often yellow-and-black or yellow-with-black, emblazoned with a tribal/cybersigil logo and vertical block lettering. Worn around the neck, draped over the head like a hood, held up to camera, or hung as a banner. Stealing one is a ritual humiliation.
2. **Retro tracksuits and color-block sportswear** — Kappa/Adidas/Umbro/Diadora-style polyester jackets and windbreakers in loud panel combinations, plus white socks and wraparound Oakley-style sunglasses.
3. **Cybersigilism / tribal thorn language** — black thorned sigils printed on tees and scarves, tribal-style tattoos, and the signature hybrid: an acid-house smiley overwritten or crowned with black thorn strokes.
4. **Rave fluorescents** — glowsticks, UV and fingerless gloves, safety vests, gas masks, surgical/protective clothing; text-documented strongest for the 1990s Ekwador era and kept alive as revival costume. (In the surviving image record these appear as occasional accents rather than full outfits — treat them as period-accurate but not mandatory.)
5. **Meme and internet layer** — all-caps captioning, dog-meme collages (the Piesapol/Dogpol vein), chaotic merch montages, pixel-bitmap text stamped over video frames.
6. **Mundane Polish infrastructure as stage** — football pitch netting and chain-link fences, concrete stairwells and bunker-like steps, grass verges, grey overcast skies, club corridors with white pillars. The banality is the point.
7. **Protest-rave apparatus** — soundsystem trucks, carried banners and flags in public space, slogans shouted over gabber; the satirical procession parodying religious pilgrimage sits in this family.
8. **Masks and balaclavas** — visible in the video-still record (white balaclavas, face-covering caps, hands hiding faces); text supports them only indirectly through gabber-inspired fashion, so use them as a secondary cue.

### Materials and textures

Polyester sheen and nylon crinkle of tracksuits and windbreakers; coarse knit acrylic with fringe on scarves; plain cotton jersey tees; mesh and fishnet tops; plastic glowsticks; cheap vinyl banner print. Photographic texture is part of the material palette: on-camera flash grain and red-hot skin highlights, slight motion blur, video compression artifacts, letterbox bars, rasterized overlay text.

### Recurring subjects

Groups of friends in deadpan or exaggerated poses (arms slung over shoulders, faces hidden by hands or caps, rude gestures and finger-hearts offered flatly); DJs and crowds in packed dark clubs; dancers mid-footwork; solo portraits against pitch netting; absurdist tableaux in public space (robed processions, banner-bearing formations on a beach); meme collages and merch spreads; video stills of masked performers against saturated graphic backdrops.

### Composition and lighting

Three licensed regimes:
- **Harsh direct flash at night** — club floors, stairwells, venues: black void backgrounds, blown-out skin, hard shadows, snapshot framing, bodies cropped by the edge of the frame.
- **Flat overcast daylight outdoors** — grey-white sky, desaturated green grass, centered group tableaux or lone figures against netting; deadpan, almost documentary banality.
- **Saturated club light / graphic video-still** — blue-magenta beams and UV haze indoors, or flat candy-colored patterned backdrops in letterboxed 16:9 frames with magenta pixel text and logo discs overlaid.

Composition is anti-precious: centered, casual, slightly crooked, crowds allowed to spill out of frame. When graphics appear, they are loud: italic bold sans in all caps, abused © and ™ marks, domain-style jokes, vertical scarf lettering, racing-sponsor jersey prints repurposed as parody.

## Mood & Values

Irreverence, irony, anti-purism, hedonism, high-energy clubbing, satirical social commentary. The emotional register is "post-shame": bad taste embraced so fully it becomes a position, trolling as pleasure, punk-style abrasion delivered with a straight face. Beneath the joke is a genuine ethic of inclusion — the dancefloor as a place where football hooligans and LGBTQ kids stand side by side — and of resistance, turning protest into a mobile rave. Expect euphoria performed deadpan: ecstasy of the body, smirk of the face.

## Era & Origins

Originated in Poland in the late 1990s post-communist transition, centered on the Ekwador nightclub in Manieczki (golden age roughly 2000–2003), under Catholic-media stigma that called rave "satanist"; faded mid-2000s as festivals banned its gadgets. Revived from around 2012 by Warsaw's WIXAPOL S.A. (DJ SPORTY SPICE, DJ TORRENTZ.EU, MIKOUAJ REJW), a name parodying corporate registration, in reaction against the purist seriousness of Polish techno — and, after 2015, against the conservative national turn, culminating in soundsystem-truck protest raves during the 2020 Women's Strike. The aesthetic therefore always carries two clocks: 1999 fluorescent rave memory and 2010s internet-meme present.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Build on the black–white–acid-yellow spine; add at most one or two neon accents or color-block panel families.
2. Choose one of the three lighting regimes (night flash, overcast flat daylight, saturated club/graphic video-still) and commit to its texture: flash grain, grey banality, or compression-plus-overlay-text.
3. Include at least one ultras/sportswear element (scarf, tracksuit block, white socks, wraparound shades) and at least one tribal/cybersigil element (thorn print, sigil tattoo, thorned smiley).
4. Keep surfaces cheap and real: polyester, knit fringe, cotton jersey, concrete, netting, vinyl banner — never luxe fabric or studio seamless.
5. Inject one irony cue: deadpan face, absurd sacred/profaned juxtaposition, all-caps caption, subverted logo, face hidden in mock shame.
6. Let energy show in bodies — hakken-style footwork, raised arms, packed crowds — even when faces stay flat.

**Variation axes.** Subject matter may range across club crowd, group portrait, solo pitch-side figure, dancer, masked performer, protest tableau, meme collage, merch spread, or video still. Composition may range from cropped snapshot chaos to centered deadpan tableau to wide absurdist public-space shot. Lighting may range from black-void flash to grey overcast to UV haze. Color handling may shift yellow dominance toward pink or blue paneling as long as the black-white-yellow spine survives. Mood may slide from stone-faced absurdity to full euphoric sweat. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**
- Minimalist, tasteful, editorial polish; soft golden-hour light; dreamy bokeh; retouched skin.
- Monochrome black techno purism (austere Berghain-style darkness) with no yellow, neon, or joke.
- Sincere PLUR rave: kandi beads, peace signs, unity hands, rainbow smileys without subversion.
- Generic Western EDM festival grammar: main-stage confetti, palm trees, LED wristband crowds, tropical house pastels.
- Clean cyberpunk/vaporwave chrome and pastel grids lacking snapshot grit and streetwear.
- Dresiarz sincerity: tracksuit alone, played straight, with no club, rave, scarf or meme layer.
- Luxury logos, haute couture tailoring, matte premium product photography.
- Solemn activist-poster gravitas; Wixa protest is a rave with a smirk, not an NGO campaign.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Vs Dresiarz:** Dresiarz is the sincere working-class original — tracksuit, flat cap, cigarette, block estate — with no rave neon, no cybersigil, no club, no irony. Wixa wears the same polyester but adds yellow ultras scarves, fluorescents, flash-lit dancefloors and a wink.
- **Vs Gabber:** Dutch gabber is darker, sportier, shaved-head serious; its imagery is militaristic and purist. Wixa is brighter (yellow/white/neon), meme-saturated, trance- and hands-up-inherited, and openly satirical — gabber orthodoxies explicitly reject Wixa's joke-core.
- **Vs Hands Up:** Hands Up visuals are glossy eurodance CD-cover chrome and unironic euphoria. Wixa shares the tempo heritage but substitutes snapshot grit, concrete Polish backdrops and ultras paraphernalia for that sheen.
- **Vs Cybersigilism:** Cybersigilism is the thorn language itself, often as tattoo close-ups or chrome vector art standing alone. In Wixa the sigil is one ingredient — printed on a scarf or tee, worn by a person in a club or on a pitch — never the whole composition.
- **Vs Rave:** Generic rave signals PLUR unity, smiley positivity and candy neon. Wixa swaps love-and-unity for post-shame irony, football-hooligan scarves, Polish political satire and harsher, cheaper photography; its smiley arrives crossed out by thorns.
