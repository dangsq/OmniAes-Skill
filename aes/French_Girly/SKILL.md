# French Girly

French Girly (フレンチガーリー) is an elegant, feminine aesthetic created by and popular among young women in Japan in the late 2010s. It is a substyle of Japanese Girly Kei that borrows the material culture of antique France — Haussmann-era moldings, marble staircases, gilded mirrors, botanical rose prints — and filters it through kawaii sensibility and luxury-prep fashion codes (tweed, pearls, quilting, gold). It lives on Instagram, in Japanese fashion magazines, in YouTube lookbooks, and around a constellation of indie brands, above all Epine, whose rose-and-thorn wordplay ("nulle rose sans épine") captures the style's wit: sweetness with a sharp, tailored edge. It is distinct from the Western "French Girl" look and from the Tumblr-era Pink Parisian aesthetic; this is a Japanese reimagination of Paris, kept indoors and dressed up.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The core is four colors plus two metallic neutrals: **pink** (blush through dusty rose), **beige/cream**, **white**, and **black** used structurally in small sharp doses — a neck bow, skirt check, boot, trim — never as a field color. Gold/brass and pearl act as hardware neutrals; gray enters only through houndstooth and plaid weave, and illustration work may cool toward lavender-gray backgrounds.

Rule: saturation stays low-to-medium, whites lean warm, and any black is outline-like — it defines silhouettes and bows rather than dominating the frame.

### Signature motifs (ranked by textual prominence)

1. **Vintage French architecture and interiors**: Haussmann-style wall paneling and wainscoting, damask or floral wallpaper, marble staircases, gilded oval mirrors, brass wall sconces, arched alcoves, carved white-painted rococo-revival furniture.
2. **Roses and flowers**: roses and baby's breath as stems, botanical rose prints in gold frames, floral lace patterns, flower-bordered illustration frames.
3. **Bows**: oversized satin bows on bags, hair and packaging; large tied neck bows on blouses; bow trim on shoes and skirts.
4. **Silk and lace**: satin sheen on ribbons and fabric drapes, lace collars, cuffs and hem trims, sheer organza overlays.
5. **Desserts**: cream cakes with fruit, tea-time table settings, marble café tables.
6. **Gold mirrors and gilded surfaces** as recurring props and selfie devices.

Fashion vocabulary: berets, tweed fabric, floral lace, houndstooth or plaid, blouses with a large tied neck bow, gold and pearl accents (pearl chain belts, pearl straps), quilted handbags (often with pearl chain straps), pumps and Mary Janes with white knee socks or tights. Presentation conventions: French-language serif typography and aphorisms as decorative overlay, "LOOKBOOK" thumbnails and coordinate collages (sometimes annotated with height/weight), decorative-framed shojo-style fashion illustration, and brand-shop interiors as pilgrimage sites.

### Materials and textures

Tweed bouclé, quilted leather, pearl strands, polished gold metal, satin and silk, floral lace, fluffy fur throws and rugs, veined marble, carved painted wood, damask wallpaper, gingham and houndstooth weave, sheer chiffon/organdy. Surfaces should read touchable and slightly precious; nothing matte-industrial.

### Recurring subjects

Young women in fully coordinated outfits ("coordinates") posed in interiors or on staircases; object still lifes of handbags, shoes, boxes and books; bedroom and entryway vignettes; desserts mid-tea-time; fashion illustration figures with elongated legs and braided or curled hair; indie-brand boutique interiors; mirror selfies.

### Composition and lighting

Portrait orientation dominates. Figures are centered, full- or three-quarter-body, often symmetrical against a paneled wall or staircase; flat lays shot straight down on fur or satin; corner views of rooms; oval-mirror mirror selfies; illustrations enclosed in ornamental borders or set on collage backgrounds. Light is soft, diffused and warm — daylight softened by lace curtains, brass sconce glow, or flat bright studio light for lookbook grids; dessert scenes may dim toward candle-warm. Contrast stays low, highlights bloom gently, and a beige-pink cast unifies the frame.

## Mood & Values

Poised sweetness with a tailored spine. French Girly values curation, propriety and delicacy — the well-made coordinate, the tidy boudoir, the perfect slice of cake — while permitting whimsy: a plush bear on gingham bedding, a comic oversized bow. It is aspirational luxury translated into kawaii terms, and nostalgic for an imagined antique France rather than the real one. Elegance first, cuteness as seasoning; never sloppy, never loud.

## Era & Origins

Emerged in Japan in the late 2010s as a branch of Girly Kei, fed by Instagram, Japanese women's fashion media, YouTube lookbook channels, and indie vendors (Epine, Apres Jour, Cherie Ange, Désir et Raison, Meltie, Michell Macaron, and others). It absorbs Parisian fashion elements shared with Pink Parisian (berets, tweed, plaid) but keeps them inside a Japanese coordinate culture. Adjacent media include *Emily in Paris* and films like *Populaire* as Western touchpoints, not origins.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**
1. Build every frame from the four-color core (pink / beige-cream / white / structural black) plus gold or pearl hardware; keep saturation low and whites warm.
2. Anchor the scene in French antique material culture — molding, damask, gilding, marble — or imply it through objects (a gilt mirror, a carved shelf, a pearl-chain bag).
3. Pair one luxury-prep code (tweed, quilting, pearls, gold buttons) with one kawaii code (satin bow, lace trim, dessert, plush, baby's breath).
4. Use black as outline: bows, trims, checks, shoe leather — small, crisp, defining.
5. Light softly and warmly; low contrast, gentle haze, no hard flash shadows.
6. Prefer portrait framing with symmetrical or centered staging; flat lays and mirror selfies are native compositions.
7. Optional: French serif typography, aphorisms, or LOOKBOOK-style captioning as overlay.

**Variation axes.** Subject may be a fashion coordinate, an interior vignette, an object still life, a dessert, a boutique, a staircase portrait, or a shojo-style illustration. Composition may range from full-body centered figure to overhead flat lay, mirror selfie, room corner, framed illustration, or annotated collage. Lighting may range from lace-filtered daylight to sconce warmth to dim candle-glow for desserts, or flat bright light for lookbook grids. Color handling may lead with pink, lead with beige, lean on gray houndstooth, or cool to lavender-gray in illustration — as long as the four-color core holds. Mood may slide from whimsical-kawaii to poised-elegant. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid**
- Neon or highly saturated color; cool cyan/blue dominance; earthy boho rusts and olives.
- Streetwear, sportswear, denim, logo mania, sneakers; grunge distress or heavy black grain.
- Overt sexiness: bodycon cuts, bare midriffs, plunge necklines, club lighting.
- Western "French Girl" casualness: basket bags, blue jeans, trench coats, Breton stripes as the statement, undone hair.
- Literal Paris tourism: Eiffel Tower, Arc de Triomphe, Seine cafés, bicycle-with-baguette (that is Pink Parisian).
- Lolita costuming: pannier skirts, bonnets, princess sleeves at cosplay density.
- Jirai Kei edge: platform boots, heavy tear-bag makeup, vinyl, chain hardware, sanrio or menhera props, LED-lit dark rooms.
- Minimalist Scandinavian or industrial interiors: raw concrete, matte black fixtures, blank white walls.
- Rococo period costume: pannier gowns, powdered wigs, court dress.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Balletcore** shares pale pink and ribbons but is dancewear-functional: wrap tops, leg warmers, tights, buns, barres and studios. French Girly replaces rehearsal gear with tweed, pearls, quilting and pumps, and stages itself in parlors and staircases, not dance rooms.
- **Pink Parisian** is the Western Tumblr counterpart that goes outdoors: Eiffel Tower, café terraces, streets, red lips, tricolor accents. French Girly keeps Paris indoors — moldings, gilt mirrors, marble steps — and adds kawaii bows, plush and dessert plus Japanese coordinate/lookbook culture.
- **Girly Kei** is the parent style: broader pastels, casier cute mixes, no mandatory French signifiers. French Girly is the subset committed to antique-French material culture, prep-luxury codes and the pink/beige/white/black palette.
- **Jirai Kei** also pairs black with pink and bows, but signals menace: chunky platforms, glossy vinyl, heavy eyeliner, chain hardware, dark bedrooms. French Girly's black is trim and tweed; its hardware is gold and pearl; its light is warm daylight.
- **Larme Kei** is magazine eclecticism: glossy doll makeup, oversized knits, fur stoles, bolder color pops, urban café backdrops. French Girly is more coordinated and antique-anchored, with restrained makeup and architecture doing the styling work.

Recognition checklist: pink-cream-white with small sharp black + pearls/gold; tweed, houndstooth, lace, satin bows; Haussmann-style interior or marble/gilt prop; soft warm haze; a coordinate or still life that feels curated, sweet, and slightly formal.
