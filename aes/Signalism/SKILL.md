# Signalism

## Overview

Signalism (Serbo-Croatian: *Signalizam*) was a neo-avant-garde literary and art movement founded in 1969 in Novi Sad, Yugoslavia, by the poet and artist Miroljub Todorović. Its name comes from the "signal" — the letter, the number, the punctuation mark, the mathematical sign — understood as the fundamental unit of communication. Signalists dismantled traditional linguistic structure and rebuilt it as visual matter: text became texture, formula became ornament, and the page became a laboratory bench. Working across visual poetry, collage, photography, mail art and occasional performance, and circulating through the journal *Signal* and the international Mail Art network, the movement became a hub of Eastern European neo-avant-garde activity. Its pictures look like chaotic machine schematics of poetry: reproduced, duplicated, stamped, annotated — never painted, never polished.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The rule is **monochrome ground plus one signal red**. Photocopy black (roughly #0A0A0A–#222222) and paper white / off-white (#F4F4F0–#FFFFFF) carry the image; a single saturated stamp red (in the region of #D7263D–#ED1C24) punctuates it as gears, rosettes, overprints, tint fields. Red is allowed to decay: opacity ladders from full red through salmon to pale pink (#F2A0A0–#F8C9C9) are a characteristic serial device, as are flat salmon-pink fields printed with punched-dot matrices. Other hues exist in the style but only as tiny found-paper scraps — a flag fragment, a label, a clipped coupon — never as organizing color. What never occurs: full-spectrum or naturalistic color, pastel softness, metallics, neon, smooth digital gradients.

### Signature motifs (ranked by how centrally the movement documents them)

1. **Text as material (visual poetry):** words, letters and punctuation arranged into visual fields; duplicated, mirrored, inverted or distorted type; microtext columns and borders; cluttered "text labyrinths" where reading fails and seeing begins.
2. **Scientific and mathematical notation:** formulas, equations, tables, charts, measurement ticks.
3. **Isolated linguistic signs:** single glyphs, numerals and punctuation marks treated as objects or specimens.
4. **Arrows and diagrammatic connectors:** vectors, pointers, annotation lines binding elements into a pseudo-schematic.
5. **Celestial bodies and charts:** stars, planets, orbits, star maps.
6. **Machine-like diagrams:** gears, cogs, rosettes, schematics, punched-card and perforation dot matrices.
7. **Found reproduced specimens:** antique engraving portraits, banknotes, close-up eyes, stamps, halftone newsprint photographs — borrowed imagery anonymized into data.
8. **Researcher's marginalia:** handwritten ink notes, captions and signatures scrawled beside the machine-printed field.

### Materials and textures

Everything reads as *reproduced* matter, not original paint: photocopier halftone dots, degraded xerox contrast with burnt blacks and blown whites, typewriter and letterpress type, antique book-plate engraving lines, newsprint screens, rubber-stamp ink with slight misregistration, cut-paper edges, stamp-sheet and perforation logic. Surfaces are paper-flat; texture comes from print processes, not from brush or chisel.

### Recurring subjects

Any subject may enter, but only as processed signal: a portrait becomes a specimen framed in microtext, a bird becomes a repeated unit in a row, a body becomes an anthropomorphic diagram assembled from symbols and arrows, money becomes pure pattern. Eyes, profile heads, hands and historical engraved figures recur as the human trace inside the machine.

### Composition and lighting

Four compositional logics dominate: the **serial strip** (three to five near-identical modules in a row or grid on a white ground, varied slightly or faded through an opacity ladder); the **dense full-bleed field** of scattered or packed type; the **annotated diagram** (elements linked by arrows with handwritten notes in the margins); and the **framed specimen** (a central found image enclosed by microtext columns and diagonal text bands). Clutter is a compositional principle, and empty white margin functions as laboratory bench, not as refinement. Lighting is flat and even — scanner or document light. There are no modeled forms, no cast shadows beyond a faint cut-paper edge; all contrast lives in the reproduction process itself.

## Mood & Values

Playful rigor: the clinic of signs run by a mischievous researcher. Signalism values experimentation, anti-traditionalism, the synthesis of science and art, and the "scientification" of poetry — beauty and personal expression discarded in favor of objective experiment, the artist recast as investigator of communication. Beneath the clinical apparatus sits mail-art intimacy: stamped, posted, copied artifacts exchanged across borders, DIY and internationalist at once.

## Era & Origins

Emergent 1969 in Novi Sad, Yugoslavia, around Todorović and colleagues (Zoran Popović, Jaroslav Supek, Slavko Matković and others), published through the journal *Signal*. It reacts against traditional lyric poetry and official art institutions, and inherits Dada collage, the Yugoslav interwar avant-garde (Zenitism), international concrete and visual poetry, early conceptualism, and the period's imagination of cybernetics, computing and linguistics.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.** Build the image as reproduced document, not depiction: halftone, xerox contrast, typewriter/letterpress type, stamp ink. Treat language as matter — set type where drawing would go. Organize with diagrammatic intent: arrows, labels, formulas, modules, even when the result looks chaotic. Repeat: serial duplication of a module or glyph is core grammar. Reserve one saturated red for the signal accent on a black-white ground; let pink be red's echo. Leave the white of the paper active. Add the human hand last: a scrawled annotation, a misregistered stamp.

**Variation axes.** Subject: anything — figure, animal, object, machine, star chart, money, body part — once processed as signal. Composition: serial strip, dense labyrinth, annotated diagram, framed specimen, or hybrids. Color handling: strict black-white-red; black-white with red-to-pink opacity ladder; salmon field with punched dots; monochrome plus a few small color scraps. Mood: clinical schematic, chaotic text storm, or playful mail-art stamp sheet. Lighting: always flat document light; vary only the degree of reproduction degradation. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Painterly brushwork, impasto, canvas texture; illusionistic depth, perspective scenes, rendered space; studio or photographic lighting, soft shadows, glossy 3D renders; clean Swiss-modernist grids with clarity as the virtue; full-color naturalism, rainbow palettes, airbrush gradients; neon, chrome, glow, digital-UI sleekness; romantic or sentimental imagery, heroic figurative painting; pure geometric abstraction containing no linguistic or scientific signs; chance-only chaos with no diagrammatic logic; decorative ornament, gilding, florals.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Zenitism** (the 1920s Yugoslav avant-garde) is interwar, painterly and poster-loud: constructivist diagonals, expressionist brush energy, cosmic heroic figures, blues and reds on hand-made surfaces. Signalism is postwar reproduction media — xerox halftone, serial modules, mathematical notation, stamp-red on monochrome — cool and diagrammatic where Zenitism is hot and declamatory.
- **Dadaism** collages with irony and chance: cut photographs on aged paper, ransom-note typography, absurdity as provocation. Signalism shares collage but submits it to experiment: repeated modules, formulas and computing symbols, diagrammatic connectors, and red used systematically rather than arbitrarily.
- **Modernism / International Style** prizes the clean grid, generous whitespace and universal sans-serif clarity. Signalism inverts every one of these: clutter over clarity, duplication over the unique element, degraded copies over crisp print, white as empty bench rather than compositional refinement.
- **Surrealism** juxtaposes within painted dreamspace: smooth finish, modeled light, uncanny scenes in depth. Signalism juxtaposes signs, not scenes; its space is the flat page, its finish the photocopier, its uncanny the failure of language rather than the logic of dreams.

*Note on evidence:* performance art is documented in the movement's written record but survives visually only rarely, so treat it as context, not as an image template; color beyond the red family is attested but marginal, appearing as small found-paper fragments rather than as palette.
