# Ethnogoth

Ethnogoth — a portmanteau of "ethnic" and "goth," coined by its own practitioners — is a niche fashion substyle of the Goth subculture that emerged in the mid-2010s in Europe, crystallizing around the Wave Gotik Treffen festival scene in Germany and spreading through Instagram's #ethnogoth tag. Its proponents framed it as a thoughtful invocation of history and tradition: traditional and folk clothing from the Middle East, North Africa and Europe (kaftans, kandooras, turbans, veils) worn over a dark, 80s-inspired Goth foundation of dramatic makeup and heavy metallic adornment, most notably Kuchi jewelry from Afghanistan. The style is deliberately gender-neutral, with wearers using traditional garments regardless of their original gendered meaning. It is also a style shadowed by controversy: sustained accusations of cultural appropriation — decontextualizing Berber, Ottoman and Afghan garments and sacred adornments into Western Goth props — drove many original pioneers, including pioneer figure Aurora Neraneve's circle, to abandon the tag. Visually, that history leaves a compact, highly recognizable grammar: a concealed, robed silhouette; a pale, mask-like face; and an avalanche of hammered metal.

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The colour rule is simple: **black cloth carries the image, metal supplies the light, and the face is a pale mask.** Garments default to black; jewel-tone kaftans appear as a documented secondary range (the text names deep red and blue; the image record adds aubergine purple and forest/emerald green), always in dark, low-saturation fields enriched with metallic embroidery rather than bright colour. White exists almost only as face paint and as highlight on silver.

- Cloth black: `#0b0b0d` – `#1c1a1e`
- Face pallor (matte, ivory-cool): `#efe8dd` – `#ddd2c4`
- Silver, tarnished to hammered-bright: `#9aa0a6` – `#e6e8ea`
- Gold, deep to bright: `#8a6a1f` – `#d9b45b`
- Jewel garment accents: oxblood `#4a1420`–`#6b1f2a`, indigo `#1c2540`–`#2a3a6b`, aubergine `#2e1b3a`–`#4b2a5e`, forest green `#14382c`–`#1f5540`
- Lips: black `#0d0a0c`, deep plum `#3d1626`, oxblood `#4a1420`

### Signature motifs, ranked by textual prominence

1. **The kaftan / kandoora robe** — the documented centerpiece: loose, flowing, full-body, silhouette-concealing, in natural fabrics, typically black.
2. **Excessive layered metal jewelry** — Kuchi-derived silver and gold: large pendants, stacked bib necklaces, coin chains, bracelets, and elaborate headdresses. "More" is the correct amount.
3. **Dramatic Goth makeup** — dark smoky eyes in the Romantic Goth lineage, an 80s-inspired base, and facial adornment such as the nath (nose ring chained to the ear). The image record shows the base pushed to a stark, matte, near-white pallor with sculpted brows and black or plum lips; treat the mask-white face as the practiced norm.
4. **Shaved or partially shaved heads** — hairline influence from Sopor Aeternus; remaining hair sometimes backcombed in Trad Goth manner, or left as long side falls against a shaved crown.
5. **Head coverings** — turbans (text-named), extended in practice by sheer black veils and metal crowns: crescent-moon diadems, horned crowns, filigree caps.
6. **Folk ornament on cloth** — embroidered front panels, mirror-work, metallic trim and dotted tie-dye patterns on the garment field.
7. **Forehead adornment** — a bindi-like jewel, dot or painted sigil between the brows (image-observed variant, optional spice rather than core rule).
8. **Occasional props and settings** — a black feather fan, ornate hand-held objects, patterned tapestry or mandala cloth backdrops, carved dark wood interiors (image-observed; use sparingly).

### Materials and textures

Matte, weighty natural cloth (cotton, wool, sheer gauze veils) against hammered, filigreed and coined metal; embroidery thread and mirror-work catching light in small points; feather softness; the slight sheen of sheer sleeves. The essential texture contrast is **absorbent black fabric versus reflective metal** — the metal should read as the brightest object in frame after the face.

### Recurring subjects

Wearers, not objects: solo portraits most of all, then pairs and duos in complementary robes, and mirror-selfie collages showing two views of one outfit. Any gender presentation is in scope; the style's gender neutrality is a stated value. Frames run from bust-length "icon" crops to full-length standing figures, with hands sometimes raised to display rings and bracelets.

### Composition and lighting

Composition tends hieratic: frontal, centered, still, symmetrical — the wearer presented like a religious icon or altar portrait, ornament stacked along the vertical axis of chest and head. Mirror selfies and domestic interiors are the vernacular medium (Instagram-era smartphone photography, flat flash, slight grain, occasional watermark). Lighting ranges from flat direct flash that overexposes the pale face and sparks the silver, to soft window light, to low-key single-source interiors where metal glints out of shadow. Backgrounds — plain walls, tapestries, carved furniture, hallway corners — stay subordinate to the figure.

## Mood & Values

Solemn, sacerdotal, icon-like: ornament as devotion, history worn as tribute. The style values historical invocation, artistic expression, gender neutrality and maximal ornamentation over restraint. Even its casual selfies carry ceremonial pride — a person presenting themselves as an effigy rather than a consumer. Beneath the gravity sits an unresolved tension: the aesthetic's beauty is built on borrowed sacred and traditional objects, and the appropriation critique is part of the style's documented history, not an aside.

## Era & Origins

Mid-2010s, European Goth scene, primarily the German Wave Gotik Treffen milieu; popularized on Instagram. It reacts against the fitted, modern wardrobe of mainstream Goth by proposing concealment and historical citation instead, while inheriting its makeup base from 80s Goth, its eye drama from Romantic Goth and its backcombing from Trad Goth. Accusations of cultural appropriation in the later 2010s caused its decline among original pioneers, which is why the surviving visual record is compact and Instagram-shaped.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Conceal first: a loose, full-length robed silhouette; no tailoring that reveals body shape.
2. Keep both halves of the fusion legible: folk garment *and* Goth face. Either alone is a different style.
3. Mass the metal: multiple necklace layers plus a headpiece; hammered, coined, filigreed surfaces.
4. Mask the face: matte pallor, sculpted or graphic brows, smoky or winged black eyes, near-black lips; optional nath chain or forehead jewel.
5. Resolve the hair logically: shaved crown or sides with long falls, backcombed crest, or fully covered by turban, veil or crown.
6. Pose hieratically: frontal, still, centered; gestures small and ornamental (showing rings, holding a fan).
7. Light for metal: whatever the scheme, silver and gold must catch highlights against matte black.
8. Stage indoors: rooms, tapestry corners, carved wood, festival interiors — the documented stage of the style.

**Variation axes (all allowed to vary)**

- Garment field: black (default) ↔ aubergine, emerald, oxblood, indigo with metallic embroidery.
- Metal temperature: silver-dominant ↔ gold-dominant ↔ mixed stacks.
- Head treatment: bare shaved head with falls ↔ backcombed crest ↔ turban ↔ sheer veil ↔ crescent/horned/filigree crown.
- Framing: bust icon ↔ three-quarter ↔ full length ↔ mirror-selfie collage ↔ symmetric duo ↔ jewelry detail crop.
- Lighting: flat flash ↔ soft window light ↔ low-key warm single source ↔ cool ambient room light.
- Backdrop: plain wall ↔ patterned tapestry ↔ dark carved wood ↔ lived-in domestic clutter ↔ festival interior.
- Mood: sacerdotal solemn ↔ calm pride ↔ faint theatrical smirk.
- Subject: any gender presentation; solo, pair, or small group.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image. A green-kaftan domestic selfie and a candle-lit crowned bust are equally valid Ethnogoth if the rules hold.

**Avoid**

- Fitted Goth silhouettes: corsets, cinched waists, bodycon cuts, miniskirts, fishnets, platform boots as the focal point.
- Bare midriffs, coin bras, hip belts and dance-performance costuming (that is Gothic Belly Dance).
- Victorian mourning lace, roses, bustles and cemetery romance (Romantic Goth).
- Band tees, leather jackets, net sleeves and a full crimped mane as the whole statement (Trad Goth).
- Neon, pastel or candy colour; cybergoth UV elements; rivets and industrial hardware; steampunk brass as a theme.
- Minimalist jewelry: a single delicate chain or small studs.
- Glossy high-key commercial studio looks with seamless backdrops; bright daylight outdoor scenes with casual stance.
- Sexualized posing or framing; the style's ethic is concealment and solemnity.
- Generic boho-festival wear without the Goth face, or generic fantasy-priestess costume without the folk robe cut and Kuchi-type metal.
- Folk vocabularies outside the style's documented fusion grammar (Berber, Ottoman, Afghan, South Asian, European folk) — e.g. war bonnets or kimono lines break the lineage.

## Discrimination Cues

| Style | Silhouette | Face | Metal | Tell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ethnogoth | Concealing kaftan/kandoora robe | Matte pallor mask, smoky eyes, black lips | Stacked Kuchi/Berber bibs, coin chains, crown | Folk robe + Goth face + metal overload, frontal icon pose |
| Goth (mainstream) | Fitted modern black: coats, boots, leather/PVC | Varied, often pale with liner | Studs, chokers, ankh, rings | Modern wardrobe, street or club stance |
| Gothic Belly Dance | Performance costume: coin bra, hip belt, sheer skirt | Stage glow, dramatic but not mask-white | Coin belts and jingling strands | Exposed torso and dance posture |
| Romantic Goth | Victorian mourning: bustle, lace, velvet | Soft smoky, rosy or wan | Cameos, brooches, rosary-like chains | Curls, roses, cemetery-garden air |
| Trad Goth | 80s post-punk: leather, fishnets, band merch | Sharp liner, pale but not icon-mask | Punk silver: chokers, spikes, rings | Full backcombed mane and club energy |

Against Tribal Goth, its closest cousin in jewelry vocabulary, Ethnogoth is distinguished by the robe-centered, fully concealing silhouette and its WGT/Instagram lineage. When evidence is thin — props like feather fans, round dark lenses, forehead sigils — treat them as observed variants, not requirements: the irreducible core is the concealed folk robe, the pallid Goth mask, and the layered hammered metal.
