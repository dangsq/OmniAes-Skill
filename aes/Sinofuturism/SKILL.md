# Sinofuturism

Sinofuturism (中华未来主义) explores future visions bound to Chinese cultural identity. It inherits the Soviet and Eastern European imprint — monumental concrete, slogan banners, faith in heavy infrastructure — but redirects that imagination from rockets-alone toward digital technology, electronics, artificial intelligence, smart cities and digital surveillance. Its defining tension is a double exposure: the hollowed ghost towns of the old industrial north set against first-tier cities that have become intelligent metropolises, so that everyday urban and rural streetscapes read like science-fiction sets. Coined by Lawrence Lek in his 2016 essay film and energized by the global rise of Liu Cixin's *The Three-Body Problem* and *The Wandering Earth*, it is simultaneously a celebration of post-reform acceleration and an ironic mirror held up to Western stereotypes of China (Calculation, Replication, Gaming, Learning, Addiction, Labor, Gambling).

## Visual Identity

**Palette rule.** Build images from four dominant families: **red** (flag and slogan red `#B01116`–`#E60012`, shifting to neon magenta-red `#FF2D55`–`#FF4D8F` on signage), **blue** (deep space-program navy `#0A2A5E` through institutional blue `#1E6FD9` to cyan glow `#35C4E8`–`#7FE3F2`), **beige** (board-formed concrete and poster paper `#B9AE9C`–`#E4DCC8`, cream grounds `#F1E9D6`), and **metallics** (steel and satellite silver `#8E979D`–`#C7CCD1`, iridescent lilac-steel film `#A99BB8`–`#C9BBD6`). Two conditional accents are legitimate: **ink-jade mist green** `#6E8F84`–`#A9C4B5` from the ink-wash animation lineage, and **night amber** `#E8A33D` window-light against teal-black `#061A1E` skies in aerial city views. A correct image need not use all families, but red-plus-blue-plus-concrete-beige is the core chord; pure pastel, earth-organic or gold-purple palettes break the style.

**Signature motifs, ranked by how centrally the tradition documents them.**
1. **Infrastructure as protagonist:** hydroelectric dams and retaining walls, elevated viaducts, transmission pylons, gantry cranes, factory sheds, warehouse racking aisles.
2. **The Chinese city in duality:** stacked smart-metropolis skylines versus rusted ghost-town remnants; surreal streetscapes where old tiled rooftops sit in the shadow of linked megatowers.
3. **Space exploration:** rockets, luminous launch trails, astronauts in white suits, official-commemorative poster graphics with vertical Chinese type.
4. **Electronics and AI:** delivery and quadruped robots, LED dot-matrix boards, surveillance screens, human busts fragmented into data-slips or paper talismans.
5. **Labor and the collective:** workers, crowds, logistics aisles, uniforms — labor as both honor and stereotype.
6. **Socialist-modernist imprint:** red slogan banners painted on walls or stretched on frames, rows of flags, austere concrete porticos.
7. **Ink-wash atmosphere:** mist, smog and layered atmospheric perspective absorbing industrial subjects into mountain-water landscape logic; monochrome ink treatments of factories are canonical.
8. **Cultural signifiers in collision with tech:** bronze vessels, porcelain glaze, classical rooflines, panda motifs — always juxtaposed against neon, chrome or code, never presented as pure heritage.

**Materials and textures.** Board-formed and weathered concrete, welded steel lattice, glass curtain wall, neon tube and LED pixel grain, paper banners and cloth flags, haze and fog as a spatial medium, iridescent synthetic fabric in fashion contexts, ink-on-paper fiber in animated lineage, brushed chrome and solar-panel metal in space contexts.

**Composition tendencies.** Monumental horizontals (dam walls, viaduct decks) and vertiginous stacking (hillside cities, tower clusters); extreme long shots in which humans are specks establishing scale; aerial night grids of window light; vertical poster layouts with column-set Chinese characters, dates and small red accents; symmetrical state-monument framing; counter-scale intimate vignettes (a child and a robot in a sunlit courtyard) are equally valid. Foreground foliage framing a misted valley belongs to the ink lineage.

**Lighting tendencies.** Diffuse overcast daylight thickened by mist; blue-hour teal dusk punctured by red LED and neon bloom; night aerials with amber sparks against near-black teal; flat unmodulated light in graphic poster work; studio gradient washes for fashion; hard specular highlights on metal and glass.

## Mood & Values

Cultural identity and a reflection-and-prospect stance toward modern technology. The Chinese Sci-Fi Futurist strand carries faith in kindness, optimism, collectivism and profound homeland sentiment; Lek's critical strand adds irony, treating technology as social structure — computing, labor, gaming — and staging Western stereotypes deliberately. Hold both: pride in acceleration alongside melancholy for hollowed industrial pasts. The emotional register is sublime but civic — awe at collective achievement, unease at surveillance and replication, tenderness for ordinary people inside enormous systems.

## Era & Origins

Coined in the 2010s (Lek's 2016 essay film, itself inspired by Afrofuturism); rooted in the explosive post-reform-and-opening growth of China and the global arrival of its science fiction (*The Three-Body Problem*, *The Wandering Earth*, Cao Fei's *Red Mist* and *Whose Utopia*, Qiu Anxiong's *New Classic of Mountains and Seas*, Fan Wennan's *China 2098*, *Arknights: Endfield*). It reacts to Western futurism's stereotypes and to the absence of Chinese modernity in global futures imagery.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles.**
1. Make infrastructure or systems the hero; humans annotate scale.
2. Stage the duality: let rust imply the smart city, or the smart city imply the rust.
3. Use Chinese-language text as texture — slogan banners, LED boards, poster columns — legible or半-legible, never lorem-ipsum Latin.
4. Bind the scene with atmosphere: mist, smog, haze, or night neon bloom.
5. Keep the palette chord red/blue/beige/metallic; add jade mist or amber night only as lineage accents.
6. Frame collectivistly: crowds, uniforms, coordinated labor, or a single small figure dwarfed — not a lone armed protagonist.
7. Medium is free: photoreal render, ink-wash animation frame, flat commemorative poster, fashion editorial, night photograph all qualify.

**Variation axes.** Subject: any of dam, viaduct, pylon, warehouse, robot, rocket, astronaut, neon market, ghost town, skyline, courtyard vignette, fashion figure, poster graphic. Composition: aerial, ground-level monumental, vertical poster, intimate counter-scale. Lighting: misted overcast, blue-hour LED, night amber grid, flat poster, studio gradient. Color handling: saturated neon, desaturated concrete, near-monochrome ink. Mood: optimistic monumentality, rustbelt melancholy, ironic critique. **A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules, not any reference image.**

**Avoid.** Heritage-only ornament (dragons, cranes, tang-palette packaging) as the primary language; bamboo, kite or clockwork artisanal technology; Afro-diasporic cosmic-jazz iconography; generic Western cyberpunk alleys with Japanese signage and noir individualism; chrome-only Y3K glitch abstraction with no Chinese infrastructural content; pre-modern literati hanfu scenes without technology; pastel vaporwave; desert wasteland post-apocalypse; heroic lone gunman framing; Latin-alphabet corporate dystopia branding.

## Discrimination Cues

| Neighbor | Tell that separates Sinofuturism |
|---|---|
| Afrofuturism | Afrofuturism centers Black diasporic cosmology, jazz-funk rhythm, gold/purple and ancestral pattern; Sinofuturism centers state-scale concrete infrastructure, Chinese typography and collectivist labor. |
| Guochao | Guochao is consumer-brand national trend: traditional motifs on commercial packaging, retro palettes; Sinofuturism is futurist, technological, infrastructural, often critical rather than celebratory-commerce. |
| Indigenous Futurisms | Land-based, community-specific Native cosmologies and decolonial futures; Sinofuturism is metropolitan, industrial and state-modern in scale and vocabulary. |
| Neo-Chinese Style | Reinterprets classical literati taste — ink, lattice, song typeface — as contemporary elegance; Sinofuturism borrows ink mist only to swallow factories, dams and servers. |
| Silkpunk | Retro-tech fantasy of bamboo, silk, kites and clockwork mechanics; Sinofuturism is near-future digital: concrete, silicon, LEDs, rockets. |

When in doubt, ask: is the technology portrayed as social structure at Chinese national scale, in red, blue, beige and metal? If yes, Sinofuturism.
