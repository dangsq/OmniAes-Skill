# Four Colors

## Overview

Four Colors is the color language of mass personalization: the moment consumer technology stopped being beige and started selling you *your* color. Emerging from late-1990s console color variants and igniting with Apple's iPod "Silhouette" campaign (launched October 2003, saturated through 2004), it ruled consumer product marketing, corporate identity, broadcast idents, packaging, album covers and video game box art from the mid-2000s to the mid-2010s. It is a subgenre of Superflat Pop and a flat, extroverted cousin of Frutiger Aero, overlapping Bright Tertiaries in palette and flat humanist design sensibility. Its conceptual core is a four-slot color system — electric lime, sky blue, hot pink, neon orange — traditionally read in Japan as the four seasons (pink = spring, green = summer, orange = autumn, blue = winter), optionally expanded into a full rainbow. Wherever a product line shipped in multiple candy hues and the advertising showed them all at once, Four Colors was there. It declined as corporations moved to uniform Flat Design and Corporate Memphis by around 2017, but never fully died in Japan (Daiso, Sony, Fujifilm and related retail design kept it alive post-2015).

## Visual Identity

### Palette

The palette is a rule, not a mood: four anchor hues placed roughly at quarter-points around the color wheel for maximum separation, all at high saturation and medium-to-high value. No anchor may be pastel, muted or earth-shifted.

| Slot | Name | Hex range | Hue zone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Electric lime | `#96E000` – `#C8F000` | yellow-green, ~80–95° |
| 2 | Sky blue | `#00A9E2` – `#55C8F7` | cyan-leaning blue, ~195–205° |
| 3 | Hot pink | `#EC008C` – `#FF3D9A` | magenta-pink, ~325–335° |
| 4 | Neon orange | `#F7941D` – `#FF6A13` | red-leaning orange, ~25–35° |

Supporting rules:

- **Neutrals are absolute:** pure white `#FFFFFF` as ground, knockout and earphone-cord color; flat black `#0A0A0A`–`#1A1A1A` as silhouette fill or as a graphic backdrop field. No creams, no greiges, no warm grays.
- **Rainbow expansion is licensed:** yellow (`#FFD400`), red (`#E60012`), violet/purple (`#7C4FA8`–`#8A4BB8`) and teal (`#00A99D`) may join, forming six- to eight-color rainbows — but the four anchors must still dominate or structurally organize the frame (one anchor per panel, per product variant, per zone).
- **Saturation floor:** anchors sit at roughly 85–100% saturation and 75–100% value. Anything dusty, faded or desaturated is outside the style.

### Signature motifs and objects (ranked by how centrally the style documents them)

1. **The color-variant product lineup.** The style's key value is personalization, and its most characteristic image is the same object repeated in different hues: a row, fan, arc or circle of portable music players, handheld consoles, phones, controllers, blenders or earphones, one per color slot. This is the style's thesis statement — choice as identity.
2. **Flat monochrome field with dancing silhouette.** Inherited from the Silhouette campaign: a solid saturated panel, a flat black human figure caught mid-dance or mid-jump, and a thin white earphone cord snaking from ear to a small white device. The figure has no face, no modeling, no interiority — pure vector posture.
3. **The quadrant / panel color grid.** A 2×2 (or strip-wise) division of the frame into solid hue panels, each hosting one silhouette, one tinted portrait, one character bust or one ornament cluster. The grid makes the four-slot system literal.
4. **Vector ornament vocabulary:** concentric rings and target circles, swirls and scroll spirals, polka dots of mixed scale, radial starburst rays, paint drips and splashes, simple floret/bloom shapes, five-point stars. These float on flat fields or erupt behind a hero figure.
5. **Product-as-hero studio render.** A single glossy device centered on white or a pale neutral gradient, evenly lit, with a soft contact shadow and specular streaks along its plastic curves.
6. **Playful corporate logotypes and broadcast idents:** rounded humanist or geometric sans letterforms, often colored per letter, knocked out of solid hue blocks or set against black; event and channel identities built from the four-color circle, bloom or panel system.
7. **Applied surfaces:** video game box art, pop album covers, dance-game key art, packaging, shopping bags, sticky-note stacks — the style travels on commercial substrates.

A note on evidence: the seasonal color symbolism (spring/summer/autumn/winter) is documented conceptually rather than as a fixed visual formula, and treated portrait photography — duotone-tinted faces split across hue panels, fashion models overlaid with flat dots and splashes — is an image-supported extension of the panel-grid rule rather than a text-mandated motif. Music-video appearances are documented by title only; treat them as context, not visual canon.

### Materials and textures

Glossy polycarbonate plastic with hard specular highlights; matte flat vector fields with razor-clean edges; printed paper and board for packaging, bags and note stacks; screen-print and offset registration precision. Surfaces are either perfectly flat (graphic mode) or perfectly slick (product mode). There is no grain, no paper tooth, no distress, no hand-made wobble anywhere in the style.

### Recurring subjects

Portable music players and their accessories; handheld and home game consoles and controllers; phones and small consumer electronics; household gadgets (blenders, earphones); retail packaging, shopping bags and stationery; logos, channel idents and song-contest branding; pop group album covers; dance and party video game key art; stylized character portraits in panel grids; cartoon mascots and toy-like robots.

### Composition and lighting

Two legitimate modes:

- **Graphic mode:** no lighting at all. Flat hue fields, quadrant grids, radial starbursts or all-over ornament scatter; silhouettes and ornaments sit at 100% flatness with hard edges. Black may serve as a backdrop field for blooms and logotypes. Energy is diagonal and centrifugal — dancing figures lean, rays explode, drips fall.
- **Product mode:** high-key, shadowless-to-soft studio light. Even diffuse illumination, a gentle contact shadow under the object, crisp specular streaks on glossy plastic, and a neutral white-to-pale-gray (or faint lilac) gradient backdrop. Multiple color variants fan, stack or line up with catalog precision and generous negative space.

In both modes, composition obeys **one hue per zone**: a panel, a product, a background field or a letterform owns a single anchor color. Hues never blend, never gradient into each other (gradients belong to the backdrop or to plastic gloss, never to the color system itself).

## Mood & Values

Optimism, play, approachability and the democratic promise of personalization: mass production that still lets you pick *yours*. The style feels extroverted, youthful and commercially generous — candy-bright but corporate-clean, energetic but precisely registered. It celebrates consumption as self-expression and technology as toy. Its emotional register is the exclamation point delivered by a design department: loud color, clean edges, zero irony, zero melancholy.

## Era & Origins

Roots in the late 1990s, inside Superflat Pop, Y2K Futurism and primitive Frutiger Aero — notably console color variants such as the Nintendo 64 "Funtastic" series. Ignition in October 2003/2004 with the Apple iPod Silhouette campaign, whose enormous ad spend fixed the silhouette-on-color formula across Western advertising. Prime from roughly 2004 through the early 2010s: iPod nano and shuffle generations, Nintendo Wii, PlayStation 2/3 marketing, Motorola flip phones, culminating in the iPhone 5c (2013). Parallel flourishing in Japan across retail and broadcast design, with the palette read as the four seasons. Decline in the mid-2010s as brands consolidated into uniform Flat Design; by 2017 Corporate Memphis and monochrome minimalism had phased it out in the West, while Japanese retail (Daiso, Sony, Fujifilm and kin) kept it in circulation post-2015, with occasional revivalist game and broadcast work late in the decade.

## Generation Guidance

**Principles**

1. Build the frame from the four anchor slots first; color is structure, not decoration. Assign one anchor per panel, product, field or letter group.
2. Keep everything flat or slick: vector-flat fields with hard edges, or glossy plastic under even studio light. Never both textures at once on the same element.
3. Invoke personalization: show the same object in several hues, or imply the choice (a lineup, a fan, a shelf, a grid of variants).
4. If humans appear, prefer flat black silhouettes in dynamic dance poses with a thin white earphone cord; alternatively use tinted duotone portrait panels. Never fully modeled naturalistic figures as the hero.
5. Use the ornament vocabulary boldly but sparingly per frame: concentric rings, swirls, dots, starburst rays, drips, blooms, stars.
6. Lighting is either absent (graphic mode) or high-key diffuse studio (product mode) with soft contact shadows and specular streaks on gloss.
7. Typography, when present, is rounded humanist or geometric sans, often lowercase, frequently colored per letter or knocked out of a hue block.
8. Finish corporate-clean: precise registration, crisp edges, balanced negative space in product mode, controlled density in graphic mode.

**Variation axes**

- *Subject:* any consumer product, gadget, packaging, shopping bag, stationery, logo or ident, event branding, album cover, game box art, character portrait grid, mascot, retail display, fashion ad.
- *Composition:* quadrant/panel grid; horizontal lineup; fan, arc or circle arrangement; centered hero on a saturated or white field; radial starburst; all-over dot/swirl scatter; logotype lockup on solid ground.
- *Lighting:* none (pure flat graphic) through high-key shadowless studio with pale gradient backdrop.
- *Color handling:* strict four anchors; four plus white/black; rainbow expansion to six–eight hues with anchors dominant; duotone-tinted portrait panels; black-ground variant for blooms and logotypes.
- *Mood:* exuberant dance-party energy; calm catalog display; playful kids' branding; chic pop-album cool.

A correct image can use any subject within this range; it should echo the style's rules — the four-slot palette, flatness, one-hue-per-zone, personalization logic, clean corporate finish — not any reference image. A still life of kitchen scales in four hues on white, a fictional festival ident, or a quadrant grid of dancing animal silhouettes are all valid Four Colors even though no reference shows them.

**Avoid**

- Muted, earthy, pastel or desaturated palettes; beige, cream, kraft, terracotta, sage.
- Grunge, film grain, paper tooth, distress, vintage fade, hand-drawn wobble, painterly brushwork.
- Chiaroscuro, dramatic directional light, night scenes, atmospheric haze, candlelit warmth.
- Corporate Memphis rubber-limbed humans in dusty pastels; Flat Design single-hue corporate minimalism.
- Frutiger Aero's aqua glass, bubbles, lenses, sky-and-grass photo-surrealism; Y2K liquid chrome and metal blobs.
- Technozen matte black voids with a single glowing accent.
- Misregistered, cluttered, budget-print retail chaos (Dollar Store Vernacular).
- Narrative illustration with psychological interiority or story moments (Figuration Narrative).
- Untreated documentary photography: natural skin tones and ambient light as the unedited hero of the frame.
- Hue-on-hue gradients inside the color system; blended or ombre panels.

## Discrimination Cues

- **Bright Tertiaries:** near-identical saturation and flatness, but organized around the tertiary wheel (teal, fuchsia, lime, orange) and at home in illustration, web and flat humanist design generally. Four Colors is diagnosed by its exact anchor set — the sky-blue + hot-pink pair especially — and by its consumer-marketing DNA: color-variant lineups, silhouette-campaign heritage, corporate ident logic. Tertiary harmony without product/personalization context leans Bright Tertiaries.
- **Dollar Store Vernacular:** same loud hues, opposite polish. Look for misregistration, cheap foil, cluttered shelf packaging, mismatched type, budget substrate. Four Colors is precise, clean-edged, generously spaced, corporate-budget glossy.
- **Superflat Pop:** the parent genre. Superflat Pop freely uses Japanese otaku flatness, Murakami-style florals and anime iconography in any palette, including monochrome. Four Colors is its marketing arm: always the four-anchor system plus a product, ident or packaging context; anime-inflected faces appear only as tinted panel portraits or mascots, never as otaku subject matter.
- **Technozen:** dark, matte, meditative technology — graphite and black with a single neon glow, zen minimalism. Four Colors is extroverted, multi-hue, daylight-bright; its black is a flat graphic ground or silhouette fill, never an atmospheric void with luminous accents.
- **Figuration Narrative:** painterly or comics-derived scenes of characters inhabiting story moments, with psychological depth and often classic or muted palettes. Four Colors figures are non-narrative: silhouettes and mascots dancing in color space, no plot, no interiority, no modeled form.

Successor confusion check: uniform single-palette minimalism with abstract pastel humans is Flat Design / Corporate Memphis — the styles that replaced Four Colors, and therefore its clearest negatives.
